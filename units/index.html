How each syllabus unit moved between the 2025 and 2026 papers, and how often each topic came up.

What changed from 2025 to 2026

Most units held steady. Analysis fell sharply, while linear algebra, methods and number theory grew.

Every topic, every paper

Darker or longer means more questions. Sorted by the total across 2025 and 2026, out of 140. The 2019 column covers only the 35 questions that survive, so it stays out of the total.

All 10 units

  • 1Abstract algebra6–7expectedSteady

    Six or seven questions every time, mostly short calculations on groups and fields.

  • 2Linear algebra8–10expectedRising

    The biggest unit. It grew to 10 questions in 2026, with Jordan forms and quadratic forms.

  • 3Real and functional analysis3–9expectedFell in 2026

    Fell from 9 questions to 3 in 2026. With only two full papers that is a range, not a trend: plan for anything from 3 to 9.

  • 4Complex analysis6–7expectedSteady

    Six or seven questions, mostly residues, contour integrals and Möbius maps.

  • 5Topology and differential geometry7–8expectedSteady

    About half topology, half curves and surfaces. Manifolds are new in the syllabus and appeared in 2026.

  • 6Ordinary and partial differential equations7expectedSteady

    Exactly 7 in both papers. The same ideas repeat: singular solutions and PDE types.

  • 7Calculus of variations, integral equations, transforms5–8expectedRising

    Grew from 5 to 8. Integral equations alone went from 2 questions to 6.

  • 8Mechanics and fluid dynamics7expectedSteady

    Exactly 7 in both papers. Mostly Lagrangian and Hamiltonian ideas, with two or three fluid questions.

  • 9Operations research and numerical analysis7–8expectedSteady

    Seven or eight questions, roughly half linear programming and half numerical methods.

  • 10Number theory, Vedic maths and history6–7expectedRising

    Number theory itself went from 3 questions to 7, three of them on Legendre symbols.

Built from every paper that survives: the full 2025 and 2026 papers (140 maths questions) and half of 2019 (35, from a solution video). 2015 left only an answer key, and nothing was found for 2021. The commission has never published its booklets. Unit counts use 2025 and 2026 only, so they show a range, not a trend.

2025 answers match the official key. 2026 has no key yet, so its answers were worked out by hand and marked high, medium or low confidence, and every unsure one was solved again, blind. 2019 answers come from the video and are not checked against the official key.
