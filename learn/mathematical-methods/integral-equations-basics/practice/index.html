  1. Question 1Easy
    The equation \(\varphi(x)=x+\int_0^x(x-t)\,\varphi(t)\,dt\) is

    Why

    The upper limit of the integral is the variable \(x\), so it is Volterra. The unknown \(\varphi(x)\) also appears outside the integral, so it is of the second kind.
  2. Question 2Easy
    Which of the following is a Volterra integral equation of the first kind?

    Why

    Volterra means a variable upper limit; first kind means \(\varphi\) appears only under the integral. Only the first option has both. The second option is Fredholm of the first kind.
  3. Question 3Easy
    Which of the following kernels on \([0,1]\) is symmetric but not separable?

    Why

    \(xt\), \(x+t=x\cdot1+1\cdot t\) and \(\sin(x+t)=\sin x\cos t+\cos x\sin t\) are all finite sums \(\sum a_i(x)b_i(t)\), hence separable (and symmetric). \(\min(x,t)\) is symmetric but needs infinitely many terms.
  4. Question 4Medium
    The smallest number of terms \(n\) in a representation \(K(x,t)=\sum_{i=1}^na_i(x)b_i(t)\) of \(K(x,t)=(x+t)^2\) is

    Why

    \((x+t)^2=x^2\cdot1+x\cdot2t+1\cdot t^2\). The functions \(x^2,x,1\) are linearly independent and so are \(1,2t,t^2\), so no representation with fewer terms exists: \(n=3\) (the rank of the kernel). Expanding into four terms \(x^2\cdot1+x\cdot t+x\cdot t+1\cdot t^2\) is not minimal.
  5. Question 5Medium
    The initial value problem \(y''+y=x\), \(y(0)=1\), \(y'(0)=0\) is equivalent to the integral equation

    Why

    Write \(y''=x-y\) and integrate twice from \(0\): \(y(x)=y(0)+xy'(0)+\int_0^x(x-t)\big[t-y(t)\big]dt=1+\frac{x^3}6-\int_0^x(x-t)y(t)\,dt\). Verified numerically with the true solution \(y=x+\cos x-\sin x\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    The value of \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\displaystyle\int_0^x x\,t^2\,dt\) at \(x=2\) is

    Why

    Leibniz: \(F(x,x)+\int_0^x F_x\,dt=x\cdot x^2+\int_0^xt^2dt=x^3+\frac{x^3}{3}=\frac{4x^3}3\). At \(x=2\): \(\frac{32}3\). Dropping the integral gives \(8\); dropping the boundary term gives \(\frac83\). Direct check: the integral is \(\frac{x^4}3\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    Let \(L[y]=(p\,y')'+q\,y\) and let \(G(x,t)\) be its Green’s function (\(L[G]=\delta(x-t)\)) with homogeneous boundary conditions. At \(x=t\) the derivative \(\partial G/\partial x\) has a jump equal to

    Why

    Integrating \((pG_x)'+qG=\delta(x-t)\) across \(x=t\) gives \(p(t)\,\big[G_x(t^+,t)-G_x(t^-,t)\big]=1\), so the jump is \(1/p(t)\). \(G\) itself is continuous but its first derivative is not. Example: for \(y''\) the jump is \(1\).
  8. Question 8Hard
    The Green’s function of \(y''=f(x)\) on \([0,1]\) with \(y(0)=0\) and \(y'(1)=0\), so that \(y(x)=\int_0^1G(x,t)f(t)\,dt\), is

    Why

    \(u_1=x\) satisfies \(u(0)=0\); \(u_2=1\) satisfies \(u'(1)=0\). \(W=u_1u_2'-u_1'u_2=-1\), \(p=1\), so \(c=1/(pW)=-1\) and \(G=-u_1(x_<)u_2(x_>)=-\min(x,t)\). Check with \(f=1\): \(y''=1\), \(y(0)=0\), \(y'(1)=0\) gives \(y=\frac{x^2}2-x\), and \(\int_0^1-\min(x,t)\,dt=\frac{x^2}2-x\). The third option is the Dirichlet Green’s function.
  9. Question 9Medium
    The boundary value problem \(y''+\lambda y=0\), \(y(0)=y(2)=0\) is written as \(y(x)=\lambda\int_0^2\mathcal G(x,t)\,y(t)\,dt\) with the (positive) Green’s kernel \(\mathcal G\). The smallest characteristic value is

    Why

    Non-trivial solutions are \(y=\sin\frac{n\pi x}2\), because \(y(2)=0\) requires \(\sin2\sqrt\lambda=0\), \(\sqrt\lambda=\frac{n\pi}2\). So \(\lambda_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2}{4}\) and the smallest is \(\frac{\pi^2}4\). (\(\pi^2\) is the answer for the interval \([0,1]\).)
  10. Question 10Medium
    Assertion (A): A second-order initial value problem can be converted into a Volterra integral equation of the second kind. Reason (R): With both conditions given at \(x=0\), the repeated integral \(\int_0^x\int_0^s g(u)\,du\,ds\) equals \(\int_0^x(x-t)g(t)\,dt\), which has a variable upper limit.

    Why

    Integrating \(y''=g-Ay'-By\) twice from \(0\) gives integrals with upper limit \(x\), and Cauchy’s formula \(\int_0^x\!\!\int_0^s g=\int_0^x(x-t)g(t)\,dt\) turns them into a single integral with a variable upper limit: a Volterra equation. R is exactly why A works.
  11. Question 11Easy
    If \(\varphi(x)=1+x+\displaystyle\int_0^x(x-t)\,\varphi(t)\,dt\), then \(\varphi(1)=\)

    Why

    Differentiate twice: \(\varphi'=1+\int_0^x\varphi\,dt\), \(\varphi''=\varphi\). At \(x=0\): \(\varphi(0)=1\), \(\varphi'(0)=1\). So \(\varphi=\cosh x+\sinh x=e^x\) and \(\varphi(1)=e\). (The terms \(\sinh\) and \(\cosh\) alone would need \(\varphi'(0)=0\) or \(\varphi(0)=0\).)
  12. Question 12Hard
    Consider: (i) \(K(x,t)=x-t\) is a separable kernel; (ii) a Volterra equation of the second kind with continuous kernel can have eigenvalues; (iii) with the convention \(\varphi=\lambda\int_0^1xt\,\varphi(t)\,dt\), the characteristic value is \(\lambda=3\); (iv) every Fredholm equation is a special case of a Volterra equation. Which are true?

    Why

    (i) \(x-t=x\cdot1-1\cdot t\), so it is separable of rank \(2\). True. (ii) False: a continuous-kernel Volterra equation has no eigenvalues. (iii) \(\varphi=c\,x\) with \(c=\lambda c\int_0^1t^2dt=\lambda c/3\), so \(\lambda=3\). True. (iv) False: it is the Volterra equation that is a special Fredholm equation (kernel zero for \(t>x\)).
  13. Question 13Easy
    The equation \(\displaystyle\int_0^x\frac{\varphi(t)}{\sqrt{x-t}}\,dt=f(x)\) is known as

    Why

    It has the singular kernel \((x-t)^{-1/2}\) with variable upper limit; this is Abel’s integral equation (the tautochrone problem), with solution \(\varphi=\frac1\pi\frac{d}{dx}\int_0^x\frac{f(t)}{\sqrt{x-t}}dt\).
