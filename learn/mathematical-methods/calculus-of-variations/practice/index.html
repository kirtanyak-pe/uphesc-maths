  1. Question 1Easy
    The Euler-Lagrange equation for \(J=\int_a^bF(x,y,y')\,dx\) with fixed end points is

    Why

    Varying \(y\to y+\varepsilon\eta\) and integrating \(F_{y'}\eta'\) by parts gives \(\int(F_y-\frac{d}{dx}F_{y'})\eta\,dx=0\) for all \(\eta\) vanishing at the ends, so \(F_y-\frac{d}{dx}F_{y'}=0\). Test on \(F=y'^2+y^2\): \(2y-2y''=0\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    The extremal of \(\int_0^1(y'^2+2y)\,dx\) with \(y(0)=0\), \(y(1)=1\) is

    Why

    \(F_y=2\), \(F_{y'}=2y'\), so \(2-2y''=0\), \(y''=1\) and \(y=\frac{x^2}2+c_1x+c_2\). \(y(0)=0\Rightarrow c_2=0\); \(y(1)=1\Rightarrow c_1=\frac12\). A common slip is \(y''=2\) (forgetting the factor \(2\) in \(F_{y'}\)), which gives \(x^2\). Checked: perturbing by \(\varepsilon\sin\pi x\) raises \(J\) for both signs of \(\varepsilon\).
  3. Question 3Medium
    The extremal of \(\int_1^2\dfrac{y'^2}{x^3}\,dx\) with \(y(1)=0\), \(y(2)=15\) is

    Why

    \(F\) is free of \(y\), so \(F_{y'}=\dfrac{2y'}{x^3}=C\), giving \(y'=kx^3\) and \(y=\dfrac{k}{4}x^4+c\). \(y(1)=0\) and \(y(2)=15\) give \(\frac k4(16-1)=15\), \(k=4\), \(y=x^4-1\). The other options also meet both end values but do not satisfy \(y'/x^3=\) const.
  4. Question 4Medium
    For \(F=y^2+y'^2\) (free of \(x\)), the first integral given by the Beltrami identity \(F-y'F_{y'}=C\) is

    Why

    \(F-y'F_{y'}=y^2+y'^2-y'\cdot2y'=y^2-y'^2\). The E-L equation is \(y''=y\), and indeed \(\frac{d}{dx}(y^2-y'^2)=2y'(y-y'')=0\). The option \(y'=C\) is the first integral for \(F\) free of \(y\), not of \(x\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    Consider: (i) if \(F\) does not contain \(y'\), the Euler-Lagrange equation reduces to the algebraic equation \(F_y=0\); (ii) if \(F\) does not contain \(x\), then \(F-y'F_{y'}\) is constant on an extremal; (iii) the straight line joining two points is the curve of quickest descent between them; (iv) if \(F\) does not contain \(y\), then \(F_{y'}\) is constant on an extremal. Which are true?

    Why

    (i): \(F_{y'}=0\), so E-L is \(F_y=0\). True. (ii): Beltrami identity. True. (iii): false; the brachistochrone is a cycloid. (iv): \(F_y=0\) so \(\frac{d}{dx}F_{y'}=0\). True. So (i), (ii), (iv).
  6. Question 6Hard
    The minimum value of \(\int_0^1y'^2\,dx\) over curves with \(y(0)=y(1)=0\) and \(\int_0^1y\,dx=2\) is

    Why

    \(H=y'^2+\lambda y\); E-L: \(\lambda-2y''=0\). So \(y=\frac\lambda4(x^2-x)\) after using \(y(0)=y(1)=0\). \(\int_0^1y\,dx=-\frac\lambda{24}=2\) gives \(\lambda=-48\), \(y=12x(1-x)\). Then \(y'=12-24x\) and \(\int_0^1(12-24x)^2dx=48\). (\(12\) is the value when the constraint is \(\int y=1\); the value scales with the square of the constraint.) Numerical check: \(y=\pi\sin\pi x\) also has \(\int y=2\) but gives \(\approx 48.7\).
  7. Question 7Easy
    For \(J=\int_a^bF(x,y,y')\,dx\) with \(y(a)\) prescribed and \(y(b)\) free, the natural boundary condition at \(x=b\) is

    Why

    The boundary term in \(\delta J\) is \(\big[F_{y'}\eta\big]_a^b\). Since \(\eta(a)=0\) but \(\eta(b)\) is arbitrary, we need \(F_{y'}(b)=0\). This reduces to \(y'(b)=0\) only for particular \(F\), for example \(F=\frac12y'^2+g(x,y)\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    Let \(y\) minimise \(\int_0^1\big(\tfrac12y'^2-y\big)\,dx\) with \(y(0)=0\) and \(y(1)\) free. Then \(y(1)=\)

    Why

    E-L: \(-1-y''=0\), so \(y''=-1\) and \(y=-\frac{x^2}2+c_1x\) (using \(y(0)=0\)). Natural condition \(F_{y'}=y'(1)=0\): \(-1+c_1=0\), \(c_1=1\). So \(y=x-\frac{x^2}2\) and \(y(1)=\frac12\). A sign slip (\(y''=+1\)) gives \(-\frac12\). Checked numerically that the perturbation \(\varepsilon x\) increases \(J\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    For \(J=\int F(x,y,y',y'')\,dx\) the Euler-Poisson equation is

    Why

    Integrating by parts once on the \(F_{y'}\eta'\) term gives \(-\frac{d}{dx}F_{y'}\); twice on the \(F_{y''}\eta''\) term gives \(+\frac{d^2}{dx^2}F_{y''}\) (two sign changes, second derivative). The signs alternate and the order of differentiation matches the order of the derivative in \(y^{(k)}\).
  10. Question 10Hard
    The extremal of \(\int_0^1\big(y''^2+24xy\big)\,dx\) with \(y(0)=y'(0)=0\) and \(y(1)=y'(1)=0\) takes the value \(y(\tfrac12)=\)

    Why

    Euler-Poisson: \(F_y-\frac{d}{dx}F_{y'}+\frac{d^2}{dx^2}F_{y''}=24x+2y''''=0\), so \(y''''=-12x\) and \(y=-\frac{x^5}{10}+a+bx+cx^2+dx^3\). \(y(0)=y'(0)=0\) gives \(a=b=0\). \(y(1)=0\): \(c+d=\frac1{10}\); \(y'(1)=0\): \(2c+3d=\frac12\). So \(d=\frac3{10}\), \(c=-\frac15\). \(y(\frac12)=-\frac1{320}-\frac1{20}+\frac3{80}=-\frac1{64}\). The option \(+\frac1{64}\) comes from the wrong sign \(y''''=+12x\).
  11. Question 11Medium
    Assertion (A): The straight line joining two points \(P\) and \(Q\) with \(Q\) lower than \(P\) and not directly below it is not the path of quickest descent under gravity between them. Reason (R): The descent time is \(\int\sqrt{(1+y'^2)/(2gy)}\,dx\), and its extremal, found from \(y(1+y'^2)=c\), is a cycloid.

    Why

    The time functional is free of \(x\), so Beltrami gives \(y(1+y'^2)=c\), whose solution is the cycloid \(x=\frac c2(\theta-\sin\theta)\), \(y=\frac c2(1-\cos\theta)\). A line is only the shortest-distance curve, so it is not optimal for time (when \(Q\) is directly below \(P\) the vertical line is the quickest path, which is why the stem excludes that case). R explains A.
  12. Question 12Medium
    If an extremal gives a minimum of \(J=\int F(x,y,y')\,dx\), the Legendre condition requires, along the extremal,

    Why

    The second variation is \(\int(F_{y'y'}\eta'^2+Q\eta^2)\,dx\) with \(Q=F_{yy}-\frac{d}{dx}F_{yy'}\). Rapidly oscillating \(\eta\) make the \(\eta'^2\) term dominate, so a minimum needs \(F_{y'y'}\ge0\) (and \(\le0\) for a maximum). It is necessary, not sufficient.
  13. Question 13Easy
    Match each problem (P) shortest path in a plane, (Q) brachistochrone, (R) minimal surface of revolution, (S) closed curve of given length enclosing maximum area, with its extremal (1) catenary, (2) circle, (3) straight line, (4) cycloid.

    Why

    Plane geodesic: straight line. Brachistochrone: cycloid. Minimal surface of revolution (catenoid): catenary \(y=c\cosh\frac{x-x_0}{c}\). Isoperimetric (Dido) problem: circle.
