  1. Question 1Easy
    \(\mathscr L\{e^{-2t}\sin3t\}=\)

    Why

    First shifting theorem: \(\mathscr L\{e^{at}F\}=f(s-a)\) with \(a=-2\), so \(s\to s+2\) in \(\mathscr L\{\sin3t\}=\frac3{s^2+9}\). The last option is the transform of \(e^{-2t}\cos3t\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    \(\mathscr L\{t^2e^{3t}\}=\)

    Why

    \(\mathscr L\{t^2\}=\frac2{s^3}\) and the shift \(s\to s-3\) gives \(\frac2{(s-3)^3}\) for \(s>3\). In general \(\mathscr L\{t^ne^{at}\}=\frac{n!}{(s-a)^{n+1}}\). Checked numerically at \(s=4\).
  3. Question 3Medium
    \(\mathscr L\{t\sin2t\}=\)

    Why

    \(\mathscr L\{t\sin at\}=-\frac{d}{ds}\frac{a}{s^2+a^2}=\frac{2as}{(s^2+a^2)^2}\). With \(a=2\) the numerator is \(4s\). The last option is \(\mathscr L\{t\cos2t\}\). Checked numerically.
  4. Question 4Medium
    With \(H\) the unit step function, \(\mathscr L\{t\,H(t-1)\}=\)

    Why

    \(\mathscr L\{G(t)H(t-a)\}=e^{-as}\mathscr L\{G(t+a)\}\). With \(G=t\), \(a=1\): \(G(t+1)=t+1\), transform \(\frac1{s^2}+\frac1s\). The second shifting theorem needs the shifted form \((t-1)H(t-1)\), which would give \(\frac{e^{-s}}{s^2}\). Checked numerically at \(s=1.7\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    \(\mathscr L^{-1}\Big\{\dfrac{3s+7}{s^2-2s-3}\Big\}=\)

    Why

    \(s^2-2s-3=(s-3)(s+1)\). \(\frac{3s+7}{(s-3)(s+1)}=\frac{A}{s-3}+\frac{B}{s+1}\) with \(A=\frac{16}4=4\) and \(B=\frac{4}{-4}=-1\). So \(F=4e^{3t}-e^{-t}\). Checked: its transform at \(s=5\) matches.
  6. Question 6Medium
    \(\mathscr L^{-1}\Big\{\dfrac{1}{s^2(s^2+1)}\Big\}=\)

    Why

    \(\frac1{s^2(s^2+1)}=\frac1{s^2}-\frac1{s^2+1}\), so \(F=t-\sin t\). By convolution: \(\int_0^t(t-u)\sin u\,du=t-\sin t\). The option \(1-\cos t\) is the inverse of \(\frac1{s(s^2+1)}\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    The solution of \(y''+2y'+y=0\), \(y(0)=1\), \(y'(0)=0\) satisfies \(y(1)=\)

    Why

    Transform: \(s^2Y-s+2(sY-1)+Y=0\), so \(Y=\frac{s+2}{(s+1)^2}=\frac1{s+1}+\frac1{(s+1)^2}\). Hence \(y=e^{-t}+te^{-t}=(1+t)e^{-t}\) and \(y(1)=\frac2e\). (Forgetting the \(2y(0)\) term gives \(e^{-t}\), i.e. \(\frac1e\).)
  8. Question 8Medium
    If \(\mathscr L\{F(t)\}=\dfrac{3s+2}{s(s+4)}\), then \(F(0^+)\) and \(\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}F(t)\) are, respectively,

    Why

    \(sf(s)=\frac{3s+2}{s+4}\). Initial value: \(\lim_{s\to\infty}sf=3\). Final value: \(\lim_{s\to0}sf=\frac24=\frac12\) (valid since the only pole of \(sf\) is at \(s=-4\)). Indeed \(F=\frac12+\frac52e^{-4t}\).
  9. Question 9Hard
    For which of the following \(F\) does \(\displaystyle\lim_{s\to0}s\,f(s)\) exist while \(\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}F(t)\) does not?

    Why

    For \(F=\sin t\), \(sf=\frac s{s^2+1}\to0\) as \(s\to0\), yet \(\sin t\) oscillates: the poles of \(sf\) at \(\pm i\) are on the imaginary axis, so the final value theorem does not apply. The other three functions tend to \(0\), \(1\) and \(0\), matching \(\lim_{s\to0}sf=0,1,0\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty e^{-2t}\,t\cos t\,dt=\)

    Why

    The integral is \(\mathscr L\{t\cos t\}\) at \(s=2\): \(\frac{s^2-1}{(s^2+1)^2}=\frac{3}{25}\). Numerically \(0.12\).
  11. Question 11Hard
    \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty e^{-t}\,\frac{1-\cos t}{t}\,dt=\)

    Why

    \(\mathscr L\Big\{\dfrac{1-\cos t}{t}\Big\}=\int_s^\infty\Big(\frac1u-\frac u{u^2+1}\Big)du=\frac12\ln\frac{s^2+1}{s^2}\). At \(s=1\): \(\frac12\ln2\approx0.3466\). Checked numerically.
  12. Question 12Medium
    Assertion (A): The Laplace transform of \(F(t)=1/t\) does not exist. Reason (R): \(\int_0^\infty e^{-st}\,\dfrac{dt}{t}\) diverges at \(t=0\) for every \(s\).

    Why

    Near \(t=0\), \(e^{-st}/t\sim1/t\), which is not integrable, so the integral diverges for every \(s\). \(F=1/t\) is not piecewise continuous at \(0\), so the existence theorem does not apply and the transform fails. R explains A.
  13. Question 13Easy
    Match (P) \(\mathscr L\{\delta(t-a)\}\), (Q) \(\mathscr L\{t^2\}\), (R) \(\mathscr L\{H(t-a)\}\), (S) \(\mathscr L\{F'(t)\}\) with (1) \(\dfrac2{s^3}\), (2) \(\dfrac{e^{-as}}s\), (3) \(e^{-as}\), (4) \(sf(s)-F(0)\).

    Why

    The Dirac delta transforms to \(e^{-as}\), the unit step to \(e^{-as}/s\), \(t^2\) to \(2/s^3\), and the derivative to \(sf-F(0)\).
