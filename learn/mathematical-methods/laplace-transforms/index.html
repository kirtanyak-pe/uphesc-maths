Why it matters for the exam

Laplace transforms were asked in 2025 (Q75 on \(\mathscr L\{t^nF\}\), Q77 on \(\mathscr L\{t\cos t\}\)) and in 2026 (Q68, the integral of \(\sin x/x\), which the Laplace transform also evaluates). Questions are direct: a transform from the table, a theorem formula, or an inverse by partial fractions or convolution. The same machinery solves the Volterra equations of the previous page.

Core ideas

Definition and existence

For \(F(t)\) defined on \(t\ge0\),

\[f(s)=\mathscr L\{F(t)\}=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}F(t)\,dt .\]

Sufficient conditions for existence. If \(F\) is piecewise continuous on every finite interval \([0,T]\) and of exponential order \(\alpha\) (there are \(M,T_0\) with \(\lvert F(t)\rvert\le Me^{\alpha t}\) for \(t>T_0\)), then \(\mathscr L\{F\}\) exists for all \(s>\alpha\) and \(f(s)\to0\) as \(s\to\infty\).

These conditions are sufficient, not necessary: \(F=t^{-1/2}\) is not piecewise continuous at \(0\) but has transform \(\sqrt{\pi/s}\). Examples with no transform: \(e^{t^2}\) (not of exponential order) and \(1/t\) (not integrable at \(0\)). Also, for an ordinary function (locally integrable and of exponential order), \(f(s)\to0\) as \(s\to\infty\), so \(s\) or \(\frac{s}{s+1}\) cannot be transforms. The impulse \(\delta\) (\(\mathscr L\{\delta\}=1\)) and its derivative \(\delta'\) (\(\mathscr L\{\delta'\}=s\)) are generalised functions and fall outside this rule.

Linearity: \(\mathscr L\{aF+bG\}=af+bg\). Uniqueness (Lerch): two continuous functions with the same transform are equal, so \(\mathscr L^{-1}\) is well defined.

Basic theorems

First shifting theorem. If \(\mathscr L\{F\}=f(s)\) then

\[\mathscr L\{e^{at}F(t)\}=f(s-a).\]

Examples: \(\mathscr L\{e^{-2t}\sin3t\}=\dfrac3{(s+2)^2+9}\), \(\mathscr L\{t^ne^{at}\}=\dfrac{n!}{(s-a)^{n+1}}\).

Second shifting theorem. With \(H\) the unit step,

\[\mathscr L\{F(t-a)\,H(t-a)\}=e^{-as}f(s),\qquad a>0 .\]

Equivalent form: \(\mathscr L\{G(t)H(t-a)\}=e^{-as}\,\mathscr L\{G(t+a)\}\). The function must be shifted and switched on at \(a\).

Change of scale. \(\mathscr L\{F(at)\}=\dfrac1af\Big(\dfrac sa\Big)\), \(a>0\).

Transform of derivatives.

\[\mathscr L\{F'\}=sf(s)-F(0),\qquad\mathscr L\{F''\}=s^2f(s)-sF(0)-F'(0),\]
\[\mathscr L\{F^{(n)}\}=s^nf(s)-s^{n-1}F(0)-s^{n-2}F'(0)-\cdots-F^{(n-1)}(0).\]

(\(F\) and its derivatives continuous, of exponential order.)

Transform of integrals. \(\mathscr L\Big\{\displaystyle\int_0^tF(u)\,du\Big\}=\dfrac{f(s)}s\).

Multiplication by \(t^n\).

\[\boxed{\mathscr L\{t^nF(t)\}=(-1)^n\frac{d^n}{ds^n}f(s)}\qquad(n=1,2,\dots)\]

Examples: \(\mathscr L\{t\cos t\}=-\dfrac d{ds}\dfrac s{s^2+1}=\dfrac{s^2-1}{(s^2+1)^2}\). \(\mathscr L\{t^2\}=\dfrac{d^2}{ds^2}\dfrac1s=\dfrac2{s^3}\).

Division by \(t\). If \(\lim_{t\to0^+}F(t)/t\) exists,

\[\mathscr L\Big\{\frac{F(t)}t\Big\}=\int_s^\infty f(u)\,du .\]

Example: \(\mathscr L\Big\{\dfrac{\sin t}t\Big\}=\int_s^\infty\dfrac{du}{u^2+1}=\arctan\dfrac1s\) (as \(\frac\pi2-\arctan s\)).

Periodic functions. If \(F(t+T)=F(t)\),

\[\mathscr L\{F\}=\frac{\int_0^Te^{-st}F(t)\,dt}{1-e^{-sT}} .\]

Examples: square wave (\(+1\) on \((0,a)\), \(-1\) on \((a,2a)\), period \(2a\)): \(\dfrac1s\tanh\dfrac{as}2\). Sawtooth (\(F=t\) on \((0,a)\), period \(a\)): \(\int_0^ate^{-st}dt=\dfrac{1-(1+as)e^{-as}}{s^2}\), so \(\mathscr L=\dfrac1{s^2}-\dfrac{ae^{-as}}{s(1-e^{-as})}\) (for \(a=1\): \(\dfrac1{s^2}-\dfrac{e^{-s}}{s(1-e^{-s})}\)). Full-wave rectified sine \(\lvert\sin t\rvert\) (period \(\pi\)): \(\dfrac1{s^2+1}\coth\dfrac{\pi s}2\).

Unit step and Dirac delta

Unit step \(H(t-a)=0\) for \(t<a\), \(1\) for \(t\ge a\). A window \(H(t-a)-H(t-b)\) is \(1\) on \([a,b)\). \(\mathscr L\{H(t-a)\}=\dfrac{e^{-as}}s\).

Dirac delta \(\delta(t-a)\) (an impulse at \(t=a\)): \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty\delta(t-a)\varphi(t)\,dt=\varphi(a)\) (sifting property). Hence

\[\mathscr L\{\delta(t-a)\}=e^{-as},\qquad\mathscr L\{\delta(t)\}=1,\qquad\mathscr L\{\delta(t-a)F(t)\}=e^{-as}F(a).\]

\(\delta\) is the derivative of \(H\): \(H'=\delta\).

Convolution theorem

\[(F*G)(t)=\int_0^tF(u)G(t-u)\,du,\qquad\mathscr L\{F*G\}=f(s)\,g(s).\]

Convolution is commutative, associative and distributive. In inverse form: \(\mathscr L^{-1}\{f g\}=F*G\). Used for inverses of products, and for Volterra equations of convolution type.

Inverse transforms

Methods, in the order to try them:

  1. Table and linearity.
  2. Partial fractions (proper rational \(f=P/Q\)). Simple root \(a\): \(\dfrac A{s-a}\to Ae^{at}\). Repeated root: \(\dfrac B{(s-a)^2}\to Bte^{at}\). Irreducible quadratic \(\dfrac{As+B}{(s-p)^2+q^2}\): complete the square and use the shift.
  3. First shifting for \(f(s-a)\): invert \(f\), multiply by \(e^{at}\).
  4. Second shifting for \(e^{-as}f(s)\): \(F(t-a)H(t-a)\).
  5. Convolution for products such as \(\dfrac1{s(s^2+1)}\).
  6. Heaviside expansion. If \(f=P/Q\) with distinct simple roots \(a_k\) of \(Q\) (\(\deg P<\deg Q\)): \(F(t)=\sum_k\dfrac{P(a_k)}{Q'(a_k)}e^{a_kt}\).
  7. Differentiation/integration in \(s\): \(\mathscr L^{-1}\{f'(s)\}=-tF(t)\), \(\mathscr L^{-1}\Big\{\displaystyle\int_s^\infty f\Big\}=F(t)/t\). Logarithms are inverted this way: \(f=\ln\dfrac{s+a}{s+b}\) has \(f'=\frac1{s+a}-\frac1{s+b}\), so \(\mathscr L^{-1}\{f\}=\dfrac{e^{-bt}-e^{-at}}t\) (note the order \(b\) then \(a\)). For example \(\mathscr L^{-1}\Big\{\ln\dfrac{s+3}{s+1}\Big\}=\dfrac{e^{-t}-e^{-3t}}t\).

Standard inverses: \(\mathscr L^{-1}\Big\{\dfrac1{(s^2+a^2)^2}\Big\}=\dfrac{\sin at-at\cos at}{2a^3}\), \(\mathscr L^{-1}\Big\{\dfrac s{(s^2+a^2)^2}\Big\}=\dfrac{t\sin at}{2a}\).

Initial and final value theorems

If \(F\) and \(F'\) are transformable,

\[\textbf{Initial value:}\quad\lim_{t\to0^+}F(t)=\lim_{s\to\infty}s\,f(s).\]
\[\textbf{Final value:}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}F(t)=\lim_{s\to0}s\,f(s),\]

provided the limit of \(F\) at infinity exists (a sufficient test: every pole of \(sf(s)\) lies in \(\operatorname{Re}s<0\)). If \(F\) oscillates or grows, the right side can exist while the left does not: \(F=\sin t\), \(sf=s/(s^2+1)\to0\) as \(s\to0\), but \(\sin t\) has no limit.

Applications

Linear ODEs with constant coefficients. Transform, use the derivative formulas (initial conditions enter automatically), solve algebraically for \(Y(s)\), invert.

Systems of ODEs. Transform each equation to get an algebraic system in \(Y_1,Y_2,\dots\).

ODEs with polynomial coefficients. \(\mathscr L\{ty\}=-Y'\), \(\mathscr L\{ty'\}=-(sY-y(0))'\), \(\mathscr L\{ty''\}=-(s^2Y-sy(0)-y'(0))'\) turn them into lower-order equations in \(s\). Example: \(ty''+y'+ty=0\), \(y(0)=1\) gives \((s^2+1)Y'+sY=0\), \(Y=\dfrac1{\sqrt{s^2+1}}\), \(y=J_0(t)\).

Integral and integro-differential equations of convolution type. See the previous page: \(\varphi=f+\lambda k*\varphi\) gives \(\Phi=F/(1-\lambda\kappa)\).

Discontinuous or impulsive forcing. Express the forcing with \(H\) and \(\delta\), then use the second shifting theorem.

Evaluating integrals

Two tools:

  1. Direct substitution. \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty e^{-st}F(t)\,dt=f(s)\). Choose \(s\) and \(F\) to match the integrand. Example: \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty te^{-3t}\sin t\,dt=\mathscr L\{t\sin t\}\Big\vert_{s=3}=\frac{2\cdot3}{(9+1)^2}=\frac3{50}\).

  2. Integral over \(s\) (division by \(t\)). Taking \(s\to0\) in \(\mathscr L\{F/t\}=\int_s^\infty f(u)\,du\):

\[\int_0^\infty\frac{F(t)}t\,dt=\int_0^\infty f(s)\,ds .\]

Dirichlet integral. \(F=\sin t\): \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin t}t\,dt=\int_0^\infty\frac{ds}{s^2+1}=\frac\pi2\). (This is 2026 Q68; the paper asked for it via a Fourier transform, but the value, \(\pi/2\), is the same by any method.)

Frullani-type integral. \(F=e^{-at}-e^{-bt}\): \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-at}-e^{-bt}}t\,dt=\int_0^\infty\Big(\frac1{s+a}-\frac1{s+b}\Big)ds=\ln\frac ba\) (\(a,b>0\)).

Also: \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty e^{-t}\frac{\sin t}t\,dt=\arctan1=\frac\pi4\).

Worked examples

Example 1: shifting with \(t\cos\)

Find \(\mathscr L\{e^{-2t}t\cos3t\}\).

\(\mathscr L\{t\cos3t\}=\dfrac{s^2-9}{(s^2+9)^2}\). Replace \(s\) by \(s+2\): \(\dfrac{(s+2)^2-9}{\big((s+2)^2+9\big)^2}\). At \(s=2\) this is \(\dfrac{16-9}{25^2}=\dfrac7{625}\) .

Example 2: division by \(t\)

Find \(\mathscr L\Big\{\dfrac{1-\cos t}t\Big\}\).

\(\mathscr L\{1-\cos t\}=\dfrac1s-\dfrac s{s^2+1}\). Integrate from \(s\) to \(\infty\): \(\Big[\ln u-\tfrac12\ln(u^2+1)\Big]_s^\infty=-\ln\dfrac s{\sqrt{s^2+1}}=\tfrac12\ln\dfrac{s^2+1}{s^2}\).

Example 3: inverse by partial fractions and by convolution

(a) \(\mathscr L^{-1}\Big\{\dfrac s{(s^2+1)(s^2+4)}\Big\}\). \(\dfrac s{(s^2+1)(s^2+4)}=\dfrac13\Big[\dfrac s{s^2+1}-\dfrac s{s^2+4}\Big]\). So \(F=\tfrac13(\cos t-\cos2t)\).

(b) \(\mathscr L^{-1}\Big\{\dfrac1{s(s^2+1)}\Big\}\) by convolution of \(1\) and \(\sin t\): \(\displaystyle\int_0^t\sin u\,du=1-\cos t\).

Example 4: ODE with initial conditions

Solve \(y''-3y'+2y=4e^{2t}\), \(y(0)=-3\), \(y'(0)=5\).

Transform: \(s^2Y+3s-5-3(sY+3)+2Y=\dfrac4{s-2}\), so \((s^2-3s+2)Y=\dfrac4{s-2}-3s+14\). Hence

\[Y=\frac4{(s-1)(s-2)^2}+\frac{14-3s}{(s-1)(s-2)}.\]

Partial fractions: first term \(\dfrac4{s-1}-\dfrac4{s-2}+\dfrac4{(s-2)^2}\); second \(-\dfrac{11}{s-1}+\dfrac8{s-2}\). Adding: \(Y=-\dfrac7{s-1}+\dfrac4{s-2}+\dfrac4{(s-2)^2}\), so

\[y=-7e^t+4e^{2t}+4te^{2t}.\]

Check: \(y(0)=-3\), \(y'(0)=-7+8+4=5\), and substitution in the ODE confirms them.

Example 5: delta forcing

Solve \(y''+y=\delta(t-\pi)\), \(y(0)=0\), \(y'(0)=1\).

\((s^2+1)Y=1+e^{-\pi s}\), so \(Y=\dfrac1{s^2+1}+\dfrac{e^{-\pi s}}{s^2+1}\). Inverse: \(y=\sin t+H(t-\pi)\sin(t-\pi)=\sin t\,\big[1-H(t-\pi)\big]\). So \(y=\sin t\) for \(t<\pi\) and \(y=0\) for \(t>\pi\) (the impulse cancels the motion).

Example 6: Volterra integral equation

Solve \(\varphi(t)=\sin t+2\int_0^t\cos(t-\tau)\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau\).

\(\Phi=\dfrac1{s^2+1}+2\cdot\dfrac s{s^2+1}\Phi\). So \(\Phi\dfrac{s^2+1-2s}{s^2+1}=\dfrac1{s^2+1}\), \(\Phi=\dfrac1{(s-1)^2}\), \(\varphi=te^t\).

Integro-differential: \(y'=1-\int_0^ty\,d\tau\), \(y(0)=0\) gives \(sY=\frac1s-\frac Ys\), \(Y=\frac1{s^2+1}\), \(y=\sin t\).

Example 7: periodic function and value theorems

(a) Square wave \(F=1\) on \((0,a)\), \(-1\) on \((a,2a)\), period \(2a\): \(\mathscr L=\dfrac{\int_0^ae^{-st}dt-\int_a^{2a}e^{-st}dt}{1-e^{-2as}}=\dfrac{(1-e^{-as})^2}{s(1-e^{-2as})}=\dfrac{1-e^{-as}}{s(1+e^{-as})}=\dfrac1s\tanh\dfrac{as}2\).

(b) Let \(F\) have \(f(s)=\dfrac{s+3}{s(s+1)(s+2)}\), so \(sf(s)=\dfrac{s+3}{(s+1)(s+2)}\). Then \(F(0^+)=\lim_{s\to\infty}sf=0\) and \(F(\infty)=\lim_{s\to0}sf=\frac32\). (Poles of \(sf\) at \(-1,-2\) are in the left half-plane, so the final value theorem applies.)

Example 8: an integral

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-3t}}t\,dt\).

\(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\Big(\frac1{s+1}-\frac1{s+3}\Big)ds=\Big[\ln\frac{s+1}{s+3}\Big]_0^\infty=0-\ln\frac13=\ln3\) .

Common traps

  • Sign of the derivative formula. \(\mathscr L\{t^nF\}=(-1)^nf^{(n)}(s)\). Dropping the \((-1)^n\) is the standard wrong option (2025 Q75). For \(n=1\): \(\mathscr L\{t\cos t\}=-f'\), positive \(\dfrac{s^2-1}{(s^2+1)^2}\).
  • Shift direction. \(\mathscr L\{e^{at}F\}=f(s-a)\): \(s\) moves to \(s-a\). \(e^{-2t}\) gives \(s+2\).
  • Second shift needs both. \(F(t-a)H(t-a)\), not \(F(t)H(t-a)\). For \(G(t)H(t-a)\) use \(e^{-as}\mathscr L\{G(t+a)\}\). \(\mathscr L\{t\,H(t-1)\}=e^{-s}\big(\frac1{s^2}+\frac1s\big)\), not \(e^{-s}/s^2\).
  • Initial conditions. \(\mathscr L\{F''\}=s^2f-sF(0)-F'(0)\): forgetting \(F(0)\) or putting a plus sign.
  • Final value theorem needs the limit to exist: \(\sin t\), \(e^t\) and \(t\) break it. The initial value theorem looks only at \(t\to0^+\), so it does not need the behaviour at infinity, but it does need \(F(0^+)\) to exist (finite, or \(+\infty\) as for \(t^{-1/2}\), where \(sf=\sqrt{\pi s}\to\infty\)).
  • Table slips. \(\mathscr L\{\sin at\}=\frac a{s^2+a^2}\) (numerator \(a\)), \(\mathscr L\{\cos at\}=\frac s{s^2+a^2}\). \(\mathscr L\{\sinh at\}=\frac a{s^2-a^2}\). \(\mathscr L\{t^n\}=n!/s^{n+1}\), not \(n!/s^n\). \(\mathscr L\{t^2\}=2/s^3\).
  • Convolution is not a product. \(\mathscr L\{FG\}\ne fg\); \(\mathscr L\{F*G\}=fg\).
  • Division by \(t\) requires \(\lim F(t)/t\) at \(0\) to exist (\(\cos t/t\) has no transform).
  • Repeated roots in partial fractions need both \(\frac1{s-a}\) and \(\frac1{(s-a)^2}\) terms.
  • Dirichlet integral. \(\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}x\,dx=\frac\pi2\), not \(\pi\) (\(\int_{-\infty}^\infty\) gives \(\pi\)). Not absolutely convergent.

Previous-year questions

2025 Q75 (official key A)

Question. If \(\mathscr L\{F(t)\}=f(s)\), then \(\mathscr L\{t^nF(t)\}\) equals: (A) \((-1)^n\dfrac{d^n}{ds^n}f(s)\), (B) \(\dfrac{d^n}{ds^n}f(s)\), and two further variants of sign and exponent.

Answer. A.

Solution. Differentiate \(f(s)=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}F\,dt\) under the integral: each \(\frac d{ds}\) brings down \(-t\). So \(\frac{d^n}{ds^n}f=(-1)^n\mathscr L\{t^nF\}\), giving A.

2025 Q77 (official key B)

Question. The Laplace transform of \(f(t)=t\cos t\) is (four options; the answer is B, \(\dfrac{s^2-1}{(s^2+1)^2}\)).

Answer. B, \(\dfrac{s^2-1}{(s^2+1)^2}\).

Solution. \(\mathscr L\{\cos t\}=\frac s{s^2+1}\) and \(\mathscr L\{tF\}=-f'\). \(-\dfrac{d}{ds}\dfrac s{s^2+1}=-\dfrac{(s^2+1)-2s^2}{(s^2+1)^2}=\dfrac{s^2-1}{(s^2+1)^2}\).

2026 Q68 (answer D; unofficial)

Question (restated). Evaluate the improper integral \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}x\,dx\) (the paper pointed to a Fourier-transform argument).

Answer. D, \(\dfrac\pi2\).

Solution. Dirichlet integral. By Laplace: \(\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin t}t\,dt=\int_0^\infty\mathscr L\{\sin t\}\,ds=\int_0^\infty\frac{ds}{s^2+1}=\frac\pi2\). By Fourier: the transform of a rectangular pulse is a sinc, and Plancherel or the inversion value at \(0\) gives \(\frac\pi2\).

Quick revision

  • \(\mathscr L\{F\}=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}F\,dt\); exists for piecewise continuous \(F\) of exponential order, \(s>\alpha\).
  • \(\mathscr L\{t^n\}=n!/s^{n+1}\); \(\sin at\to a/(s^2+a^2)\); \(\cos at\to s/(s^2+a^2)\).
  • First shift: \(e^{at}F\to f(s-a)\). Second shift: \(F(t-a)H(t-a)\to e^{-as}f\).
  • \(\mathscr L\{F'\}=sf-F(0)\); \(\mathscr L\{F''\}=s^2f-sF(0)-F'(0)\).
  • \(\mathscr L\{t^nF\}=(-1)^nf^{(n)}(s)\); \(\mathscr L\{F/t\}=\int_s^\infty f\).
  • \(\mathscr L\{t\cos t\}=\frac{s^2-1}{(s^2+1)^2}\); \(\mathscr L\{t\sin at\}=\frac{2as}{(s^2+a^2)^2}\).
  • Periodic: \(\int_0^Te^{-st}F\,dt\big/(1-e^{-sT})\).
  • \(\mathscr L\{\delta(t-a)\}=e^{-as}\); \(\mathscr L\{H(t-a)\}=e^{-as}/s\).
  • Convolution: \(\mathscr L\{F*G\}=fg\); use for \(\mathscr L^{-1}\) of products.
  • Initial value: \(\lim_{s\to\infty}sf\); final value: \(\lim_{s\to0}sf\) (limit of \(F\) must exist).
  • \(\int_0^\infty F/t\,dt=\int_0^\infty f\,ds\); Dirichlet integral is \(\pi/2\).
  • Volterra convolution equation: \(\Phi=F/(1-\lambda\kappa)\).
