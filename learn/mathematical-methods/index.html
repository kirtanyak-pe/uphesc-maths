What this unit covers

Three blocks, all computational and short.

  1. Calculus of variations: functionals, the Euler-Lagrange equation and its special cases, several functions and higher derivatives, isoperimetric problems, natural end conditions, and the Legendre test.
  2. Integral equations: Fredholm and Volterra equations, kernels, conversion from differential equations, Green’s function, separable kernels, the Fredholm alternative, Neumann series and resolvents.
  3. Laplace transforms: table, shifting, derivative and \(t^n\) theorems, periodic and impulse functions, inverses, convolution, and applications to ODEs, integral equations and integrals.

The last two blocks are joined by one idea: a Volterra equation with kernel \(k(x-t)\) is solved by the Laplace transform.

High-yield list

  1. The E-L equation is second order and in general non-linear (2025 Q78).
  2. \(F\) free of \(y\): \(F_{y'}=C\); free of \(x\): Beltrami \(F-y'F_{y'}=C\).
  3. Isoperimetric problems use \(F+\lambda G\); free ends use \(F_{y'}=0\).
  4. Brachistochrone is a cycloid; minimal surface of revolution is a catenoid.
  5. IVP leads to Volterra, BVP leads to Fredholm (through the Green’s function).
  6. Separable kernels: \(c_i=\int b_i\varphi\); eigenvalues from \(\det(I-\lambda A)=0\).
  7. \(\varphi=1+\lambda\int_0^1xt\varphi\) gives \(\varphi=1+\frac{3\lambda x}{2(3-\lambda)}\); \(\lambda=3\) is the characteristic value.
  8. Rank-\(n\) symmetric kernel has \(n\) eigenvalues; eigenfunctions of distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal.
  9. Singular system is solvable iff \(\operatorname{rank}A=\operatorname{rank}[A\mid b]\); non-trivial solution iff \(D(\lambda)=0\).
  10. Volterra equations have no eigenvalues; the Neumann series always converges.
  11. Laplace needs a convolution kernel; otherwise change variables or differentiate to an ODE.
  12. \(\mathscr L\{t^nF\}=(-1)^nf^{(n)}\) and \(\mathscr L\{t\cos t\}=\frac{s^2-1}{(s^2+1)^2}\).
  13. Shift directions, second shift with \(H(t-a)\), derivative formulas with initial conditions.
  14. Final value theorem needs the limit to exist; \(\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}x\,dx=\frac\pi2\).
