  1. Question 1Easy
    For \(\lambda=1\), the solution of \(\varphi(x)=x+\lambda\displaystyle\int_0^1t\,\varphi(t)\,dt\) is

    Why

    The kernel is \(K=1\cdot t\). Put \(c=\int_0^1t\varphi\,dt\). Then \(\varphi(x)=x+\lambda c\), and \(c=\int_0^1t(t+\lambda c)\,dt=\frac13+\frac{\lambda c}2\). So \(c=\frac{2}{3(2-\lambda)}\), which is \(\frac23\) at \(\lambda=1\). Hence \(\varphi=x+\frac23\). Checked numerically.
  2. Question 2Easy
    The characteristic value of \(\varphi(x)=\lambda\displaystyle\int_0^2xt\,\varphi(t)\,dt\) is

    Why

    Put \(c=\int_0^2t\varphi\,dt\). Then \(\varphi=\lambda cx\) and \(c=\lambda c\int_0^2t^2dt=\frac{8\lambda c}{3}\). For \(c\ne0\): \(\lambda=\frac38\). (\(\frac83\) is the eigenvalue of the operator, the reciprocal.)
  3. Question 3Medium
    The eigenvalues of \(\varphi(x)=\lambda\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\cos(x+t)\,\varphi(t)\,dt\) are

    Why

    \(\cos(x+t)=\cos x\cos t+(-\sin x)\sin t\). With \(c_1=\int\varphi\cos t\), \(c_2=\int\varphi\sin t\): \(\varphi=\lambda(c_1\cos x-c_2\sin x)\), giving \(c_1=\lambda\pi c_1\) and \(c_2=-\lambda\pi c_2\). So \(\lambda=\frac1\pi\) with \(\varphi=\cos x\) and \(\lambda=-\frac1\pi\) with \(\varphi=\sin x\) (orthogonal on \([0,2\pi]\)). The value \(\pm\pi\) would be the eigenvalues of the matrix, not of the equation.
  4. Question 4Medium
    For \(\lambda=3\) and \(K(x,t)=xt\) on \([0,1]\), the equation \(\varphi(x)=f(x)+3\displaystyle\int_0^1xt\,\varphi(t)\,dt\) has a solution if and only if \(\displaystyle\int_0^1tf(t)\,dt=0\). Which of these \(f\) gives a solvable equation?

    Why

    \(\lambda=3\) is the characteristic value with eigenfunction \(x\), so by the Fredholm alternative we need \(f\perp x\). \(\int_0^1t(2-3t)\,dt=1-1=0\). For \(f=1,x,x^2\) the integrals are \(\frac12,\frac13,\frac14\ne0\), so no solution exists. For \(f=2-3x\) the solutions are \(\varphi=2-3x+3cx\) with \(c\) arbitrary.
  5. Question 5Medium
    Assertion (A): The homogeneous Volterra equation \(\varphi(x)=\lambda\int_0^xK(x,t)\varphi(t)\,dt\) with continuous kernel has only the trivial solution for every \(\lambda\). Reason (R): The iterated kernels satisfy \(\lvert K_n(x,t)\rvert\le M^n(x-t)^{n-1}/(n-1)!\), so the Neumann series converges for every \(\lambda\).

    Why

    Iterating \(\varphi=\lambda T\varphi\) gives \(\varphi=\lambda^nT^n\varphi\), and the bound on \(K_n\) shows \(\lambda^nT^n\varphi\to0\), hence \(\varphi=0\). So a Volterra equation has no eigenvalues, and R is the reason.
  6. Question 6Medium
    For the Volterra kernel \(K(x,t)=x-t\) (\(0\le t\le x\)), the third iterated kernel \(K_3(x,t)\) is

    Why

    \(K_2=\int_t^x(x-s)(s-t)\,ds=\frac{(x-t)^3}{3!}\) and \(K_3=\int_t^x(x-s)\frac{(s-t)^3}{3!}\,ds=\frac{(x-t)^5}{5!}\). In general \(K_n=\frac{(x-t)^{2n-1}}{(2n-1)!}\). Verified numerically.
  7. Question 7Medium
    The solution of \(\varphi(x)=2+3\displaystyle\int_0^x\varphi(t)\,dt\) takes the value \(\varphi(\ln2)=\)

    Why

    Here \(K=1\), \(\lambda=3\), \(f=2\). The resolvent is \(R=e^{3(x-t)}\), so \(\varphi=2+3\int_0^x2e^{3(x-t)}dt=2e^{3x}\) (or: \(\varphi'=3\varphi\), \(\varphi(0)=2\)). At \(x=\ln2\): \(2\cdot2^3=16\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    For the kernel \(K(x,t)=x+t\) on \([0,1]\) we have \(\lvert K\rvert\le M=2\). The sufficient condition \(\lvert\lambda\rvert M(b-a)<1\) for convergence of the Neumann series gives

    Why

    \(M(b-a)=2\cdot1=2\), so \(\lvert\lambda\rvert<\frac12\). This is a sufficient range, not the exact radius of convergence; the smallest eigenvalue of this kernel is \(\lambda=-6+4\sqrt3\approx0.93\) in modulus (the root of \(\lambda^2+12\lambda-12=0\) nearest \(0\)).
  9. Question 9Hard
    Solve \(\varphi(t)=t+\displaystyle\int_0^t\sin(t-\tau)\,\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau\) by the Laplace transform. Then \(\varphi(2)=\)

    Why

    The kernel is a convolution with \(\mathscr L\{\sin t\}=\frac1{s^2+1}\). So \(\Phi=\frac1{s^2}+\frac{\Phi}{s^2+1}\), \(\Phi\cdot\frac{s^2}{s^2+1}=\frac1{s^2}\), \(\Phi=\frac{s^2+1}{s^4}=\frac1{s^2}+\frac1{s^4}\). Hence \(\varphi=t+\frac{t^3}6\) and \(\varphi(2)=2+\frac43=\frac{10}3\). Verified by substitution into the equation.
  10. Question 10Medium
    Let \(K\) be a real symmetric kernel of rank \(2\) on \([a,b]\). Consider: (i) all eigenvalues are real; (ii) eigenfunctions belonging to distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal; (iii) \(K\) may have three distinct eigenvalues; (iv) every eigenfunction lies in the span of the two functions \(a_1,a_2\) of the separable form. Which are true?

    Why

    (i) and (ii) hold for any symmetric kernel. (iii) is false: the determinant \(\det(I-\lambda A)\) has degree at most \(2\), so there are at most two eigenvalues. (iv) is true because \(\varphi=\lambda\sum c_ia_i(x)\). So (i), (ii), (iv).
  11. Question 11Easy
    The Fredholm determinant \(D(\lambda)=\det(I-\lambda A)\) of the kernel \(K(x,t)=\sin(x+t)\) on \([0,2\pi]\) is

    Why

    With \(a=(\sin x,\cos x)\) and \(b=(\cos t,\sin t)\), \(A=\begin{pmatrix}0&\pi\\\pi&0\end{pmatrix}\), so \(\det\begin{pmatrix}1&-\pi\lambda\\-\pi\lambda&1\end{pmatrix}=1-\pi^2\lambda^2\), zero at \(\lambda=\pm\frac1\pi\).
  12. Question 12Hard
    Let \(\mathcal G(x,t)=x_<(1-x_>)\) on \([0,1]\) (characteristic values \(n^2\pi^2\), eigenfunctions \(\sin n\pi x\)). For \(\lambda=2\pi^2\), the solution of \(\varphi(x)=\sin2\pi x+\lambda\displaystyle\int_0^1\mathcal G(x,t)\,\varphi(t)\,dt\) takes the value \(\varphi(\tfrac14)=\)

    Why

    \(f=\sin2\pi x\) is the eigenfunction with \(\lambda_2=4\pi^2\), so \(\varphi=c\sin2\pi x\) with \(c=1+\frac{\lambda c}{4\pi^2}\), \(c=\frac{4\pi^2}{4\pi^2-\lambda}=2\). So \(\varphi(\frac14)=2\sin\frac\pi2=2\). Using \(\lambda_1=\pi^2\) by mistake gives \(c=-1\). Verified numerically.
  13. Question 13Easy
    The solution of the Volterra equation of the first kind \(\dfrac{t^3}{6}=\displaystyle\int_0^t(t-\tau)\,\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau\) is

    Why

    Laplace: \(\frac1{s^4}=\frac1{s^2}\Phi\), so \(\Phi=\frac1{s^2}\) and \(\varphi=t\). Check: \(\int_0^t(t-\tau)\tau\,d\tau=\frac{t^3}2-\frac{t^3}3=\frac{t^3}6\).
