  1. Question 1Easy
    Arrange in chronological order of composition: I. Brahmasphutasiddhanta, II. Aryabhatiya, III. Siddhanta Siromani, IV. Ganita Sara Sangraha.

    Why

    Aryabhatiya 499, Brahmasphutasiddhanta 628, Ganita Sara Sangraha c. 850, Siddhanta Siromani 1150. Order: II, I, IV, III.
  2. Question 2Medium
    Match the author with the work. a. Mahavira, b. Sridhara, c. Jyesthadeva, d. Nilakantha Somayaji. I. Tantrasangraha, II. Yuktibhasa, III. Patiganita, IV. Ganita Sara Sangraha.

    Why

    Mahavira wrote Ganita Sara Sangraha, Sridhara the Patiganita, Jyesthadeva the Yuktibhasa (in Malayalam), Nilakantha the Tantrasangraha (1501).
  3. Question 3Easy
    The first Indian text to state systematic rules for arithmetic with zero and with negative numbers (fortunes and debts) is

    Why

    Brahmagupta’s Brahmasphutasiddhanta (628 CE) states the rules, for example \(a-a=0\), \(a\times 0=0\), and that the product of two debts is a fortune.
  4. Question 4Medium
    The area of a cyclic quadrilateral with sides \(6, 7, 8, 11\), by Brahmagupta’s formula, is

    Why

    Here \(s=16\), so \(K=\sqrt{10\cdot9\cdot8\cdot5}=\sqrt{3600}=60\). The value \(63=\frac{6+8}2\cdot\frac{7+11}2\) is the old rough rule (mean of opposite sides), which overestimates.
  5. Question 5Medium
    Bhaskara I’s approximation is \(\sin x\approx\dfrac{16x(\pi-x)}{5\pi^2-4x(\pi-x)}\). For \(x=\pi/6\) it gives

    Why

    \(x(\pi-x)=\dfrac\pi6\cdot\dfrac{5\pi}6=\dfrac{5\pi^2}{36}\). The numerator is \(\dfrac{20\pi^2}{9}\) and the denominator is \(5\pi^2-\dfrac{5\pi^2}{9}=\dfrac{40\pi^2}{9}\), so the value is \(\dfrac12\). The formula is exact at \(0\), \(\pi/6\), \(\pi/2\) and \(\pi\).
  6. Question 6Easy
    The value of \(\pi\) given in the Aryabhatiya is

    Why

    Aryabhata I gives \(62832/20000=3.1416\), as an approximate value. \(22/7\) is the common school value; \(355/113\) is a later Chinese and European convergent.
  7. Question 7Easy
    The series \(\dfrac\pi4=1-\dfrac13+\dfrac15-\cdots\) was first obtained, about 1400 CE, by

    Why

    Madhava of the Kerala school found it (and the sine and cosine series), well before Gregory (1671) and Leibniz (1674).
  8. Question 8Medium
    The number of partitions of \(8\) (ways of writing \(8\) as an unordered sum of positive integers) is

    Why

    Euler’s pentagonal recurrence gives \(p(8)=p(7)+p(6)-p(3)-p(1)=15+11-3-1=22\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    Which of the following is a highly composite number (more divisors than every smaller positive integer)?

    Why

    \(48=2^4\cdot3\) has \(10\) divisors, more than any smaller number (the previous record was \(9\), for \(36\)). \(18\) and \(20\) have \(6\) divisors, no more than \(12\); \(30\) has \(8\), no more than \(24\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    Kaprekar’s routine (descending arrangement minus ascending arrangement, keeping leading zeros) is applied to \(2111\). The number of steps needed to reach \(6174\) is

    Why

    \(2111-1112=0999\), \(9990-0999=8991\), \(9981-1899=8082\), \(8820-0288=8532\), \(8532-2358=6174\): five steps. Seven is the maximum over all four-digit numbers, not the count for this one.
  11. Question 11Medium
    Which of the following is NOT a Kaprekar number?

    Why

    \(45^2=2025\to20+25\), \(55^2=3025\to30+25\), \(297^2=88209\to88+209\). But \(46^2=2116\) gives \(21+16=37\) and \(2+116=118\), neither equal to \(46\).
  12. Question 12Hard
    Which of the following is one of Ramanujan’s congruences for the partition function \(p(n)\)?

    Why

    The three congruences are \(p(5n+4)\equiv0\ (5)\), \(p(7n+5)\equiv0\ (7)\), \(p(11n+6)\equiv0\ (11)\). The others fail at \(n=0\): \(p(1)=1\), \(p(4)=5\), \(p(5)=7\).
  13. Question 13Medium
    Assertion (A): For every quadrilateral with sides \(a,b,c,d\) the area is \(\sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)}\). Reason (R): Brahmagupta’s formula is a generalisation of Heron’s formula.

    Why

    The formula is exact only for cyclic quadrilaterals, so A is false. R is true: putting \(d=0\) gives Heron’s formula for a triangle.
  14. Question 14Hard
    Baudhayana’s Sulba Sutra approximation \(\sqrt2\approx1+\dfrac13+\dfrac1{3\cdot4}-\dfrac1{3\cdot4\cdot34}\) equals

    Why

    \(\dfrac{408+136+34-1}{408}=\dfrac{577}{408}\approx1.414216\). The other fractions are other convergents of \(\sqrt2\): \(\frac{17}{12}\) comes from truncating after the first three terms.
  15. Question 15Hard
    Ramanujan’s tau function is defined by \(\sum\tau(n)q^n=q\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{24}\), with \(\tau(2)=-24\) and \(\tau(5)=4830\). Then \(\tau(10)=\)

    Why

    \(\tau\) is multiplicative, so \(\tau(10)=\tau(2)\tau(5)=(-24)(4830)=-115920\). Adding the values (\(4806\)) or dropping the sign are the usual slips.
