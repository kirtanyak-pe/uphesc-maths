Topic 1: Indian mathematicians, Ramanujan, Kaprekar

  • Sulba Sutras (Baudhayana, Apastamba, Katyayana, Manava): \(\sqrt2\approx1+\frac13+\frac1{12}-\frac1{408}=\frac{577}{408}\).
  • Aryabhata I: Aryabhatiya (499), \(\pi\approx\frac{62832}{20000}\); \(\sum k^2=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6\); \(\sum k^3=\left(\frac{n(n+1)}2\right)^2\); kuttaka for \(by=ax\pm c\).
  • Brahmagupta: Brahmasphutasiddhanta (628); cyclic quadrilateral \(K=\sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)}\); \((a^2+nb^2)(c^2+nd^2)=(ac-nbd)^2+n(ad+bc)^2\); least solution of \(92x^2+1=y^2\) (his own example) is \((120,\ 1151)\); Bhaskara II’s chakravala example \(61x^2+1=y^2\) has least solution \((226153980,\ 1766319049)\).
  • Bhaskara I: \(\sin x\approx\frac{16x(\pi-x)}{5\pi^2-4x(\pi-x)}\). Bhaskara II: born 1114; Siddhanta Siromani (1150); chakravala.
  • Madhava: \(\frac\pi4=1-\frac13+\frac15-\cdots\); series for \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\).
  • Ramanujan: \(p(n)\sim\frac1{4n\sqrt3}e^{\pi\sqrt{2n/3}}\); \(p(5n+4)\equiv0\ (5)\), \(p(7n+5)\equiv0\ (7)\), \(p(11n+6)\equiv0\ (11)\); \(1729=1^3+12^3=9^3+10^3\); \(\frac1\pi=\frac{2\sqrt2}{9801}\sum\frac{(4k)!(1103+26390k)}{(k!)^4396^{4k}}\).
  • Kaprekar: constant \(6174\) (4 digits), \(495\) (3 digits); Kaprekar numbers \(1,9,45,55,99,297,703,\dots\)

Topic 2: Vedic mathematics

  • Book: Bharati Krishna Tirtha, 1965; 16 sutras, 13 sub-sutras.
  • Nikhilam: \((B+a)(B+b)=B(B+a+b)+ab\). Yavadunam: \((B+d)^2=B(B+2d)+d^2\).
  • Squares ending in 5: \((10n+5)^2=100\,n(n+1)+25\).
  • Urdhva-tiryagbhyam: \((10a+b)(10c+d)=100ac+10(ad+bc)+bd\).
  • Antyayor Dasake’pi: \((10a+b)(10a+c)\) with \(b+c=10\) gives \(100a(a+1)+bc\).
  • Digit sum: \(n\equiv\mathrm{ds}(n)\pmod9\); alternating digit sum for 11; \(1001=7\cdot11\cdot13\).
  • Osculators: \(+5\) for 7, \(+4\) for 13, \(+2\) for 19.

Topic 3: Divisibility and primes

  • Division algorithm: \(a=bq+r\), \(0\le r<b\).
  • \(\gcd(a,b)=ax+by\) (Bézout); \(\gcd(a,b)\cdot\mathrm{lcm}(a,b)=ab\); \(ax+by=c\) is solvable iff \(\gcd(a,b)\mid c\), with general solution \(x=x_0+\frac bdt\), \(y=y_0-\frac adt\).
  • Euclid’s lemma: \(p\mid ab\Rightarrow p\mid a\) or \(p\mid b\).
  • Fundamental theorem of arithmetic: every \(n>1\) is a product of primes, uniquely up to order.
  • A composite \(n\) has a prime factor \(\le\sqrt n\). There are infinitely many primes (Euclid). Bertrand: a prime lies between \(n\) and \(2n\) for \(n>1\). Prime number theorem: \(\pi(x)\sim x/\ln x\). Dirichlet: a progression \(a+nd\) with \(\gcd(a,d)=1\) has infinitely many primes.
  • Legendre’s formula: \(v_p(n!)=\sum_{k\ge1}\lfloor n/p^k\rfloor\).
  • Perfect numbers: \(2^{p-1}(2^p-1)\) with \(2^p-1\) prime (Euclid); every even perfect number has this form (Euler). First four: \(6,28,496,8128\).
  • Pythagorean triples (primitive): \((m^2-n^2,\ 2mn,\ m^2+n^2)\) with \(\gcd(m,n)=1\) and \(m-n\) odd.
  • Twin primes between 51 and 100: \((59,61)\) and \((71,73)\).

Topic 4: Arithmetical functions

For \(n=p_1^{a_1}\cdots p_r^{a_r}\):

  • \(\tau(n)=\prod(a_i+1)\); \(\sigma(n)=\prod\dfrac{p_i^{a_i+1}-1}{p_i-1}\); \(\sigma_k(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d^k\).
  • \(\varphi(n)=n\prod\left(1-\frac1{p_i}\right)\); \(\varphi(p^a)=p^{a}-p^{a-1}\).
  • \(\mu(1)=1\); \(\mu(n)=(-1)^r\) if \(n\) is square-free with \(r\) prime factors; \(\mu(n)=0\) otherwise.
  • Number of square-free divisors \(=2^r\). Product of divisors \(=n^{\tau(n)/2}\).
  • \(\sigma(180)=546\) (since \(180=2^2\cdot3^2\cdot5\)).
  • \(\tau,\sigma,\varphi,\mu\) are multiplicative: \(f(mn)=f(m)f(n)\) for \(\gcd(m,n)=1\).
  • \(\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)=n\); \(\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)=1\) if \(n=1\) and \(0\) otherwise.
  • Möbius inversion: \(F(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}f(d)\iff f(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)F(d)\). Hence \(\varphi(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\,\frac nd\).
  • Dirichlet convolution \((f*g)(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}f(d)g(n/d)\); \(\mu*1=\varepsilon\); \(\varphi*1=\mathrm{id}\).
  • Perfect: \(\sigma(n)=2n\); abundant: \(\sigma(n)>2n\); deficient: \(\sigma(n)<2n\).
  • Mersenne: \(2^p-1\) prime requires \(p\) prime.

Topic 5: Congruences

  • \(a\equiv b\pmod m\iff m\mid a-b\). Congruence is compatible with \(+\) and \(\times\) but you may cancel \(c\) only if \(\gcd(c,m)=1\).
  • Linear congruence \(ax\equiv b\pmod m\): solvable iff \(d=\gcd(a,m)\mid b\), then there are exactly \(d\) solutions modulo \(m\). If \(\gcd(a,m)=1\) the solution is \(x\equiv a^{-1}b\).
  • Fermat: \(a^p\equiv a\pmod p\); if \(p\nmid a\), \(a^{p-1}\equiv1\).
  • Euler: \(\gcd(a,m)=1\Rightarrow a^{\varphi(m)}\equiv1\pmod m\).
  • Wilson: \((p-1)!\equiv-1\pmod p\) iff \(p\) is prime. Also \(((p-1)/2)!^2\equiv(-1)^{(p+1)/2}\pmod p\).
  • Chinese remainder theorem: for pairwise coprime \(m_i\) with \(M=\prod m_i\) and \(M_i=M/m_i\), the system \(x\equiv a_i\pmod{m_i}\) has the unique solution \(x\equiv\sum a_iM_iy_i\pmod M\) where \(M_iy_i\equiv1\pmod{m_i}\).
  • Order of \(a\) mod \(m\) divides \(\varphi(m)\). Primitive roots exist modulo \(m\) iff \(m=1,2,4,p^k,2p^k\) (\(p\) odd prime).
  • A polynomial congruence of degree \(n\) has at most \(n\) roots modulo a prime (Lagrange).
  • \(x^2\equiv1\pmod n\) (\(n\) odd) has \(2^{\omega(n)}\) solutions.
  • \(341=11\cdot31\) is a pseudoprime to base 2: \(2^{340}\equiv1\pmod{341}\). \(561\) is the smallest Carmichael number.

Topic 6: Quadratic residues

For an odd prime \(p\) and \(p\nmid a\):

  • Euler’s criterion: \(a^{(p-1)/2}\equiv\left(\dfrac ap\right)\pmod p\). Exactly \(\frac{p-1}2\) residues and \(\frac{p-1}2\) non-residues.
  • \(\left(\frac{ab}p\right)=\left(\frac ap\right)\left(\frac bp\right)\); \(\left(\frac ap\right)\) depends only on \(a\bmod p\).
  • \(\left(\frac{-1}p\right)=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\): \(+1\) iff \(p\equiv1\pmod4\).
  • \(\left(\frac2p\right)=(-1)^{(p^2-1)/8}\): \(+1\) iff \(p\equiv\pm1\pmod8\).
  • Quadratic reciprocity: for distinct odd primes \(p,q\),
\[\left(\frac pq\right)\left(\frac qp\right)=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}2\cdot\frac{q-1}2}.\]

So the two symbols are equal unless \(p\equiv q\equiv3\pmod4\), in which case they are opposite.

  • Gauss’s lemma: \(\left(\frac ap\right)=(-1)^\mu\) where \(\mu\) counts the residues \(ka\bmod p\) (\(1\le k\le\frac{p-1}2\)) exceeding \(p/2\).
  • \(\left(\frac3p\right)=1\) iff \(p\equiv\pm1\pmod{12}\); \(\left(\frac5p\right)=1\) iff \(p\equiv\pm1\pmod5\); \(\left(\frac{-3}p\right)=1\) iff \(p\equiv1\pmod3\); \(\left(\frac{-2}p\right)=1\) iff \(p\equiv1,3\pmod8\).
  • Jacobi symbol \(\left(\frac an\right)=\prod\left(\frac a{p_i}\right)^{e_i}\) for \(n=\prod p_i^{e_i}\) odd; it obeys the same reciprocity and supplementary laws, but \(\left(\frac an\right)=1\) does not imply that \(a\) is a residue mod \(n\).
  • Number of solutions of \(x^2\equiv a\pmod p\) is \(1+\left(\frac ap\right)\).
  • Special values: \(\left(\frac2{97}\right)=1\) since \(97\equiv1\pmod8\); \(\left(\frac{-1}{101}\right)=1\) since \(101\equiv1\pmod4\); \(\left(\frac{29}{43}\right)=-1\).
