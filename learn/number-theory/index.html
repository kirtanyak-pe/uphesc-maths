What this unit covers

Unit 10 mixes two kinds of material.

  • Recall (topics 1 and 2): Indian mathematicians and their works with dates, Ramanujan and Kaprekar, and the methods of Vedic mathematics (Nikhilam, Urdhva-tiryagbhyam, Ekadhikena, digit sums).
  • Calculation and proof (topics 3 to 6): the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, primes, gcd and the Euclidean algorithm, arithmetical functions (\(\tau,\sigma,\varphi,\mu\)), Möbius inversion, congruences, Fermat, Euler and Wilson, the Chinese remainder theorem, Legendre and Jacobi symbols and the quadratic reciprocity law with its applications.

How it is asked

The unit gave 5 questions in 2025 and 7 in 2026 (the 2019 paper is only partly recovered). The official syllabus note says at least six questions come from each unit, so expect six or more again.

  • 2025: Q88 twin-prime pairs between 51 and 100; Q89 the sum of the divisors of 180; Q90 \(2^{340}\bmod341\) (a Fermat pseudoprime question); Q91 order of four Indian texts; Q92 match 1729, 1887, 6174 and the birth year of Bhaskara II.
  • 2026: Q37 divisor count with a congruence condition; Q43 square-free divisors of \(pqr\); Q48 \(1003\times997\bmod13\) by the Vedic method (the printed options contain no correct value); Q53 and Q73 Legendre symbols; Q63 which \(n<50\) with \(\mu(n)\ne0\) has most divisors (two options are correct); Q85 the symbol \((29/43)\) by reciprocity.
  • Pattern: every question is a short numeric computation with a small number, or a name-date recall. There are no long proofs; but the options are chosen from typical slips (forgetting the factor 1 in \(\tau\), using \(\varphi\) in place of \(\sigma\), losing a sign in reciprocity).

High-yield list

  1. Chronology of Indian texts: Aryabhatiya 499, Panchasiddhantika 575, Brahmasphutasiddhanta 628, then Mahavira (c. 850), Sridhara (Trisatika), Bhaskara II (1150).
  2. 1729 (taxi-cab), 1887 (Ramanujan’s birth), 6174 (Kaprekar’s constant), 1114 (Bhaskara II’s birth).
  3. Who wrote what: Brahmasphutasiddhanta (Brahmagupta), Lilavati and Bijaganita (Bhaskara II), Ganita Sara Sangraha (Mahavira), Yuktibhasa (Jyesthadeva), Tantrasangraha (Nilakantha).
  4. The 16 sutras by name and meaning; Nikhilam on numbers near 100 and 1000.
  5. \(\tau,\sigma,\varphi\) of a number from its prime factorisation; the count of square-free divisors \(2^r\).
  6. The count of \(n\) with a given number of divisors; for \(\mu(n)\ne0\) the number is square-free.
  7. Fermat and Euler reductions of large powers; Fermat pseudoprimes (\(341\)).
  8. Chinese remainder theorem with three moduli such as \(3,5,7\).
  9. Legendre symbols of small numbers by Euler’s criterion, the supplementary laws and reciprocity.
  10. Wilson’s theorem and primality tests; Möbius inversion for \(\varphi\).
