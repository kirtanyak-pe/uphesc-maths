  1. Question 1Easy
    The book Vedic Mathematics of Bharati Krishna Tirtha, with its list of 16 sutras, was first published in

    Why

    It appeared in 1965, five years after the author’s death in 1960, edited by V. S. Agrawala. 1884 is the author’s year of birth.
  2. Question 2Easy
    By the method of adding neighbouring digits, \(486\times11\) equals

    Why

    Write \(4\;|\;4+8\;|\;8+6\;|\;6=4,12,14,6\). Carrying from the right: \(6\); \(14\to4\) carry \(1\); \(12+1=13\to3\) carry \(1\); \(4+1=5\). Result \(5346\).
  3. Question 3Medium
    By Nikhilam with base \(100\), \(94\times88\) equals

    Why

    Deviations \(-6\) and \(-12\). Left part \(94-12=82\) (or \(88-6=82\)), right part \((-6)(-12)=72\). Result \(8272\).
  4. Question 4Medium
    By Nikhilam with base \(1000\), \(1012\times1004\) equals

    Why

    Deviations \(+12,+4\). Left \(1012+4=1016\), right \(12\cdot4=48\), written with three digits as \(048\). Result \(1016048\). The slip in B is writing the right part as \(408\) or \(480\).
  5. Question 5Easy
    Using Ekadhikena Purvena, \(85^2\) equals

    Why

    \(8\times9=72\) then \(25\): \(7225\). The slip in C is \(8\times8\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    Using the cube rule near the base \(100\), \(102^3\) equals

    Why

    With \(d=2\) the cube is \((100+3d)\,|\,3d^2\,|\,d^3=106\,|\,12\,|\,08\), each right part taking two digits. Result \(1061208\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    Applying Ekadhikena Purvena to \(1/29\) (multiplier \(3\)), the repeating block of the decimal has how many digits?

    Why

    The period is the order of \(10\) modulo \(29\), which is \(28\) since \(10\) is a primitive root mod \(29\). (For \(1/19\) it is \(18\).) A block of \(29\) digits is impossible: the period divides \(\varphi(29)=28\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    Using Urdhva-tiryagbhyam, \(124\times132\) equals

    Why

    Columns from the right: \(4\cdot2=8\); \(2\cdot2+4\cdot3=16\); \(1\cdot2+2\cdot3+4\cdot1=12\); \(1\cdot3+2\cdot1=5\); \(1\cdot1=1\). Carrying gives \(16368\).
  9. Question 9Hard
    A student claims \(47\times38=N\) and checks it by casting out nines. Which value of \(N\) passes the check although it is wrong?

    Why

    \(\mathrm{ds}(47)\mathrm{ds}(38)=2\cdot2=4\). \(\mathrm{ds}(1768)=22\to4\), so \(1768\) passes, but the true product is \(1786\) (the digits \(6,8\) are transposed, which the check cannot see). The others have digit sums \(5,3,3\) and are caught.
  10. Question 10Medium
    By splitting into groups of three digits and taking the alternating sum (using \(1001=7\cdot11\cdot13\)), which number is divisible by \(7\)?

    Why

    The groups are \(5,471,893\) and the alternating sum is \(893-471+5=427=7\cdot61\), so \(7\mid5471893\). The other three numbers, \(2345678\), \(123456789\) and \(1234567\), leave remainders \(6\), \(1\) and \(5\) modulo \(7\).
  11. Question 11Easy
    Match. a. Ekadhikena Purvena, b. Ekanyunena Purvena, c. Urdhva-tiryagbhyam, d. Nikhilam Navatascaramam Dasatah. I. All from 9 and the last from 10, II. By one less than the previous one, III. By one more than the previous one, IV. Vertically and crosswise.

    Why

    Ekadhikena is “one more”, Ekanyunena “one less”, Urdhva-tiryagbhyam “vertically and crosswise”, Nikhilam “all from 9 and the last from 10”.
  12. Question 12Hard
    Which one of the following is a main sutra and NOT a sub-sutra in the list of the book?

    Why

    Puranapuranabhyam (“by the completion or non-completion”) is sutra No. 8. Vilokanam (by mere observation), Lopanasthapanabhyam (by alternate elimination and retention) and Anurupyena (proportionately) are sub-sutras.
  13. Question 13Hard
    Using \(1006\times994=1000^2-6^2=999964\) and \(999999=999\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13\), the value of \(1006\times994\pmod{11}\) is

    Why

    \(999999\equiv0\pmod{11}\), so \(999964\equiv-35\equiv-2\equiv9\). Direct check: \(1006\equiv5\) and \(994\equiv4\) modulo \(11\) (since \(1001\equiv0\)), and \(5\cdot4=20\equiv9\). The option \(2\) is the residue of \(35\) with the sign lost.
  14. Question 14Medium
    By Antyayor Dasake’pi (first parts equal, last digits adding to \(10\)), \(73\times77\) equals

    Why

    Left \(7\times(7+1)=56\), right \(3\times7=21\). Result \(5621\).
  15. Question 15Medium
    Dividing \(4567\) by \(12\) with the flag \(-2\) gives quotient digits \(4,-3,12\) and a last remainder digit \(-17\). The quotient and the remainder (between \(0\) and \(11\)) are

    Why

    \(4\,|\,{-3}\,|\,12=400-30+12=382\) with remainder \(-17\). A negative remainder is corrected by lowering the quotient and adding the divisor: two steps give \(380\) and \(-17+24=7\). Check: \(12\cdot380+7=4567\).
