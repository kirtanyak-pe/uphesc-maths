Why it matters for the exam

Field theory is the most reliable algebra topic outside group theory: all three papers have it (2019: degree of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)\) and a roots-of-unity sum; 2025 Q36: degree of the splitting field of \(x^4+x^2+1\); 2026 Q52: elements of \(\mathbb F_{27}\) with \(x^n=1\)). Questions are numeric: degrees by the tower law, sizes and subfields of finite fields, cyclotomic polynomials, orders in \(\mathbb F_q^{*}\).

Core ideas

Field extensions and degree

If \(F\subseteq E\) are fields, \(E\) is an extension of \(F\), written \(E/F\). \(E\) is a vector space over \(F\); \([E:F]=\dim_F E\) is the degree. Finite extension means finite degree.

Tower law. For \(F\subseteq K\subseteq E\): \([E:F]=[E:K]\,[K:F]\).

Consequences used constantly:

  • \([K:F]\) divides \([E:F]\). If \([E:F]\) is prime there is no intermediate field, and \(E=F(\alpha)\) for every \(\alpha\in E\setminus F\).
  • If \(K,L\) are subfields of a common field containing \(F\) and \(\gcd([K:F],[L:F])=1\), then \([KL:F]=[K:F][L:F]\) (the compositum has degree divisible by both, and at most the product).
  • Every finite extension is algebraic.

The prime subfield of a field is \(\mathbb Q\) (char \(0\)) or \(\mathbb F_p\) (char \(p\)).

Algebraic elements and minimal polynomials

\(\alpha\in E\) is algebraic over \(F\) if \(f(\alpha)=0\) for some nonzero \(f\in F[x]\); otherwise transcendental.

Minimal polynomial \(m_{\alpha,F}\): the monic polynomial of least degree with \(m(\alpha)=0\). Facts:

  • \(m\) is irreducible over \(F\); and if \(f(\alpha)=0\) then \(m\mid f\). Conversely a monic irreducible \(f\) with \(f(\alpha)=0\) is the minimal polynomial.
  • \(F(\alpha)\cong F[x]/(m)\), so \([F(\alpha):F]=\deg m\), with basis \(1,\alpha,\dots,\alpha^{\deg m-1}\). If \(\alpha\) is transcendental, \(F(\alpha)\cong F(x)\) and the degree is infinite.
  • The algebraic elements of \(E\) over \(F\) form a subfield (sums, products, inverses of algebraic numbers are algebraic). The algebraic numbers are countable; \(e\) and \(\pi\) are transcendental (Hermite, Lindemann); so most reals are transcendental.
  • Minimal polynomial of \(\sqrt2+\sqrt3\) over \(\mathbb Q\): \(x^4-10x^2+1\) (degree \(4\)). Of \(1+\sqrt2\): \(x^2-2x-1\). Of \(\sqrt[3]2\): \(x^3-2\). Of \(\sqrt2+\sqrt[3]2\): degree \(6\).

Primitive element theorem. A finite separable extension (in particular any finite extension in characteristic \(0\), and any finite extension of a finite field) is simple: \(E=F(\gamma)\).

Splitting fields

A splitting field of \(f\in F[x]\) over \(F\) is an extension \(K\) in which \(f\) splits into linear factors and \(K=F(\text{roots of }f)\).

  • Exists, and is unique up to \(F\)-isomorphism.
  • \([K:F]\le n!\) and \([K:F]\) divides \(n!\) where \(n=\deg f\). For irreducible \(f\) of degree \(n\), \(n\mid[K:F]\).
  • Splitting field of a product is the compositum of the splitting fields of the factors.

2025 Q36. \(x^4+x^2+1=(x^2+x+1)(x^2-x+1)\). The roots are \(\omega,\omega^2,-\omega,-\omega^2\), the primitive cube and sixth roots of unity, and \(-\omega,-\omega^2\) lie in \(\mathbb Q(\omega)\) because \(-1\in\mathbb Q\). All four lie in \(\mathbb Q(\omega)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\), and \(\omega\notin\mathbb Q\). The splitting field is \(\mathbb Q(\omega)\), degree \(\mathbf 2\) (option A). The trap is to say “degree \(4\) polynomial, so degree \(4\) or \(24\)”.

For irreducible cubics: the splitting field has degree \(3\) if the discriminant is a rational square, and \(6\) otherwise (\(S_3\)). \(x^3-2\): discriminant \(-108\), not a square, degree \(6\).

Over a finite field: if \(f\in\mathbb F_q[x]\) is irreducible of degree \(n\), its splitting field is \(\mathbb F_{q^n}\). In general the splitting field of \(f\) is \(\mathbb F_{q^m}\) with \(m=\operatorname{lcm}\) of the degrees of the irreducible factors. For example \(x^3-2\) over \(\mathbb F_5\): \(x=3\) is a root (\(27\equiv2\)) and since \(\gcd(3,4)=1\) no other cube roots of \(2\) exist in \(\mathbb F_5\), so \(x^3-2=(x-3)\cdot(\text{irreducible quadratic})\) and the splitting field is \(\mathbb F_{25}\). Over \(\mathbb F_7\): no root (\(2\) is not a cube mod \(7\); cubes are \(\pm1\)), so irreducible and the splitting field is \(\mathbb F_{343}\).

Separability and Galois, only as much as degrees need

\(f\) irreducible over \(F\) is separable if it has distinct roots in a splitting field, iff \(\gcd(f,f')=1\), iff \(f'\neq0\). Irreducible polynomials over fields of characteristic \(0\) and over finite fields are always separable (“perfect” fields). Inseparable example: \(x^p-t\) over \(\mathbb F_p(t)\).

For a Galois extension \(E/F\) (splitting field of a separable polynomial), \(\lvert\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)\rvert=[E:F]\). In general \(\lvert\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)\rvert\le[E:F]\), with strict inequality for \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)/\mathbb Q\) (only the identity). \(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)/\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/n)^{*}\). \(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb F_{p^n}/\mathbb F_p)\) is cyclic of order \(n\), generated by Frobenius \(x\mapsto x^p\).

Finite fields

Theorem (Galois).

  1. A finite field has \(q=p^n\) elements, \(p=\operatorname{char}\), \(n=[F:\mathbb F_p]\). No field has \(6,10,12,15,18,20,\dots\) elements.
  2. For every prime power \(q=p^n\) there is a field \(\mathbb F_q=\mathrm{GF}(q)\), the splitting field of \(x^q-x\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), and it is unique up to isomorphism. Note \(\mathbb Z_{p^n}\not\cong\mathbb F_{p^n}\) for \(n>1\).
  3. \(x^q-x=\prod_{a\in\mathbb F_q}(x-a)\). Every \(a\in\mathbb F_q\) satisfies \(a^q=a\); the nonzero ones satisfy \(a^{q-1}=1\).
  4. \(\mathbb F_{p^m}\) is a subfield of \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\) iff \(m\mid n\), and then it is the unique subfield of order \(p^m\). So \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\) has exactly \(\tau(n)\) subfields. For example \(\mathbb F_{2^{12}}\) has \(6\) subfields; \(\mathbb F_{2^6}\supset\mathbb F_{2^3},\mathbb F_{2^2}\supset\mathbb F_2\); \(\mathbb F_4\not\subset\mathbb F_8\).
  5. The multiplicative group \(\mathbb F_q^{*}\) is cyclic of order \(q-1\). Proof idea: for each \(d\mid q-1\) the polynomial \(x^d-1\) has at most \(d\) roots, so there are at most \(d\) elements of order dividing \(d\); counting by orders forces exactly \(\varphi(d)\) elements of order \(d\), in particular \(\varphi(q-1)>0\) generators. More generally every finite subgroup of \(F^{*}\) (\(F\) any field) is cyclic.
  6. \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb F_{p^n})\) is cyclic of order \(n\) generated by Frobenius \(\sigma(x)=x^p\).
  7. \(\mathbb F_q\) is the quotient \(\mathbb F_p[x]/(f)\) for any irreducible \(f\) of degree \(n\). The root of \(f\) need not be a generator of \(\mathbb F_q^{*}\); if it is, \(f\) is called primitive.
  8. An irreducible polynomial of degree \(d\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) divides \(x^{p^n}-x\) iff \(d\mid n\). Thus \(x^{p^n}-x\) is the product of all monic irreducibles over \(\mathbb F_p\) of degree dividing \(n\).

Counting results:

  • Number of \(x\in\mathbb F_q^{*}\) with \(x^n=1\) is \(\gcd(n,q-1)\) (cyclic group). 2026 Q52: \(\mathbb F_{27}^{*}\) is cyclic of order \(26=2\cdot13\); \(x^{13}=1\) has \(\gcd(13,26)=13\) solutions in \(\mathbb F_{27}\) (the OCR of the exponent is uncertain; the worked key reads \(x^{13}=1\) and gives \(13\)). If the exponent differs, apply \(\gcd(n,26)\) and remember \(x=0\) is not a solution of \(x^n=1\).
  • Number of generators of \(\mathbb F_q^{*}\): \(\varphi(q-1)\). \(\mathbb F_{25}^{*}\): \(\varphi(24)=8\).
  • Number of squares in \(\mathbb F_q^{*}\) (\(q\) odd): \((q-1)/2\).
  • Elements of \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\) lying in no proper subfield: by inclusion-exclusion. For \(\mathbb F_{2^6}\): \(64-8-4+2=54\), and \(54/6=9\) monic irreducible sextics (matches \(N_2(6)=9\)). For \(\mathbb F_{16}\): \(16-4=12\) elements generate it over \(\mathbb F_2\).
  • Number of monic irreducible polynomials of degree \(n\) over \(\mathbb F_q\): \(\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)q^{n/d}\).
  • Wilson’s theorem is the constant term in \(x^{p-1}-1=\prod_{a\ne0}(x-a)\): \((p-1)!\equiv-1\pmod p\).

Small examples: \(\mathbb F_4=\{0,1,\omega,\omega^2\}\) with \(\omega^2=\omega+1\); \(\mathbb F_8=\mathbb F_2[x]/(x^3+x+1)\); \(\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3[x]/(x^2+1)\) (here \(x\) has order \(4\), not a generator; \(x+1\) has order \(8\)); \(\mathbb F_{27}=\mathbb F_3[x]/(x^3-x+1)\).

Roots of unity

The complex \(n\)-th roots of \(1\) are \(\zeta_n^k=e^{2\pi ik/n}\), \(k=0,\dots,n-1\). They form a cyclic group \(\mu_n\) of order \(n\); \(\zeta^k\) is primitive iff \(\gcd(k,n)=1\), so there are \(\varphi(n)\) primitive ones.

  • \(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\zeta^k=0\) for any \(\zeta\neq1\) with \(\zeta^n=1\). So the sum of all \(n\)-th roots is \(0\) (\(n>1\)), and the sum of any \(3n\) consecutive powers \(1+\omega+\dots+\omega^{3n-1}\) is \(n\cdot0=0\) (2019 Q99, answer \(0\)).
  • Product of all \(n\)-th roots of unity: \((-1)^{n+1}\) (from the constant term of \(x^n-1\)). For \(n=6\): \(-1\).
  • Sum of the primitive \(n\)-th roots is \(\mu(n)\) (Möbius): \(\mu(30)=-1\), \(\mu(12)=0\), \(\mu(10)=1\).
  • Roots of unity in \(\mathbb Q\): \(\pm1\). In \(\mathbb Q(i)\): the \(4\)th roots. In \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\): the \(6\)th roots. A finite subgroup of \(\mathbb C^{*}\) is some \(\mu_n\).
  • \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2n})\) for odd \(n\).

Cyclotomic polynomials

\(\Phi_n(x)=\prod_{\gcd(k,n)=1}(x-\zeta_n^k)\), monic of degree \(\varphi(n)\) with integer coefficients, and irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) (so \([\mathbb Q(\zeta_n):\mathbb Q]=\varphi(n)\)).

  • \(x^n-1=\prod_{d\mid n}\Phi_d(x)\); compute recursively by division.
  • \(\Phi_p=x^{p-1}+\dots+1\); \(\Phi_{p^k}(x)=\Phi_p(x^{p^{k-1}})\); \(\Phi_{2n}(x)=\Phi_n(-x)\) for odd \(n>1\).
  • \(\Phi_n(0)=1\) for \(n>1\). \(\Phi_n(1)=p\) if \(n=p^k\), and \(\Phi_n(1)=1\) if \(n>1\) is not a prime power.

Constructibility (degrees only)

A real \(\alpha\) is constructible iff it lies in a tower of quadratic extensions of \(\mathbb Q\), so \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\) must be a power of \(2\). Hence doubling the cube (\(\sqrt[3]2\) has degree \(3\)), trisecting \(60^\circ\) (cubic \(4x^3-3x-\tfrac12\)) and squaring the circle (\(\pi\) transcendental) are impossible. A regular \(n\)-gon is constructible iff \(\varphi(n)\) is a power of \(2\), iff \(n=2^k p_1\cdots p_r\) with \(p_i\) distinct Fermat primes (\(3,5,17,257,65537\)). So \(3,4,5,6,8,10,12,15,16,17,\dots\) yes; \(7,9,11,13,14,18,19\) no.

Worked examples

Example 1: degree and minimal polynomial of \(\sqrt2+\sqrt3\)

Let \(\alpha=\sqrt2+\sqrt3\). \(\alpha^2=5+2\sqrt6\), \((\alpha^2-5)^2=24\), so \(\alpha^4-10\alpha^2+1=0\) (numerically \(\approx10^{-14}\)). Now \(\alpha^3=11\sqrt2+9\sqrt3\), so \(\sqrt2=(\alpha^3-9\alpha)/2\in\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and then \(\sqrt3=\alpha-\sqrt2\in\mathbb Q(\alpha)\). Hence \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)\) has degree \(4\), so the minimal polynomial has degree \(4\). The monic quartic with root \(\alpha\) is \(x^4-10x^2+1\), so that is the minimal polynomial.

Example 2: splitting field degrees

\(x^3-2\): roots \(\sqrt[3]2,\sqrt[3]2\omega,\sqrt[3]2\omega^2\). \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)\) has degree \(3\) and is real, so \(\omega\notin\) it; \([\mathbb Q(\omega):\mathbb Q]=2\); so the splitting field has degree divisible by \(2\) and \(3\), and at most \(3!=6\): degree \(6\). \(x^4-2\): \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]2)\) has degree \(4\) (Eisenstein), real, so \(i\notin\) it, so \([\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]2,i):\mathbb Q]=8\). \(x^4+x^2+1\): see 2025 Q36 above, degree \(2\).

Example 3: subfields and generators of \(\mathbb F_{64}\)

\(64=2^6\), divisors of \(6\): \(1,2,3,6\). Subfields: \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_8,\mathbb F_{64}\) (four, \(=\tau(6)\)). \(\mathbb F_4\cap\mathbb F_8=\mathbb F_2\). \(\mathbb F_{64}^{*}\) is cyclic of order \(63\); generators: \(\varphi(63)=36\). Elements of exact degree \(6\): \(64-8-4+2=54\).

Example 4: \(x^n=1\) in \(\mathbb F_{27}\) (2026 Q52)

\(\mathbb F_{27}^{*}\cong\mathbb Z_{26}\). Solutions of \(x^{13}=1\): the subgroup of order \(\gcd(13,26)=13\). Solutions of \(x^2=1\): \(2\). Solutions of \(x^3=1\): \(\gcd(3,26)=1\) (only \(x=1\)).

Example 5: cyclotomic polynomials

\(\Phi_{12}\): \(x^{12}-1=\prod_{d\mid12}\Phi_d\) with \(d=1,2,3,4,6,12\). The divisors \(1,2,3,6\) are the divisors of \(6\), so \(\Phi_1\Phi_2\Phi_3\Phi_6=x^6-1\); also \(\Phi_4=x^2+1\). Hence \(\Phi_{12}=\dfrac{x^{12}-1}{(x^6-1)(x^2+1)}=\dfrac{x^6+1}{x^2+1}=x^4-x^2+1\). Check: \(\Phi_{12}(1)=1\) as \(12\) is not a prime power. \([\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12}):\mathbb Q]=\varphi(12)=4\).

Example 6: roots of unity sums

If \(\omega\neq1\), \(\omega^3=1\), then \(1+\omega+\dots+\omega^{3n-1}\) is \(n\) copies of \((1+\omega+\omega^2)=0\). So the sum is \(0\) (2019 Q99). If the sum runs one term further, to \(\omega^{3n}\), it equals \(1\); always count the terms before grouping them in threes.

Common traps

  • Degree of the polynomial \(\neq\) degree of the splitting field. \(x^4+x^2+1\) has splitting degree \(2\); \(x^3-2\) has degree \(6\), not \(3\).
  • Adjoining two quadratic roots. \([\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt8):\mathbb Q]=2\) because \(\sqrt8=2\sqrt2\). Check whether \(ab\) is a square.
  • \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)\) is not normal. It does not contain \(\omega\); its automorphism group over \(\mathbb Q\) is trivial although the degree is \(3\).
  • Tower law needs a tower. \([\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2,\sqrt2):\mathbb Q]=6\) because \(2\) and \(3\) are coprime; for non-coprime degrees the product can be too big.
  • \(\mathbb Z_{p^n}\) is not \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\). \(\mathbb Z_4\) has zero divisors; \(\mathbb F_4=\mathbb F_2[x]/(x^2+x+1)\).
  • Subfields of finite fields. \(\mathbb F_{p^m}\subseteq\mathbb F_{p^n}\) needs \(m\mid n\). \(\mathbb F_4\) is not in \(\mathbb F_8\) nor in \(\mathbb F_{32}\); \(\mathbb F_9\subset\mathbb F_{81}\).
  • Order of a field. No field of order \(6,10,12,15,18\); fields of order \(4,8,9,16,25,27\) exist.
  • Solutions of \(x^n=1\) in \(\mathbb F_q\) are \(\gcd(n,q-1)\), not \(\gcd(n,q)\); and \(0\) never counts.
  • Number of generators of \(\mathbb F_q^{*}\) is \(\varphi(q-1)\), not \(q-1\) or \(\varphi(q)\).
  • Primitive roots of unity. Only \(\varphi(n)\) of the \(n\)-th roots are primitive; sum of them is \(\mu(n)\), not \(0\) in general (the sum of all \(n\)-th roots is \(0\)).
  • Roots of unity sum with the first term. \(1+\omega+\dots+\omega^{3n-1}\) has exactly \(3n\) terms; count terms before using \(1+\omega+\omega^2=0\).
  • \(\Phi_n(1)\). It is \(p\) only for prime powers \(p^k\); it is \(1\) for other \(n>1\).
  • Transcendental. \(\pi\) and \(e\) are transcendental; \(\pi+e\) and \(\pi e\) cannot both be algebraic, but it is not known whether either is.

Previous-year questions

2019 Q99 (re-typed from a video; answer (a))

Let \(\omega\neq1\) be a cube root of unity. Value of \(1+\omega+\omega^2+\dots+\omega^{3n-1}\)? Answer: \(0\). Group the \(3n\) terms in \(n\) blocks of three; each block is \(\omega^{3j}(1+\omega+\omega^2)=0\).

2019, unnumbered question 17 (partial, from a video; answer (d) \(4\))

Degree of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)\) over \(\mathbb Q\). Answer: \(4\). \([\mathbb Q(\sqrt2):\mathbb Q]=2\) and \(\sqrt3\notin\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), so the tower law gives \(2\cdot2=4\). (The video’s option mark was not seen; the mathematical answer is \(4\).)

2025 Q36 (official key: A)

Degree of the splitting field of \(x^4+x^2+1\in\mathbb Q[x]\): (A) \(2\) (B) \(3\) (C) \(4\) (D) \(6\). Answer: A, \(2\). \(x^4+x^2+1=(x^2+x+1)(x^2-x+1)\); the four roots are the primitive \(3\)rd and \(6\)th roots of unity, all in \(\mathbb Q(\omega)\), degree \(2\).

2026 Q52 (worked key: D, \(13\))

Number of elements of \(\mathbb F_{27}\) satisfying \(x^n=1\) (worked key reads the equation as \(x^{13}=1\); the exponent in the OCR is uncertain). Answer: \(13\). \(\mathbb F_{27}^{*}\) is cyclic of order \(26\), and \(\lvert\{x:x^{13}=1\}\rvert=\gcd(13,26)=13\).

Quick revision

  • Tower law: \([E:F]=[E:K][K:F]\); \([F(\alpha):F]=\deg\) of minimal polynomial.
  • \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt a,\sqrt b)\) has degree \(4\) unless \(a,b\) or \(ab\) is a square: \([\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\mathbb Q]=4\).
  • Splitting fields: \(x^3-2\to6\), \(x^4-2\to8\), \(x^4+x^2+1\to2\), \(x^n-1\to\varphi(n)\).
  • Splitting field of irreducible degree \(n\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) is \(\mathbb F_{q^n}\).
  • \(\lvert\mathbb F\rvert=p^n\); unique field for each prime power; \(\mathbb F_{p^m}\subseteq\mathbb F_{p^n}\) iff \(m\mid n\).
  • \(\mathbb F_q^{*}\) is cyclic: \(\varphi(q-1)\) generators; \(x^n=1\) has \(\gcd(n,q-1)\) solutions.
  • \(x^q-x=\prod_{a\in\mathbb F_q}(x-a)\); Frobenius \(x\mapsto x^p\) generates \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb F_{p^n})\cong\mathbb Z_n\).
  • Sum of all \(n\)-th roots of unity is \(0\); \(1+\omega+\dots+\omega^{3n-1}=0\); product is \((-1)^{n+1}\).
  • \(\Phi_n\) has degree \(\varphi(n)\), is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\); \(x^n-1=\prod_{d\mid n}\Phi_d\).
  • \(\Phi_{12}=x^4-x^2+1\), \(\Phi_8=x^4+1\), \(\Phi_6=x^2-x+1\).
  • Constructible \(\Rightarrow\) degree is a power of \(2\); \(n\)-gon needs \(\varphi(n)\) a power of \(2\).
