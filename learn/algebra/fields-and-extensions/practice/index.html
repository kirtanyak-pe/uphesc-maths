  1. Question 1Easy
    The degree \([\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{2}):\mathbb Q]\) is

    Why

    \(\sqrt[3]2\) is a root of \(x^3-2\), which is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) (Eisenstein at \(2\); or no rational root among \(\pm1,\pm2\)). So the degree is \(3\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    Which of the following numbers is NOT the order of any finite field?

    Why

    A finite field has \(p^n\) elements (\(p\) prime). \(8=2^3\), \(9=3^2\), \(16=2^4\) are prime powers; \(12=2^2\cdot3\) is not.
  3. Question 3Easy
    \([\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12}):\mathbb Q]\), where \(\zeta_{12}=e^{2\pi i/12}\), equals

    Why

    The cyclotomic polynomial \(\Phi_{12}=x^4-x^2+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), so the degree is \(\varphi(12)=4\) (not \(12\), which counts all roots of \(x^{12}-1\)).
  4. Question 4Medium
    \([\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt5):\mathbb Q]\) equals

    Why

    \(\sqrt3\notin\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) gives degree \(4\) for \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)\); \(\sqrt5\notin\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)\) (its elements are \(a+b\sqrt2+c\sqrt3+d\sqrt6\) and squaring shows none equals \(\sqrt5\)), so one more factor of \(2\): \(2\cdot2\cdot2=8\).
  5. Question 5Hard
    The number of elements in the splitting field of \(x^3-2\) over \(\mathbb Z_5\) is

    Why

    \(x=3\) is a root (\(27\equiv2\)). Dividing, \(x^3-2=(x-3)(x^2+3x+4)\) over \(\mathbb Z_5\), and \(x^2+3x+4\) has no root mod \(5\), so it is irreducible. The splitting field is generated by a root of this quadratic: \(\mathbb F_{5^2}\), with \(25\) elements. (Not \(5^3\): the cubic is reducible.)
  6. Question 6Medium
    The number of subfields of the finite field \(\mathbb F_{2^{12}}\) is

    Why

    \(\mathbb F_{2^m}\subseteq\mathbb F_{2^{12}}\) iff \(m\mid12\), one subfield for each divisor: \(m=1,2,3,4,6,12\), so \(6\) subfields.
  7. Question 7Hard
    The number of elements \(x\) of the field \(\mathbb F_{49}\) with \(x^{18}=1\) is

    Why

    \(\mathbb F_{49}^{*}\) is cyclic of order \(48\), so \(x^{18}=1\) has \(\gcd(18,48)=6\) solutions (confirmed by building \(\mathbb F_{49}=\mathbb F_7[i]\) and testing all elements).
  8. Question 8Medium
    The number of generators of the cyclic group \(\mathbb F_{49}^{*}\) is

    Why

    \(\lvert\mathbb F_{49}^{*}\rvert=48\) and a cyclic group of order \(n\) has \(\varphi(n)\) generators: \(\varphi(48)=\varphi(16)\varphi(3)=8\cdot2=16\) (confirmed by computing the order of every element).
  9. Question 9Medium
    The cyclotomic polynomial \(\Phi_{18}(x)\) is

    Why

    \(\varphi(18)=6\), and \(\Phi_{2n}(x)=\Phi_n(-x)\) for odd \(n\): \(\Phi_9(x)=x^6+x^3+1\) so \(\Phi_{18}(x)=x^6-x^3+1\). (\(x^6+x^3+1\) is \(\Phi_9\); \(x^4-x^2+1\) is \(\Phi_{12}\).) Checked by dividing \(x^{18}-1\) by \(\Phi_d\), \(d\mid18\), \(d<18\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    The sum of all primitive \(30\)-th roots of unity is

    Why

    The sum of the primitive \(n\)-th roots of unity is the Möbius value \(\mu(n)\). \(\mu(30)=\mu(2\cdot3\cdot5)=(-1)^3=-1\) (there are \(\varphi(30)=8\) of them; numerical sum \(=-1\)).
  11. Question 11Medium
    Consider: (I) \(\mathbb F_4\) is a subfield of \(\mathbb F_8\); (II) \(\mathbb F_9\) is a subfield of \(\mathbb F_{81}\); (III) the multiplicative group of every finite field is cyclic; (IV) \(\mathbb Z_4\) is a field. Which statements are true?

    Why

    I false (\(2\nmid3\)). II true (\(2\mid4\)). III true. IV false (\(2\cdot2=0\) in \(\mathbb Z_4\)).
  12. Question 12Medium
    Assertion (A): \(x^4+x^2+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\). Reason (R): \(x^4+x^2+1\) has no root in \(\mathbb Q\).

    Why

    R is true (the polynomial is positive, so no real root). A is false: \(x^4+x^2+1=(x^2+x+1)(x^2-x+1)\). For degree \(4\) the absence of roots does not imply irreducibility.
  13. Question 13Hard
    The degree of the splitting field of \(x^4-2\) over \(\mathbb Q\) is

    Why

    The roots are \(i^k\sqrt[4]2\). \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]2)\) has degree \(4\) (Eisenstein) and is real, so adjoining \(i\) doubles it: \([\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]2,i):\mathbb Q]=8\). The Galois group is \(D_4\) of order \(8\), not \(S_4\).
  14. Question 14Medium
    Match the field extension (List I) with its degree over \(\mathbb Q\) (List II). List I: (a) \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)\); (b) \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)\); (c) \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)\); (d) \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\). List II: (1) \(2\); (2) \(3\); (3) \(4\); (4) \(6\).

    Why

    \([\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\mathbb Q]=4\); \([\mathbb Q(\zeta_7):\mathbb Q]=\varphi(7)=6\); \([\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2):\mathbb Q]=3\); \([\mathbb Q(\sqrt2):\mathbb Q]=2\).
  15. Question 15Easy
    The minimal polynomial of \(1+\sqrt2\) over \(\mathbb Q\) is

    Why

    \((x-1)^2=2\) gives \(x^2-2x-1=0\), which has no rational root, so it is irreducible and monic: the minimal polynomial. (\(x^2-2\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\sqrt2\).)
