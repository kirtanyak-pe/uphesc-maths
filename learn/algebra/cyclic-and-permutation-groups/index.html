Why it matters for the exam

This is the most examined group topic. In 2025, three of the seven algebra questions (Q33, Q34, Q35) came from here, and 2026 Q72 asked for the number of generators of a cyclic group again. The questions are short computations: \(\varphi(n)\), the order of a permutation, the elements of \(A_n\). You must be fast and exact on them.

Core ideas

Cyclic groups

\(G\) is cyclic if \(G=\langle a\rangle=\{a^k\}\) for some \(a\) (a generator). Up to isomorphism the cyclic groups are \(\mathbb Z\) (infinite) and \(\mathbb Z_n\) (order \(n\)).

Subgroups of a cyclic group.

  • Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic.
  • If \(G=\langle a\rangle\) has order \(n\), then for each divisor \(d\) of \(n\) there is exactly one subgroup of order \(d\), namely \(\langle a^{n/d}\rangle\), and there are no others.
  • So the number of subgroups of \(\mathbb Z_n\) equals the number of divisors \(d(n)\). For \(n=p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_r^{e_r}\), \(d(n)=(e_1+1)\cdots(e_r+1)\).
  • The converse of Lagrange holds in cyclic groups.
  • Subgroups of \(\mathbb Z\) are exactly \(m\mathbb Z\), \(m\ge0\).
  • Quotients of cyclic groups are cyclic.

Generators and orders.

  • \(a^k\) generates \(\langle a\rangle\) (of order \(n\)) iff \(\gcd(k,n)=1\). The number of generators is \(\varphi(n)\), Euler’s totient.
  • \(o(a^k)=n/\gcd(n,k)\). So in \(\mathbb Z_n\), \(o(k)=n/\gcd(n,k)\).
  • For each \(d\mid n\), a cyclic group of order \(n\) has exactly \(\varphi(d)\) elements of order \(d\). Adding over all \(d\): \(\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)=n\).
  • The infinite cyclic group \(\mathbb Z\) has exactly 2 generators, \(\pm1\).

Euler’s totient.

  • \(\varphi(p^k)=p^k-p^{k-1}=p^{k-1}(p-1)\).
  • \(\varphi(mn)=\varphi(m)\varphi(n)\) if \(\gcd(m,n)=1\).
  • \(\varphi(n)=n\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac1p\right)\).
  • Examples: \(\varphi(8)=4\), \(\varphi(105)=\varphi(3)\varphi(5)\varphi(7)=2\cdot4\cdot6=48\), \(\varphi(360)=\varphi(8)\varphi(9)\varphi(5)=4\cdot6\cdot4=96\).

When is a group cyclic?

Group Cyclic?
Any group of prime order Yes
\(G\) of order \(n\) with an element of order \(n\) Yes (by definition)
\(\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n\) iff \(\gcd(m,n)=1\) (then it is \(\cong\mathbb Z_{mn}\))
Finite subgroup of \(F^\times\), \(F\) a field Always (used for finite fields)
\(U(n)\) iff \(n=1,2,4,p^k,2p^k\), \(p\) odd prime
Group whose only subgroups are trivial and itself Yes, of prime order (or trivial)
Abelian group of order \(pq\), \(p\ne q\) Yes
\(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2\), \(S_n\) (\(n\ge3\)), \(Q_8\) No

Also, \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_n)\cong U(n)\) has order \(\varphi(n)\) (covered again in the topic on homomorphisms).

Permutations and cycle notation

\(S_n\) is the group of bijections of \(\{1,\dots,n\}\), under composition. \(\lvert S_n\rvert=n!\).

Two-line notation. The top row lists \(1,2,\dots,n\), the bottom row lists the images. The array with top \(1\,2\,3\,4\) and bottom \(1\,3\,4\,2\) means \(1\to1,\ 2\to3,\ 3\to4,\ 4\to2\).

Cycle notation. Follow each point until it returns. The permutation above is \((2\,3\,4)\), with 1 fixed. A \(k\)-cycle \((a_1\,a_2\dots a_k)\) sends \(a_i\to a_{i+1}\) and \(a_k\to a_1\).

  • Every permutation is a product of disjoint cycles, uniquely up to order of the factors and cyclic rotation inside a cycle.
  • Disjoint cycles commute.
  • Composition here is right to left: \(\sigma\tau\) means apply \(\tau\) first. (The opposite convention changes the product but not the order or the sign, so exam answers do not depend on it.)
  • Inverse of \((a_1\dots a_k)\) is \((a_k\dots a_1)\).

Order of a permutation. \(o(\sigma)=\operatorname{lcm}\) of the lengths of its disjoint cycles. A \(k\)-cycle has order \(k\).

Cycle type and conjugacy. The cycle type is the multiset of cycle lengths (including fixed points as 1-cycles). Two permutations are conjugate in \(S_n\) iff they have the same cycle type. The number of permutations of cycle type with \(m_k\) cycles of length \(k\) is

\[\frac{n!}{\prod_k k^{m_k}\,m_k!}.\]

Conjugation formula. For \(\sigma\in S_n\) and any cycle, \(\sigma\,(a_1\,a_2\dots a_k)\,\sigma^{-1}=(\sigma(a_1)\,\sigma(a_2)\dots\sigma(a_k))\): conjugating relabels the points and keeps the cycle type. Example: for \(\sigma=(1\,2\,3\,4)\) and \(\tau=(1\,3\,5)\), \(\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=(\tau(1)\,\tau(2)\,\tau(3)\,\tau(4))=(3\,2\,5\,4)\). To find \(g\) with \(g\sigma g^{-1}=\sigma'\), write the cycles of \(\sigma\) above those of \(\sigma'\) and let \(g\) send each point to the one below it. The centraliser of a permutation with \(m_k\) cycles of length \(k\) has order \(\prod_k k^{m_k}m_k!\), which is why the class size is \(n!\) divided by that.

Classes in \(A_n\). A class of \(S_n\) either stays one class of \(A_n\) or splits into two classes of equal size. It splits exactly when the cycle lengths (fixed points counted as \(1\)-cycles) are all distinct and odd. In \(A_4\) the \(8\) three-cycles (type \(3,1\)) split into two classes of \(4\); in \(A_5\) the \(24\) five-cycles split into two classes of \(12\), while the \(20\) three-cycles (type \(3,1,1\)) and the \(15\) double transpositions do not split, giving class sizes \(1,12,12,15,20\).

Even and odd permutations

  • Every permutation is a product of transpositions. The number of transpositions in any such product has the same parity (well-defined).
  • A \(k\)-cycle is a product of \(k-1\) transpositions: \((a_1\dots a_k)=(a_1\,a_2)(a_2\,a_3)\cdots(a_{k-1}\,a_k)\) (right to left). So a \(k\)-cycle is even iff \(k\) is odd.
  • The sign map \(\operatorname{sgn}:S_n\to\{\pm1\}\) is a homomorphism. \(\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=(-1)^{n-c}\) where \(c\) is the number of cycles including fixed points.
  • \(A_n=\ker(\operatorname{sgn})\) is the alternating group: even permutations, order \(n!/2\) (for \(n\ge2\)), index 2 so normal.
  • \(S_n\) is generated by transpositions, also by \((1\,2)\) and \((1\,2\dots n)\). \(A_n\) (\(n\ge3\)) is generated by 3-cycles.
  • \(A_3=\{e,(1\,2\,3),(1\,3\,2)\}\cong\mathbb Z_3\). \(A_4\) has order 12: the identity, three double transpositions \((1\,2)(3\,4)\), \((1\,3)(2\,4)\), \((1\,4)(2\,3)\) (with \(e\) they form the normal Klein subgroup \(V_4\)) and eight 3-cycles. \(A_n\) is simple for \(n\ge5\) (and \(A_5\) is the smallest nonabelian simple group, order 60).
  • The centre of \(S_n\) is trivial for \(n\ge3\), and the centre of \(A_n\) is trivial for \(n\ge4\) (\(A_3\) is abelian).

Elements by order in small symmetric groups

Group Order \(1,2,3,4,5,6\): number of elements
\(S_3\) \(1,3,2,-,-,-\)
\(S_4\) \(1,9,8,6,-,-\)
\(A_4\) \(1,3,8,-,-,-\)
\(S_5\) \(1,25,20,30,24,20\)
\(A_5\) \(1,15,20,-,24,-\)

In \(S_5\): the 25 elements of order 2 are 10 transpositions and 15 double transpositions; 20 elements of order 3 (3-cycles); 30 of order 4 (4-cycles); 24 of order 5 (5-cycles); 20 of order 6 (a 3-cycle times a disjoint transposition).

Subgroups of \(S_4\)

\(S_4\) has \(30\) subgroups in all.

Order Number Description
\(1\) \(1\) trivial
\(2\) \(9\) \(6\) generated by a transposition, \(3\) by a double transposition
\(3\) \(4\) generated by a \(3\)-cycle
\(4\) \(7\) \(3\) cyclic (generated by a \(4\)-cycle), \(3\) non-normal Klein groups like \(\{e,(1\,2),(3\,4),(1\,2)(3\,4)\}\), and the normal Klein group \(V_4\)
\(6\) \(4\) copies of \(S_3\) (the stabiliser of a point)
\(8\) \(3\) Sylow \(2\)-subgroups, each \(\cong D_4\)
\(12\) \(1\) \(A_4\)
\(24\) \(1\) \(S_4\)

The normal subgroups are only \(\{e\}\), \(V_4\), \(A_4\), \(S_4\), and \(S_4/V_4\cong S_3\). There is no subgroup of order \(12\) other than \(A_4\), and \(A_4\) has no subgroup of order \(6\) although \(S_4\) has four. Trap: “subgroups of order \(4\)” is \(7\), not \(3\); the Klein groups outnumber the cyclic ones.

Maximal order of an element of \(S_n\)

The largest order is the largest lcm of a partition of \(n\).

\(n\) 3 4 5 6 7 8 9 10
Max order 3 4 6 6 12 15 20 30

For example \(S_8\): \(3+5=8\) gives \(\operatorname{lcm}(3,5)=15\), better than \(8\) or \(3+4+1\).

Cayley’s theorem

Statement. Every group \(G\) is isomorphic to a subgroup of \(\operatorname{Sym}(G)\). A finite group of order \(n\) embeds in \(S_n\).

Proof idea. For \(g\in G\) let \(\lambda_g:G\to G\), \(x\mapsto gx\). Each \(\lambda_g\) is a bijection (inverse \(\lambda_{g^{-1}}\)), \(\lambda_g\lambda_h=\lambda_{gh}\), so \(g\mapsto\lambda_g\) is a homomorphism \(G\to\operatorname{Sym}(G)\). It is injective since \(\lambda_g(e)=g\). Hence \(G\cong\{\lambda_g\}\).

Remarks examiners use:

  • The embedding \(G\to S_n\) for \(n=\lvert G\rvert\) is not the smallest possible. \(D_4\) (order 8) sits inside \(S_4\). \(S_3\) is already in \(S_3\), not \(S_6\). The smallest faithful degree of \(Q_8\) is 8.
  • The image of the regular representation consists of permutations with no fixed points (except \(e\)). Each non-identity element of order \(m\) becomes a product of \(n/m\) disjoint \(m\)-cycles. For example the regular representation of \(\mathbb Z_4\) sends \(1\) to a 4-cycle, and of \(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2\) gives the three double transpositions plus \(e\).
  • General version: if \(H\le G\) has index \(n\), then \(G\) acts on the \(n\) cosets \(G/H\), giving a homomorphism \(G\to S_n\) whose kernel is contained in \(H\). If \(G\) is simple, this is an embedding. If moreover \(G\) is nonabelian simple, the image lies in \(A_n\), so \(\lvert G\rvert\) divides \(n!/2\). This is the main tool for non-simplicity questions (see the notes on Sylow).
  • If \(p\) is the smallest prime dividing \(\lvert G\rvert\) and \(H\le G\) has index \(p\), then \(H\) is normal. In particular index 2 means normal.

Formulas and results

  • \(\varphi(p^k)=p^k-p^{k-1}\); \(\varphi(mn)=\varphi(m)\varphi(n)\) for coprime \(m,n\).
  • Generators of cyclic group of order \(n\): \(\varphi(n)\). Elements of order \(d\) in it: \(\varphi(d)\) for each \(d\mid n\).
  • \(o(k)\) in \(\mathbb Z_n\) is \(n/\gcd(n,k)\).
  • Cyclic group of order \(n\): one subgroup per divisor; total number of subgroups \(d(n)\).
  • \(\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n\) is cyclic iff \(\gcd(m,n)=1\).
  • Order of a permutation = lcm of disjoint cycle lengths. \(k\)-cycle is even iff \(k\) is odd.
  • \(\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=(-1)^{n-c}\). \(\lvert A_n\rvert=n!/2\).
  • Number of permutations with cycle type \((k^{m_k})\): \(n!/\prod k^{m_k}m_k!\).
  • Conjugation: \(\sigma(a_1\dots a_k)\sigma^{-1}=(\sigma(a_1)\dots\sigma(a_k))\). A class of \(S_n\) splits in \(A_n\) iff its cycle lengths are distinct and odd.
  • \(S_4\) has \(30\) subgroups: \(1,9,4,7,4,3,1,1\) of orders \(1,2,3,4,6,8,12,24\).
  • \(A_3=\{e,(1\,2\,3),(1\,3\,2)\}\). \(A_4\): 1 identity, 3 of order 2, 8 of order 3, no subgroup of order 6.
  • Cayley: \(G\hookrightarrow S_{\lvert G\rvert}\) via left multiplication. \(A_n\) simple for \(n\ge5\).
  • Max element order in \(S_5,S_6,S_7,S_8,S_9,S_{10}\): \(6,6,12,15,20,30\).

Worked examples

Example 1: Subgroups and generators in \(\mathbb Z_{60}\)

\(60=2^2\cdot3\cdot5\), so \(d(60)=3\cdot2\cdot2=12\) subgroups. The subgroup of order 15 is \(\langle4\rangle\) (since \(60/15=4\)). Its generators are \(4k\) with \(\gcd(k,15)=1\), so there are \(\varphi(15)=8\) generators. Elements of order 12 in \(\mathbb Z_{60}\): \(\varphi(12)=4\).

Example 2: Order and parity

Take \(\sigma=(1\,2\,3)(4\,5)(6\,7\,8\,9)\in S_9\).

  • Order: \(\operatorname{lcm}(3,2,4)=12\).
  • Parity: a 3-cycle is even, a 2-cycle is odd, a 4-cycle is odd. Even times odd times odd is even.
  • Check with \(\operatorname{sgn}=(-1)^{n-c}\): the cycles use \(3+2+4=9\) points, so there is no fixed point and \(c=3\). Then \((-1)^{9-3}=+1\), even. Both methods agree.

Example 3: Multiply two permutations

\(\sigma=(1\,2\,3\,4)\), \(\tau=(2\,4)\) in \(S_4\). Compute \(\sigma\tau\) (apply \(\tau\) first):

  • \(1\to1\to2\), \(2\to4\to1\), \(3\to3\to4\), \(4\to2\to3\).

So \(\sigma\tau=(1\,2)(3\,4)\), of order 2. Parity: \(\sigma\) odd, \(\tau\) odd, product even. Consistent.

Example 4: Counting elements of order 6 in \(S_5\)

An element of order 6 needs a lcm of 6 with total length at most 5: only cycle type \((3,2)\) (a 6-cycle needs 6 points). Number: \(\dfrac{5!}{3^1\cdot1!\cdot2^1\cdot1!}=\dfrac{120}{6}=20\). Check by summing all orders in \(S_5\): \(1+25+20+30+24+20=120\).

Example 5: \(A_4\) has no subgroup of order 6

Suppose \(H\le A_4\) with \(\lvert H\rvert=6\). Then \([A_4:H]=2\), so \(H\) is normal and \(A_4/H\) has order 2. For any \(g\in A_4\), \(g^2\in H\) (the quotient has exponent 2). If \(g\) is a 3-cycle then \(g=(g^2)^2\in H\) (since \(g^4=g\)). So all eight 3-cycles lie in \(H\), but \(8>6\). Contradiction.

Example 6: Cayley representation of \(\mathbb Z_3\)

Label elements \(0,1,2\). \(\lambda_1\) sends \(0\to1\to2\to0\), which is the 3-cycle \((0\,1\,2)\); \(\lambda_2=(0\,2\,1)\); \(\lambda_0=e\). So \(\mathbb Z_3\cong A_3\subset S_3\).

Example 7: Maximal order in \(S_7\)

Partitions of 7 and their lcm: \(7\to7\), \(6+1\to6\), \(5+2\to10\), \(4+3\to12\), \(4+2+1\to4\), \(3+2+2\to6\), \(3+3+1\to3\), \(5+1+1\to5\). The largest is 12, from cycle type \((4,3)\).

Common traps

  • Generators of \(\mathbb Z_n\). The answer is \(\varphi(n)\), not \(n-1\) and not the number of divisors. For prime \(n\) it is \(n-1\). For \(n=8\) it is \(4\) (the odd residues \(1,3,5,7\)), not \(7\) or \(2\).
  • Totient arithmetic. \(\varphi(105)=48\), not \(104\) or \(105-3-5-7\). Use the product over distinct primes.
  • Order of a permutation. Take the lcm of the cycle lengths, not the sum and not the product. \((1\,2)(3\,4)\) has order 2; \((1\,2)(3\,4\,5)\) has order 6.
  • Fixed points. Cycles of length 1 are not written but they matter when you count cycles for the sign.
  • Odd length means even. A 3-cycle is even, a transposition is odd, a 4-cycle is odd.
  • \(A_n\) is not generated by transpositions (they are odd). It is generated by 3-cycles.
  • \(A_4\) has no subgroup of order 6. Do not claim the converse of Lagrange here.
  • \(\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n\) is cyclic only for coprime \(m,n\). \(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_4\) has no element of order 8.
  • Cayley. The theorem gives an embedding into \(S_n\) with \(n=\lvert G\rvert\). It does not say this is the smallest symmetric group containing \(G\).
  • Reading the two-line notation. The permutation in 2025 Q35 maps \(2\to3,\ 3\to4,\ 4\to2\) (not \(1\to2\)). Read bottom row entries below each top entry.

Previous-year questions

2025 Q33

Question. The number of generators of a cyclic group of order 8 is: (A) 2 (B) 3 (C) 4 (D) 6.

Answer: C (official key; 4).

Solution. The generators of \(\mathbb Z_8\) are the residues coprime to 8, namely \(1,3,5,7\). Count \(\varphi(8)=8-4=4\).

2025 Q34

Question. The alternating group \(A_3\) on the three symbols \(1,2,3\) is: (A) \(\{I,(1\,2)\}\) (B) \(\{I,(1\,3)\}\) (C) \(\{I,(2\,3)\}\) (D) \(\{I,(1\,2\,3),(1\,3\,2)\}\).

Answer: D (official key).

Solution. \(A_3\) is the set of even permutations of three symbols: the identity and the two 3-cycles. Transpositions are odd, so options A, B, C are not subgroups of \(A_3\) (and each is the wrong order, since \(\lvert A_3\rvert=3!/2=3\)).

2025 Q35

Question. The order of the permutation with top row \(1\,2\,3\,4\) and bottom row \(1\,3\,4\,2\) is: (A) 2 (B) 3 (C) 4 (D) 1.

Answer: B (official key; 3).

Solution. The map is \(1\to1,\ 2\to3,\ 3\to4,\ 4\to2\), which is the 3-cycle \((2\,3\,4)\) with 1 fixed. Its order is 3.

2026 Q72

Question. The number of generators of a cyclic group of order 105 is: (A) 24 (B) 48 (C) 36 (D) 60.

Answer: B (worked key; 48).

Solution. \(105=3\cdot5\cdot7\), all distinct primes, so \(\varphi(105)=\varphi(3)\varphi(5)\varphi(7)=2\cdot4\cdot6=48\).

Quick revision

  • Cyclic group of order \(n\): \(\varphi(n)\) generators; one subgroup per divisor \(d\) of \(n\); \(\varphi(d)\) elements of order \(d\).
  • \(\varphi(p^k)=p^k-p^{k-1}\); \(\varphi(8)=4\); \(\varphi(105)=48\); \(\varphi(360)=96\).
  • \(o(k)\) in \(\mathbb Z_n\) is \(n/\gcd(n,k)\); \(\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n\) is cyclic iff \(\gcd(m,n)=1\).
  • Every subgroup and quotient of a cyclic group is cyclic.
  • Permutation order is the lcm of disjoint cycle lengths; disjoint cycles commute.
  • A \(k\)-cycle is even iff \(k\) is odd; \(A_n\) = even permutations, order \(n!/2\), generated by 3-cycles.
  • \(A_3=\{e,(1\,2\,3),(1\,3\,2)\}\); \(A_4\) has 8 elements of order 3, 3 of order 2, no subgroup of order 6.
  • Conjugate in \(S_n\) iff same cycle type; \(\sigma(a_1\dots a_k)\sigma^{-1}=(\sigma(a_1)\dots\sigma(a_k))\).
  • \(S_4\): \(30\) subgroups, \(7\) of order \(4\), \(4\) of order \(6\), \(3\) of order \(8\); normal ones are \(e,V_4,A_4,S_4\).
  • Cayley: \(G\hookrightarrow S_{\lvert G\rvert}\) by left multiplication (not the smallest embedding).
  • Max element order in \(S_n\) for \(n=5,6,7,8\): \(6,6,12,15\).
  • \(A_n\) is simple for \(n\ge5\); \(S_n\) is generated by transpositions.

2025 answers match the official key. 2026 has no key yet, so its answers were worked out by hand and marked high, medium or low confidence, and every unsure one was solved again, blind. 2019 answers come from the video and are not checked against the official key.
