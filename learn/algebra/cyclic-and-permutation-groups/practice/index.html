Cyclic and permutation groups

  1. Question 1Easy
    The number of generators of the cyclic group \(\mathbb Z_{30}\) is

    Why

    \(30=2\cdot3\cdot5\), so \(\varphi(30)=1\cdot2\cdot4=8\): the residues \(1,7,11,13,17,19,23,29\). The value \(29\) would be right only if \(30\) were prime.
  2. Question 2Easy
    The order of \((1\,2\,3)(4\,5)\in S_5\) is

    Why

    The cycles are disjoint, of lengths 3 and 2, so the order is \(\operatorname{lcm}(3,2)=6\) (not the sum 5).
  3. Question 3Easy
    The number of elements of order \(3\) in \(A_4\) is

    Why

    \(A_4\) has 12 elements: the identity, 3 double transpositions (order 2) and 8 three-cycles (order 3). There are \(\binom43\cdot2=8\) three-cycles.
  4. Question 4Medium
    The number of elements of order \(6\) in \(S_5\) is

    Why

    Order 6 forces cycle type \((3,2)\). Count: \(5!/(3\cdot2)=20\) (choose the 3 points: 10 ways, 2 three-cycles on them, the other two points form the transposition: \(10\cdot2=20\)).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The maximum possible order of an element of \(S_8\) is

    Why

    Compare partitions of 8: \(8\to8\), \(5+3\to15\), \(4+3+1\to12\), \(4+2+2\to4\), \(3+3+2\to6\). The best is \(\operatorname{lcm}(5,3)=15\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    The number of subgroups of the cyclic group \(\mathbb Z_{100}\) is

    Why

    One subgroup for each divisor of \(100=2^2\cdot5^2\): \((2+1)(2+1)=9\) divisors, hence 9 subgroups.
  7. Question 7Easy
    The number of elements of order \(8\) in \(\mathbb Z_{24}\) is

    Why

    A cyclic group of order 24 has \(\varphi(8)=4\) elements of order 8 (namely \(3,9,15,21\)).
  8. Question 8Medium
    Consider: (i) \(\mathbb Z_6\times\mathbb Z_5\) is cyclic; (ii) \(\mathbb Z_6\times\mathbb Z_4\) is cyclic; (iii) \(\mathbb Z_8\times\mathbb Z_9\) is cyclic; (iv) \(\mathbb Z_3\times\mathbb Z_3\) is cyclic. Which are true?

    Why

    \(\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n\) is cyclic iff \(\gcd(m,n)=1\). (i) \(\gcd(6,5)=1\) true; (ii) \(\gcd(6,4)=2\) false; (iii) \(\gcd(8,9)=1\) true; (iv) \(\gcd(3,3)=3\) false (every non-identity element has order 3, but the group has order 9).
  9. Question 9Easy
    Assertion (A): every group of order \(n\) is isomorphic to a subgroup of \(S_n\). Reason (R): the map \(g\mapsto\lambda_g\), \(\lambda_g(x)=gx\), is an injective homomorphism \(G\to\operatorname{Sym}(G)\). Choose: (A) both true and R explains A; (B) both true, R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

    Why

    This is Cayley’s theorem. R describes the left regular representation, and its injectivity is exactly what makes \(G\) isomorphic to a subgroup of \(\operatorname{Sym}(G)\cong S_n\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    Let \(\alpha=(1\,2\,3\,4)\) and \(\beta=(1\,2)(3\,4)\) in \(S_4\). The order of \(\alpha\beta\) is

    Why

    Compose right to left: \(1\to2\to3\), \(3\to4\to1\), \(2\to1\to2\), \(4\to3\to4\). So \(\alpha\beta=(1\,3)\), a transposition, of order 2. (Composing the other way gives \((2\,4)\), also of order 2.) The lcm rule does not apply because \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are not disjoint cycles.
  11. Question 11Hard
    The number of elements of order \(6\) in \(S_6\) is

    Why

    Cycle types with lcm 6: \((6)\): \(5!=120\) elements; \((3,2,1)\): \(6!/(3\cdot2\cdot1)=120\) elements. (\((3,3)\) has order 3 and \((2,2,2)\) has order 2.) Total \(120+120=240\).
  12. Question 12Hard
    The smallest \(n\) such that the quaternion group \(Q_8\) is isomorphic to a subgroup of \(S_n\) is

    Why

    \(Q_8\) has a unique involution \(-1\). In a faithful action on \(\{1,\dots,n\}\), some orbit must be moved nontrivially by \(-1\); the stabiliser of a point in that orbit cannot contain \(-1\), hence is trivial (every nontrivial subgroup of \(Q_8\) contains \(-1\)), so that orbit has 8 points and \(n\ge8\). Cayley gives \(n=8\). (Computer check: \(S_7\) contains no copy of \(Q_8\).) The tempting answer 4 belongs to \(D_4\), not \(Q_8\).
