Formula sheet

Every formula and result of the unit. Each topic page explains where they come from.

Groups: general

  • Lagrange: \(\lvert G\rvert=[G:H]\,\lvert H\rvert\) for finite \(G\). So \(o(a)\mid\lvert G\rvert\) and \(a^{\lvert G\rvert}=e\).
  • Converse of Lagrange is false: \(A_4\) has order 12 and no subgroup of order 6.
  • \(\lvert HK\rvert=\dfrac{\lvert H\rvert\lvert K\rvert}{\lvert H\cap K\rvert}\). A subgroup of index 2 is normal.
  • \(o(a^k)=\dfrac{o(a)}{\gcd(o(a),k)}\). For commuting \(a,b\) with \(\gcd(o(a),o(b))=1\), \(o(ab)=o(a)\,o(b)\).
  • In any group, \(o(ab)=o(ba)\). Elements with \(g\ne g^{-1}\) pair off with their inverses, so in a group of even order the number of elements with \(g^2=e\) is even. Since \(e\) is one of them, there is an element of order 2.
  • A group in which every element has order at most 2 is abelian.
  • If \(G/Z(G)\) is cyclic then \(G\) is abelian. A group of order \(p^2\) is abelian.
  • Order \(pq\) with \(p<q\) primes: if \(p\nmid q-1\) the group is cyclic. If \(p\mid q-1\) there is also a non-abelian group.

Cyclic groups

  • A cyclic group of order \(n\) has exactly one subgroup for each divisor \(d\) of \(n\), of order \(d\), and \(\varphi(d)\) elements of order \(d\).
  • Number of generators of \(\mathbb{Z}_n\) is \(\varphi(n)\). The generators are the \(k\) with \(\gcd(k,n)=1\).
  • \(\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)=n\).
  • \(\varphi(p^k)=p^k-p^{k-1}\) and \(\varphi\) is multiplicative on coprime arguments. Example: \(\varphi(105)=2\cdot4\cdot6=48\).
  • \(\mathbb{Z}_m\times\mathbb{Z}_n\) is cyclic iff \(\gcd(m,n)=1\).
  • \(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_n)\cong U(n)\), of order \(\varphi(n)\).
  • \(\lvert\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_m,\mathbb{Z}_n)\rvert=\gcd(m,n)\).

Permutation groups

  • \(\lvert S_n\rvert=n!\) and \(\lvert A_n\rvert=n!/2\). \(A_n\) is generated by 3-cycles. \(A_n\) is simple for \(n\ge5\).
  • Order of a permutation: the lcm of its disjoint cycle lengths.
  • A \(k\)-cycle has sign \((-1)^{k-1}\). In general \(\mathrm{sgn}=(-1)^{\,n-c}\) where \(c\) is the number of cycles counting fixed points.
  • Conjugacy classes of \(S_n\) correspond to cycle types, so there are \(p(n)\) of them. Class size for cycle type with \(m_k\) cycles of length \(k\) is \(\dfrac{n!}{\prod_k k^{m_k}\,m_k!}\).
  • Cayley’s theorem: every group of order \(n\) embeds in \(S_n\) (via left multiplication).
  • Maximal element order in \(S_n\) for \(n=3,\dots,10\): \(3,4,6,6,12,15,20,30\). In \(S_7\) it is \(12\).
  • \(Z(S_n)\) is trivial for \(n\ge3\). \(Z(A_n)\) is trivial for \(n\ge4\).

Homomorphisms and quotients

  • First isomorphism theorem: \(G/\ker\varphi\cong\varphi(G)\).
  • Second: \(HN/N\cong H/(H\cap N)\) for \(N\trianglelefteq G\). Third: \((G/N)/(K/N)\cong G/K\) for \(N\subseteq K\) both normal in \(G\).
  • Correspondence: subgroups of \(G/N\) correspond to subgroups of \(G\) containing \(N\), and normality is preserved.
  • \(o(\varphi(g))\) divides \(o(g)\). For \(\varphi:G\to H\) the image has order dividing both \(\lvert G\rvert\) and \(\lvert H\rvert\).
  • \(\mathrm{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G)\). \(G/G'\) is the largest abelian quotient. \(S_n/A_n\cong\mathbb{Z}_2\).
  • Number of abelian groups of order \(n=\prod p_i^{e_i}\) is \(\prod p(e_i)\). For \(n=36\) it is \(2\cdot2=4\). For \(n=72\) and \(n=360\) it is \(3\cdot2=6\).
  • For coprime \(m,n\): \(U(mn)\cong U(m)\times U(n)\) and \(\mathbb{Z}_m\times\mathbb{Z}_n\cong\mathbb{Z}_{mn}\). The second fails when \(\gcd(m,n)>1\).

Group actions and Sylow

  • Orbit-stabiliser: \(\lvert\mathrm{Orb}(x)\rvert=[G:\mathrm{Stab}(x)]\).
  • Burnside: number of orbits \(=\dfrac1{\lvert G\rvert}\sum_{g}\lvert\mathrm{Fix}(g)\rvert\).
  • Class equation: \(\lvert G\rvert=\lvert Z(G)\rvert+\sum[G:C_G(x_i)]\), sum over class representatives outside the centre.
  • Every p-group has a nontrivial centre. Every group of order \(p^n\) has a subgroup of order \(p^k\) for each \(k\le n\).
  • Non-abelian group of order \(p^3\): centre of order \(p\) and \(p^2+p-1\) conjugacy classes.
  • Cauchy: if a prime \(p\) divides \(\lvert G\rvert\) then \(G\) has an element of order \(p\).
  • Sylow: write \(\lvert G\rvert=p^k m\) with \(p\nmid m\). Then Sylow p-subgroups exist, they are all conjugate, and the count satisfies \(n_p\equiv1\pmod p\), \(n_p\mid m\).
  • \(n_p=1\) iff the Sylow p-subgroup is normal. \(n_p=[G:N_G(P)]\).
  • A group of order \(pq\), \(p^2q\), \(pqr\), or a group of order below 60 is never a non-abelian simple group.
  • Known counts: \(S_4\) has \(n_2=3\) and \(n_3=4\). \(A_5\) has \(n_2=5\), \(n_3=10\), \(n_5=6\). \(S_5\) has \(n_2=15\), \(n_3=10\), \(n_5=6\).

Rings and ideals

  • An ideal test: \(I\) is an ideal iff it is nonempty, closed under subtraction, and \(rI\subseteq I\) for all \(r\in R\).
  • Ideals of \(\mathbb{Z}\) are \((n)\). \((m)\subseteq(n)\) iff \(n\mid m\). \((m)+(n)=(\gcd)\) and \((m)\cap(n)=(\mathrm{lcm})\).
  • \(R/M\) is a field iff \(M\) is maximal. \(R/P\) is an integral domain iff \(P\) is prime. Maximal implies prime in a commutative ring with 1.
  • In \(\mathbb{Z}\): \((n)\) is prime iff \(n\) is prime or \(0\), and \((n)\) is maximal iff \(n\) is prime. In \(\mathbb{Z}[x]\): \((x)\) is prime not maximal, \((p,x)\) is maximal, \((2,x)\) is not principal.
  • Characteristic of an integral domain is \(0\) or a prime. A finite integral domain is a field. A field has only the trivial ideals.
  • Ring homomorphism: kernel is an ideal. \(R/\ker\varphi\cong\varphi(R)\). A (unital) ring homomorphism from a field to a nonzero ring is injective (without the unital condition the zero map is the exception).
  • \(\mathbb{Z}_n\) is a field iff \(n\) is prime. Units of \(\mathbb{Z}_n\) are the \(k\) with \(\gcd(k,n)=1\). Every nonzero non-unit in \(\mathbb{Z}_n\) is a zero divisor.

Domains and polynomial rings

  • Chain: Euclidean domain \(\Rightarrow\) PID \(\Rightarrow\) UFD \(\Rightarrow\) integral domain. None of the arrows reverses.
  • Examples: \(\mathbb{Z}\), \(F[x]\), \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\) are Euclidean. \(\mathbb{Z}[x]\) and \(F[x,y]\) are UFDs but not PIDs. \(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]\) is not a UFD, because \(6=2\cdot3=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})\) with all four factors irreducible and non-associate.
  • In a PID, irreducible \(\Leftrightarrow\) prime \(\Leftrightarrow\) generates a maximal ideal (nonzero). In a UFD, irreducible \(\Rightarrow\) prime.
  • Gauss’s lemma: a primitive polynomial irreducible in \(\mathbb{Z}[x]\) stays irreducible in \(\mathbb{Q}[x]\). If \(R\) is a UFD then \(R[x]\) is a UFD.
  • Eisenstein: if prime \(p\) divides every coefficient except the leading one, \(p\nmid a_n\), and \(p^2\nmid a_0\), then the polynomial is irreducible over \(\mathbb{Q}\).
  • Rational root test: a rational root \(r/s\) in lowest terms has \(r\mid a_0\) and \(s\mid a_n\).
  • A polynomial of degree 2 or 3 over a field is irreducible iff it has no root there.
  • \(F[x]/(f)\) is a field iff \(f\) is irreducible. \(\Phi_p(x)=x^{p-1}+\dots+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb{Q}\).
  • \(\lvert\mathbb{Z}[i]/(a+bi)\rvert=a^2+b^2\). Gaussian primes: \(p\equiv3\pmod4\) stays prime, \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) splits, and \(2=-i(1+i)^2\).
  • \(\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^2+1)\cong\mathbb{Z}[i]\). \(\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2+1)\cong\mathbb{C}\).

Fields and extensions

  • Tower law: \([L:F]=[L:K][K:F]\). If \(\alpha\) is algebraic over \(F\) with minimal polynomial of degree \(d\) then \([F(\alpha):F]=d\).
  • Finite extension \(\Rightarrow\) algebraic. \([K:F]\) finite iff \(K\) is generated by finitely many algebraic elements.
  • \(\alpha=\sqrt2+\sqrt3\) has minimal polynomial \(x^4-10x^2+1\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\), and \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2+\sqrt3)\) has degree \(4\).
  • \([\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2},\omega):\mathbb{Q}]=6\), and this is the splitting field of \(x^3-2\). \([\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n):\mathbb{Q}]=\varphi(n)\).
  • The splitting field of a degree \(n\) polynomial has degree dividing \(n!\) over the base field.
  • Splitting field of \(x^4+x^2+1=(x^2+x+1)(x^2-x+1)\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\) is \(\mathbb{Q}(\omega)\), of degree \(2\).
  • Constructible lengths lie in towers of degree a power of 2. Doubling the cube, trisecting a general angle and squaring the circle are impossible.
  • Finite fields: there is a field of order \(q\) iff \(q=p^n\), and it is unique up to isomorphism, written \(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}\).
  • \(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}^{\times}\) is cyclic of order \(p^n-1\). The number of solutions of \(x^k=1\) in it is \(\gcd(k,p^n-1)\). It has \(\varphi(p^n-1)\) generators.
  • \(\mathbb{F}_{p^m}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^n}\) iff \(m\mid n\). So \(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}\) has one subfield for each divisor of \(n\).
  • \(x^{p^n}-x\) is the product of all monic irreducibles over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) of degree dividing \(n\).
  • Number of monic irreducible polynomials of degree \(n\) over \(\mathbb{F}_q\) is \(\dfrac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\,q^{n/d}\). Over \(\mathbb{F}_2\) for \(n=1,\dots,6\): \(2,1,2,3,6,9\). Over \(\mathbb{F}_3\) for \(n=1,\dots,4\): \(3,3,8,18\).
  • Frobenius \(x\mapsto x^p\) is an automorphism of a finite field of characteristic \(p\). The Galois group of \(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p\) is cyclic of order \(n\).
  • \(\mathbb{Z}_5[x]/(x^2+2)\) is a field of order \(25\), since \(-2\equiv3\) is not a square mod 5.
  • \((a+b)^p=a^p+b^p\) in characteristic \(p\).

Modules

  • A \(\mathbb{Z}\)-module is the same as an abelian group. An \(F[x]\)-module is a vector space \(V\) together with a linear operator \(T\). Submodules are \(T\)-invariant subspaces.
  • Submodule test: nonempty, closed under addition, closed under scalar multiplication. Quotient \(M/N\) is a module. Isomorphism theorems hold as for groups.
  • Structure theorem over a PID: a finitely generated module is \(R^r\oplus R/(d_1)\oplus\dots\oplus R/(d_k)\) with \(d_1\mid d_2\mid\dots\mid d_k\). Finitely generated torsion-free over a PID is free.
  • Noetherian module: ascending chain condition on submodules, equivalently every submodule is finitely generated.
  • For \(0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0\): \(M\) is Noetherian iff \(M'\) and \(M''\) both are. So finite direct sums and quotients of Noetherian modules are Noetherian.
  • A finitely generated module over a Noetherian ring is Noetherian. A PID is Noetherian. A field and \(\mathbb{Z}\) are Noetherian.
  • Hilbert basis theorem: if \(R\) is Noetherian then \(R[x]\) is Noetherian. So \(F[x_1,\dots,x_n]\) and \(\mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]\) are Noetherian.
  • Not Noetherian: \(F[x_1,x_2,\dots]\) in infinitely many variables, and \(\mathbb{Q}\) as a \(\mathbb{Z}\)-module (the chain \(\mathbb{Z}\subset\frac12\mathbb{Z}\subset\frac14\mathbb{Z}\subset\cdots\) does not stop).
  • \(\mathbb{Z}_n\) as a \(\mathbb{Z}\)-module is not free, since every element is torsion.
