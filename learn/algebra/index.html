What this unit covers

Unit 1 carries 6 to 7 of the 70 maths questions in the two recent papers. It has three blocks.

  • Group theory (topics 1 to 4). Cyclic and permutation groups, Cayley’s theorem, the fundamental theorem of homomorphism and the other isomorphism theorems, group actions, the class equation, and the Sylow theorems with their applications. Lagrange’s theorem and basic subgroup facts are assumed throughout, so topic 1 gives that groundwork first.
  • Ring theory (topics 5 and 6). Rings, ideals, prime and maximal ideals, quotient rings, Euclidean domains, principal ideal domains, unique factorisation domains, and polynomial rings.
  • Fields and modules (topics 7 and 8). Fields, finite fields, field extensions, then modules, Noetherian modules and the Hilbert basis theorem. The module part is marked as extra in the longer (Version A) syllabus sheet.

Everything in the official syllabus line for this unit is covered in these eight topics. Nothing is left to an outside book.

What this tells you:

  • Cyclic groups and generators are a near-certain question. The number of generators of a cyclic group (2025 Q33, 2026 Q72) was asked in both recent papers. The answer is \(\varphi(n)\).
  • Group theory is about 60 percent of the unit. Of the 13 algebra questions in 2025 and 2026, 8 are group theory and 5 are rings or fields.
  • Questions are short and direct. Most are a single computation or a one-line theorem check (order of a permutation, size of a centre, is a quotient ring a field). Few need more than two steps.
  • Finite fields are a favourite in the ring and field half. The 2026 paper asked about \(\mathbb{Z}_5[x]/(x^2+2)\) and the roots of \(x^n=1\) in the field of order 27. Learn the structure of finite fields well.
  • Honest gap: topic 8 has not appeared. In the three papers seen, no question has been asked directly on modules, Noetherian modules, the Hilbert basis theorem or the Sylow theorems as such. Sylow ideas reach you only through the class equation and p-group questions. These topics are in the syllabus, so a question can still come, but spend your time in proportion: they are worth a short, careful pass, not a long one.
  • The 2019 data covers only half the paper, so its three questions undercount.

High-yield list

Groups (answer these first)

  1. Number of generators of a cyclic group of order \(n\) is \(\varphi(n)\). Asked in 2025 and 2026.
  2. Order of a permutation is the lcm of its cycle lengths. Parity from the cycle count.
  3. \(A_n\) has \(n!/2\) elements. \(A_3\) is cyclic of order 3. \(A_4\) has no subgroup of order 6.
  4. A group of prime order is cyclic, hence simple. Lagrange gives the possible subgroup orders.
  5. Isomorphism theorems: \(HN/N\cong H/(H\cap N)\) and \(G/\ker\varphi\cong\mathrm{im}\,\varphi\).
  6. \(G/Z(G)\) cyclic implies \(G\) abelian. A conjugation action with all classes of size 1 means the group is abelian.
  7. Class equation, and the centre of a p-group is nontrivial. Non-abelian order \(p^3\): centre of order \(p\), \(p^2+p-1\) classes.
  8. Sylow counts: \(n_p\equiv1\pmod p\) and \(n_p\mid m\). Use them to find the normal Sylow subgroup in orders like \(15\), \(20\), \(21\), \(30\).
  9. Cayley: a group of order \(n\) embeds in \(S_n\).
  10. Counting abelian groups of order \(n\): multiply the partition numbers of the exponents.

Rings and domains

  1. \(R/M\) is a field iff \(M\) is maximal. Prime ideals give integral domains. Maximal implies prime.
  2. Kernel of a ring homomorphism is an ideal. Identify it carefully before quoting an answer (2026 Q47).
  3. ED \(\Rightarrow\) PID \(\Rightarrow\) UFD, and the counterexamples: \(\mathbb{Z}[x]\) (UFD not PID), \(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]\) (not UFD).
  4. \(F[x]/(f)\) is a field iff \(f\) is irreducible. Check degree 2 and 3 by looking for roots.
  5. Eisenstein and Gauss’s lemma. \(R\) UFD implies \(R[x]\) UFD.

Fields

  1. Tower law and degree of a simple extension. Degrees such as \([\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\mathbb{Q}]=4\) appear again and again.
  2. Splitting field degree: compute from the factorisation (2025 Q36).
  3. Finite field facts: order \(p^n\), cyclic multiplicative group, subfield iff \(m\mid n\), solutions of \(x^k=1\) is \(\gcd(k,q-1)\) (2026 Q52).
  4. Frobenius, characteristic \(p\) identity \((a+b)^p=a^p+b^p\).

Modules (short pass)

  1. \(\mathbb{Z}\)-modules are abelian groups. Noetherian means ACC or every submodule finitely generated.
  2. Hilbert basis theorem: \(R\) Noetherian implies \(R[x]\) Noetherian. Extensions of Noetherian modules are Noetherian.
  3. Structure theorem over a PID gives the classification of finite abelian groups.
