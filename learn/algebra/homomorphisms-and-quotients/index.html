Why it matters for the exam

The Fundamental Theorem of homomorphism (first isomorphism theorem) is named in the syllabus, and 2025 Q32 tested the second isomorphism theorem directly. Counting homomorphisms between cyclic groups, finding the order of an element in a quotient, and classifying abelian groups of a given order are standard computational questions. The ring versions of these theorems (topic 5) use the same ideas.

Core ideas

Normal subgroups

\(N\le G\) is normal (\(N\trianglelefteq G\)) if \(gNg^{-1}=N\) for all \(g\in G\). Equivalent conditions:

  • \(gN=Ng\) for all \(g\) (left cosets equal right cosets).
  • \(gng^{-1}\in N\) for all \(g\in G,n\in N\).
  • \(N\) is the kernel of some homomorphism.
  • \(N\) is a union of conjugacy classes of \(G\).

Examples and facts:

  • \(\{e\}\), \(G\), \(Z(G)\), the commutator subgroup \(G'=[G,G]\) are normal. Every subgroup of an abelian group is normal.
  • A subgroup of index 2 is normal. More generally, if \(p\) is the smallest prime dividing \(\lvert G\rvert\), a subgroup of index \(p\) is normal.
  • \(A_n\trianglelefteq S_n\); \(SL_n(F)\trianglelefteq GL_n(F)\); the Klein group \(V_4\trianglelefteq A_4\) and \(V_4\trianglelefteq S_4\).
  • Intersections of normal subgroups are normal. If \(N\trianglelefteq G\) and \(H\le G\) then \(HN\) is a subgroup; if both are normal, \(HN\) is normal.
  • Normality is not transitive. In \(A_4\), \(V_4\) is normal, and \(K=\{e,(1\,2)(3\,4)\}\) is normal in \(V_4\), but \(K\) is not normal in \(A_4\).
  • Not every subgroup is normal. \(\langle(1\,2)\rangle\) is not normal in \(S_3\). In \(Q_8\) every subgroup is normal although \(Q_8\) is nonabelian.
  • A group is simple if its only normal subgroups are \(\{e\}\) and \(G\). Abelian simple groups are exactly \(\mathbb Z_p\). \(A_n\) (\(n\ge5\)) is simple; the smallest nonabelian simple group is \(A_5\) (order 60).

Quotient groups

For \(N\trianglelefteq G\), the set \(G/N\) of cosets is a group under \((aN)(bN)=abN\), with identity \(N\) and inverse \((aN)^{-1}=a^{-1}N\). \(\lvert G/N\rvert=[G:N]\).

  • \(G/N\) is abelian if \(G\) is abelian (converse false: \(Q_8/Z(Q_8)\cong V_4\)).
  • \(G/N\) is cyclic if \(G\) is cyclic.
  • The order of \(aN\) in \(G/N\) divides \(o(a)\). It is the least \(k\) with \(a^k\in N\).
  • \(G/G'\) is abelian and is the largest abelian quotient of \(G\). If \(N\trianglelefteq G\) and \(G/N\) is abelian then \(G'\le N\).
  • \(G/Z(G)\) cyclic implies \(G\) abelian. Proof: if \(G/Z=\langle gZ\rangle\) then every element is \(g^kz\) with \(z\in Z\). Two such elements \(g^kz\) and \(g^mw\) commute since \(z,w\) are central.
  • Consequently a nonabelian group has \(G/Z(G)\) noncyclic; a group of order \(p^2\) is abelian (since \(\lvert Z\rvert>1\) and \(G/Z\) then has order 1 or \(p\)).

Homomorphisms

A map \(f:G\to H\) with \(f(ab)=f(a)f(b)\).

  • \(f(e)=e\), \(f(a^{-1})=f(a)^{-1}\), and \(o(f(a))\) divides \(o(a)\).
  • The image \(f(G)\le H\) and the kernel \(\ker f=\{g:f(g)=e\}\) is a normal subgroup of \(G\). Preimages of subgroups are subgroups, images of normal subgroups are normal in the image.
  • \(f\) is injective iff \(\ker f=\{e\}\). An isomorphism is a bijective homomorphism.
  • Isomorphism invariants (a quick way to show two groups differ): order, being abelian, being cyclic, number of elements of each order, size of the centre, number of subgroups. Examples: \(\mathbb Z_4\not\cong\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2\) (an element of order 4); \(S_3\not\cong\mathbb Z_6\) (abelian); \(D_4\not\cong Q_8\) (5 versus 1 elements of order 2); \(\mathbb Z\not\cong\mathbb Q\) (\(\mathbb Q\) is not cyclic).

The isomorphism theorems

First isomorphism theorem (Fundamental theorem of homomorphism). If \(f:G\to H\) is a homomorphism, then \(G/\ker f\cong f(G)\), via \(g\ker f\mapsto f(g)\). In particular \(\lvert G\rvert=\lvert\ker f\rvert\cdot\lvert f(G)\rvert\).

A stronger form: if \(N\trianglelefteq G\) and \(N\subseteq\ker f\), then \(f\) factors through \(G/N\), i.e. there is a unique hom \(\bar f:G/N\to H\) with \(\bar f(gN)=f(g)\).

Use it to identify quotients: find a surjective homomorphism and compute its kernel. Examples: \(\det:GL_n(\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R^\times\) gives \(GL_n/SL_n\cong\mathbb R^\times\); \(t\mapsto e^{2\pi it}\) gives \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\cong S^1\); \(z\mapsto z/\lvert z\rvert\) gives \(\mathbb C^\times/\mathbb R_{>0}\cong S^1\); \(\operatorname{sgn}:S_n\to\{\pm1\}\) gives \(S_n/A_n\cong\mathbb Z_2\).

Second isomorphism theorem. Let \(H\le G\) and \(N\trianglelefteq G\). Then \(HN\le G\), \(H\cap N\trianglelefteq H\), \(N\trianglelefteq HN\) and

\[HN/N\cong H/(H\cap N).\]

(This is 2025 Q32. The wrong options are \(N/(H\cap N)\), \(G/(H\cap N)\) and \(G/N\).)

Third isomorphism theorem. If \(N\subseteq K\) are both normal in \(G\) then \(K/N\trianglelefteq G/N\) and

\[(G/N)/(K/N)\cong G/K.\]

Correspondence theorem. For \(N\trianglelefteq G\), the map \(H\mapsto H/N\) is a bijection between subgroups of \(G\) containing \(N\) and subgroups of \(G/N\). It preserves inclusion, index, and normality. So the subgroups of \(\mathbb Z_n/\langle d\rangle\) (with \(d\mid n\)) correspond to the subgroups \(\langle e\rangle\) of \(\mathbb Z_n\) with \(e\mid d\), i.e. to the divisors of \(d\). This matches \(\mathbb Z_n/\langle d\rangle\cong\mathbb Z_d\). For example, \(\mathbb Z_{12}/\langle4\rangle\cong\mathbb Z_4\) has 3 subgroups (orders 1, 2, 4), one for each divisor of 4.

Counting homomorphisms

A homomorphism out of a cyclic group is determined by the image of a generator.

  • \(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z_m,\mathbb Z_n)\): choose \(x=f(1)\) with \(mx\equiv0\pmod n\). The solutions are the multiples of \(n/\gcd(m,n)\), so there are \(\gcd(m,n)\) homomorphisms.
  • Injective \(\mathbb Z_m\to\mathbb Z_n\): exists iff \(m\mid n\), and then there are \(\varphi(m)\) of them (the image of \(1\) must have order \(m\)).
  • Surjective \(\mathbb Z_m\to\mathbb Z_n\): exists iff \(n\mid m\), and then there are \(\varphi(n)\) of them.
  • Isomorphisms \(\mathbb Z_n\to\mathbb Z_n\) (automorphisms): \(\varphi(n)\).
  • \(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z,G)\leftrightarrow G\): \(\lvert G\rvert\) homomorphisms for finite \(G\).
  • \(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n,\mathbb Z_k)\) has \(\gcd(m,k)\gcd(n,k)\) elements.
  • A hom from \(G\) to an abelian group factors through \(G/G'\). So homs from \(S_n\) (\(n\ge2\)) to an abelian group \(A\) correspond to homs \(\mathbb Z_2\to A\), i.e. to elements \(a\in A\) with \(2a=0\). Examples: \(S_3\to\mathbb Z_6\) has 2 homs, \(S_3\to\mathbb Z_3\) has only the trivial one.
  • Number of homs \(S_3\to S_3\) is 10: the trivial one, 3 with kernel \(A_3\) (the image is one of 3 transposition subgroups), and 6 automorphisms.

Automorphisms

\(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) is the group of isomorphisms \(G\to G\) under composition. Inner automorphisms \(c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}\) form \(\operatorname{Inn}(G)\trianglelefteq\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), and

\[\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G).\]

(First isomorphism theorem for \(g\mapsto c_g\), whose kernel is \(Z(G)\).) So \(\operatorname{Inn}(G)\) is trivial iff \(G\) is abelian, \(\lvert\operatorname{Inn}(S_3)\rvert=6\), \(\lvert\operatorname{Inn}(D_4)\rvert=\lvert\operatorname{Inn}(Q_8)\rvert=4\). If \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) is cyclic then \(G\) is abelian (cyclic \(\operatorname{Inn}(G)\) means \(G/Z\) cyclic).

Direct products

For groups \(G,H\), the product \(G\times H\) has componentwise operation.

  • \(\lvert G\times H\rvert=\lvert G\rvert\lvert H\rvert\). \(o(g,h)=\operatorname{lcm}(o(g),o(h))\).
  • \(G\times H\) is abelian iff both are. \(Z(G\times H)=Z(G)\times Z(H)\).
  • \(\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n\cong\mathbb Z_{mn}\) iff \(\gcd(m,n)=1\).
  • Internal direct product: \(G=HK\) with \(H,K\trianglelefteq G\) and \(H\cap K=\{e\}\). Then \(G\cong H\times K\) and elements of \(H\) commute with elements of \(K\).
  • Elements with \(g^k=e\) in \(\mathbb Z_{n_1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb Z_{n_r}\): \(\prod_i\gcd(n_i,k)\).

Finite abelian groups

Fundamental theorem. Every finite abelian group is a direct product of cyclic groups of prime-power order, and the multiset of those prime powers (the elementary divisors) is unique. Equivalently it is \(\mathbb Z_{d_1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb Z_{d_r}\) with \(d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_r\) (invariant factors), unique.

Counting. For \(\lvert G\rvert=\prod p_i^{e_i}\) the number of abelian groups of order \(n\) is \(\prod_i P(e_i)\) where \(P(e)\) is the number of partitions of \(e\): \(P(1..7)=1,2,3,5,7,11,15\).

\(p^3\) 3

The four abelian groups of order 36: \(\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_9\cong\mathbb Z_{36}\), \(\mathbb Z_2^2\times\mathbb Z_9\cong\mathbb Z_{2}\times\mathbb Z_{18}\), \(\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_3^2\cong\mathbb Z_3\times\mathbb Z_{12}\), \(\mathbb Z_2^2\times\mathbb Z_3^2\cong\mathbb Z_6\times\mathbb Z_6\).

A finite abelian group is cyclic iff each of its Sylow parts is cyclic, iff it has at most one subgroup of order \(p\) for every prime \(p\). There is exactly one abelian group of order \(n\) iff \(n\) is squarefree.

Commutator subgroup

\(G'\) is generated by \(aba^{-1}b^{-1}\). \(S_n'=A_n\), \(A_n'=A_n\) for \(n\ge5\), \(D_n'=\langle r^2\rangle\), \(Q_8'=\{\pm1\}\), \(A_4'=V_4\). \(G/G'\) is the abelianisation.

Formulas and results

  • \(N\trianglelefteq G\): \(gNg^{-1}=N\); index 2 implies normal; kernels are normal.
  • \(\lvert G/N\rvert=\lvert G\rvert/\lvert N\rvert\); \(o(aN)\) is the least \(k\) with \(a^k\in N\).
  • First: \(G/\ker f\cong\operatorname{im}f\). Second: \(HN/N\cong H/(H\cap N)\). Third: \((G/N)/(K/N)\cong G/K\).
  • \(G/Z(G)\) cyclic \(\Rightarrow\) \(G\) abelian. \(\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G)\).
  • \(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z_m,\mathbb Z_n)\rvert=\gcd(m,n)\). Injective ones: \(\varphi(m)\) if \(m\mid n\).
  • \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_n)\cong U(n)\), \(\lvert\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_n)\rvert=\varphi(n)\). \(\lvert\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_p\times\mathbb Z_p)\rvert=(p^2-1)(p^2-p)\), whereas \(\lvert\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_{p^2})\rvert=p^2-p\).
  • \(\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n\) cyclic iff \(\gcd(m,n)=1\). \(o(g,h)=\operatorname{lcm}\).
  • Number of abelian groups of order \(\prod p_i^{e_i}\) is \(\prod P(e_i)\), with \(P(1),\dots,P(5)=1,2,3,5,7\).
  • \(G/G'\) is the largest abelian quotient.

Worked examples

Example 1: A quotient of \(\mathbb Z_{12}\)

\(\langle4\rangle=\{0,4,8\}\) has order 3, so \(\mathbb Z_{12}/\langle4\rangle\) has order 4. The coset \(1+\langle4\rangle\) has order 4 (the least \(k\) with \(k\in\langle4\rangle\) is 4). So the quotient is cyclic: \(\mathbb Z_4\), not \(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2\).

Example 2: Kernel and image of a homomorphism

Let \(f:\mathbb Z_{24}\to\mathbb Z_{36}\), \(f(1)=3\). This is well defined because \(24\cdot3=72\equiv0\pmod{36}\). The image is \(\langle3\rangle\) of order 12. By the first theorem \(\lvert\ker f\rvert=24/12=2\), so \(\ker f=\{0,12\}\) (check: \(3\cdot12=36\equiv0\)). Also \(\mathbb Z_{24}/\{0,12\}\cong\langle3\rangle\cong\mathbb Z_{12}\).

Example 3: Homomorphism counts

  • \(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z_{12},\mathbb Z_{18})\rvert=\gcd(12,18)=6\).
  • Injective homs \(\mathbb Z_{6}\to\mathbb Z_{30}\): \(6\mid30\), so \(\varphi(6)=2\) (send \(1\) to \(5\) or \(25\), the elements of order 6).
  • Surjective homs \(\mathbb Z_{12}\to\mathbb Z_6\): \(6\mid12\), so \(\varphi(6)=2\).
  • \(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z_{12}\times\mathbb Z_8,\mathbb Z_6)\rvert=\gcd(12,6)\gcd(8,6)=6\cdot2=12\).

Example 4: Second isomorphism theorem in \(\mathbb Z\)

Let \(H=\langle4\rangle=4\mathbb Z\) and \(N=\langle6\rangle=6\mathbb Z\) in \(\mathbb Z\). Then \(H+N=2\mathbb Z\) and \(H\cap N=12\mathbb Z\). The theorem says \(2\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\cong4\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z\). Both have order 3 (generated by \(2+6\mathbb Z\) and \(4+12\mathbb Z\)), so both are \(\mathbb Z_3\).

Example 5: \(\operatorname{Inn}(D_4)\)

\(Z(D_4)=\{e,r^2\}\) has order 2, so \(\lvert\operatorname{Inn}(D_4)\rvert=8/2=4\). \(D_4/Z(D_4)\) has order 4 and is not cyclic (otherwise \(D_4\) would be abelian), so \(\operatorname{Inn}(D_4)\cong\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2\).

Example 6: Counting abelian groups and their elements

Abelian groups of order \(72=2^3\cdot3^2\): for \(2^3\) the partitions are \(3,\ 2+1,\ 1+1+1\) giving \(\mathbb Z_8,\ \mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_2,\ \mathbb Z_2^3\); for \(3^2\) the choices are \(\mathbb Z_9,\ \mathbb Z_3^2\). Total \(3\cdot2=6\). In \(\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_6\times\mathbb Z_2\), the elements satisfying \(2g=0\) number \(\gcd(4,2)\gcd(6,2)\gcd(2,2)=8\), so there are 7 elements of order 2.

Example 7: \(G/Z\) cyclic

If \(G/Z(G)\) has prime order, \(G\) is abelian. So a nonabelian group of order \(pq\) has \(Z(G)=\{e\}\), and a nonabelian group of order 8 has \(\lvert Z(G)\rvert=2\) (centre of order 4 would give \(G/Z\) of order 2).

Common traps

  • Which isomorphism theorem is which. Second: \(HN/N\cong H/(H\cap N)\). Options \(N/(H\cap N)\) and \(G/N\) look similar but are wrong.
  • Normality is not transitive. \(K\trianglelefteq V_4\trianglelefteq A_4\) does not give \(K\trianglelefteq A_4\).
  • All subgroups normal does not mean abelian. \(Q_8\) is the example. The converse (abelian implies all subgroups normal) is true.
  • Quotient of a nonabelian group can be abelian. \(S_3/A_3\cong\mathbb Z_2\).
  • Quotient of a cyclic group is cyclic; the quotient of \(G\) by its centre being cyclic forces \(G\) abelian (so a nonabelian \(G\) never has cyclic \(G/Z\)).
  • Number of homomorphisms. \(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z_m,\mathbb Z_n)\rvert=\gcd(m,n)\), not \(\min\) or \(\operatorname{lcm}\). Not every hom is injective or surjective.
  • Injective needs \(m\mid n\). There is no injective hom \(\mathbb Z_4\to\mathbb Z_6\) although \(\gcd(4,6)=2>1\).
  • \(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2\) vs \(\mathbb Z_4\). Both have order 4; only \(\mathbb Z_4\) is cyclic.
  • Abelian groups of order \(n\). Count partitions of each exponent, then multiply. Do not count divisors.
  • \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_n)\). It has order \(\varphi(n)\) (and is \(U(n)\), not necessarily cyclic: \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_{8})\cong\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2\)).
  • Order of an image. \(o(f(a))\) divides \(o(a)\) but need not equal it (equality for all \(a\) holds exactly when \(f\) is injective).

Previous-year questions

2025 Q32

Question. Let \(G\) be a group, \(H\) any subgroup of \(G\) and \(N\) any normal subgroup of \(G\). Then \(HN/N\) is isomorphic to: (A) \(H/H\cap N\) (B) \(N/H\cap N\) (C) \(G/H\cap N\) (D) \(G/N\).

Answer: A (official key).

Solution. This is the second isomorphism theorem. The map \(H\to HN/N\), \(h\mapsto hN\), is a surjective homomorphism with kernel \(H\cap N\), so by the first theorem \(HN/N\cong H/(H\cap N)\).

Quick revision

  • Normal: \(gNg^{-1}=N\); kernels, index-2 subgroups, centres, \(A_n\trianglelefteq S_n\) are normal.
  • First theorem: \(G/\ker f\cong\operatorname{im}f\). Second: \(HN/N\cong H/(H\cap N)\). Third: \((G/N)/(K/N)\cong G/K\).
  • Correspondence: subgroups of \(G/N\) \(\leftrightarrow\) subgroups of \(G\) containing \(N\).
  • \(G/Z(G)\) cyclic implies \(G\) abelian; \(\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G)\).
  • \(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z_m,\mathbb Z_n)\rvert=\gcd(m,n)\); injective homs exist iff \(m\mid n\) (count \(\varphi(m)\)).
  • \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_n)\cong U(n)\) of order \(\varphi(n)\); \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_p\times\mathbb Z_p)=GL_2(\mathbb F_p)\), not to be confused with \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_{p^2})\) of order \(p^2-p\).
  • \(\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n\) is cyclic iff \(\gcd(m,n)=1\); \(o(g,h)=\operatorname{lcm}(o(g),o(h))\).
  • Abelian groups of order \(\prod p_i^{e_i}\): \(\prod P(e_i)\) (partition numbers \(1,2,3,5,7\)).
  • Order 36 has 4 abelian groups; order 360 has 6.
  • \(Q_8\): all subgroups normal but nonabelian; \(Q_8/Z\cong\mathbb Z_2^2\).
  • \(S_3\) has 10 endomorphisms into itself (1 trivial, 3 with kernel \(A_3\), 6 automorphisms).
