Homomorphisms, quotient groups and abelian groups

15 questions. Pick an answer to see if it is right and why.

0 of 15 answered · 0 correct

  1. Question 1Easy
    The number of group homomorphisms from \(\mathbb Z_{20}\) to \(\mathbb Z_{30}\) is

    Why

    A homomorphism is fixed by \(x=f(1)\) with \(20x\equiv0\pmod{30}\), i.e. \(x\) a multiple of \(30/\gcd(20,30)=3\). So \(x\in\{0,3,\dots,27\}\): \(\gcd(20,30)=10\) homomorphisms.
  2. Question 2Easy
    Which of the following abelian groups of order \(36\) is cyclic?

    Why

    \(\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n\) is cyclic iff \(\gcd(m,n)=1\), and \(\gcd(4,9)=1\), so it is \(\cong\mathbb Z_{36}\). The others have \(\gcd(6,6)=6\), \(\gcd(2,18)=2\), \(\gcd(3,12)=3\), so each contains a copy of \(\mathbb Z_p\times\mathbb Z_p\) and is not cyclic.
  3. Question 3Easy
    The quotient group \(GL_n(\mathbb R)/SL_n(\mathbb R)\) is isomorphic to

    Why

    The determinant \(GL_n(\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R^\times\) is a surjective homomorphism with kernel \(SL_n(\mathbb R)\). Apply the first isomorphism theorem.
  4. Question 4Easy
    Let \(G=\mathbb Z_{60}\), \(N=\langle12\rangle\) and \(M=\langle6\rangle\), so \(N\le M\le G\). By the third isomorphism theorem, \((G/N)\big/(M/N)\) is isomorphic to

    Why

    The third isomorphism theorem gives \((G/N)/(M/N)\cong G/M=\mathbb Z_{60}/\langle6\rangle\cong\mathbb Z_6\). The group \(G/N\cong\mathbb Z_{12}\) is an intermediate step, and \(M/N\) has order \(2\), so the quotient has order \(12/2=6\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The quotient group \((\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z)/\langle(1,2)\rangle\) is isomorphic to

    Why

    The map \((a,b)\mapsto b-2a\) is a surjective homomorphism \(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z\) with kernel \(\{b=2a\}=\langle(1,2)\rangle\). By the first isomorphism theorem the quotient is \(\mathbb Z\). Quotienting by one generator of a rank-\(2\) free group leaves rank \(1\), and no torsion appears because \((1,2)\) is not a multiple of any other lattice vector.
  6. Question 6Medium
    The number of non-isomorphic abelian groups of order \(360\) is

    Why

    \(360=2^3\cdot3^2\cdot5\). Partitions of the exponents: \(P(3)=3\), \(P(2)=2\), \(P(1)=1\). Total \(3\cdot2\cdot1=6\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    The group \(\operatorname{Inn}(Q_8)\) of inner automorphisms of the quaternion group is isomorphic to

    Why

    \(\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G)\) and \(Z(Q_8)=\{\pm1\}\), so \(\operatorname{Inn}(Q_8)\cong Q_8/\{\pm1\}\) has order \(4\). Every non-identity coset squares to the identity (since \(i^2=j^2=k^2=-1\)), so there is no element of order \(4\): the quotient is \(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2\), not \(\mathbb Z_4\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    Consider: (i) every subgroup of index \(2\) is normal; (ii) a normal subgroup of a normal subgroup of \(G\) is normal in \(G\); (iii) if \(G/Z(G)\) is cyclic then \(G\) is abelian; (iv) every subgroup of an abelian group is normal. Which statements are true?

    Why

    (i) true. (ii) false: \(\{e,(1\,2)(3\,4)\}\trianglelefteq V_4\trianglelefteq A_4\) but it is not normal in \(A_4\). (iii) true (a generator of \(G/Z\) lifts to an element that commutes with \(Z\) and with itself, so any two elements of \(G\) commute). (iv) true since \(gHg^{-1}=H\) when \(G\) is abelian.
  9. Question 9Medium
    Assertion (A): if every subgroup of a group \(G\) is normal, then \(G\) is abelian. Reason (R): every subgroup of an abelian group is normal.

    Why

    R is true: in an abelian group \(gHg^{-1}=H\). A is false: \(Q_8\) is nonabelian but every subgroup of it is normal. R is the converse of what A claims, so it does not support A.
  10. Question 10Medium
    The order of the automorphism group of \(\mathbb Z_3\times\mathbb Z_3\) is

    Why

    \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_p\times\mathbb Z_p)\cong GL_2(\mathbb F_p)\), of order \((p^2-1)(p^2-p)\). For \(p=3\) this is \(8\cdot6=48\). A common slip is to use \(\lvert\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_3)\rvert^2=4\), which only counts automorphisms that act separately on each factor.
  11. Question 11Medium
    The automorphism group of the cyclic group \(\mathbb Z_{15}\) is isomorphic to

    Why

    \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_{15})\cong U(15)\cong U(3)\times U(5)\cong\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_4\), of order \(\varphi(15)=8\). It is not cyclic (no element of order \(8\): the orders are \(1,2,2,2,4,4,4,4\)) and not elementary abelian (it has elements of order \(4\)).
  12. Question 12Medium
    In \(G=\mathbb Z_{60}\) let \(H=\langle12\rangle\) and \(K=\langle15\rangle\). By the second isomorphism theorem, \((H+K)/K\) is isomorphic to

    Why

    The theorem gives \((H+K)/K\cong H/(H\cap K)\). Here \(H\) has order \(5\) and \(H\cap K=\langle\operatorname{lcm}(12,15)\rangle=\langle60\rangle=0\), so the quotient is \(\cong H\cong\mathbb Z_5\). Check by orders: \(H+K=\langle3\rangle\) has order \(20\), \(K\) has order \(4\), and \(20/4=5\).
  13. Question 13Hard
    The number of group homomorphisms from \(S_3\) to \(S_3\) is

    Why

    Classify by kernel. Kernel \(S_3\): \(1\) trivial homomorphism. Kernel \(A_3\): \(S_3/A_3\cong\mathbb Z_2\) embeds in \(S_3\) in \(3\) ways (choose the image of order \(2\) among \(3\) transpositions): \(3\) homomorphisms. Kernel trivial: automorphisms, \(\lvert\operatorname{Aut}(S_3)\rvert=6\). There are no other normal subgroups. Total \(1+3+6=10\).
  14. Question 14Hard
    The number of group homomorphisms from \(\mathbb Z_3\times\mathbb Z_3\) to \(S_3\) is

    Why

    The image is an abelian subgroup of \(S_3\) whose order divides \(9\), so it has order \(1\) or \(3\) (an image of order \(2\) would need an index-\(2\) subgroup of \(\mathbb Z_3\times\mathbb Z_3\), which has odd order). Hence every homomorphism lands in \(A_3\cong\mathbb Z_3\), and \(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z_3\times\mathbb Z_3,\mathbb Z_3)\) has \(3\cdot3=9\) elements (the trivial one included).
  15. Question 15Hard
    The quotient group \(S_4/V_4\), where \(V_4=\{e,(1\,2)(3\,4),(1\,3)(2\,4),(1\,4)(2\,3)\}\), is isomorphic to

    Why

    \(V_4\trianglelefteq S_4\) has order \(4\), so the quotient has order \(6\): either \(\mathbb Z_6\) or \(S_3\). The subgroup \(H\cong S_3\) fixing the point \(4\) satisfies \(H\cap V_4=\{e\}\) and \(HV_4=S_4\) (order \(6\cdot4=24\)). By the second isomorphism theorem \(S_4/V_4=HV_4/V_4\cong H/(H\cap V_4)\cong S_3\), which is non-abelian.
