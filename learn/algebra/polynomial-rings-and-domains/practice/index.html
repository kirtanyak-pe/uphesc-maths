Polynomial rings and factorisation domains

  1. Question 1Easy
    Which of the following quotient rings is a field?

    Why

    \(F[x]/(f)\) is a field iff \(f\) is irreducible. Squares mod \(3\) are \(0,1\) and \(-1\equiv2\) is not a square, so \(x^2+1\) has no root mod \(3\) and is irreducible. Mod \(5\): \(x^2+1=(x-2)(x-3)\). Mod \(2\): \((x+1)^2\). \(x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)\) always.
  2. Question 2Easy
    For a commutative ring \(R\) with unity, the polynomial ring \(R[x]\) is a principal ideal domain if and only if

    Why

    \(R[x]\) is a PID iff \(R\) is a field. \(R=\mathbb Z\) is a PID, UFD and domain, yet \((2,x)\) is not principal in \(\mathbb Z[x]\).
  3. Question 3Easy
    The number of units in the ring of Gaussian integers \(\mathbb Z[i]\) is

    Why

    Units are the elements of norm \(1\): \(a^2+b^2=1\) gives \(\pm1,\pm i\), so \(4\).
  4. Question 4Medium
    Which of the following integral domains is NOT a unique factorisation domain?

    Why

    \(\mathbb Z[x]\) and \(\mathbb Q[x,y]\) are UFDs by Gauss’s theorem (\(R\) UFD \(\Rightarrow R[x]\) UFD); \(\mathbb Z[i]\) is Euclidean. In \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\), \(6=2\cdot3=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})\) with all four factors irreducible and non-associate.
  5. Question 5Medium
    The number of monic irreducible polynomials of degree \(3\) over \(\mathbb Z_3\) is

    Why

    A monic cubic is reducible iff it has a root. Each monic irreducible cubic has \(3\) roots in \(\mathbb F_{27}\setminus\mathbb F_3\), and there are \(27-3=24\) such elements, so there are \(24/3=8\) (formula \(\frac13(3^3-3)\)). A brute-force search over the \(27\) monic cubics also gives \(8\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    Which of the following polynomials is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\)?

    Why

    \(x^4+4=(x^2+2x+2)(x^2-2x+2)\); \(x^4+x^2+1=(x^2+x+1)(x^2-x+1)\); \(x^4-1=(x^2-1)(x^2+1)\). The last is \(\Phi_5\): \(f(x+1)=x^4+5x^3+10x^2+10x+5\) is Eisenstein at \(5\) (it is also irreducible mod \(2\), checked by search).
  7. Question 7Medium
    The number of roots of \(x^2-1\) in the ring \(\mathbb Z_8\) is

    Why

    \(1,3,5,7\) all satisfy \(a^2\equiv1\pmod8\). The bound ``at most \(\deg f\) roots’’ needs an integral domain, and \(\mathbb Z_8\) is not one.
  8. Question 8Medium
    Consider: (I) \(\mathbb Z[x]\) is a UFD; (II) \(\mathbb Z[x]\) is a PID; (III) \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\) is a UFD; (IV) every PID is a UFD. Which are true?

    Why

    I true (Gauss). II false: \((2,x)\) is not principal. III false: \(6=2\cdot3=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})\). IV true.
  9. Question 9Medium
    Assertion (A): \(2\) is a prime element of \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\). Reason (R): \(2\) is irreducible in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\).

    Why

    R is true: \(N(2)=4\), and no element has norm \(2\), so \(2\) cannot split into two nonunits. A is false: \(2\mid6=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})\) but \(2\) divides neither factor, since \((1\pm\sqrt{-5})/2\notin\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\). Irreducible does not imply prime outside a UFD.
  10. Question 10Medium
    The monic gcd of \(x^4-1\) and \(x^6-1\) in \(\mathbb Q[x]\) is

    Why

    \(x^4-1=(x^2-1)(x^2+1)\) and \(x^6-1=(x^2-1)(x^4+x^2+1)\). Since \(x^4+x^2+1=x^2(x^2+1)+1\), the cofactors \(x^2+1\) and \(x^4+x^2+1\) are coprime. In general \(\gcd(x^m-1,x^n-1)=x^{\gcd(m,n)}-1\).
  11. Question 11Hard
    The quotient ring \(\mathbb Z[i]/(3+4i)\) is

    Why

    \(\lvert\mathbb Z[i]/(3+4i)\rvert=N(3+4i)=25\). Since \(3+4i=(2+i)^2\) is a square of a Gaussian prime, \((2+i)\) is nonzero in the quotient but \((2+i)^2=0\): a nilpotent, hence not a domain.
  12. Question 12Hard
    For which of the following primes \(p\) is \(\mathbb Z_p[x]/(x^2+1)\) a field?

    Why

    It is a field iff \(-1\) is not a square mod \(p\), iff \(p\equiv3\pmod4\). \(5,13,17\equiv1\pmod4\) (e.g. \(2^2=-1\) mod \(5\), \(5^2=-1\) mod \(13\), \(4^2=-1\) mod \(17\)), while \(19\equiv3\pmod4\).
  13. Question 13Hard
    The polynomial \(x^4+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\). Which statement is true?

    Why

    \(x^4+1=\Phi_8\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) (\(f(x+1)\) is Eisenstein at \(2\)). Mod \(p\) it factors into factors of degree \(\operatorname{ord}_8(p)\le2\) for every odd \(p\) (since \(p^2\equiv1\pmod8\)), and \((x+1)^4\) mod \(2\). A search over all primes up to \(37\) confirms. So a mod-\(p\) test can fail for every \(p\).
