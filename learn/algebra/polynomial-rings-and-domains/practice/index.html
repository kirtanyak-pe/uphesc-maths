Polynomial rings and factorisation domains

15 questions. Pick an answer to see if it is right and why.

0 of 15 answered · 0 correct

  1. Question 1Easy
    Which of the following quotient rings is a field?

    Why

    \(F[x]/(f)\) is a field iff \(f\) is irreducible, and a quadratic is irreducible iff it has no root. Mod \(5\): \(x^2+x+1\) takes the values \(1,3,2,3,1\) at \(x=0,\dots,4\), so it has no root. The others fail: \(x^2+1=(x+1)^2\) mod \(2\); \(x^2+x+1=(x-1)^2\) mod \(3\); \(x^2+x+1=(x-2)(x-4)\) mod \(7\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    For a commutative ring \(R\) with unity, the polynomial ring \(R[x]\) is a principal ideal domain if and only if

    Why

    \(R[x]\) is a PID iff \(R\) is a field. \(R=\mathbb Z\) is a PID, UFD and domain, yet \((2,x)\) is not principal in \(\mathbb Z[x]\).
  3. Question 3Easy
    The number of units in the ring of Gaussian integers \(\mathbb Z[i]\) is

    Why

    Units are the elements of norm \(1\): \(a^2+b^2=1\) gives \(\pm1,\pm i\), so \(4\).
  4. Question 4Easy
    The units of the polynomial ring \(\mathbb Q[x]\) are

    Why

    Over a field degrees add, \(\deg(fg)=\deg f+\deg g\), so \(fg=1\) forces both degrees to be \(0\). The units are exactly the nonzero constants, which are the nonzero rationals. (Over a ring with nilpotents such as \(\mathbb Z_4\) there can be more, e.g. \(1+2x\).)
  5. Question 5Medium
    Which of the following integral domains is NOT a unique factorisation domain?

    Why

    \(\mathbb Z[x]\) and \(\mathbb Q[x,y]\) are UFDs by Gauss’s theorem (\(R\) UFD \(\Rightarrow R[x]\) UFD); \(\mathbb Z[i]\) is Euclidean. In \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\), \(6=2\cdot3=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})\) with all four factors irreducible and non-associate.
  6. Question 6Medium
    The number of monic irreducible polynomials of degree \(3\) over \(\mathbb Z_3\) is

    Why

    A monic cubic is reducible iff it has a root. Each monic irreducible cubic has \(3\) roots in \(\mathbb F_{27}\setminus\mathbb F_3\), and there are \(27-3=24\) such elements, so there are \(24/3=8\) (formula \(\frac13(3^3-3)\)). Equivalently, of the \(27\) monic cubics exactly \(8\) have no root in \(\mathbb Z_3\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    The number of roots of \(x^2-1\) in the ring \(\mathbb Z_8\) is

    Why

    \(1,3,5,7\) all satisfy \(a^2\equiv1\pmod8\). The bound “at most \(\deg f\) roots” needs an integral domain, and \(\mathbb Z_8\) is not one.
  8. Question 8Medium
    Consider: (I) every Euclidean domain is a PID; (II) every PID is a UFD; (III) every UFD is a PID; (IV) \(\mathbb Z[x]\) is a Euclidean domain. Which are true?

    Why

    I and II are true (the standard chain Euclidean \(\Rightarrow\) PID \(\Rightarrow\) UFD). III is false: \(\mathbb Z[x]\) is a UFD but \((2,x)\) is not principal. IV is false for the same reason, since a Euclidean domain is a PID.
  9. Question 9Medium
    Which of the following polynomials is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\)?

    Why

    \(x^5-4x+2\) is Eisenstein at \(2\): the non-leading coefficients \(-4,2\) (and the zero ones) are divisible by \(2\), the constant \(2\) is not divisible by \(4\). The others factor: \(x^4+4=(x^2+2x+2)(x^2-2x+2)\) (no rational roots, yet reducible); \(x^3-3x+2=(x-1)^2(x+2)\); \(x^6-1\) has the root \(1\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    Consider \(f=3x^2+6x+9\). Which statement is true?

    Why

    \(f=3(x^2+2x+3)\) with content \(3\). Over \(\mathbb Q\) the constant \(3\) is a unit, and \(x^2+2x+3\) has discriminant \(4-12<0\), so it has no rational root and \(f\) is irreducible. In \(\mathbb Z[x]\) the factor \(3\) is a nonunit, so \(f=3\cdot(x^2+2x+3)\) is a proper factorisation. Gauss: a primitive polynomial irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) stays irreducible in \(\mathbb Z[x]\), but a polynomial with nonunit content does not.
  11. Question 11Medium
    Which of the following integral domains is a Euclidean domain?

    Why

    \(N(a+b\sqrt2)=\lvert a^2-2b^2\rvert\) is a Euclidean function: for \(x+y\sqrt2\in\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), round \(x,y\) to nearest integers \(a,b\); then \(\lvert(x-a)^2-2(y-b)^2\rvert\le\max(\tfrac14,\tfrac24)<1\). \(\mathbb Z[x]\) is not a PID. \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\) and \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-3}]\) are not UFDs (\(6=2\cdot3=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})\); \(4=2\cdot2=(1+\sqrt{-3})(1-\sqrt{-3})\)).
  12. Question 12Medium
    In \(\mathbb Z[i]\) divide \(7+3i\) by \(2+i\): write \(7+3i=(2+i)q+r\) with \(N(r)<N(2+i)=5\). A possible remainder \(r\) is

    Why

    \(\dfrac{7+3i}{2+i}=\dfrac{(7+3i)(2-i)}5=\dfrac{17-i}5=3.4-0.2i\), whose nearest Gaussian integer is \(q=3\), giving \(r=7+3i-3(2+i)=1\) with \(N(r)=1<5\). The remainder is not unique (\(q=3-i\) gives \(r=2i\)). But \(i\), \(1+i\) and \(2\) are not congruent to \(7+3i\) modulo \(2+i\).
  13. Question 13Hard
    Assertion (A): \(2\) is a prime element of \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\). Reason (R): \(2\) is irreducible in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\).

    Why

    R is true: \(N(2)=4\), and no element has norm \(2\), so \(2\) cannot split into two nonunits. A is false: \(2\mid6=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})\) but \(2\) divides neither factor, since \((1\pm\sqrt{-5})/2\notin\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\). Irreducible does not imply prime outside a UFD.
  14. Question 14Hard
    The polynomial \(x^4+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\). Which statement is true?

    Why

    \(x^4+1=\Phi_8\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) (\(f(x+1)\) is Eisenstein at \(2\)). Mod an odd \(p\) it factors into factors of degree \(\operatorname{ord}_8(p)\le2\) (since \(p^2\equiv1\pmod8\)), and \(x^4+1=(x+1)^4\) mod \(2\). So a mod-\(p\) irreducibility test can fail for every \(p\).
  15. Question 15Hard
    The quotient ring \(\mathbb Z[i]/(5)\) is isomorphic to

    Why

    In \(\mathbb Z[i]\) we have \(5=(2+i)(2-i)\) and the ideals \((2+i)\), \((2-i)\) are comaximal (their sum contains \(4\) and \(5\), hence \(1\)). By the Chinese remainder theorem \(\mathbb Z[i]/(5)\cong\mathbb Z[i]/(2+i)\times\mathbb Z[i]/(2-i)\cong\mathbb Z_5\times\mathbb Z_5\), which has zero divisors, so it is not a field; it has characteristic \(5\), so it is not \(\mathbb Z_{25}\), and it has no nonzero nilpotents, unlike \(\mathbb Z_5[x]/(x^2)\).
