  1. Question 1Easy
    The Wronskian of \(1,\ x,\ x^2\) is

    Why

    \(W=\begin{vmatrix}1&x&x^2\\0&1&2x\\0&0&2\end{vmatrix}=2\). It is non-zero, so the three functions are linearly independent.
  2. Question 2Medium
    Two solutions of \(x^2y''+4xy'+2y=0\) on \(x>0\) have Wronskian \(W(1)=3\). Then \(W(2)=\)

    Why

    Standard form: \(p=4/x\), so \(W=Ce^{-\int 4/x\,dx}=Cx^{-4}\). \(C=3\), hence \(W(2)=3/16\). (Check with \(x^{-1}\), \(x^{-2}\): \(W=-x^{-4}\).)
  3. Question 3Medium
    Which pair is linearly independent on \(\mathbb R\)?

    Why

    \(\sin2x=2\sin x\cos x\), \(\cos2x=1-2\sin^2x\) and \(e^{x+1}=e\cdot e^x\) are proportional. \(x\) and \(\lvert x\rvert\) are not proportional on \(\mathbb R\) (equal for \(x>0\), opposite for \(x<0\)), even though \(W=0\) at every point where both are differentiable.
  4. Question 4Medium
    Three solutions of the equation \(y''-y=-x\) are \(u_1=x\), \(u_2=x+e^{x}\) and \(u_3=x+e^{-x}\). Its general solution is

    Why

    The differences \(u_2-u_1=e^x\) and \(u_3-u_1=e^{-x}\) solve the homogeneous equation, so \(y=u_1+c_1(u_2-u_1)+c_2(u_3-u_1)=x+c_1e^x+c_2e^{-x}\). The third form has the wrong particular part: it equals \((1+c_1+c_2)x+c_1e^x+c_2e^{-x}\), which solves the equation only when \(c_1+c_2=0\). The last has three constants for a second-order equation.
  5. Question 5Easy
    A particular integral of \(y''+4y=\sin 2x\) is

    Why

    Resonance: \(\frac{1}{D^2+a^2}\sin ax=-\frac{x\cos ax}{2a}\) with \(a=2\).
  6. Question 6Hard
    A particular integral of \(y''+y=\tan x\) (on \(-\pi/2<x<\pi/2\)) is

    Why

    \(y_1=\cos x,y_2=\sin x,W=1\). \(y_p=-\cos x\int\sin x\tan x\,dx+\sin x\int\sin x\,dx=-\cos x(\ln\lvert\sec x+\tan x\rvert-\sin x)-\sin x\cos x=-\cos x\ln\lvert\sec x+\tan x\rvert\).
  7. Question 7Easy
    The general solution of \(x^2y''-3xy'+4y=0\) (\(x>0\)) is

    Why

    \(m(m-1)-3m+4=m^2-4m+4=(m-2)^2\): repeated root \(2\), giving \(x^2\) and \(x^2\ln x\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    Which of the following is a solution of \(x\,y''-(2x+1)\,y'+(x+1)\,y=0\) (\(x>0\))?

    Why

    Write \(P=-\frac{2x+1}{x}\) and \(Q=\frac{x+1}{x}\). Then \(1+P+Q=\frac{x-2x-1+x+1}{x}=0\), so \(y=e^x\) is a solution. Direct substitution shows the other three fail: for \(y=x\) the left side is \(x^2-x-1\), for \(e^{-x}\) it is \((4x+2)e^{-x}\), for \(x^2\) it is \(x^3-3x^2\).
  9. Question 9Hard
    \(y_1=x\) solves \((1-x^2)y''-2xy'+2y=0\) on \((-1,1)\). A second independent solution (by reduction of order) is

    Why

    \(p=\frac{-2x}{1-x^2}\), \(e^{-\int p}=\frac{1}{1-x^2}\). \(y_2=x\int\frac{dx}{x^2(1-x^2)}=x\int\left(\frac1{x^2}+\frac1{1-x^2}\right)dx=x\left(-\frac1x+\frac12\ln\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)=\frac x2\ln\frac{1+x}{1-x}-1\).
  10. Question 10Easy
    If \(P_3(x)=\tfrac12(5x^3-3x)\) is the Legendre polynomial of degree \(3\), then \(\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}x\,P_3(x)\,dx=\)

    Why

    \(x=P_1(x)\), and Legendre polynomials of different degrees are orthogonal on \([-1,1]\), so the integral is \(0\). (The value \(\frac23\) would be \(\int xP_1\,dx\).)
  11. Question 11Medium
    For \(\mathbf x'=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2\\-2&-1\end{pmatrix}\mathbf x\) the origin is a

    Why

    Trace \(-2\), determinant \(5\): eigenvalues \(-1\pm2i\), complex with negative real part, so a stable spiral.
  12. Question 12Medium
    The series solution \(y=\sum a_nx^n\) of \(y''+xy'+y=0\) with \(a_0=1\), \(a_1=0\) has \(a_4=\)

    Why

    Coefficient of \(x^n\): \((n+2)(n+1)a_{n+2}+na_n+a_n=0\), so \(a_{n+2}=-\frac{a_n}{n+2}\). \(a_2=-\frac12\), \(a_4=-\frac{a_2}{4}=\frac18\) (the series is \(e^{-x^2/2}\)).
  13. Question 13Medium
    A power series solution about \(x=1\) of \((x^2-4x+13)\,y''+y=0\) is guaranteed to converge at least for \(\lvert x-1\rvert<\)

    Why

    The singular points are the roots \(2\pm3i\) of \(x^2-4x+13\). Their distance from \(x=1\) is \(\sqrt{(2-1)^2+3^2}=\sqrt{10}\). (\(\sqrt{13}\) is the distance from \(0\).)
  14. Question 14Hard
    For \(x^2y''+(e^{x}-1)\,y'+x\,y=0\), the point \(x=0\) is

    Why

    \(P=\frac{e^x-1}{x^2}\) is unbounded at \(0\), so \(0\) is singular. \(xP=\frac{e^x-1}{x}\to1\) and \(x^2Q=x\to0\) are analytic, so the point is regular with \(p_0=1\), \(q_0=0\). The indicial equation is \(r(r-1)+r=r^2=0\), so the roots are \(0,0\).
  15. Question 15Medium
    The general solution of \(x^3y'''-4x^2y''+8xy'-8y=0\) (\(x>0\)) is

    Why

    Put \(y=x^m\): \(m(m-1)(m-2)-4m(m-1)+8m-8=m^3-7m^2+14m-8=(m-1)(m-2)(m-4)\). So \(y=c_1x+c_2x^2+c_3x^4\).
