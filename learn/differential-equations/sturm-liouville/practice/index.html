  1. Question 1Easy
    The eigenvalues of \(y''+\lambda y=0\), \(y(0)=0\), \(y'(\pi)=0\) are (\(n=1,2,3,\dots\))

    Why

    \(y=\sin\mu x\) and \(y'(\pi)=\mu\cos\mu\pi=0\) give \(\mu\pi=\left(n-\frac12\right)\pi\), so \(\lambda_n=\left(n-\frac12\right)^2=\frac{(2n-1)^2}{4}\). Option D is missing the factor \(\frac14\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    The smallest eigenvalue of \(y''+\lambda y=0\), \(y'(0)=y'(\pi)=0\) is

    Why

    \(\lambda=0\) gives \(y=\) constant, which satisfies \(y'(0)=y'(\pi)=0\). So \(0\) is an eigenvalue (the others are \(n^2\)), and it is the smallest.
  3. Question 3Easy
    The operator \(L[y]=x^2y''+k\,x\,y'+y\) on \([1,2]\) is self-adjoint (already of the form \((py')'+qy\)) exactly when \(k=\)

    Why

    \(L=a_0y''+a_1y'+a_2y\) is self-adjoint iff \(a_1=a_0'\). Here \(a_0=x^2\), \(a_0'=2x\) and \(a_1=kx\), so \(k=2\), giving \(L[y]=(x^2y')'+y\).
  4. Question 4Medium
    The eigenfunction expansion of \(f(x)=1\) on \([0,\pi]\) in \(\{\sin nx\}\) (the eigenfunctions of \(y''+\lambda y=0\), \(y(0)=y(\pi)=0\)) is \(\sum b_n\sin nx\). Then \(b_1=\)

    Why

    \(b_n=\frac{\int_0^\pi\sin nx\,dx}{\int_0^\pi\sin^2nx\,dx}=\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{1-(-1)^n}{n}\). For \(n=1\): \(\frac2\pi\cdot2=\frac4\pi\) (and \(b_2=0\)).
  5. Question 5Medium
    Put \(y''+4y'+\lambda y=0\) on \([0,1]\) in Sturm-Liouville form \((py')'+\lambda wy=0\). The weight function is

    Why

    \(p=\exp\int\frac41\,dx=e^{4x}\) and \(w=p\cdot\frac{a_3}{a_0}=e^{4x}\). Check: \((e^{4x}y')'=e^{4x}(y''+4y')\), so multiplying the equation by \(e^{4x}\) gives \((e^{4x}y')'+\lambda e^{4x}y=0\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    The eigenvalues of the Legendre problem \((1-x^2)y''-2xy'+\lambda y=0\) on \([-1,1]\), with solutions bounded at \(x=\pm1\), are

    Why

    The bounded solutions are the polynomials \(P_n\), and substitution gives \(\lambda=n(n+1)\). (Hermite gives \(2n\), Chebyshev \(n^2\).) For example \(P_3=\tfrac12(5x^3-3x)\) satisfies the equation with \(\lambda=12\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    The positive eigenvalues \(\lambda=\mu^2\) of \(y''+\lambda y=0\), \(y(0)=0\), \(y'(1)+2y(1)=0\) satisfy

    Why

    \(y=\sin\mu x\), so \(y'(1)+2y(1)=\mu\cos\mu+2\sin\mu=0\), which gives \(\tan\mu=-\mu/2\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    For a regular Sturm-Liouville problem with separated boundary conditions consider: (I) all eigenvalues are real; (II) eigenfunctions of distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal with respect to the weight \(w\); (III) every eigenvalue has two linearly independent eigenfunctions; (IV) the eigenvalues form an increasing sequence tending to \(+\infty\). Which are true?

    Why

    Eigenvalues are simple (one-dimensional eigenspace) for separated conditions, so III is false. I, II and IV are standard properties.
  9. Question 9Hard
    Assertion (A): The problem \(y''+\lambda y=0\), \(y(-\pi)=y(\pi)\), \(y'(-\pi)=y'(\pi)\) has an eigenvalue with two linearly independent eigenfunctions. Reason (R): Periodic conditions are not separated, so simplicity of eigenvalues is not guaranteed.

    Why

    \(\lambda=n^2\) (\(n\ge1\)) has eigenfunctions \(\cos nx\) and \(\sin nx\), so A is true. The simplicity theorem is proved for separated conditions, which is why periodic conditions can fail it; R is true and is the reason.
  10. Question 10Medium
    Let \(y_1=\sin x\) and let \(y_2\) be any solution of \(y''+y=0\) linearly independent of \(y_1\). The number of zeros of \(y_2\) in the open interval \((0,\pi)\) is

    Why

    \(y_2=A\sin x+B\cos x\) with \(B\ne0\) equals \(R\cos(x-\varphi)\), with zeros spaced \(\pi\) apart. By the Sturm separation theorem exactly one lies between the consecutive zeros \(0\) and \(\pi\) of \(\sin x\).
  11. Question 11Hard
    If \(q(x)\ge4\) for all \(x\), then the distance between consecutive zeros of any non-trivial solution of \(y''+q(x)y=0\) is at most

    Why

    Compare with \(z''+4z=0\), whose solution \(\sin2(x-a)\) has consecutive zeros \(a\) and \(a+\frac\pi2\). By Sturm comparison, \(y\) has a zero in \([a,a+\frac\pi2]\) for every \(a\), so consecutive zeros are at most \(\frac\pi2\) apart (sharp for \(q=4\)).
  12. Question 12Medium
    For \(y''=f(x)\) on \([0,1]\) with \(y(0)=0\) and \(y'(1)=0\), the Green’s function is \(G(x,\xi)=-\min(x,\xi)\). Then \(G\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac34\right)=\)

    Why

    Take \(u_1=x\) (satisfies \(y(0)=0\)) and \(u_2=1\) (satisfies \(y'(1)=0\)); \(W=u_1u_2'-u_1'u_2=-1\) and \(p=1\), so \(G=\frac{u_1(x_<)u_2(x_>)}{pW}=-x_<\). At \(x=\frac14\), \(\xi=\frac34\): \(x_<=\frac14\), so \(G=-\frac14\).
  13. Question 13Medium
    Which statement is NOT true for the Green’s function \(G(x,\xi)\) of \((py')'+qy=f\) with homogeneous separated boundary conditions (when it exists)?

    Why

    \(\partial G/\partial x\) has a jump of \(1/p(\xi)\) at \(x=\xi\); that jump produces the \(\delta\)-function in \((pG_x)_x+qG=\delta(x-\xi)\). The other three properties hold.
  14. Question 14Hard
    The problem \(y''+y=\cos x\), \(y(0)=y(\pi)=0\) has

    Why

    The homogeneous problem has the solution \(\phi=\sin x\). By the Fredholm alternative, solutions exist only if \(\int_0^\pi\cos x\sin x\,dx=0\), which holds. Explicitly \(y=\frac x2\sin x+A\cos x+B\sin x\); \(y(0)=0\) gives \(A=0\), \(y(\pi)=0\) then holds for every \(B\).
  15. Question 15Easy
    For \(y''+\lambda y=0\), \(y(0)=y(\pi)=0\), the fifth eigenfunction has how many zeros in the open interval \((0,\pi)\)?

    Why

    \(\phi_5=\sin5x\) has zeros at \(\frac{k\pi}{5}\), \(k=1,\dots,4\) in \((0,\pi)\). The \(n\)-th eigenfunction has \(n-1\) interior zeros.
