  1. Question 1Easy
    The equation \((2xy+3y^2)\,dx+(x^2+kxy)\,dy=0\) is exact for \(k=\)

    Why

    \(M_y=2x+6y\) and \(N_x=2x+ky\). Equality for all \(x,y\) needs \(k=6\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    An integrating factor of \(x\,y'+2y=x^3\) (\(x>0\)) is

    Why

    Write \(y'+\frac2xy=x^2\). Then \(\mu=e^{\int 2/x\,dx}=x^2\), and \((x^2y)'=x^4\).
  3. Question 3Easy
    The general solution of \(x\,y'-2y=x^3\) for \(x>0\) is

    Why

    \(y'-\frac2xy=x^2\), \(\mu=x^{-2}\), so \((y/x^2)'=1\) and \(y/x^2=x+C\), i.e. \(y=x^3+Cx^2\).
  4. Question 4Medium
    The substitution \(v=y^{-3}\) reduces \(y'+y=xy^4\) to the linear equation

    Why

    \(v'=-3y^{-4}y'=-3y^{-4}(xy^4-y)=-3x+3v\), so \(v'-3v=-3x\). In general \(v=y^{1-n}\) gives \(v'+(1-n)Pv=(1-n)Q\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The singular solution of \(y=px+\dfrac1p\) is

    Why

    Clairaut with \(f(p)=1/p\). \(x=-f'(p)=1/p^2\) and \(y=px+1/p=2/p\), so \(y^2=4/p^2=4x\).
  6. Question 6Easy
    Which of these is a Clairaut equation?

    Why

    Clairaut means \(y=px+f(p)\) with the coefficient of \(x\) exactly \(p\). Only \(y=xp+\sin p\) has that form; the others are Lagrange or neither.
  7. Question 7Medium
    The singular solution of \(y=px-p^2\) is

    Why

    \(f(p)=-p^2\), \(x=-f'(p)=2p\), so \(p=x/2\) and \(y=\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^2}{4}=\frac{x^2}{4}\).
  8. Question 8Easy
    The orthogonal trajectories of the family \(y=cx^2\) are

    Why

    \(y'=2cx=2y/x\). Orthogonal slope \(-\frac{x}{2y}\) gives \(2y\,dy=-x\,dx\), so \(x^2+2y^2=k\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    Which IVP is guaranteed a unique local solution by the Picard-Lindelof theorem?

    Why

    \(f=\lvert y\rvert\) satisfies \(\lvert f(y_1)-f(y_2)\rvert\le\lvert y_1-y_2\rvert\), so it is Lipschitz even though \(f_y\) fails at \(0\). The other three are continuous but not Lipschitz near \(y=0\) (difference quotients \(\sim\lvert y\rvert^{-1/3}\), \(\lvert y\rvert^{-1/2}\), \(\lvert y\rvert^{-2/3}\) blow up).
  10. Question 10Hard
    For \(y'=y^2\), \(y(0)=1\), on \(R=\{\lvert x\rvert\le a,\ \lvert y-1\rvert\le b\}\) with \(a\) large, the largest guaranteed interval half-length \(h=\min(a,b/M)\) over all choices of \(b\) is

    Why

    On \(R\), \(M=(1+b)^2\), so \(h=\frac{b}{(1+b)^2}\). This is maximal at \(b=1\) with \(h=\frac14\) (the true solution \(1/(1-x)\) actually lives on \(x<1\)).
  11. Question 11Hard
    For \(y'=1+y^2\), \(y(0)=0\), the coefficient of \(x^5\) in the third Picard iterate \(y_3(x)\) is

    Why

    \(y_1=x\), \(y_2=x+\frac{x^3}{3}\), \(y_3=\int_0^x\left(1+(t+\frac{t^3}{3})^2\right)dt=x+\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{2x^5}{15}+\frac{x^7}{63}\). (\(17/315\) is the \(x^7\) coefficient of \(\tan x\), not of \(y_3\).)
  12. Question 12Medium
    Consider: (I) If \(f\) is continuous on a rectangle \(R\) then \(y'=f(x,y)\), \(y(x_0)=y_0\) has a solution near \(x_0\). (II) If \(f\) is continuous on \(R\) the solution is unique. (III) If \(f_y\) exists and is bounded on \(R\) then \(f\) is Lipschitz in \(y\) on \(R\). (IV) If \(f\) is Lipschitz in \(y\) then \(f_y\) exists everywhere on \(R\). Which are true?

    Why

    I is Peano. II is false (\(y^{1/3}\)). III follows from the mean value theorem. IV is false (\(\lvert y\rvert\) is Lipschitz but not differentiable at \(0\)).
  13. Question 13Medium
    The IVP \(x\,y'=3y\), \(y(0)=0\) has

    Why

    \(y=Cx^3\) for any constant \(C\) satisfies the ODE and \(y(0)=0\). (The coefficient \(3y/x\) is discontinuous at \(x=0\), so no theorem gives uniqueness.)
  14. Question 14Easy
    Which function is NOT a solution of \(y'=3y^{2/3}\), \(y(0)=0\)?

    Why

    For \(y=x^3\): \(y'=3x^2=3(x^3)^{2/3}\). The function in C is \(C^1\) and solves it on each piece. For \(y=x^2\), \(y'=2x\) but \(3y^{2/3}=3x^{4/3}\).
  15. Question 15Hard
    Which description of how loci appear in the discriminants is correct?

    Why

    Standard table: \(E\) appears once in both; \(N\) twice in \(c\) (absent in \(p\)); \(C\) three times in \(c\), once in \(p\); \(T\) twice in \(p\) (absent in \(c\)). Only A is consistent with it.
