  1. Question 1Easy
    The equation \((2xy+3y^2)\,dx+(x^2+kxy)\,dy=0\) is exact for \(k=\)

    Why

    \(M_y=2x+6y\) and \(N_x=2x+ky\). Equality for all \(x,y\) needs \(k=6\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    An integrating factor of \(x\,y'+2y=x^3\) (\(x>0\)) is

    Why

    Write \(y'+\frac2xy=x^2\). Then \(\mu=e^{\int 2/x\,dx}=x^2\), and \((x^2y)'=x^4\).
  3. Question 3Medium
    The singular solutions of \(y^2(1+p^2)=4\), where \(p=dy/dx\) and the general solution is \((x-c)^2+y^2=4\), are

    Why

    Write the ODE as \(y^2p^2+(y^2-4)=0\). Its \(p\)-discriminant is \(-4y^2(y^2-4)\), with factors \(y^2\) (twice), \(y-2\) and \(y+2\). The lines \(y=\pm2\) satisfy the ODE (\(p=0\), \(4=4\)) and are not members of the family, so they are envelopes and singular solutions. The factor \(y=0\) does not satisfy the ODE (\(0\ne4\)): it is a tac-locus, not a solution. The circle \(x^2+y^2=4\) is just the member \(c=0\).
  4. Question 4Medium
    The substitution \(v=y^{-3}\) reduces \(y'+y=xy^4\) to the linear equation

    Why

    \(v'=-3y^{-4}y'=-3y^{-4}(xy^4-y)=-3x+3v\), so \(v'-3v=-3x\). In general \(v=y^{1-n}\) gives \(v'+(1-n)Pv=(1-n)Q\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The singular solution of \(y=px+\dfrac1p\) is

    Why

    Clairaut with \(f(p)=1/p\). \(x=-f'(p)=1/p^2\) and \(y=px+1/p=2/p\), so \(y^2=4/p^2=4x\).
  6. Question 6Easy
    Which of these is a Clairaut equation?

    Why

    Clairaut means \(y=px+f(p)\) with the coefficient of \(x\) exactly \(p\). Only \(y=xp+\sin p\) has that form; the others are Lagrange or neither.
  7. Question 7Medium
    The Riccati equation \(y'=y^2-2xy+x^2+1\) has the particular solution \(y=x\). Its general solution is

    Why

    Put \(y=x+\frac1v\). Then \(y'=1-\frac{v'}{v^2}\), while the right side simplifies to \(1+\frac1{v^2}\). So \(v'=-1\), \(v=c-x\) and \(y=x+\frac1{c-x}\). Substituting the other forms back into the ODE fails.
  8. Question 8Easy
    The orthogonal trajectories of the family of hyperbolas \(xy=c\) are

    Why

    From \(xy=c\): \(y+xy'=0\), so \(y'=-y/x\). The orthogonal slope is \(x/y\), so \(y\,dy=x\,dx\) and \(y^2-x^2=k\). (The family \(xy=k\) is the original family, not the orthogonal one.)
  9. Question 9Medium
    Which IVP is guaranteed a unique local solution by the Picard-Lindelof theorem?

    Why

    \(f=\lvert y\rvert\) satisfies \(\lvert f(y_1)-f(y_2)\rvert\le\lvert y_1-y_2\rvert\), so it is Lipschitz even though \(f_y\) fails at \(0\). The other three are continuous but not Lipschitz near \(y=0\) (difference quotients \(\sim\lvert y\rvert^{-1/3}\), \(\lvert y\rvert^{-1/2}\), \(\lvert y\rvert^{-2/3}\) blow up).
  10. Question 10Hard
    For \(y'=y^2\), \(y(0)=1\), on \(R=\{\lvert x\rvert\le a,\ \lvert y-1\rvert\le b\}\) with \(a\) large, the largest guaranteed interval half-length \(h=\min(a,b/M)\) over all choices of \(b\) is

    Why

    On \(R\), \(M=(1+b)^2\), so \(h=\frac{b}{(1+b)^2}\). This is maximal at \(b=1\) with \(h=\frac14\) (the true solution \(1/(1-x)\) actually lives on \(x<1\)).
  11. Question 11Hard
    For \(y'=x^2+y\), \(y(0)=0\), the coefficient of \(x^5\) in the third Picard iterate \(y_3(x)\) is

    Why

    \(y_1=\int_0^xt^2\,dt=\frac{x^3}{3}\). \(y_2=\int_0^x\left(t^2+\frac{t^3}{3}\right)dt=\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{x^4}{12}\). \(y_3=\int_0^x\left(t^2+\frac{t^3}{3}+\frac{t^4}{12}\right)dt=\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{x^4}{12}+\frac{x^5}{60}\).
  12. Question 12Hard
    Consider: (I) If \(f\) is continuous on a rectangle \(R\) then \(y'=f(x,y)\), \(y(x_0)=y_0\) has a solution near \(x_0\). (II) If \(f\) is continuous on \(R\) the solution is unique. (III) If \(f_y\) exists and is bounded on \(R\) then \(f\) is Lipschitz in \(y\) on \(R\). (IV) If \(f\) is Lipschitz in \(y\) then \(f_y\) exists everywhere on \(R\). Which are true?

    Why

    I is Peano. II is false (\(y^{1/3}\)). III follows from the mean value theorem. IV is false (\(\lvert y\rvert\) is Lipschitz but not differentiable at \(0\)).
  13. Question 13Medium
    For \(y'=x^2+y^2\), \(y(0)=0\) on the rectangle \(R=\{\lvert x\rvert\le2,\ \lvert y\rvert\le1\}\), the interval half-length \(h=\min\{a,b/M\}\) guaranteed by the existence theorem is

    Why

    Here \(a=2\), \(b=1\) and \(M=\max_R(x^2+y^2)=4+1=5\). So \(h=\min\{2,\frac15\}=\frac15\). (\(\frac12\) is the value for the smaller rectangle \(a=b=1\).)
  14. Question 14Easy
    The general solution of \(p^2-p-6=0\), where \(p=dy/dx\), is

    Why

    \(p^2-p-6=(p-3)(p+2)\), so \(p=3\) or \(p=-2\). These give \(y-3x-c=0\) and \(y+2x-c=0\). The general solution is the product with one common constant.
  15. Question 15Medium
    Which description of how loci appear in the discriminants is correct?

    Why

    Standard table: \(E\) appears once in both; \(N\) twice in \(c\) (absent in \(p\)); \(C\) three times in \(c\), once in \(p\); \(T\) twice in \(p\) (absent in \(c\)). Only A is consistent with it.
