  1. Question 1Easy
    The eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of \(X''+\lambda X=0\), \(X(0)=0,\ X(L)=0\) are

    Why

    \(\lambda\le0\) gives only \(X=0\). For \(\lambda=\omega^2>0\), \(X=A\cos\omega x+B\sin\omega x\); \(X(0)=0\) gives \(A=0\) and \(X(L)=0\) gives \(\sin\omega L=0\), so \(\omega=n\pi/L\). Option C belongs to \(X(0)=0,\ X'(L)=0\).
  2. Question 2Medium
    Match the boundary conditions for \(X''+\lambda X=0\) on \([0,L]\) (List I) with the eigenfunctions (List II). List I: (a) \(X(0)=X(L)=0\); (b) \(X'(0)=X'(L)=0\); (c) \(X(0)=0,\ X'(L)=0\); (d) \(X'(0)=0,\ X(L)=0\). List II: (I) \(\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\); (II) \(\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\); (III) \(\sin\dfrac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}\); (IV) \(\cos\dfrac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}\).

    Why

    A condition \(X=0\) at \(x=0\) selects \(\sin\) at that end, \(X'=0\) selects \(\cos\). Both ends of the same kind give integer multiples of \(\pi/L\); one end of each kind gives odd multiples of \(\pi/(2L)\).
  3. Question 3Easy
    For \(u_t=ku_{xx}\) on \(0<x<L\) with \(u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0\), the \(n\)th separated solution is

    Why

    \(X=\sin(n\pi x/L)\) with \(\lambda_n=(n\pi/L)^2\), and \(T'=-k\lambda_nT\) gives \(T=e^{-k\lambda_nt}\). C is a wave-equation form; D grows; A has \(n\) instead of \(n^2\) and no \(k\).
  4. Question 4Medium
    In the solution of \(u_t=u_{xx}\) on \(0<x<\pi\) with \(u(0,t)=u(\pi,t)=0\) and \(u(x,0)=x(\pi-x)\), written \(u=\sum b_ne^{-n^2t}\sin nx\), the coefficient \(b_1\) is

    Why

    \(b_1=\dfrac2\pi\displaystyle\int_0^\pi x(\pi-x)\sin x\,dx\). Since \(\displaystyle\int_0^\pi x\sin x\,dx=\pi\) and \(\displaystyle\int_0^\pi x^2\sin x\,dx=\pi^2-4\), the integral is \(\pi^2-(\pi^2-4)=4\). So \(b_1=\dfrac8\pi\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The solution of \(u_t=u_{xx}\) on \(0<x<\pi\), \(u(0,t)=u(\pi,t)=0\), \(u(x,0)=\sin x+2\sin 3x\) has \(u(\pi/2,\,1)\) equal to

    Why

    \(u=e^{-t}\sin x+2e^{-9t}\sin3x\) (mode \(n\) decays as \(e^{-n^2t}\)). At \(x=\pi/2\): \(\sin(\pi/2)=1\), \(\sin(3\pi/2)=-1\). So \(u(\pi/2,1)=e^{-1}-2e^{-9}\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    For \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\) on \(0<x<L\) with fixed ends, \(u(x,0)=0\) and \(u_t(x,0)=g(x)\), the solution is \(u=\sum b_n\sin\dfrac{n\pi ct}{L}\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\) with \(b_n=\)

    Why

    \(u_t(x,0)=\sum b_n\dfrac{n\pi c}{L}\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}=g(x)\), so \(b_n\dfrac{n\pi c}{L}=\dfrac2L\int_0^Lg\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}dx\), giving \(b_n=\dfrac{2}{n\pi c}\int g\sin\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    The solution of \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\) on \(-\infty<x<\infty\) with \(u(x,0)=x^2,\ u_t(x,0)=2x\) is

    Why

    \(\tfrac12[(x+ct)^2+(x-ct)^2]=x^2+c^2t^2\) and \(\dfrac1{2c}\displaystyle\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}2s\,ds=\dfrac{1}{2c}\cdot4xct=2xt\).
  8. Question 8Easy
    A string of length \(L\) fixed at both ends with wave speed \(c\) has natural angular frequencies \(\omega_n=\)

    Why

    \(T''=-\lambda_nc^2T\) with \(\lambda_n=(n\pi/L)^2\) gives \(T=\cos\omega_nt,\ \sin\omega_nt\) with \(\omega_n=c\sqrt{\lambda_n}=n\pi c/L\). (The frequency in cycles per unit time is \(nc/(2L)\).)
  9. Question 9Hard
    The function \(u\) is harmonic in \(0<x<\pi,\ 0<y<1\) with \(u=0\) on \(x=0\), \(x=\pi\), \(y=0\) and \(u(x,1)=\sin2x\). Then \(u(\pi/4,\,1/2)=\)

    Why

    \(u=\sin2x\,\dfrac{\sinh2y}{\sinh2}\) (one mode, \(n=2\), \(a=\pi\)). At \((\pi/4,1/2)\): \(\sin(\pi/2)=1\) and \(\dfrac{\sinh1}{\sinh2}=\dfrac{\sinh1}{2\sinh1\cosh1}=\dfrac1{2\cosh1}\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    The harmonic function in the unit disc with \(u(1,\theta)=1+\cos\theta+3\sin2\theta\) has \(u\!\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac\pi4\right)=\)

    Why

    Inside the disc each mode is multiplied by \(r^n\): \(u=1+r\cos\theta+3r^2\sin2\theta\). At \(r=\tfrac12,\ \theta=\tfrac\pi4\): \(1+\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}+\dfrac34\cdot1=\dfrac{7+\sqrt2}{4}\).
  11. Question 11Easy
    A disc \(r<a\) has boundary values \(u=T_0\) on the upper semicircle (\(0<\theta<\pi\)) and \(u=0\) on the lower semicircle. The harmonic function \(u\) at the centre equals

    Why

    By the mean value property, \(u(0)=\dfrac1{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}u(a,\theta)\,d\theta=\dfrac{T_0\pi}{2\pi}=\dfrac{T_0}{2}\) (equivalently \(a_0/2\)).
  12. Question 12Medium
    A bar \(0<x<3\) with insulated ends has \(u_t=ku_{xx}\), \(u_x(0,t)=u_x(3,t)=0\) and \(u(x,0)=x^2\). Then \(\lim_{t\to\infty}u(x,t)=\)

    Why

    Insulated ends give a cosine series \(\frac{a_0}2+\sum a_ne^{-k\lambda_nt}\cos\frac{n\pi x}{3}\). Every term with \(n\ge1\) decays, leaving \(\dfrac{a_0}{2}=\dfrac13\displaystyle\int_0^3x^2dx=3\). (\(a_0\) itself is \(6\).)
  13. Question 13Medium
    A rod \(0<x<L\) obeys \(u_t=ku_{xx}\) with \(u(0,t)=20\) and \(u(L,t)=80\). The steady-state temperature at \(x=L/3\) is

    Why

    The steady state solves \(u''=0\): \(u_s=20+\dfrac{80-20}{L}x\). At \(x=L/3\): \(20+20=40\).
  14. Question 14Hard
    For \(X''+\lambda X=0\) with \(X(0)=X(L)=0\) (arising from \(u_t=ku_{xx}\)): (i) \(\lambda=0\) gives only \(X\equiv0\). (ii) \(\lambda<0\) gives non-trivial solutions \(X\). (iii) Every eigenvalue is positive. Which statements are correct?

    Why

    \(\lambda=0\): \(X=A+Bx\) with \(X(0)=X(L)=0\) gives \(A=B=0\). \(\lambda=-\mu^2<0\): \(X=Ae^{\mu x}+Be^{-\mu x}\), and both conditions force \(A=B=0\), so (ii) is false. Only \(\lambda_n=(n\pi/L)^2>0\) remain.
  15. Question 15Hard
    Assertion (A): For Laplace’s equation inside the disc \(r<a\) with boundary data \(u(a,\theta)=f(\theta)\), the series solution contains \(r^n\) but not \(r^{-n}\) or \(\ln r\). Reason (R): The functions \(r^{-n}\ (n\ge1)\) and \(\ln r\) are unbounded at \(r=0\).

    Why

    Separation gives \(R(r)\in\{r^n,r^{-n}\}\) for \(n\ge1\) and \(\{1,\ln r\}\) for \(n=0\). The solution must be bounded (indeed finite) at the centre, which rules out \(r^{-n}\) and \(\ln r\). This is why the interior solution is \(\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum(r/a)^n(\dots)\).
