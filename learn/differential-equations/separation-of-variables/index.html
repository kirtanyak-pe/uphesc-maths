Why it matters for the exam

No question of this exact kind appears yet in the 2019 (partial), 2025 or 2026 papers, but the syllabus names it (“separation of variables (Laplace, heat, wave)”) and it is the natural next question after classification. Expect a solution form, a Fourier coefficient, an eigenvalue, a value of \(u\) at a point, or a d’Alembert solution. It also supports the 2025-Q59 match (heat, wave and Laplace equations as parabolic, hyperbolic and elliptic).

Core ideas

The method

To solve a linear homogeneous PDE with homogeneous boundary conditions:

  1. Assume \(u(x,t)=X(x)T(t)\) (or \(X(x)Y(y)\), or \(R(r)\Theta(\theta)\)).
  2. Substitute and divide so each side depends on one variable only. Each side equals a constant (the separation constant, written \(-\lambda\)).
  3. The boundary conditions on \(X\) give an eigenvalue problem; only certain \(\lambda_n\) give non-zero \(X_n\).
  4. Solve the other equation for each \(\lambda_n\).
  5. Superpose: \(u=\sum c_n X_n T_n\).
  6. Fit the remaining (initial or boundary) condition. Orthogonality of the \(X_n\) gives the coefficients: these are Fourier coefficients.

Eigenvalue problems \(X''+\lambda X=0\) on \([0,L]\)

Boundary conditions Eigenvalues \(\lambda_n\) Eigenfunctions \(X_n\)
\(X(0)=0,\ X(L)=0\) \(\left(\dfrac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2,\ n=1,2,\dots\) \(\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\)
\(X'(0)=0,\ X'(L)=0\) \(\left(\dfrac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2,\ n=0,1,2,\dots\) \(\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\) (\(n=0\): constant)
\(X(0)=0,\ X'(L)=0\) \(\left(\dfrac{(2n-1)\pi}{2L}\right)^2,\ n=1,2,\dots\) \(\sin\dfrac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}\)
\(X'(0)=0,\ X(L)=0\) \(\left(\dfrac{(2n-1)\pi}{2L}\right)^2,\ n=1,2,\dots\) \(\cos\dfrac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}\)
periodic on \([-\pi,\pi]\) \(n^2,\ n=0,1,2,\dots\) \(1,\ \cos nx,\ \sin nx\)

Why only \(\lambda>0\) works for \(X(0)=X(L)=0\): for \(\lambda<0\), \(X=Ae^{\mu x}+Be^{-\mu x}\) and both conditions force \(A=B=0\); for \(\lambda=0\), \(X=A+Bx\) and again \(A=B=0\); for \(\lambda=\omega^2>0\), \(X=A\cos\omega x+B\sin\omega x\), \(X(0)=0\) gives \(A=0\) and \(X(L)=0\) gives \(\sin\omega L=0\).

Fourier coefficients (the tool for the last step)

Orthogonality on \([0,L]\): \(\displaystyle\int_0^L\sin\frac{m\pi x}{L}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx=\begin{cases}L/2&m=n\\0&m\ne n\end{cases}\), and the same for cosines (\(n\ge1\)).

  • Half-range sine series: \(f(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}b_n\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\), \(\ b_n=\dfrac2L\displaystyle\int_0^Lf(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx\).
  • Half-range cosine series: \(f(x)=\dfrac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n\ge1}a_n\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\), \(\ a_n=\dfrac2L\displaystyle\int_0^Lf(x)\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx\) (including \(n=0\)). \(\dfrac{a_0}2\) is the mean value of \(f\).
  • Full series on \((-\pi,\pi)\): \(f=\dfrac{a_0}{2}+\sum(a_n\cos n\theta+b_n\sin n\theta)\) with \(a_n=\dfrac1\pi\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f\cos n\theta\,d\theta\) and \(b_n=\dfrac1\pi\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f\sin n\theta\,d\theta\).
  • Convergence: if \(f\) is piecewise smooth, the series converges to \(f\) at points of continuity and to the average of the left and right limits at a jump.

Standard sine coefficients on \((0,L)\) (needed constantly):

\(f(x)\) \(b_n\)
\(1\) \(\dfrac{2(1-(-1)^n)}{n\pi}\), i.e. \(\dfrac{4}{n\pi}\) for odd \(n\), \(0\) for even \(n\)
\(x\) \(\dfrac{2L(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}\)
\(x(L-x)\) \(\dfrac{4L^2(1-(-1)^n)}{n^3\pi^3}\), i.e. \(\dfrac{8L^2}{n^3\pi^3}\) for odd \(n\), \(0\) for even \(n\)
triangle: \(\dfrac{2hx}{L}\) for \(x\le\dfrac L2\), \(\dfrac{2h(L-x)}{L}\) for \(x\ge\dfrac L2\) \(\dfrac{8h}{n^2\pi^2}\sin\dfrac{n\pi}{2}\)

Standard cosine coefficient: for \(f(x)=x\) on \((0,L)\), \(a_0=L\) and \(a_n=\dfrac{2L((-1)^n-1)}{n^2\pi^2}\) (so \(-\dfrac{4L}{n^2\pi^2}\) for odd \(n\), \(0\) for even \(n\ge2\)).

The heat equation \(u_t=k\,u_{xx}\), \(0<x<L\), \(k>0\)

Separation: \(\dfrac{T'}{kT}=\dfrac{X''}{X}=-\lambda\). Then \(T=e^{-\lambda kt}\): only \(\lambda>0\) gives solutions that do not blow up. Each mode decays at rate \(k\lambda_n\).

Ends at zero temperature: \(u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0,\ u(x,0)=f(x)\): \(u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\,e^{-k n^2\pi^2t/L^2}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L},\qquad b_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx.\) As \(t\to\infty\), \(u\to0\) and the \(n=1\) term dominates.

Insulated ends: \(u_x(0,t)=u_x(L,t)=0\): \(u=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n\,e^{-kn^2\pi^2t/L^2}\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L},\qquad a_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx.\) As \(t\to\infty\), \(u\to\dfrac{a_0}{2}=\dfrac1L\int_0^Lf\) (the mean initial temperature, conservation of heat).

Mixed ends \(u(0,t)=0,\ u_x(L,t)=0\): \(u=\sum b_ne^{-k\lambda_nt}\sin\dfrac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}\), \(\lambda_n=\left(\dfrac{(2n-1)\pi}{2L}\right)^2\).

Non-zero constant end temperatures \(u(0,t)=T_1,\ u(L,t)=T_2\): the BCs are not homogeneous, so subtract the steady state \(u_s(x)=T_1+\frac{T_2-T_1}{L}x,\qquad u=u_s+v,\) where \(v\) satisfies the heat equation with zero ends and initial value \(f-u_s\). As \(t\to\infty\), \(u\to u_s\).

(Infinite rod: Fourier transform in \(x\) gives \(u=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi kt}}\displaystyle\int f(\xi)e^{-(x-\xi)^2/(4kt)}d\xi\). This is not separation of variables on an interval, but it is the third standard solution of the heat equation.)

The wave equation \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\) (vibrating string)

Here \(c=\sqrt{T/\rho}\) (tension over linear density). Separation gives \(T''=-\lambda c^2T\), so \(T\) oscillates when \(\lambda>0\).

Fixed ends: \(u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0,\ u(x,0)=f(x),\ u_t(x,0)=g(x)\): \(u=\sum_{n\ge1}\Big(a_n\cos\frac{n\pi ct}{L}+b_n\sin\frac{n\pi ct}{L}\Big)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L},\) \(a_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx,\qquad b_n=\frac{2}{n\pi c}\int_0^Lg\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx.\) The \(b_n\) formula comes from \(u_t(x,0)=\sum b_n\dfrac{n\pi c}{L}\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}=g\).

  • Angular frequencies \(\omega_n=\dfrac{n\pi c}{L}\); frequencies \(\dfrac{nc}{2L}\); fundamental frequency \(\dfrac{c}{2L}\); period of the motion \(\dfrac{2L}{c}\).
  • The \(n\)th harmonic has \(n-1\) interior nodes.
  • Plucked string (triangle of height \(h\) at the midpoint, released from rest): \(g=0\), \(b_n=0\), \(u=\frac{8h}{\pi^2}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\sin(n\pi/2)}{n^2}\cos\frac{n\pi ct}{L}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\) (only odd \(n\) appear).
  • Free (Neumann) ends \(u_x=0\) at \(x=0,L\) give cosine modes instead.

d’Alembert’s solution (infinite string, \(-\infty<x<\infty\)): the general solution is \(u=F(x-ct)+G(x+ct)\) (waves moving right and left at speed \(c\)). Imposing \(u(x,0)=f,\ u_t(x,0)=g\): \(u(x,t)=\frac12\big[f(x+ct)+f(x-ct)\big]+\frac1{2c}\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}g(s)\,ds.\)

  • \(u(x,t)\) depends only on \(f\) at the two points \(x\pm ct\) and \(g\) on the interval \([x-ct,\,x+ct]\) (domain of dependence). A disturbance at \(x_0\) at \(t=0\) influences the region \(\lvert x-x_0\rvert\le ct\).
  • For a finite string with fixed ends, apply d’Alembert’s formula to the odd \(2L\)-periodic extensions of \(f\) and \(g\). A pulse reflected at a fixed end changes sign.
  • If \(g=0\), the solution is half of the initial shape travelling right plus half travelling left.

Laplace’s equation \(u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\)

Rectangle \(0<x<a,\ 0<y<b\). Take \(u(0,y)=u(a,y)=0,\ u(x,0)=0,\ u(x,b)=f(x)\). Separation \(u=X(x)Y(y)\): \(X''+\lambda X=0\) gives \(X_n=\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{a}\), \(\lambda_n=\left(\dfrac{n\pi}{a}\right)^2\), and \(Y''-\lambda Y=0\) with \(Y(0)=0\) gives \(Y_n=\sinh\dfrac{n\pi y}{a}\) (hyperbolic, not trigonometric, in the direction without homogeneous conditions on both sides): \(u(x,y)=\sum_{n\ge1}B_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}\,\frac{\sinh(n\pi y/a)}{\sinh(n\pi b/a)},\qquad B_n=\frac2a\int_0^af(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}dx.\) (Equivalently \(u=\sum A_n\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}a\sinh\dfrac{n\pi y}a\) with \(A_n\sinh\dfrac{n\pi b}{a}=B_n\).)

  • Data on the bottom side \(u(x,0)=f(x)\) instead (others zero): replace \(\sinh(n\pi y/a)/\sinh(n\pi b/a)\) by \(\sinh(n\pi(b-y)/a)/\sinh(n\pi b/a)\).
  • Data on several sides: solve one problem per side and add (linearity).
  • Semi-infinite strip \(y>0\) with \(u\) bounded as \(y\to\infty\): use \(e^{-n\pi y/a}\).
  • Neumann conditions give cosine modes.

Circle (disc) \(r<a\), \(u(a,\theta)=f(\theta)\). In polar coordinates \(u_{rr}+\dfrac1ru_r+\dfrac1{r^2}u_{\theta\theta}=0\). Put \(u=R(r)\Theta(\theta)\): \(\Theta''+\lambda\Theta=0\ (2\pi\text{-periodic})\Rightarrow\lambda=n^2,\ n=0,1,2,\dots;\qquad r^2R''+rR'-n^2R=0\Rightarrow R=r^{n},\ r^{-n}\ (n\ge1),\ \text{or}\ 1,\ \ln r\ (n=0).\) For the interior, \(u\) must be bounded at \(r=0\), so \(r^{-n}\) and \(\ln r\) are discarded: \(u(r,\theta)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n\ge1}\Big(\frac ra\Big)^n(a_n\cos n\theta+b_n\sin n\theta),\) \(a_n=\frac1\pi\int_0^{2\pi}f(\varphi)\cos n\varphi\,d\varphi,\quad b_n=\frac1\pi\int_0^{2\pi}f(\varphi)\sin n\varphi\,d\varphi.\)

  • Summing the series gives the Poisson integral \(u(r,\theta)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{a^2-r^2}{a^2-2ar\cos(\theta-\varphi)+r^2}f(\varphi)\,d\varphi\).
  • Value at the centre: \(u(0)=\dfrac{a_0}{2}=\dfrac1{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}f\,d\varphi\) (mean value property). A harmonic function attains its maximum and minimum on the boundary.
  • Exterior problem \(r>a\), \(u\) bounded at infinity: use \((a/r)^n\) instead of \((r/a)^n\) (discard \(r^n\) and \(\ln r\)).
  • Annulus \(a<r<b\): all of \(r^n,\ r^{-n},\ 1,\ \ln r\) are kept and two boundary conditions fix the constants.
  • Neumann problem on the disc (\(u_r=g\) on \(r=a\)): solvable only if \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}g\,d\theta=0\), and then \(u\) is unique up to an additive constant.

Formulas and results

  • Eigenvalues: Dirichlet-Dirichlet \(\left(\dfrac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2\) with \(\sin\); Neumann-Neumann \(\left(\dfrac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2\) with \(\cos\) (\(n\ge0\)); mixed \(\left(\dfrac{(2n-1)\pi}{2L}\right)^2\).
  • \(b_n=\dfrac2L\displaystyle\int_0^Lf\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}dx\); \(a_n=\dfrac2L\displaystyle\int_0^Lf\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}dx\).
  • Heat (Dirichlet): \(u=\sum b_ne^{-kn^2\pi^2t/L^2}\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\). Insulated: cosine series, limit \(\dfrac{a_0}2\).
  • Steady state with \(u(0)=T_1,\ u(L)=T_2\): \(T_1+(T_2-T_1)x/L\).
  • Wave: \(u=\sum(a_n\cos\omega_nt+b_n\sin\omega_nt)\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\), \(\omega_n=\dfrac{n\pi c}{L}\); \(a_n\) from \(f\), \(b_n=\dfrac{2}{n\pi c}\displaystyle\int g\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}dx\).
  • d’Alembert: \(u=\tfrac12[f(x+ct)+f(x-ct)]+\dfrac1{2c}\displaystyle\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}g\).
  • Laplace on rectangle (data on top): \(u=\sum B_n\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{a}\dfrac{\sinh(n\pi y/a)}{\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\), \(B_n=\dfrac2a\displaystyle\int f\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{a}\).
  • Laplace on disc: \(u=\dfrac{a_0}2+\sum(r/a)^n(a_n\cos n\theta+b_n\sin n\theta)\), coefficients with \(\dfrac1\pi\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\); centre value \(=\dfrac{a_0}2\).
  • Standard \(b_n\): \(f=1\): \(\dfrac{4}{n\pi}\) (odd \(n\)); \(f=x\): \(\dfrac{2L(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}\); \(f=x(L-x)\): \(\dfrac{8L^2}{n^3\pi^3}\) (odd \(n\)).

Worked examples

Example 1: heat equation with a parabolic initial profile

Solve \(u_t=u_{xx}\), \(0<x<\pi\), \(u(0,t)=u(\pi,t)=0\), \(u(x,0)=x(\pi-x)\).

Here \(L=\pi,\ k=1\), so \(u=\sum b_ne^{-n^2t}\sin nx\) with \(b_n=\dfrac2\pi\displaystyle\int_0^\pi x(\pi-x)\sin nx\,dx=\dfrac{4(1-(-1)^n)}{\pi n^3}\). Even \(n\): \(0\); odd \(n\): \(\dfrac{8}{\pi n^3}\). \(u=\frac8\pi\sum_{n\ \mathrm{odd}}\frac{e^{-n^2t}}{n^3}\sin nx.\) Check at \(t=0,\ x=\pi/2\): \(\dfrac8\pi\Big(1-\dfrac1{27}+\dfrac1{125}-\cdots\Big)=\dfrac8\pi\cdot\dfrac{\pi^3}{32}=\dfrac{\pi^2}{4}=x(\pi-x)\).

Example 2: insulated bar

Solve \(u_t=ku_{xx}\), \(u_x(0,t)=u_x(L,t)=0\), \(u(x,0)=x\).

Cosine series: \(a_0=\dfrac2L\displaystyle\int_0^Lx\,dx=L\), \(a_n=\dfrac{2L((-1)^n-1)}{n^2\pi^2}\). \(u=\frac L2-\frac{4L}{\pi^2}\sum_{n\ \mathrm{odd}}\frac1{n^2}e^{-kn^2\pi^2t/L^2}\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}.\) As \(t\to\infty\), \(u\to\dfrac L2\), the mean of the initial temperature.

Example 3: plucked string

A string of length \(L\) is pulled to height \(h\) at the midpoint (triangular shape) and released from rest. Find \(u\).

\(g=0\) so \(b_n=0\). \(a_n=\dfrac{8h}{n^2\pi^2}\sin\dfrac{n\pi}2\): \(a_1=\dfrac{8h}{\pi^2},\ a_2=0,\ a_3=-\dfrac{8h}{9\pi^2},\dots\) \(u=\frac{8h}{\pi^2}\Big[\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}\cos\frac{\pi ct}{L}-\frac19\sin\frac{3\pi x}L\cos\frac{3\pi ct}L+\cdots\Big].\) Check at \(t=0,\ x=L/2\): \(\dfrac{8h}{\pi^2}\Big(1+\dfrac19+\dfrac1{25}+\cdots\Big)=\dfrac{8h}{\pi^2}\cdot\dfrac{\pi^2}{8}=h\).

Example 4: d’Alembert

Solve \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\) on the line with \(u(x,0)=x^2,\ u_t(x,0)=2x\).

\(\dfrac12[(x+ct)^2+(x-ct)^2]=x^2+c^2t^2\), and \(\dfrac1{2c}\displaystyle\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}2s\,ds=\dfrac1{2c}\cdot4xct=2xt\). \(u=x^2+c^2t^2+2xt.\) Check: \(u_{tt}=2c^2=c^2u_{xx}\); \(u(x,0)=x^2\); \(u_t(x,0)=2x\).

Example 5: Laplace on a rectangle

Solve \(u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\) on \(0<x<a,\ 0<y<b\) with \(u=0\) on \(x=0\), \(x=a\), \(y=0\) and \(u(x,b)=1\).

\(B_n=\dfrac2a\displaystyle\int_0^a\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{a}dx=\dfrac{4}{n\pi}\) for odd \(n\) and \(0\) for even \(n\). \(u=\frac4\pi\sum_{n\ \mathrm{odd}}\frac1n\sin\frac{n\pi x}a\,\frac{\sinh(n\pi y/a)}{\sinh(n\pi b/a)}.\) At \(y=b\) this is the Fourier series of the constant \(1\) on \((0,a)\).

Example 6: Laplace on a disc

A disc \(r<a\) has \(u=T_0\) on the upper semicircle (\(0<\theta<\pi\)) and \(u=0\) on the lower. Find \(u\) and the centre value.

\(a_0=\dfrac1\pi\displaystyle\int_0^\pi T_0\,d\varphi=T_0\), \(a_n=0\) (\(n\ge1\)), \(b_n=\dfrac{T_0}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\sin n\varphi\,d\varphi=\dfrac{T_0(1-(-1)^n)}{n\pi}\). \(u=\frac{T_0}2+\frac{2T_0}\pi\sum_{n\ \mathrm{odd}}\frac1n\Big(\frac ra\Big)^n\sin n\theta,\qquad u(0)=\frac{T_0}{2}.\)

Common traps

  • Sign of the separation constant. For the heat equation with Dirichlet conditions use \(-\lambda\) (\(\lambda>0\)) so the time factor is \(e^{-\lambda kt}\). A positive constant gives growth and is excluded by the boundary conditions.
  • Heat equation: only one initial condition (\(u(x,0)\)); wave equation: two (\(u\) and \(u_t\)). Forgetting \(b_n\) in the wave equation when \(g\ne0\).
  • The \(b_n\) in the wave equation carries \(\dfrac{2}{n\pi c}\), not \(\dfrac2L\); the factor \(\dfrac{n\pi c}{L}\) from differentiating \(\sin\omega_nt\) must be divided out.
  • Sine series for Dirichlet ends, cosine series for Neumann ends. Using the wrong one is a common distractor.
  • The constant term of a cosine series is \(\dfrac{a_0}2\) where \(a_0=\dfrac2L\displaystyle\int_0^Lf\), so it equals the mean value \(\dfrac1L\displaystyle\int_0^Lf\). Using \(a_0\) itself as the constant term doubles it.
  • In the rectangle, \(\sinh\) (or \(\cosh\)) appears in the direction where the conditions are not both homogeneous. Writing \(\sin\dfrac{n\pi y}{a}\) is wrong. The normalising factor is \(\sinh(n\pi b/a)\), not \(\cosh\).
  • Non-homogeneous boundary values (such as \(u(0,t)=T_1\)) are not separable directly. Subtract the steady state first.
  • Disc: \(r^n\) (not \(r^{-n}\)) inside, \((r/a)^n\) in the series, and the Fourier coefficients use \(\dfrac1\pi\), not \(\dfrac2\pi\), over \((0,2\pi)\).
  • A bounded solution on a disc excludes \(\ln r\) and \(r^{-n}\) because they are infinite at the centre.
  • Frequency versus angular frequency of a string: \(\omega_n=\dfrac{n\pi c}{L}\) but frequency (cycles per unit time) \(=\dfrac{nc}{2L}\).
  • d’Alembert’s integral has factor \(\dfrac1{2c}\) and limits \(x\pm ct\); forgetting the \(\dfrac12\) in front of \(f\) is a classic error.
  • Fourier series at a jump converges to the mean of the two one-sided limits, not to either value.

Quick revision

  • \(u=X(x)T(t)\), both sides equal \(-\lambda\); boundary conditions give eigenvalues, then superpose.
  • Dirichlet \(X(0)=X(L)=0\): \(\lambda_n=(n\pi/L)^2\), \(X=\sin\). Neumann: \(\cos\), \(n\ge0\).
  • \(b_n=\dfrac2L\displaystyle\int_0^Lf\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}L\,dx\).
  • Heat: modes decay as \(e^{-kn^2\pi^2t/L^2}\); insulated ends tend to the mean value \(\dfrac{a_0}2\).
  • Non-zero end temperatures: subtract the linear steady state first.
  • Wave: \(\omega_n=\dfrac{n\pi c}{L}\); \(a_n\) from \(f\), \(b_n=\dfrac2{n\pi c}\displaystyle\int g\sin\); fundamental frequency \(\dfrac{c}{2L}\).
  • d’Alembert: \(u=\tfrac12[f(x+ct)+f(x-ct)]+\dfrac1{2c}\displaystyle\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}g\).
  • Rectangle Laplace: \(\sin(n\pi x/a)\,\sinh(n\pi y/a)/\sinh(n\pi b/a)\), coefficient \(\dfrac2a\displaystyle\int f\sin\).
  • Disc Laplace: \(\dfrac{a_0}2+\sum(r/a)^n(a_n\cos n\theta+b_n\sin n\theta)\), coefficients \(\dfrac1\pi\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\); centre value \(=\dfrac{a_0}2\).
  • Disc: discard \(r^{-n}\) and \(\ln r\) (unbounded at \(0\)). Exterior: \((a/r)^n\).
  • Standard \(b_n\): \(1\to4/(n\pi)\) (odd), \(x\to2L(-1)^{n+1}/(n\pi)\), \(x(L-x)\to8L^2/(n^3\pi^3)\) (odd).
