Why it matters for the exam

This is the most asked PDE topic: six of the seven PDE questions in the 2025 and 2026 papers come from here (a particular integral, the general solution of Laplace’s equation, a classification match, a canonical-form method, a classification of a variable-coefficient PDE, and a complementary function). Each is a one-line recall if you know the rules: CF rules, PI rules and the sign of \(B^2-AC\).

Core ideas

Notation: \(D=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x},\ D'=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}\). A linear PDE with constant coefficients is \(F(D,D')\,z=f(x,y).\) It is homogeneous if every term of \(F\) has the same total degree in \(D,D'\) (for example \(D^2-3DD'+2D'^2\)). Otherwise it is non-homogeneous. The general solution is \(z=\text{CF}+\text{PI},\) where the complementary function (CF) solves \(F(D,D')z=0\) and carries as many arbitrary functions as the order of \(F\).

Complementary function: homogeneous case

Factorise \(F(D,D')=\prod (D-m_iD')\) (real or complex \(m_i\); also written as \(a_iD+b_iD'\)).

Factor Solution of that factor
\((D-mD')z=0\) \(z=\phi(y+mx)\)
\((aD+bD')z=0\) \(z=\phi(bx-ay)\)
\((D'-nD)z=0\) \(z=\phi(x+ny)\)

Reason: \((D-mD')z=0\) means \(z_x=mz_y\); the characteristic equations \(\dfrac{dx}{1}=\dfrac{dy}{-m}\) give \(y+mx=\text{const}\).

  • Distinct roots \(m_1,\dots,m_n\): \(\text{CF}=\phi_1(y+m_1x)+\dots+\phi_n(y+m_nx)\).
  • Repeated root \((D-mD')^k\): \(\text{CF}=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}x^r\phi_r(y+mx)\). For \(k=2\): \(\phi_1(y+mx)+x\phi_2(y+mx)\).
  • Example: \(D^2-5DD'+6D'^2=(D-2D')(D-3D')\) gives \(\phi_1(y+2x)+\phi_2(y+3x)\).
  • Example: \((D-D')^2z=0\) gives \(\phi_1(y+x)+x\phi_2(y+x)\).
  • Example: \(D^2-D'^2=(D-D')(D+D')\) gives \(\phi_1(y+x)+\phi_2(y-x)\).
  • Complex roots: \(D^2+D'^2=(D+iD')(D-iD')\) gives \(\phi_1(y+ix)+\phi_2(y-ix)\) (Laplace’s equation: solutions are built from functions of \(y\pm ix\)).

Complementary function: non-homogeneous but factorisable

If \(F\) splits into linear factors, \(F=\prod (D-m_iD'-c_i)\):

Factor Solution of that factor
\((D-mD'-c)z=0\) \(z=e^{cx}\phi(y+mx)\)
\((D'-nD-c)z=0\) \(z=e^{cy}\phi(x+ny)\)

Repeated factor \((D-mD'-c)^k\): \(z=e^{cx}\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}x^r\phi_r(y+mx)\).

Example: \(D^2-D'^2+D+3D'-2=(D+D'-1)(D-D'+2)\). First factor: \(m=-1,\ c=1\); second: \(m=1,\ c=-2\). So \(\text{CF}=e^{x}\phi_1(y-x)+e^{-2x}\phi_2(y+x).\) Check \((D-D'-1)^2z=0\): \(z=e^x[\phi_1(y+x)+x\phi_2(y+x)]\).

Complementary function: non-factorisable operators

If \(F(D,D')\) does not split into linear factors (for example \(D^2-D'\), the heat operator), try \(z=Ae^{hx+ky}\). Then \(F(h,k)=0\) is the condition. Solve \(k=k(h)\) (or \(h=h(k)\)) and superpose: \(\text{CF}=\sum_i A_ie^{h_ix+k(h_i)y}.\) Example: \(D^2-D'\): \(h^2-k=0\), so \(\text{CF}=\sum A_ie^{h_ix+h_i^2y}\) (the \(h_i,A_i\) arbitrary constants). For \(D-D'^2\): \(\sum A_ie^{k_i^2x+k_iy}\).

Particular integral (PI): rules for \(\dfrac{1}{F(D,D')}f\)

1. \(f=e^{ax+by}\).

  • If \(F(a,b)\ne0\): \(\text{PI}=\dfrac{e^{ax+by}}{F(a,b)}\).
  • If \(F(a,b)=0\) (failure case): \(F\) has the factor \((D-mD')\) with \(m=a/b\), i.e. \(F=(D-mD')^rG\) with \(G(a,b)\ne0\). Then \(\text{PI}=\frac{x^r}{r!\,G(a,b)}\,e^{ax+by}.\) (Equivalently, with \(F=(D'-nD)^rG\), \(n=b/a\): \(\text{PI}=\dfrac{y^r}{r!\,G(a,b)}e^{ax+by}\).) Why: \((D-mD')(xe^{ax+by})=e^{ax+by}+x(a-mb)e^{ax+by}=e^{ax+by}\) when \(a=mb\).
  • Non-homogeneous failure case: multiply by \(x\) (or \(y\)) and fix by trial, or use \(\dfrac{1}{F}e^{ax+by}V=e^{ax+by}\dfrac{1}{F(D+a,D'+b)}V\).

2. \(f=\sin(ax+by)\) or \(\cos(ax+by)\).

If \(F\) contains only \(D^2,DD',D'^2\), replace \(D^2\to-a^2,\ DD'\to-ab,\ D'^2\to-b^2\): \(\text{PI}=\frac{\sin(ax+by)}{F(-a^2,-ab,-b^2)}\quad\text{provided the denominator}\ne0.\) If \(F(-a^2,-ab,-b^2)=0\), write \(\sin=\operatorname{Im}e^{i(ax+by)}\) (or \(\cos=\operatorname{Re}\)) and use the failure rule for the exponential. If \(F\) contains odd powers (for example \(D+D'\)), use the complex exponential in general.

3. \(f=x^my^n\) (polynomial).

Write \(F=D^n\varphi(D'/D)\) if \(F\) is homogeneous and expand \(1/\varphi\) as a series in \(D'/D\) (then \(D^{-1}\) means integrate in \(x\)). Or expand in \(D/D'\) (integrate in \(y\)). If \(F(0,0)\ne0\) (non-homogeneous with a constant term) expand \(F^{-1}\) in a binomial series in \(D,D'\) directly.

  • Example (constant term): \((D+D'+1)z=x\): \(\dfrac1{1+(D+D')}x=(1-(D+D')+\dots)x=x-1\).
  • The two expansions (in \(D'/D\) and in \(D/D'\)) can give different particular integrals; they differ by a CF. Both are correct PIs. Trial polynomials also work (see 2025-Q55 below).

4. \(f=\phi(ax+by)\), \(F\) homogeneous of degree \(n\), \(F(a,b)\ne0\). \(\text{PI}=\frac{1}{F(a,b)}\underbrace{\iint\!\cdots\!\int}_{n}\phi(v)\,dv\Big|_{v=ax+by}.\)

5. \(f=e^{ax+by}V(x,y)\). \(\dfrac{1}{F(D,D')}e^{ax+by}V=e^{ax+by}\dfrac{1}{F(D+a,D'+b)}V\).

Classification of second-order PDEs

For \(A\,u_{xx}+2B\,u_{xy}+C\,u_{yy}+(\text{lower order terms})=0\) the type is decided by the discriminant \(B^2-AC\) (with the factor 2 on the mixed term):

\(B^2-AC\) Type Model
\(>0\) Hyperbolic wave equation \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\)
\(=0\) Parabolic heat equation \(u_t=ku_{xx}\)
\(<0\) Elliptic Laplace equation \(u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\)
  • If the equation is written \(Au_{xx}+Bu_{xy}+Cu_{yy}\) (no 2), the test is \(B^2-4AC\). This convention clash is the commonest source of error.
  • Lower-order terms never affect the type.
  • With variable coefficients the type is a property of the point \((x,y)\). The sign of \(B^2-AC\) then splits the plane into regions; an equation that changes type is called mixed.
  • Constants: \(u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\) has \(A=C=1,\ B=0\), so \(B^2-AC=-1\): elliptic.

Region examples:

PDE \(A,\ B,\ C\) \(B^2-AC\) Regions
\(y\,u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\) (Tricomi) \(y,\ 0,\ 1\) \(-y\) elliptic for \(y>0\), parabolic on \(y=0\), hyperbolic for \(y<0\)
\(x\,u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\) \(x,\ 0,\ 1\) \(-x\) elliptic for \(x>0\), hyperbolic for \(x<0\)
\(u_{xx}+2x\,u_{xy}+(1-y^2)u_{yy}=0\) \(1,\ x,\ 1-y^2\) \(x^2+y^2-1\) hyperbolic outside the unit circle, elliptic inside, parabolic on it
\(x^2u_{xx}+2xy\,u_{xy}+y^2u_{yy}=0\) \(x^2,\ xy,\ y^2\) \(x^2y^2-x^2y^2=0\) parabolic everywhere (the coefficients all vanish at the origin)

Characteristics and canonical forms

The characteristic equation is \(A\,(dy)^2-2B\,dx\,dy+C\,(dx)^2=0\iff\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{B\pm\sqrt{B^2-AC}}{A}\quad(A\ne0).\)

Type Characteristics Change of variables Canonical form
Hyperbolic two real families \(\varphi_1=c_1,\ \varphi_2=c_2\) \(\xi=\varphi_1,\ \eta=\varphi_2\) \(u_{\xi\eta}=\Phi\) (or \(u_{\alpha\alpha}-u_{\beta\beta}=\Phi\) with \(\xi=\alpha+\beta,\ \eta=\alpha-\beta\))
Parabolic one real family \(\varphi=c\) \(\xi=\varphi\), \(\eta\) any function independent of \(\xi\) \(u_{\eta\eta}=\Phi\)
Elliptic complex pair \(\varphi\pm i\psi=c\) \(\xi=\varphi,\ \eta=\psi\) (real and imaginary parts) \(u_{\xi\xi}+u_{\eta\eta}=\Phi\)

\(\Phi\) collects lower-order terms. The transformation is a real change of variables with non-zero Jacobian and does not change the type.

Wave equation. \(u_{tt}-c^2u_{xx}=0\): \(A=-c^2\) (for \(u_{xx}\)), \(C=1\) (for \(u_{tt}\)), \(B=0\). Characteristics \(x\pm ct=\text{const}\). With \(\xi=x-ct,\ \eta=x+ct\), the equation becomes \(u_{\xi\eta}=0\), so \(u=f(x-ct)+g(x+ct)\) (d’Alembert). The method that reduces it to canonical form is the use of characteristic variables.

Examples:

  • Hyperbolic: \(u_{xx}+2u_{xy}-3u_{yy}=0\). \(A=1,B=1,C=-3\), \(B^2-AC=4\). \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=1\pm2\): \(y-3x=c_1\) and \(y+x=c_2\). With \(\xi=y-3x,\ \eta=y+x\): \(-16u_{\xi\eta}=0\), so \(u=f(y-3x)+g(y+x)\).
  • Parabolic: \(u_{xx}-4u_{xy}+4u_{yy}=0\). \(B=-2\), \(B^2-AC=0\). \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-2\): \(\xi=y+2x\), take \(\eta=x\). Then \(u_{\eta\eta}=0\), so \(u=\eta f(\xi)+g(\xi)=x\,f(y+2x)+g(y+2x)\).
  • Elliptic: \(u_{xx}+x^2u_{yy}=0\) (\(x\ne0\)). \(A=1,B=0,C=x^2\); \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\pm ix\) so \(y\mp\tfrac i2x^2=c\). Take \(\xi=y,\ \eta=\tfrac12x^2\). Then \(u_{xx}=u_\eta+x^2u_{\eta\eta}\) and \(u_{yy}=u_{\xi\xi}\), giving \(u_{\xi\xi}+u_{\eta\eta}+\frac{1}{2\eta}u_\eta=0.\)
  • Hyperbolic, variable coefficients: \(y^2u_{xx}-x^2u_{yy}=0\) (\(xy\ne0\)). \(y\,dy=\pm x\,dx\) gives \(y^2-x^2=c_1\) and \(y^2+x^2=c_2\). With \(\xi=y^2-x^2,\ \eta=y^2+x^2\): \(u_{\xi\eta}=\frac{\xi\,u_\eta-\eta\,u_\xi}{2(\eta^2-\xi^2)}.\)

Formulas and results

  • \(D=\partial_x,\ D'=\partial_y\); \(z=\text{CF}+\text{PI}\).
  • \((D-mD')z=0\Rightarrow z=\phi(y+mx)\); \((aD+bD')z=0\Rightarrow\phi(bx-ay)\).
  • Repeated \((D-mD')^k\): \(\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}x^r\phi_r(y+mx)\).
  • \((D-mD'-c)z=0\Rightarrow z=e^{cx}\phi(y+mx)\); repeated: \(e^{cx}\sum x^r\phi_r(y+mx)\).
  • Non-factorisable: \(z=\sum Ae^{hx+ky}\) with \(F(h,k)=0\).
  • \(\dfrac{1}{F}e^{ax+by}=\dfrac{e^{ax+by}}{F(a,b)}\) if \(F(a,b)\ne0\); if \(F=(D-mD')^rG\) with \(a=mb\): \(\dfrac{x^re^{ax+by}}{r!\,G(a,b)}\).
  • \(\dfrac1F\sin(ax+by)=\dfrac{\sin(ax+by)}{F(-a^2,-ab,-b^2)}\) (same for cos), denominator non-zero.
  • \(\dfrac1F\,e^{ax+by}V=e^{ax+by}\dfrac{1}{F(D+a,D'+b)}V\).
  • \(Au_{xx}+2Bu_{xy}+Cu_{yy}\): \(B^2-AC>0,\ =0,\ <0\) is hyperbolic, parabolic, elliptic. For \(Au_{xx}+Bu_{xy}+Cu_{yy}\) use \(B^2-4AC\).
  • Characteristic equation: \(A\,dy^2-2B\,dx\,dy+C\,dx^2=0\).
  • Canonical forms: \(u_{\xi\eta}=\Phi\); \(u_{\eta\eta}=\Phi\); \(u_{\xi\xi}+u_{\eta\eta}=\Phi\).
  • Wave equation: \(\xi=x-ct,\ \eta=x+ct\); \(u=f(x-ct)+g(x+ct)\).

Worked examples

Example 1: CF and PI with an exponential

Solve \((D^2-5DD'+6D'^2)z=e^{x+y}\).

CF: \((D-2D')(D-3D')\), so \(\phi_1(y+2x)+\phi_2(y+3x)\). PI: \(F(1,1)=1-5+6=2\ne0\), so \(\text{PI}=\tfrac12e^{x+y}\). \(z=\phi_1(y+2x)+\phi_2(y+3x)+\tfrac12e^{x+y}.\)

Example 2: failure case with a repeated factor

Solve \((D-D')^2z=e^{x+y}\).

CF: \(\phi_1(y+x)+x\phi_2(y+x)\). PI: \(a=b=1\), \(F(1,1)=0\). \(F=(D-D')^2\cdot1\), so \(r=2,\ G=1,\ m=1\): \(\text{PI}=\dfrac{x^2}{2}e^{x+y}\). Check: \((D-D')(\tfrac{x^2}{2}e^{x+y})=xe^{x+y}\) and \((D-D')(xe^{x+y})=e^{x+y}\).

Example 3: non-factorable operator with a failure

Solve \((D^2-D')z=e^{x+y}\).

CF: \(e^{hx+ky}\) with \(h^2=k\): \(\sum A_ie^{h_ix+h_i^2y}\). PI: \(F(1,1)=1-1=0\). Try \(z=cxe^{x+y}\): \(z_{xx}=c(x+2)e^{x+y}\) and \(z_y=cxe^{x+y}\), so \(z_{xx}-z_y=2ce^{x+y}\). Hence \(c=\tfrac12\). \(z=\sum A_ie^{h_ix+h_i^2y}+\tfrac x2e^{x+y}.\)

Example 4: trigonometric failure

Solve \(z_{xx}-z_{yy}=\sin(x+y)\).

CF: \((D-D')(D+D')\), so \(\phi_1(y+x)+\phi_2(y-x)\). PI: \(-a^2+b^2=0\) for \(a=b=1\), so use \(\operatorname{Im}\dfrac{e^{i(x+y)}}{(D-D')(D+D')}\). The factor \((D-D')\) fails (\(a=mb\), \(m=1\)) and \(G=D+D'\to i+i=2i\). So \(\dfrac{x\,e^{i(x+y)}}{2i}=\dfrac{x}{2}(\sin\theta-i\cos\theta)\) with \(\theta=x+y\); imaginary part \(-\tfrac x2\cos(x+y)\). \(z=\phi_1(y+x)+\phi_2(y-x)-\tfrac x2\cos(x+y).\) Check: \(z_{xx}=\sin\theta+\tfrac x2\cos\theta\) and \(z_{yy}=\tfrac x2\cos\theta\).

Example 5: polynomial right-hand side

Solve \(z_{xx}-3z_{xy}+2z_{yy}=x+y\).

CF: \((D-D')(D-2D')\), so \(\phi_1(y+x)+\phi_2(y+2x)\). PI: \(\dfrac{1}{D^2(1-3t+2t^2)}\) with \(t=D'/D\); \(\dfrac1{1-3t+2t^2}=1+3t+7t^2+\dots\) Acting on \(x+y\): \(D'(x+y)=1,\ D'^2(x+y)=0\). So \(\text{PI}=D^{-2}(x+y)+3D^{-3}(1)=\Big(\frac{x^3}{6}+\frac{x^2y}{2}\Big)+\frac{x^3}{2}=\frac{2x^3}{3}+\frac{x^2y}{2}.\) Check: \(z_{xx}=4x+y,\ z_{xy}=x,\ z_{yy}=0\), so \(4x+y-3x=x+y\).

Example 6: classification and canonical form with variable coefficients

(a) Where is \(u_{xx}+2x\,u_{xy}+(1-y^2)u_{yy}=0\) hyperbolic? \(B^2-AC=x^2-(1-y^2)=x^2+y^2-1\). It is hyperbolic when \(x^2+y^2>1\) (outside the unit circle).

(b) Reduce \(u_{xx}-4u_{xy}+4u_{yy}=0\) and solve it. \(B=-2,\ B^2-AC=4-4=0\), parabolic. Characteristic \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-2\), so \(\xi=y+2x\). Take \(\eta=x\). Then \(u_x=2u_\xi+u_\eta,\ u_y=u_\xi\), and \(u_{xx}-4u_{xy}+4u_{yy}=(4-8+4)u_{\xi\xi}+(4-4)u_{\xi\eta}+u_{\eta\eta}=u_{\eta\eta}\). So \(u_{\eta\eta}=0\) and \(u=x\,f(y+2x)+g(y+2x)\).

Common traps

  • Sign in the CF argument: \((D-mD')z=0\) gives \(\phi(y+mx)\), not \(\phi(y-mx)\). Check by substituting \(z=\phi(y+mx)\): \(z_x=m\phi'=mz_y\).
  • The factor \((D-mD')\) with \(m=2\) gives \(y+2x\); students write \(x+2y\).
  • Repeated roots: the second solution is \(x\phi_2(y+mx)\) (multiply by \(x\)), not \(y\phi_2\).
  • PI by “put \(D=a,\ D'=b\)” is valid only when \(F(a,b)\ne0\). If \(F(a,b)=0\) the answer carries a factor \(x^r\) (or \(y^r\)).
  • Trig PI: substitute \(D^2\to-a^2\) and \(DD'\to-ab\), not \(D\to a\).
  • Classification convention: \(2B\) versus \(B\). \(3u_{xx}+4u_{xy}+2u_{yy}\) has \(B_{\text{full}}=4\) so \(B_{\text{full}}^2-4AC=16-24<0\): elliptic. Using \(4^2-3\cdot2>0\) gives the wrong answer hyperbolic.
  • The type depends only on \(A,B,C\), not on lower-order terms.
  • “Parabolic” requires \(B^2-AC=0\) at the point, not that the equation looks like a heat equation.
  • A variable-coefficient equation can have different types in different regions. Do not state a single type unless the sign of \(B^2-AC\) is constant.
  • Different PI expansions (in \(D'/D\) or \(D/D'\)) can look different but both are valid; they differ by a CF.
  • Canonical form of the wave equation uses characteristic variables \(x\pm ct\), not a Fourier or Laplace transform and not polar coordinates.
  • For \(F(D,D')=D^2-D'\) (not factorisable over real linear factors), writing \(\phi(y\pm x)\) is wrong. The CF is a sum of exponentials.

Previous-year questions

2025 Q55

Question. A particular integral of \(\dfrac{\partial^2z}{\partial x^2}-\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial y}=2y-x^2\) is: (A) \(xy^2\) (B) \(x^2y\) (C) \(2x^2y\) (D) \(-x^2y\).

Answer. B (official key).

Solution. Try \(z=x^2y\): \(z_{xx}=2y\), \(z_y=x^2\), so \(z_{xx}-z_y=2y-x^2\). Others: \(xy^2\) gives \(-2xy\); \(2x^2y\) gives \(4y-2x^2\); \(-x^2y\) gives the negative of the right-hand side. Series check: \(\dfrac1{D^2-D'}(2y-x^2)=D^{-2}\big(1+\tfrac{D'}{D^2}\big)(2y-x^2)=x^2y-\tfrac{x^4}{12}+2\cdot\tfrac{x^4}{24}=x^2y\).

2025 Q58

Question. The general solution of \(\dfrac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}+\dfrac{\partial^2u}{\partial y^2}=0\) is of the form: (A) \(u=f(x+y)+g(x-y)\) (B) \(u=f(x+iy)+g(x-iy)\) (C) \(u=c\,f(x+iy)\) (D) a form with a single arbitrary function.

Answer. B (official key).

Solution. Write the equation as \((D^2+D'^2)u=(D+iD')(D-iD')u=0\). Each factor gives \(u=\phi(y+mx)\) with \(m=\pm i\) (here \(m=-i\) gives \(\phi(y-ix)\), i.e. a function of \(x+iy\) up to the constant \(i\); likewise \(m=i\) gives a function of \(x-iy\)). So \(u=f(x+iy)+g(x-iy)\). Option A is the wave-equation form \(u_{xx}-u_{yy}=0\); options with one function or a constant multiple are not general (two arbitrary functions for a second-order equation).

2025 Q59

Question. Match List I with List II: (a) \(u_t=c^2u_{xx}\) (b) \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\) (c) \(u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\) (d) \(yu_x-xu_y=0\) with (I) elliptic (II) parabolic (III) hyperbolic (IV) \(u=f(x^2+y^2)\).

Answer. D: a-II, b-III, c-I, d-IV (official key).

Solution. (a) heat equation: only \(u_{xx}\) appears among the second derivatives, so \(B^2-AC=0\): parabolic. (b) wave equation: hyperbolic. (c) Laplace: elliptic. (d) is first order: \(\dfrac{dx}{y}=\dfrac{dy}{-x}\) gives \(x^2+y^2=c\), so \(u=f(x^2+y^2)\).

2026 Q51

Question. The PDE \(u_{tt}-c^2u_{xx}=0\) is transformed into canonical form using: (A) polar transformation (B) characteristic variables (C) Laplace transform (D) Fourier series.

Answer. B (worked key, unofficial).

Solution. The equation is hyperbolic with characteristics \(x\pm ct=\text{const}\). The substitution \(\xi=x-ct,\ \eta=x+ct\) gives \(u_{\xi\eta}=0\). Laplace transform and Fourier series are solution techniques, not changes of variables to a canonical form.

2026 Q74

Question. Classify the PDE \(x^2u_{xx}+2xy\,u_{xy}+y^2u_{yy}=0\) (as read from the photographed paper): (A) elliptic (B) hyperbolic (C) parabolic (D) mixed.

Answer. C (worked key, unofficial).

Solution. \(A=x^2,\ B=xy,\ C=y^2\), so \(B^2-AC=x^2y^2-x^2y^2=0\) at every point: parabolic.

2026 Q79

Question. For the PDE \((D^2-4D+4)z=0\), the complementary function involves: (A) \(e^{2x}\) only (B) \(xe^{2x}\) only (C) \((A+Bx)e^{2x}\) (D) \(e^{-2x}\).

Answer. C (worked key, unofficial).

Solution. \(D^2-4D+4=(D-2)^2\), a repeated factor with \(m=0,\ c=2\): \(z=e^{2x}[\phi_1(y)+x\phi_2(y)]\). In the exam options, \(A,B\) stand for the arbitrary functions/constants of \(y\). A single term \(e^{2x}\) or \(xe^{2x}\) is not the full CF, and \(e^{-2x}\) has the wrong sign.

Quick revision

  • \((D-mD')z=0\Rightarrow z=\phi(y+mx)\). Repeated root: add \(x\phi_2(y+mx)\).
  • \((D-mD'-c)z=0\Rightarrow z=e^{cx}\phi(y+mx)\).
  • Non-factorable \(F\): try \(e^{hx+ky}\) with \(F(h,k)=0\); \(D^2-D'\) gives \(e^{hx+h^2y}\).
  • PI of \(e^{ax+by}\): divide by \(F(a,b)\); if \(F(a,b)=0\), multiply by \(x^r/r!\) and divide by \(G(a,b)\).
  • PI of \(\sin(ax+by)\): put \(D^2\to-a^2,\ DD'\to-ab,\ D'^2\to-b^2\); if zero, use complex exponential.
  • PI of a polynomial: expand \(F^{-1}\) in \(D'/D\) (or \(D/D'\)); trial polynomial also works.
  • \(Au_{xx}+2Bu_{xy}+Cu_{yy}\): \(B^2-AC>0\) hyperbolic, \(=0\) parabolic, \(<0\) elliptic.
  • \(Au_{xx}+Bu_{xy}+Cu_{yy}\): use \(B^2-4AC\).
  • Characteristic equation \(A\,dy^2-2B\,dx\,dy+C\,dx^2=0\); canonical forms \(u_{\xi\eta}\), \(u_{\eta\eta}\), \(u_{\xi\xi}+u_{\eta\eta}\).
  • Wave equation: \(\xi=x-ct,\ \eta=x+ct\), \(u=f(x-ct)+g(x+ct)\).
  • Tricomi \(yu_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\): elliptic for \(y>0\), hyperbolic for \(y<0\), parabolic on \(y=0\).
