  1. Question 1Easy
    Eliminating the constants \(a,b\) from \(z=ax+by+ab\) gives the PDE

    Why

    \(p=a,\ q=b\), so \(z=px+qy+pq\). This is a Clairaut equation.
  2. Question 2Easy
    Eliminating the arbitrary function \(f\) from \(z=f(x^2+y^2)\) gives

    Why

    \(p=2xf',\ q=2yf'\), so \(p/q=x/y\), i.e. \(yp-xq=0\).
  3. Question 3Easy
    The PDE obtained by eliminating \(a,b\) from \(z=ax^2+by^2\) is

    Why

    \(p=2ax,\ q=2by\), so \(xp=2ax^2\) and \(yq=2by^2\). Adding, \(xp+yq=2z\).
  4. Question 4Easy
    The general integral of \(xp+yq=z\) is

    Why

    \(\dfrac{dx}{x}=\dfrac{dy}{y}=\dfrac{dz}{z}\) gives \(y/x=c_1,\ z/x=c_2\). Both are constant along \((x,y,z)\)-rays; none of the other pairs is.
  5. Question 5Medium
    The general integral of \((y-z)p+(z-x)q=x-y\) is

    Why

    Multipliers \((1,1,1)\): \(dx+dy+dz=0\), so \(x+y+z=c_1\). Multipliers \((x,y,z)\): \(x(y-z)+y(z-x)+z(x-y)=0\), so \(x\,dx+y\,dy+z\,dz=0\) and \(x^2+y^2+z^2=c_2\). (\(xyz\) belongs to a different equation.)
  6. Question 6Medium
    The singular integral of \(z=px+qy+pq\) is

    Why

    Complete integral \(z=ax+by+ab\). \(\partial_a\): \(x+b=0\); \(\partial_b\): \(y+a=0\). So \(a=-y,\ b=-x\) and \(z=-xy-xy+xy=-xy\). Option C is the singular integral of \(z=px+qy+p^2+q^2\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    A complete integral of \(p+q=pq\) is

    Why

    \(f(p,q)=0\) form: \(z=ax+by+c\) with \(a+b=ab\), so \(b(a-1)=a\) and \(b=\dfrac{a}{a-1}\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    A complete integral of \(p-q=x-y\) is

    Why

    Separate variables: \(p-x=q-y=a\). Then \(p=x+a,\ q=y+a\) and \(dz=p\,dx+q\,dy\) integrates to \(\tfrac{x^2}{2}+\tfrac{y^2}{2}+a(x+y)+b\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    Using \(u=x+ay\) for the equation \(pq=z\), a complete integral is

    Why

    With \(z=z(u)\): \(p=z',\ q=az'\), so \(a z'^2=z\), \(z'=\sqrt{z/a}\). Then \(\dfrac{dz}{\sqrt z}=\dfrac{du}{\sqrt a}\) gives \(2\sqrt z=\dfrac{u+b}{\sqrt a}\), i.e. \(4az=(x+ay+b)^2\).
  10. Question 10Hard
    Charpit’s method applied to \(px+qy=pq\) gives the complete integral

    Why

    \(F_x=p,\ F_y=q\), so \(\dfrac{dp}{p}=\dfrac{dq}{q}\) and \(q=ap\). Then \(p(x+ay)=ap^2\) gives \(p=\dfrac{x+ay}{a},\ q=x+ay\). \(dz=\dfrac1a(x+ay)\,d(x+ay)\), so \(z=\dfrac{(x+ay)^2}{2a}+b\).
  11. Question 11Hard
    The solution of \(z\,p+q=0\) with \(z(x,0)=x\) is

    Why

    Auxiliary equations \(\dfrac{dx}{z}=\dfrac{dy}{1}=\dfrac{dz}{0}\): \(z=c_1,\ x-zy=c_2\). On \(y=0\): \(c_1=x=c_2\), so \(x-zy=z\) and \(z=x/(1+y)\). Check: \(zp+q=\dfrac{x}{(1+y)^2}-\dfrac{x}{(1+y)^2}=0\).
  12. Question 12Medium
    Consider a first-order PDE \(F(x,y,z,p,q)=0\). (i) A complete integral contains two arbitrary constants. (ii) The general integral contains one arbitrary function. (iii) The singular integral is obtained from the general integral by a suitable choice of the arbitrary function. Which statements are correct?

    Why

    (i) and (ii) are the definitions. (iii) is false: the singular integral is the envelope of the two-parameter family and in general is not contained in the general integral.
  13. Question 13Hard
    Assertion (A): The PDE \(p+q=0\) has infinitely many solutions with \(z=1\) on the line \(y=x\). Reason (R): The curve \(y=x,\ z=1\) is a characteristic curve of \(p+q=0\).

    Why

    Characteristics: \(dx/1=dy/1=dz/0\), i.e. \(x-y=c_1,\ z=c_2\). The curve \((t,t,1)\) lies on one, so the data curve is a characteristic. Then \(z=1\) and \(z=1+(x-y)^2\) (indeed \(z=1+g(x-y)\) with \(g(0)=0\)) all fit the data.
  14. Question 14Medium
    The general integral of \(x^2p+y^2q=z^2\) is

    Why

    \(\dfrac{dx}{x^2}=\dfrac{dy}{y^2}=\dfrac{dz}{z^2}\). Integrating pairs: \(-\dfrac1x+\dfrac1y=\text{const}\) and \(-\dfrac1y+\dfrac1z=\text{const}\).
  15. Question 15Medium
    For \(F(x,y,z,p,q)=0\), Charpit’s auxiliary equations are

    Why

    The \(dp,dq\) denominators are \(F_x+pF_z\) and \(F_y+qF_z\) (each paired with its own variable), and the \(dx,dy,dz\) denominators carry the opposite sign: \(-F_p,\ -F_q,\ -(pF_p+qF_q)\). B has the wrong relative sign, C swaps \(p,q\), D drops the \(F_z\) terms.
