Why it matters for the exam

This block was worth 5 of 30 questions in 2025 (Q7-Q11) and 5 of 30 in 2026 (Q1, Q7, Q12, Q17, Q24), so about one question in six. It is the most scoreable GK area for a teacher-candidate: the answers are concepts and names that repeat. The questions are of three kinds: name the thinker or method (sociometry, discovery learning), put a process in order (data handling, instructional planning), and match a technique or body with its function (statistics, UGC schemes).

Core ideas

Teaching: meaning, levels, principles

Teaching is an organised activity that brings about learning. It has three poles: teacher, learner and curriculum (content). Good teaching is judged by its effectiveness: did learners reach the stated outcomes?

Levels of teaching:

Level Aim Associated with
Memory level Recall of facts Herbart
Understanding level Grasp of principles and relations Morrison
Reflective level Problem solving, critical thinking Hunt

Methods: lecture, demonstration, discussion, seminar, tutorial, project, problem-based learning, case method, role play, flipped classroom, blended and online learning. Learner-centred methods (discussion, project, problem-based) are preferred in higher education; the lecture is teacher-centred.

Learning theories and thinkers

Thinker Idea to remember
Pavlov Classical conditioning (stimulus and response pairing)
Skinner Operant conditioning, reinforcement
Thorndike Trial and error, law of effect
Piaget Four stages: sensorimotor (0-2 years), preoperational (2-7), concrete operational (7-11), formal operational (11 and above)
Vygotsky Social constructivism, zone of proximal development
Bruner Discovery learning (inquiry-based), spiral curriculum, enactive-iconic-symbolic modes
Gardner Multiple intelligences
Bloom Taxonomy of educational objectives
Dewey Learning by doing, progressive education
Kohlberg Stages of moral development

Behavioural conditioning is the opposite of a holistic approach: it shapes observable behaviour through stimulus and reinforcement. A holistic learning paradigm deliberately integrates the cognitive, affective and psychomotor domains in one learning experience. That is the answer when a question describes teaching that integrates “head, heart and hand”.

Bloom’s three domains

Domain Concerns Example outcome
Cognitive (Bloom, 1956) Knowledge and thinking Solve an integral
Affective (Krathwohl, 1964) Attitudes, values, interest Value rigorous proof
Psychomotor Physical and manipulative skills Operate a laboratory instrument

The revised cognitive taxonomy (Anderson and Krathwohl, 2001) lists six levels from lower to higher: remember, understand, apply, analyse, evaluate, create.

Instructional planning order

In a forward design, planning runs from what is intended to how it is checked: curriculum objective, then teaching strategy, then learning outcome, then assessment method. Backward design (Wiggins and McTighe) reverses this: it begins with the desired outcome, fixes the assessment evidence, and only then chooses strategies.

Evaluation

  • Formative evaluation happens during learning to give feedback; summative evaluation happens at the end to grade.
  • Diagnostic evaluation finds the cause of learning difficulties; placement evaluation fixes the entry level.
  • Norm-referenced tests compare a learner with a group; criterion-referenced tests compare a learner with a fixed standard.
  • A good test is valid (measures what it claims), reliable (consistent), objective and usable.

Communication in teaching

Communication is the exchange of a message between a sender and a receiver. The basic elements are the sender (source), the message, encoding (turning the idea into words or signs), the channel (medium), decoding, the receiver and feedback. Noise is anything that distorts the message on the way.

  • Models. Shannon and Weaver (1948) gave a linear model: source, transmitter, channel, receiver, destination, with noise acting on the channel. Berlo’s SMCR model lists Source, Message, Channel, Receiver. Lasswell’s formula asks who says what, in which channel, to whom, with what effect. Teaching is two-way: feedback from the learner is what turns transmission into communication.
  • Types. Verbal (spoken or written) and non-verbal (gesture, posture, eye contact, tone); formal (through official channels) and informal (the grapevine); one-way (a lecture) and two-way (discussion); mass communication (press, radio, television, the web) reaches a large, scattered audience.
  • Barriers. Physical (classroom noise, distance), semantic (jargon, ambiguous words), psychological (anxiety, prejudice, inattention), and cultural or language differences. An overloaded slide or a teacher who never checks understanding is a barrier of the semantic or feedback kind.
  • Effective classroom communication. Clear aims, simple language, examples, questions that check understanding, eye contact, and visual aids that match the message.

Research: meaning and types

Research is a systematic search for new knowledge or for new relations between known facts.

Basis Types
Purpose Fundamental (basic), applied, action research
Nature of data Quantitative, qualitative, mixed
Method Historical, descriptive survey, experimental, ex-post facto (causal-comparative), case study, ethnographic

Experimental research is the approach concerned with discovering causal relationships through controlled manipulation of variables: the researcher manipulates the independent variable, controls extraneous variables and observes the effect on the dependent variable. Historical research studies the past through records; descriptive survey research describes the present state; ethnographic research studies a culture through immersion.

Research process, variables, hypothesis

Steps: identify the problem, review the literature, state the objectives and hypotheses, design the study, collect data, process and analyse the data, interpret the result, report.

  • Independent variable: the presumed cause, manipulated by the researcher.
  • Dependent variable: the effect that is measured.
  • Extraneous (confounding) variable: an unwanted influence that must be controlled.
  • Intervening variable: an unobserved link between cause and effect.
  • Null hypothesis: states no effect or no difference; it is the hypothesis that is tested and either rejected or not rejected. A Type I error is rejecting a true null; a Type II error is not rejecting a false null.

Sampling

Probability sampling Non-probability sampling
Simple random Convenience
Systematic Purposive (judgement)
Stratified Quota
Cluster Snowball

Probability sampling gives every unit a known chance of selection, so the sampling error can be estimated.

Data handling: the order of steps

The usual textbook order is raw data, then processing (editing, coding, classification, tabulation), then analysis, then interpretation and presentation of results. In the 2025 paper the options offered did not include that exact order, and the official key chose the option raw data, processing, presentation, analysis. The key treats the presentation of tables and charts as part of putting the data in shape before the statistical analysis. If both orders ever appear together in one set of options, follow the textbook order; if only the key’s order is nearest, take it.

Tools and scales of measurement

  • Tools: questionnaire, interview, observation, schedule, rating scale, attitude scale (Likert scale uses agree-disagree points).
  • Stevens’ four scales: nominal (labels), ordinal (rank order), interval (equal intervals, no true zero), ratio (true zero).

Statistical techniques and their function

Technique Primary function
Frequency distribution Classification of data into classes
Mean, median, mode Central tendency
Range, variance, standard deviation Dispersion (spread)
Correlation analysis Strength and direction of relationship between variables; coefficient lies between -1 and 1
Regression analysis Predicting a dependent variable from independent variables
t-test, ANOVA Comparing means of groups
Chi-square test Association or goodness of fit for categorical data

Correlation does not prove causation. Regression predicts; correlation only associates.

Sociometry

The sociometric test was devised by J. L. Moreno (his book Who Shall Survive? appeared in 1934). Each member of a group names the peers he or she would choose or reject; the result is drawn as a sociogram showing stars, isolates and cliques. It measures social relations in a group, not intelligence.

Research ethics

The primary ethical concern in research on human participants is the participants’ welfare and rights: informed and voluntary consent, confidentiality, maintaining participant anonymity, no harm, honest reporting. Statistical significance, a large sample and advanced methods are matters of research quality, not ethics.

Plagiarism norms (UGC regulations, 2018): similarity up to 10 per cent carries no penalty; 10 to 40 per cent is Level 1; 40 to 60 per cent is Level 2; above 60 per cent is Level 3.

Referencing styles: APA, MLA, Chicago. A thesis typically has a title page, abstract, introduction, review of literature, methodology, results, discussion, conclusion and references.

Research publication and metrics

  • Peer review. Experts judge a paper before it is published in a journal. A predatory journal charges fees and publishes without real review. The UGC-CARE list (the UGC Consortium for Academic and Research Ethics, from 2019) is the UGC’s list of journals considered acceptable for academic credit. Scopus and Web of Science are the main citation databases; Google Scholar is a free search tool; a DOI is a permanent identifier for an article; ORCID is an identifier for an author.
  • Citation index. The number of times a work is cited by others.
  • h-index (Hirsch, 2005): the largest number \(h\) such that the author has \(h\) papers with at least \(h\) citations each. The i10-index (Google Scholar) is the number of papers with at least 10 citations.
  • Impact factor (Clarivate Journal Citation Reports): for a year, the citations received in that year by the items a journal published in the previous two years, divided by the number of citable items it published in those two years. It rates a journal, not an author or a single paper.
  • Plagiarism checking. A similarity report is a tool for judging; the UGC bands in the research-ethics section decide the penalty.

Higher education system

  • UGC: the University Grants Commission, a statutory body under the UGC Act, 1956, that coordinates, determines and maintains standards of university education and gives grants. It sets minimum qualifications for teachers through its regulations. The National Testing Agency (NTA, set up in 2017) has conducted UGC-NET since 2018; before that the CBSE conducted it.
  • Teacher orientation: The Academic Staff Colleges set up by UGC ran orientation and refresher courses for faculty; they have since been renamed Human Resource Development Centres. A question that describes UGC’s orientation and refresher programmes for faculty points to this scheme, not to libraries or rankings.
  • Digital learning initiatives: SWAYAM (online courses, started 2017), SWAYAM Prabha (educational TV channels), e-PG Pathshala (postgraduate e-content, UGC), National Digital Library of India (NDLI, a repository developed at IIT Kharagpur and launched in 2016), INFLIBNET (UGC’s inter-university centre for library networks, founded 1991) and its Shodhganga repository of Indian theses, and the Academic Bank of Credits (2021).
  • Quality and ranking: NAAC (accreditation, established 1994), NBA (accreditation of technical programmes), NIRF (national ranking framework, 2015), AICTE (technical education, statutory since 1987).
  • Who regulates what: UGC for universities and colleges (grants and standards); AICTE for technical education (engineering, management, pharmacy); NCTE for teacher education (statutory since 1995); NCERT (1961) for school curriculum and textbooks; NTA for entrance and eligibility tests (UGC-NET, CUET, JEE Main, NEET-UG); NAAC and NBA for accreditation; the Bar Council of India for legal education. NEP 2020 proposes to merge the regulatory roles into a single higher-education regulator. Institutions are central, state, private or deemed universities; a deemed university is given university status by the Centre on UGC advice.
  • NEP 2020: approved by the Union Cabinet on 29 July 2020, replacing the 1986 policy. It proposes a 5+3+3+4 school structure, a four-year undergraduate programme with multiple entry and exit points, the Academic Bank of Credits, a single higher-education regulator, discontinuation of the MPhil, a National Research Foundation, and a gross enrolment ratio target of 50 per cent by 2035.

Formulas and results

  • Mean \(\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum x_i\); population variance \(\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum (x_i-\bar{x})^2\); standard deviation \(\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2}\).
  • Correlation coefficient \(r\) satisfies \(-1 \le r \le 1\).
  • Sociometry: J. L. Moreno. Discovery learning: Jerome Bruner. Zone of proximal development: Vygotsky.
  • Bloom’s domains: cognitive, affective, psychomotor.
  • Experimental research: cause and effect through controlled manipulation.
  • Probability sampling: simple random, systematic, stratified, cluster.
  • Forward design: objective, strategy, outcome, assessment.
  • Plagiarism levels: 0-10 per cent no penalty, 10-40 Level 1, 40-60 Level 2, above 60 Level 3.
  • h-index: largest \(h\) with \(h\) papers of at least \(h\) citations. Impact factor: citations this year to the last two years’ items divided by those items. Shannon and Weaver (1948): source, transmitter, channel, receiver, destination, noise.

Worked examples

Example 1: Standard deviation

Data: 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9. Mean is \(40/8 = 5\). Squared deviations: 9, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 4, 16, total 32. Variance \(32/8 = 4\), so standard deviation is 2. This is the type of calculation behind “match the technique with its function”: the standard deviation measures dispersion.

Example 2: Pick the research approach

“A researcher assigns students randomly to two teaching methods, keeps all other conditions fixed, and compares test scores.” Random assignment and manipulation of the independent variable (the method) mean cause and effect is being tested. Answer: experimental research.

Example 3: Classify objectives by domain

“The student will operate a microscope correctly” is psychomotor. “The student will appreciate the beauty of a proof” is affective. “The student will analyse a convergent series” is cognitive. A single lesson that plans all three is following a holistic learning paradigm.

Example 4: Sequence the planning steps

Given: a. teaching strategy, b. learning outcome, c. assessment method, d. curriculum objective. In forward design the objective comes first, then the strategy to reach it, then the outcome observed, then the assessment: d, a, b, c.

Example 5: Which sampling?

“A list of 5,000 students is divided by faculty and 50 are chosen at random from each.” Dividing into groups and sampling within each is stratified random sampling, which is a probability method.

Example 6: Plagiarism level

A thesis shows 25 per cent similarity. It lies in the 10-40 per cent band, so it is Level 1.

Example 7: h-index

An author’s papers have 12, 9, 6, 4, 3, 1 citations. Sorted in descending order, the third paper has 6 at least 3, and the fourth has 4 at least 4, but the fifth has 3 less than 5. So \(h = 4\).

Example 8: Name the communication barrier

A lecturer uses technical jargon and never asks a question; students nod but understand little. The jargon is a semantic barrier, and the missing check on understanding is a missing feedback loop.

Common traps

  • Correlation and causation. Only experimental design supports a causal claim.
  • Sociometry and psychometry. Sociometry is Moreno and measures group relations; it is not an intelligence test.
  • Bruner, Piaget, Dewey, William James. Discovery learning is Bruner. Piaget is stages; Dewey is experience; James is psychology of habit.
  • Anonymity and significance. In a research-ethics question, anonymity, consent and confidentiality are the ethical items; sample size and significance are not.
  • Forward and backward design. Check which way the sequence runs.
  • NDLI and UGC. NDLI is run by the Ministry of Education (developed at IIT Kharagpur); UGC’s own initiatives include INFLIBNET, e-PG Pathshala and the Academic Staff College (now HRDC) scheme.
  • Swachh Bharat is a sanitation mission, not a digital learning scheme, even though it appears as a distractor.
  • Sample size versus sampling method. Large samples do not cure a biased sampling method.
  • Impact factor and h-index. Impact factor belongs to a journal; h-index belongs to an author. A paper with many citations does not raise the journal’s impact factor unless it falls in the two-year window.
  • UGC and NTA. UGC prescribes the NET eligibility rules; the NTA holds the test.
  • Noise and feedback. Noise distorts the message; feedback is the receiver’s response, and its absence makes teaching one-way.

Quick revision

  • Sociometry: J. L. Moreno (1934); output is a sociogram.
  • Discovery learning: Jerome Bruner; spiral curriculum.
  • Piaget: sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operational, formal operational.
  • Bloom: cognitive, affective, psychomotor; revised levels end with “create”.
  • Holistic learning integrates all three domains; behavioural conditioning does not.
  • Forward design: objective, strategy, outcome, assessment.
  • Experimental research is the one for cause and effect.
  • Correlation relates, regression predicts, standard deviation measures spread, frequency distribution classifies.
  • Ethics: consent, confidentiality, anonymity, no harm.
  • Probability sampling: simple random, systematic, stratified, cluster.
  • UGC Act 1956; NAAC 1994; NIRF 2015; NEP 2020 approved 29 July 2020.
  • UGC orientation and refresher courses: Academic Staff Colleges, now HRDCs.
  • UGC-NET: held by NTA since 2018. NCTE: teacher education. NCERT: school curriculum. UGC-CARE: approved journals list (2019).
