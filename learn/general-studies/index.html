What this unit covers

General Knowledge is 30 questions in both patterns. In the new pattern for the November 2026 exam (120 questions, 3 marks each, 1 mark deducted per wrong answer) GK is 90 of the 360 marks, so attempt a GK question only when you can rule out at least one option. In the 2025 and 2026 papers (100 questions, 2 marks each, no negative marking) GK was 60 of the 200 marks. The official sheet gives no GK syllabus wording that we could find, so the areas below come from the 60 questions actually asked in 2025 (Advt 51) and 2026.

See the exam guide for the full pattern, and confirm it on your admit card.

The pattern is closer to UGC NET Paper 1 than to a state GK paper. No question on Uttar Pradesh as a state was asked in either year.

How it is asked

  • One fact, four options. Most questions test a single static fact: an article, an amendment, a date, a name.
  • Match the following. In 2026 five questions were list matches (Q9, Q18, Q20, Q21, Q22: personalities, organisations, indicators, abbreviations). Solve them by fixing the one pair you are sure of and eliminating options.
  • Statement or inference form. Several 2026 questions (for example Q8, Q13, Q27 and Q29) are worded as “identify the correct inference”. The wrong options use absolute words such as “always”, “only”, “automatically”.
  • Current affairs. These are tied to the previous calendar year, for example Budget, G20, space missions, sports and personalities. Plan to read the last twelve months in detail, not ten years.
  • Applied science. Questions such as the ship in sea water or the formation of terrestrial planets are reasoning questions, not recall.
  • Time. About 15 minutes for the 30 questions (30 seconds each) leaves time for maths.

High-yield list

  1. Match-the-following on global organisations: WHO health, WTO trade, IAEA nuclear, UNEP environment.
  2. Environment treaties and what each one covers.
  3. Number systems and units: bits, bytes, binary, hexadecimal.
  4. Network protocols: TCP, HTTP, FTP, DNS, SMTP.
  5. Research types and sampling; one-line identification of each.
  6. Teaching theorists and their ideas.
  7. Which amendment did what (42nd, 44th, 52nd, 73rd, 86th, 97th, 101st).
  8. Concurrent, State and Union List placements.
  9. Finance Commission versus Planning Commission versus NITI Aayog.
  10. Economic indicators: GDP, fiscal deficit, HDI, poverty line.
  11. Dates of the national movement and the Government of India Acts.
  12. Last year’s Budget, G20, ISRO missions, sports champions and Nobel laureates.
