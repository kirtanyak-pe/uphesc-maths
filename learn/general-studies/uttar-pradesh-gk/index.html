Why it matters for the exam

Neither the 2025 nor the 2026 paper had a single question that is specific to Uttar Pradesh. The 30 GK questions followed a UGC NET Paper 1 pattern (teaching, research, ICT, environment, science, polity, economy, current affairs). So treat this topic as insurance, not a core block: one pass of about 80 minutes is enough. It is placed last in the unit for that reason. The facts below are static and well established; the district list is the one table worth memorising exactly, because a state exam can ask “which district belongs to which division” at any time.

Core ideas

The state at a glance

Item Fact
Name Called United Provinces of Agra and Oudh from 1902, United Provinces later; renamed Uttar Pradesh in January 1950 (sources give 24 to 26 January; UP Diwas is marked on 24 January)
Capital Lucknow
High Court Allahabad High Court at Prayagraj, with a bench at Lucknow
Area About 2.4 lakh square kilometres (Census 2011: 240,928); fourth largest state by area
Population 19.98 crore in Census 2011; the most populous state
Neighbours Eight states and Delhi, plus Nepal: Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Delhi, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Bihar
Legislature Vidhan Sabha 403 elected members; Vidhan Parishad 100 members
Parliament seats Lok Sabha 80 (the largest state share)
Administration 18 divisions and 75 districts
Uttarakhand Separated on 9 November 2000
Language Hindi (official); Urdu also an official language

Sarojini Naidu was the Governor of the United Provinces from 15 August 1947, the first woman to be a Governor of an Indian state. Govind Ballabh Pant became the first Chief Minister of Uttar Pradesh in January 1950. Sucheta Kriplani (1963) was India’s first woman Chief Minister.

State symbols: Barasingha (swamp deer) is the state animal, Sarus crane the state bird, Ashoka the state tree and Palash (flame of the forest) the state flower.

Memory aids: the largest divisions (six districts) are Kanpur, Lucknow and Meerut; the smallest (three) are Azamgarh, Basti, Jhansi, Mirzapur and Saharanpur. Chitrakoot division is headquartered at Banda, and Devipatan division at Gonda. Ayodhya division was formerly called Faizabad division. Some district names changed in recent years: Allahabad is now Prayagraj, Faizabad is now Ayodhya.

Physical features and rivers

  • Three natural regions: the Himalayan foothill (Terai) belt along the north, the Gangetic plain (the great majority of the state), and the Bundelkhand and Vindhyan plateau in the south.
  • Ganga enters Uttar Pradesh at Bijnor and leaves it at Ballia. Yamuna meets the Ganga at Prayagraj (the Sangam, with the mythical Saraswati).
  • Tributaries of the Ganga: Ramganga, Gomti (from Pilibhit; flows through Lucknow and joins the Ganga near Ghazipur), Ghaghara (Saryu), Son (from the south, joining in Bihar). The Rapti is a tributary of the Ghaghara, and the Gandak joins the Ganga in Bihar, so neither meets the Ganga inside Uttar Pradesh.
  • Tributaries of the Yamuna: Chambal, Betwa, Ken.
  • Rihand Dam (Govind Ballabh Pant Sagar, Sonbhadra) is on the Rihand, a tributary of the Son. Matatila is on the Betwa. The Upper Ganga Canal (opened 1854, Sir Proby Cautley) irrigates the west.
  • Agriculture: UP is the leading producer of sugarcane, wheat and milk. Dasheri mango of Malihabad (near Lucknow) is a famous product.

Protected areas and heritage sites

Category Facts
National park Dudhwa (Lakhimpur Kheri, established 1977), the state’s only national park
Tiger reserves Dudhwa, Pilibhit, Amangarh (Bijnor) and Ranipur (Chitrakoot): four
Sanctuaries Katarniaghat (Bahraich), Kishanpur (Lakhimpur Kheri), Chandra Prabha (Chandauli), National Chambal Sanctuary (gharial), Sur Sarovar (Agra bird sanctuary)
UNESCO World Heritage Taj Mahal, Agra Fort (both 1983), Fatehpur Sikri (1986), Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath (25 July 2026, India’s 45th site): four
UNESCO intangible heritage Ramlila (2008), Kumbh Mela (2017), and Deepavali (10 December 2025, India’s 16th element on the list; the festival is not tied to one state)

Katarniaghat and Kishanpur lie in the Dudhwa Tiger Reserve landscape. The state holds a long list of Ramsar wetlands, for example Bakhira, Haiderpur and Sur Sarovar. The newest is Sauj Jheel (Mainpuri district, in the Saman wetland complex), added on 15 October 2026 together with Sukhna Wetland (Chandigarh), which took India’s total to 103 sites. Do not memorise the state total: reports differ on the count, and older question banks are out of date.

UP-linked freedom struggle events (see the history topic): Meerut, 10 May 1857; Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi; Begum Hazrat Mahal of Lucknow; Lucknow Pact 1916; Chauri Chaura, 4 February 1922; Kakori, 9 August 1925 (Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan); Chandra Shekhar Azad’s death at Alfred Park, Allahabad, on 27 February 1931. Lal Bahadur Shastri was born at Mughalsarai; Jawaharlal Nehru at Prayagraj (Anand Bhawan is there). Major Dhyan Chand (hockey) was born at Prayagraj; his birthday, 29 August, is National Sports Day.

Literature, language and music

  • Dialects: Awadhi (Lucknow, Ayodhya; Tulsidas, Ramcharitmanas; Jayasi, Padmavat), Braj (Mathura, Agra; Surdas), Bhojpuri (east), Bundeli (south), Khari Boli (west, Meerut).
  • Poet-saints and writers: Kabir (Varanasi), Tulsidas, Surdas, Bharatendu Harishchandra (Varanasi, called the father of modern Hindi), Premchand (Lamhi, Varanasi), Mahadevi Verma.
  • Music: Bismillah Khan (shehnai, Bharat Ratna 2001; born at Dumraon in Bihar but identified with Varanasi, where he lived and played), Ravi Shankar (Varanasi), Begum Akhtar (Faizabad), Birju Maharaj (Lucknow).
  • Dance and folk forms: Kathak has the Lucknow and Banaras gharanas; the Lucknow gharana grew at the Awadh court. Folk forms include Raslila, Charkula (Braj), Nautanki (Kanpur, Hathras), Alha (Bundelkhand), Kajri, Chaiti and Birha.

Festivals and fairs

Kumbh and Magh Mela (Prayagraj), Dev Deepawali (Varanasi, Kartik Purnima), Lathmar Holi (Barsana and Nandgaon), Taj Mahotsav (Agra), Buddha Purnima (Sarnath, Kushinagar), Kanwar Yatra (western districts, Shravan month).

Education and research institutions

Banaras Hindu University (founded 1916 by Madan Mohan Malaviya); Aligarh Muslim University (MAO College 1875, university 1920); University of Allahabad (1887); IIT Kanpur (1959); IIM Lucknow (1984); Central Drug Research Institute, Lucknow (1951); Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences, Lucknow (1946); Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam Technical University (Lucknow).

Infrastructure

  • Expressways: Yamuna Expressway (Greater Noida to Agra, about 165 km), Agra-Lucknow Expressway (about 302 km), Purvanchal Expressway (Lucknow to Ghazipur, about 341 km, opened November 2021), Bundelkhand Expressway (Chitrakoot to Etawah, about 296 km, opened July 2022) and the Ganga Expressway, the longest in the state: 594 km from near Meerut to near Prayagraj through 12 districts, inaugurated by the Prime Minister at Hardoi on 29 April 2026 (cost about Rs 36,230 crore).
  • International airports: Lucknow, Varanasi, Kushinagar (opened October 2021), Ayodhya (Maharishi Valmiki International, December 2023) and Noida International Airport at Jewar (Gautam Buddh Nagar). Phase I was inaugurated on 28 March 2026 and commercial flights began on 15 June 2026, with IndiGo operating the first services.
  • Sequence to remember: Purvanchal (Nov 2021), Ayodhya airport (Dec 2023), Noida airport (Mar 2026), Ganga Expressway (Apr 2026).

Governance, land and economy

  • Legislature. Vidhan Sabha of 403 seats; Vidhan Parishad (Legislative Council) of 100 members; 80 Lok Sabha seats (the most of any state) and 31 Rajya Sabha seats. The Governor is the constitutional head; the Chief Minister heads the council of ministers. Lucknow is the capital; the High Court (established in 1866, at Allahabad since 1869, now Prayagraj) has a bench at Lucknow.
  • Local government. Three-tier Panchayati Raj (gram panchayat, kshetra panchayat, zila panchayat) and urban local bodies (nagar nigam, nagar palika parishad, nagar panchayat), under the 73rd and 74th Amendments.
  • Soils. The plain has alluvium: older, higher bhangar and newer, fertile khadar in the floodplains. The foothill belt has pebbly bhabar and marshy Terai soils. Bundelkhand has red and black soils. Saline-alkaline patches are called usar.
  • Climate. Monsoon climate with hot summers, a cool winter and rainfall that falls from east to west; most rain comes from June to September.
  • Economy. UP is the largest state producer of sugarcane, wheat and milk. The Uttar Pradesh Defence Industrial Corridor (announced 2018) has six nodes: Lucknow, Kanpur, Aligarh, Agra, Jhansi and Chitrakoot. The Census 2011 population was about 19.98 crore, the largest of any state; the literacy rate was 67.68 per cent and the sex ratio 912.
  • Schemes. One District One Product (January 2018) is the best-known state scheme; Kanya Sumangala Yojana (2019) gives staged payments to girls’ families.

Formulas and results

  • UP: 18 divisions, 75 districts, 80 Lok Sabha seats, 403 Vidhan Sabha seats, 100 in the Vidhan Parishad.
  • Capital Lucknow; High Court at Prayagraj; renamed January 1950; Uttarakhand separated 9 November 2000.
  • Area rank 4; population rank 1.
  • Ganga: enters at Bijnor, leaves at Ballia; Yamuna joins at Prayagraj.
  • Only national park: Dudhwa. Tiger reserves: Dudhwa, Pilibhit, Amangarh, Ranipur.
  • UNESCO World Heritage sites (four): Taj Mahal, Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri, Sarnath (2026).
  • State animal Barasingha, bird Sarus crane, tree Ashoka, flower Palash.
  • Divisions of six districts: Kanpur, Lucknow, Meerut.
  • ODOP: launched 24 January 2018.

Worked examples

Example 1: Place a district in its division

“Which district belongs to Mirzapur division?” The division has three districts: Mirzapur, Sonbhadra and Bhadohi. Bhadohi is the one that tempts students to place it in Varanasi, which has Varanasi, Chandauli, Ghazipur and Jaunpur.

Example 2: Count districts by elimination

A question gives four divisions and asks which has the most districts. Kanpur, Lucknow and Meerut have six each. If only one of those three is among the options, it wins; Prayagraj and Gorakhpur have four, Ayodhya five.

Example 3: Identify the river from the city

Lucknow sits on the Gomti, Kanpur on the Ganga, Agra and Mathura on the Yamuna, Ayodhya on the Ghaghara (Saryu), Varanasi on the Ganga, Gorakhpur on the Rapti. A city-to-river match question is solved by these six pairs.

Example 4: World Heritage and intangible heritage

Question: how many UNESCO World Heritage sites are in Uttar Pradesh? Four: the Taj Mahal, Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri and, since 25 July 2026, the Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath. Older books and question banks say three; answer four now. The Kumbh Mela, Ramlila and (since December 2025) Deepavali are on the intangible cultural heritage list, a different list, and are not counted.

Example 5: State animal versus bird

Barasingha (animal) and Sarus crane (bird) are both associated with the Terai wetlands. Mixing them up is the usual error; the Sarus crane is also the tallest flying bird in India.

Common traps

  • Changed names. Allahabad became Prayagraj and Faizabad became Ayodhya; old and new names can both appear.
  • Division versus district. Devipatan, Chitrakoot and Azamgarh are division names; their headquarters (Gonda, Banda, Azamgarh) are districts.
  • Bhadohi, Amethi and Hapur. These are in Mirzapur, Ayodhya and Meerut divisions respectively.
  • National park versus sanctuary. Dudhwa is the only national park; Katarniaghat and Kishanpur are sanctuaries.
  • Which Heritage list. The Taj, the Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri and Sarnath are World Heritage sites; Kumbh, Ramlila and Deepavali are intangible heritage.
  • Largest versus most populous. UP is fourth by area but first by population.
  • Capital versus High Court. The capital is Lucknow; the main seat of the High Court is Prayagraj.
