Why it matters for the exam

Science and space gave 2 questions in 2025 (Q20 planets by orbital period, Q21 a ship entering the ocean) and 5 in 2026 (heavy elements in terrestrial planets, quantum communication, AI in drug discovery, the cosmic microwave background, formation of terrestrial planets). All of them test a principle (Archimedes, Kepler, accretion, entanglement), not a memorised number. As a mathematician you can reason most of them out: this page gives the principle, the one number worth keeping, and a way to rule out wrong options.

Core ideas

The solar system

The Sun holds about 99.8 per cent of the mass of the solar system. There are eight planets. In order from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars (the terrestrial, rocky planets), then Jupiter, Saturn (gas giants), Uranus, Neptune (ice giants). Between Mars and Jupiter lies the asteroid belt; beyond Neptune lies the Kuiper belt. Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006.

Kepler’s third law: the square of the orbital period is proportional to the cube of the mean distance from the Sun, \(T^2 \propto a^3\). So the farther the planet, the longer its year.

To order planets by orbital period, simply order them by distance from the Sun. In descending order of period: Neptune, Uranus, Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Earth, Venus, Mercury.

Origin of the solar system

The nebular hypothesis says the Sun and planets formed from a collapsing, rotating cloud of gas and dust about 4.6 billion years ago. The cloud flattened into a protoplanetary disk. Dust grains stuck together into small bodies (planetesimals), which collided and merged: this is accretion. Close to the Sun only rock and metal could stay solid, so the inner planets are small and rocky and formed by the accretion of planetesimals in the protoplanetary disk. Farther out, ices survived and giant planets collected gas. The planets did not form by direct condensation of hydrogen envelopes around the inner planets, by gravitational collapse of independent stellar cores, or by capture of rogue bodies; capture can explain some moons, not the planets.

Iron and nickel in the terrestrial planets are heavy elements. They were not made in the planets, nor by fusion of hydrogen in planets (planets are too small for fusion). They were forged inside massive earlier stars and spread by supernova explosions before the solar system formed: supernova nucleosynthesis preceding solar system formation. Later, in molten planets, dense iron and nickel sank to form the core (planetary differentiation). Volcanism and chemical weathering move existing material around; they do not create the elements.

The universe

  • The Big Bang model: the universe began about 13.8 billion years ago in a hot, dense state and has been expanding since (Hubble’s law: recession speed proportional to distance).
  • The cosmic microwave background (CMB) is faint microwave radiation filling the sky, with a temperature of about 2.7 K. It was discovered in 1965 by Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson. It is the residual radiation from the early hot, dense universe, released when matter became transparent about 380,000 years after the Big Bang, and is strong empirical support for the Big Bang. Steady-state cosmology (continuous creation of matter) predicts no such relic and was abandoned after the CMB discovery.
  • The Sun shines by nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium.
  • Distances: 1 AU is about \(1.5\times 10^8\) km; light takes about 8.3 minutes from Sun to Earth and about 1.3 seconds from the Moon. A light-year is about \(9.46\times 10^{12}\) km.

Buoyancy: why a ship rises in the sea

Archimedes’ principle: the upward buoyant force on a body in a fluid equals the weight of the fluid it displaces. A floating ship’s weight equals the buoyant force, so for a ship of mass \(m\) in a fluid of density \(\rho\),

\(m g = \rho\, V_{\text{displaced}}\, g \quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_{\text{displaced}} = \frac{m}{\rho}.\)

Sea water (\(\rho \approx 1025\) kg per cubic metre) is denser than river water (\(\rho \approx 1000\)). The same ship therefore needs less volume submerged in the sea, so it rises slightly. The reason (density of sea water is greater) is the correct explanation of the observation. The same law explains why a swimmer floats more easily in the sea, and why ships carry a load line (Plimsoll line) showing the safe depth in different waters.

Space programme of India

ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation) was formed on 15 August 1969 (its predecessor INCOSPAR began in 1962), with headquarters in Bengaluru and a launch site at Sriharikota. Aryabhata (1975) was India’s first satellite (launched from the Soviet Union). Launch vehicles: SLV-3 (Rohini satellite, 1980), PSLV, GSLV and LVM3. Landmark missions are listed in the current affairs preparation topic: Chandrayaan-1, Mangalyaan, Chandrayaan-3, Aditya-L1.

Emerging technologies

  • Quantum communication. Quantum key distribution sends encryption keys using single photons. Long-distance demonstrations by Chinese and European research teams (the Micius satellite, 2016, and intercontinental links) relied on quantum entanglement: entangled photon pairs show correlated outcomes, and any eavesdropping disturbs the pair and is detected. It does not rely on nuclear magnetic resonance, superconducting tunnelling or optical diffraction.
  • Artificial intelligence. Machine learning finds patterns in data; deep learning uses multi-layer neural networks; generative AI creates text or images. AlphaFold (Nobel Chemistry 2024) predicts protein structures. AI-assisted drug discovery mainly shortens the early stage of preclinical molecular screening: finding and testing candidate molecules in silico. It does not remove human clinical trials, regulatory approval or vaccine distribution.
  • Biotechnology. CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing (Nobel Chemistry 2020, Doudna and Charpentier). mRNA vaccines (Nobel Medicine 2023, Karikó and Weissman).
  • Nuclear energy. India’s three-stage nuclear programme was proposed by Homi Bhabha; the Atomic Energy Regulatory Board (AERB, 1983) is the national regulator.

Formulas and results

  • Kepler’s third law: \(T^2 \propto a^3\), so \(T = a^{3/2}\) years when \(a\) is in AU.
  • Floating body: \(V_{\text{displaced}} = m/\rho\); denser fluid, smaller submerged volume.
  • Archimedes: buoyant force = weight of displaced fluid.
  • Light travel time: Sun to Earth about 499 s (8.3 min).
  • Orbital period order = distance order: Mercury shortest, Neptune longest.
  • Terrestrial planets form by accretion of planetesimals; iron and nickel come from supernova nucleosynthesis.
  • CMB: relic of the hot early universe; about 2.7 K.
  • Quantum communication: entanglement.

Worked examples

Example 1: Order planets by orbital period

Arrange Saturn (1), Mars (2), Jupiter (3), Uranus (4) in descending order of orbital period. Distances: Mars 1.52, Jupiter 5.20, Saturn 9.54, Uranus 19.2 AU. Period grows with distance, so descending: Uranus (4), Saturn (1), Jupiter (3), Mars (2): the order 4, 1, 3, 2.

Example 2: Ship in river and sea

A ship of mass \(10^7\) kg floats in river water (\(1000\) kg/m\(^3\)) and in sea water (\(1025\) kg/m\(^3\)). Displaced volume in the river is \(10^7/1000 = 10{,}000\) m\(^3\); in the sea it is \(10^7/1025 \approx 9{,}756\) m\(^3\). The ship displaces about 2.4 per cent less, so it sits higher in the sea.

Example 3: Kepler for a new planet

A planet orbits at 4 AU. Its period is \(4^{3/2} = 8\) years.

Example 4: Which origin for iron in Earth’s core?

Options: chemical weathering, continuous hydrogen fusion within planets, supernova nucleosynthesis before the solar system formed, early volcanism. Planets cannot fuse hydrogen; weathering and volcanism rearrange elements but cannot make iron. Only stellar nucleosynthesis and supernovae made it before the Sun’s birth.

Example 5: Which evidence supports the Big Bang?

The relic radiation field at 2.7 K, uniform to a few parts in \(10^5\), is the prediction of a hot early universe, not of continuous creation. So option “residual radiation from the early hot dense universe”.

Example 6: Which stage does AI drug discovery compress?

AI screens millions of candidate molecules and predicts protein targets in software. That is the preclinical molecular screening stage. Clinical trials on humans, regulatory approval and vaccine distribution are not replaced.

Common traps

  • Orbital period order. It follows distance, not size or mass.
  • Assertion and reason in the ship question. Both statements are true and the reason explains the assertion; the mistake is to think the ship sinks deeper in denser water.
  • Fusion in planets. Only stars fuse hydrogen.
  • Capture versus accretion. Planets formed by accretion; capture is for irregular moons.
  • Steady-state versus Big Bang. The CMB supports the Big Bang.
  • Entanglement versus NMR. Quantum communication uses entanglement and superposition; NMR is a medical and chemical technique.
  • Clinical trials. AI speeds early discovery, not human trials.
  • Terrestrial planets are four. Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars.
  • Pluto. A dwarf planet since 2006.
