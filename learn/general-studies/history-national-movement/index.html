Why it matters for the exam

History gave 3 questions in 2025 (Q22 the Congress decision on Partition, Q24 Megasthenes, Q25 dyarchy) and 1 in 2026 (Q5 integration of the princely states). The questions ask one date, one name, or one mechanism, and the options are neighbouring dates or neighbouring names. That makes the topic a chronology task: hold a spine of about 40 dates and the right names fall into place. A related chronological question in 2026 (Q16, order of constitutional developments) is covered with the polity topic.

Core ideas

Ancient India: the names that get asked

Period Key facts
Indus Valley civilisation Bronze Age; Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Lothal, Dholavira; town planning, drainage
Vedic age Rigveda earliest; later Vedic texts; varna system
Mahajanapadas (6th century BCE) Sixteen states; Magadha rose to dominate; Buddha and Mahavira
Mauryan empire Chandragupta Maurya (about 321 BCE) with Chanakya (Kautilya, author of the Arthashastra); Bindusara; Ashoka (Kalinga war about 261 BCE; edicts; dhamma)
Gupta empire Chandragupta I, Samudragupta, Chandragupta II; Fa-Hien visited; Kalidasa; Aryabhata; Nalanda tradition
Harsha (7th century CE) Capital Kannauj; Hiuen Tsang visited

Megasthenes was the Greek ambassador sent by Seleucus Nicator to the court of Chandragupta Maurya at Pataliputra. His account of India is the book Indica, known only through later quotations. He did not serve Bindusara (Chandragupta’s son, who received Deimachus from the Seleucid court), Ashoka, or Brihadratha (the last Mauryan king, killed about 185 BCE, roughly 135 years after Chandragupta came to the throne).

More ancient names that are asked.

  • Kushanas and Satavahanas. Kanishka, the great Kushana king, patronised Buddhism and the Gandhara and Mathura schools of art. The Satavahanas ruled the Deccan; the Sangam age is the early Tamil literature of the south.
  • Edicts. Ashoka’s edicts (rock and pillar) are in Brahmi script, which James Prinsep deciphered in 1837. The Sanchi stupa in Madhya Pradesh is associated with Ashoka’s patronage.
  • Buddhism and Jainism. Gautama Buddha (Lumbini, Bodh Gaya, Sarnath, Kushinagar); Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara. Buddhist architecture has the stupa (relic mound), the chaitya (prayer hall) and the vihara (monastery).
  • South Indian dynasties. Pallavas (Mahabalipuram, Kanchi), Chalukyas (Badami), Rashtrakutas (Ellora’s Kailasa temple, built under Krishna I), Cholas (Rajaraja I built the Brihadisvara temple at Thanjavur, completed in 1010 CE; the Uttaramerur inscriptions record village self-government).

Medieval India

  • Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526): Slave, Khilji, Tughlaq, Sayyid and Lodi dynasties.
  • Mughal empire: Babur (First Battle of Panipat, 1526), Humayun, Sher Shah Sur’s interlude, Akbar (Second Battle of Panipat, 1556), Jahangir, Shah Jahan, Aurangzeb.
  • Bhakti and Sufi movements, Vijayanagara and Maratha states fill the rest of the period.
  • Sultanate rulers to remember. Qutbuddin Aibak (1206; began the Qutb Minar, finished by Iltutmish), Razia Sultan (first woman ruler), Balban, Alauddin Khilji (market-price controls and a standing army), Muhammad bin Tughlaq (moved the capital to Daulatabad; token currency), Firoz Shah Tughlaq. Timur sacked Delhi in 1398. The Ibn Battuta account belongs to the Tughlaq period.
  • Mughal administration and culture. Akbar: the mansabdari system, Sulh-i-kul (peace to all), the Ibadat Khana. Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal (about 1632 to 1653) and the Red Fort in Delhi. Aurangzeb’s long Deccan wars drained the empire.
  • Regional powers. Vijayanagara (founded 1336 by Harihara and Bukka; Krishnadevaraya its great king), Shivaji (crowned 1674), the Sikh Gurus (Nanak to Gobind Singh).
  • Bhakti and Sufism. Kabir, Nanak, Mirabai, Tulsidas and Chaitanya in the bhakti stream; Moinuddin Chishti (Ajmer) and Nizamuddin Auliya (Delhi) among the Chishti Sufis.

The British period: constitutional developments

Act Year Main point
Regulating Act 1773 First control of the East India Company by Parliament
Pitt’s India Act 1784 Board of Control
Charter Act 1833 Governor-General of India; end of Company’s trade
Government of India Act 1858 Rule passes to the Crown; Viceroy; Secretary of State
Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley-Minto) Separate electorates for Muslims
Government of India Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford) Dyarchy in the provinces; bicameral centre; came into operation in 1921
Government of India Act 1935 Provincial autonomy (ended dyarchy in provinces); federation proposed; Federal Court
Indian Independence Act 1947 Created India and Pakistan

Dyarchy (double government) under the 1919 Act divided provincial subjects into transferred subjects, run by Indian ministers responsible to the legislature (education, health, local government), and reserved subjects, run by the Governor with his executive council (law and order, finance, land revenue). It applied to the provinces; the Centre kept a unitary structure. The question’s date is 1919 (the Act); the dyarchy actually began to work in 1921.

Partition and the Congress decision

After the Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947, the All India Congress Committee met in Delhi on 14-15 June 1947 to decide. Gandhiji, who had opposed Partition, spoke at the AICC on 14 June 1947 and advised Congressmen to accept it. The AICC accepted the plan on 15 June. The Indian Independence Act followed on 18 July 1947.

Integration of the princely states

About 565 princely states were left sovereign when British paramountcy lapsed. They were brought into the Union through the Instrument of Accession, a legal document by which a ruler acceded to India, initially on three subjects: defence, external affairs and communications. The States Department under Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, with V. P. Menon as secretary, negotiated accession and later merger.

State Outcome
Junagadh Acceded to Pakistan; India took control; plebiscite in February 1948
Hyderabad Police action (Operation Polo) in September 1948
Jammu and Kashmir Instrument of Accession signed on 26 October 1947 by Maharaja Hari Singh

The mechanism was the Instrument of Accession backed by the Indian Independence Act. It was not the Communal Award (1932), the 1935 Act’s provisions or the Montagu-Chelmsford reforms (1919). Privy purses were abolished by the 26th Amendment in 1971.

Culture in one table

Area Facts
Classical dances Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kuchipudi, Odissi, Kathakali, Sattriya, Manipuri, Mohiniyattam
Buddhist sites Lumbini (birth), Bodh Gaya (enlightenment), Sarnath (first sermon), Kushinagar (parinirvana)
Mauryan art Lion capital of Ashoka at Sarnath (India’s national emblem)
Architecture Nagara (north), Dravida (south), Vesara (mixed); Indo-Islamic and Mughal styles

Art and architecture: how to tell styles apart

  • Temple styles: Nagara (north, curved shikhara; Khajuraho, built by the Chandelas, and the Sun Temple at Konark, built by the Ganga king Narasimhadeva I in the 13th century), Dravida (south, pyramidal vimana and gateway gopuram; Thanjavur, Mahabalipuram), Vesara (Deccan, a mixture).
  • Schools of sculpture: Gandhara (Greek influence, Buddhist subjects, in the north-west), Mathura (indigenous, red sandstone), Amaravati (south-east).
  • Painting: Ajanta (Buddhist wall paintings), Mughal miniature (court scenes, portraits, natural history), Rajput and Pahari (Kangra) schools.
  • Indo-Islamic buildings: Qutb Minar, the Jama Masjid, Humayun’s Tomb, the Taj Mahal; the Mughals built charbagh gardens.
  • Music and dance: Hindustani and Carnatic traditions; the Sangeet Natak Akademi recognises eight classical dances (the list in the table above).

Formulas and results

  • Megasthenes: ambassador of Seleucus Nicator at Chandragupta Maurya’s court; book Indica.
  • Dyarchy: Government of India Act 1919; transferred and reserved subjects; operative from 1921.
  • Provincial autonomy: Government of India Act 1935.
  • Indian National Congress: 1885. Muslim League: 1906. Lucknow Pact: 1916.
  • Partition plan: 3 June 1947; AICC decision on 14-15 June 1947; Independence Act 18 July 1947; Independence 15 August 1947.
  • Princely states: Instrument of Accession; Patel and V. P. Menon.
  • J and K accession: 26 October 1947.

Worked examples

Example 1: Pick the ambassador

“In whose court did Megasthenes come as ambassador?” Megasthenes’ mission was sent by Seleucus Nicator after the Seleucid-Mauryan treaty, so the king is Chandragupta Maurya, Seleucus’ contemporary. Ashoka is Chandragupta’s grandson and Bindusara’s son; Brihadratha, the last Mauryan, ruled some 135 years after Chandragupta.

Example 2: Dyarchy date

Options 1909, 1919, 1935, 1937. The 1909 Act brought separate electorates, 1935 provincial autonomy (in force 1937). Dyarchy belongs to the Montagu-Chelmsford reforms, so 1919.

Example 3: Order the events

Arrange: Quit India, Lahore Resolution, Poona Pact, Dandi March. Dates: Dandi March 1930, Poona Pact 1932, Lahore Resolution 1940, Quit India 1942. Order: Dandi, Poona, Lahore, Quit India.

Example 4: Which instrument integrated the states?

Of the options Communal Award, 1935 Act, Instrument of Accession, Montagu-Chelmsford reforms, only the Instrument of Accession is a document by which a state joined India, so it is the answer.

Example 5: Elimination for the Congress decision

Options 14 June, 12 June, 4 June, 3 June 1947. The plan was announced on 3 June and explained by Mountbatten at a press conference on 4 June, so neither is the date of a Congress meeting. The AICC met in Delhi on 14-15 June, and the option that falls in that meeting is 14 June.

Example 6: Attach the Act to the feature

Separate electorates: 1909. Dyarchy: 1919. Provincial autonomy: 1935. Independence: 1947. This four-line table answers most Act-to-feature questions.

Common traps

  • Chandragupta Maurya versus Chandragupta I or II. Megasthenes visited the Mauryan Chandragupta.
  • 1919 and 1921. The Act is 1919; the working of dyarchy began in 1921. Options usually carry 1919, not 1921.
  • 1935 versus 1937. The Act of 1935 took effect for provincial autonomy in 1937.
  • Dyarchy at the centre. It was in the provinces. The 1935 proposals for dyarchy at the centre never operated.
  • 3 June versus 14 June 1947. The plan is 3 June; the Congress committee decision is mid-June.
  • Independence Act versus Independence Day. 18 July versus 15 August 1947.
  • Gandhi’s role in Partition. He opposed it, but on 14 June 1947 advised the AICC to accept the plan; this is a recurring one-line fact.
  • Accession versus merger. Accession came first, on three subjects; merger came later.
  • Which Chola, which Chalukya. Rajaraja I built Thanjavur; Krishna I of the Rashtrakutas built Kailasa at Ellora; Narasimhadeva I of the Ganga dynasty built Konark.
  • Reserved and transferred. Law and order stayed with the Governor; education and health went to ministers.
