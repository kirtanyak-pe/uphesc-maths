Why it matters for the exam

Current affairs is the single largest block of the General Knowledge section. In the 2025 paper, Q1-Q6 (6 of 30) were pure current affairs, and Q30 (the Union Budget 2025-26) was a seventh. In the 2026 paper the question style changed: fewer “which year” questions and more “persons in the news” and “organisation and focus” matching, plus science questions with a news hook (quantum communication, AI in drug discovery, the global biodiversity framework). You cannot predict the item, but you can predict the type, and you can cover the right window of months.

This page does not contain news. News ages in a week. It tells you how to prepare, shows the kinds of items that were asked, and gives the stable facts that those items hang on.

Two things stand out. First, the 2025 items are all facts that a daily newspaper reader would hold: one name, one date, one place. Second, the 2026 items reward knowing the field of a person or the mandate of an organisation, not a date.

Which months to cover

The 2025 paper (held 16 April 2025) drew on events from September 2023 (Aditya-L1 launch), October 2023 (Para Asian Games), February 2024 (Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam announced), November 2024 (G20 in Rio) and February 2025 (Union Budget, presented 1 February 2025). That is a spread of about 18 months before the exam, with most items in the last 12.

Working rule:

  1. Cover the 12 months before the exam date in full detail.
  2. Cover the 12 months before that in outline (only the headline events in each bucket below).
  3. Treat the last two weeks before the exam as a revision window; do not start new reading then.
  4. A static anchor (a Constitution article, a convention, a date of a scheme) is worth more than any single news item. Spend a third of your time on the stable skeleton below.

Keep one page per month. For each item write only the fields that an examiner can turn into a question.

How examiners frame the question

  • The question repeats a well-known term and asks one attribute of it (date, city, person).
  • Options are neighbours: adjacent months, adjacent host cities, predecessor and successor.
  • Sometimes the stem itself is imprecise. In 2025 Q3 the stem says the SEBI chair was appointed in 2024, and the official key is Madhabi Puri Buch, who in fact took charge in March 2022 and served until February 2025. When a stem and your memory disagree on a date, pick the option that matches the person or event, not the date.
  • Match-the-list questions reward one certain pair: fix it, eliminate options, and the rest follows.

A daily method that fits in 30 minutes

  1. Read one newspaper’s national and international pages for headlines only.
  2. Make one line per item in the eight buckets above.
  3. Every Sunday, convert the week’s lines into 10 flashcards (question one side, answer the other).
  4. Every month, take a 20-question self-test from your own cards.
  5. When a new item appears, link it to a stable anchor: “Aditya-L1 is the Sun mission; Chandrayaan-3 was the Moon landing, three weeks earlier”.

Worked examples

Example 1: A date question with neighbouring options

“When was India’s first solar mission, Aditya-L1, launched?” Options: September 2023, October 2024, June 2023, November 2023.

Anchor: Chandrayaan-3 landed on 23 August 2023, and Aditya-L1 followed days later (2 September). That removes June 2023 (before the Moon mission) and any 2024 date. Between September and November 2023, recall that the launch was within two weeks of the Moon landing. Answer: September 2023.

Example 2: A venue question

“Para Asian Games 2023 were held in: Beijing, Guangzhou, Hangzhou, Shanghai.”

Beijing hosted the 2008 Olympics, Guangzhou the 2010 Asian Games. The 2022 Asian Games, postponed to 2023, were awarded to Hangzhou, and the para edition used the same host city. Answer: Hangzhou.

Example 3: A stem with a doubtful date

“Who was appointed SEBI chairperson in 2024?” with Ajay Tyagi, Madhabi Puri Buch, U. K. Sinha, D. R. Mehta.

Ajay Tyagi (2017-2022) and U. K. Sinha (2011-2017) are earlier chairs; D. R. Mehta (1992-1995) was a much earlier one. Only Buch was in office in 2024. The stem’s year is loose, but only one option is a person who held the post then. Official key: Madhabi Puri Buch.

Example 4: Match the list using one certain pair

Match: a. D. Gukesh, b. Sam Altman, c. Demis Hassabis, d. Narges Mohammadi with I. AI research and DeepMind leadership, II. World Chess Championship, III. Human rights activism, IV. Generative AI industry leadership.

Gukesh is certain: a with II. Mohammadi is certain: d with III. That already fixes the options that begin a-II and end d-III. Hassabis (DeepMind) pairs with I and Altman (OpenAI) with IV. Mapping: a-II, b-IV, c-I, d-III.

Example 5: Budget question

“Under the Union Budget 2025-26, a social security scheme was proposed for the first time for: manufacturing workers, agricultural workers, online platform workers, women workers.”

Budget items are asked by their new beneficiary group. The year’s announcement was identity cards, e-Shram registration and PM-JAY health cover for gig and platform workers. Answer: online platform workers.

Common traps

  • Held year versus named year. The Asian Games 2022 were held in 2023. A question can use either label.
  • Adjacent hosts. 18th G20 is New Delhi, 19th is Rio, 20th is Johannesburg. Do not shift by one.
  • Moon versus Sun. Chandrayaan-3 (Moon, 23 August 2023) and Aditya-L1 (Sun, 2 September 2023) are only days apart.
  • Appointment year. An office-holder question may carry a year that is slightly off; match by person.
  • Organisation and city. WHO and WTO are both in Geneva; IAEA is in Vienna; UNEP is in Nairobi.
  • Cramming the last month. Items from 18 months before the exam appeared in 2025; do not read only the latest month.
  • Numbers and ranks. Rankings in reports change yearly. Learn the publisher and the measure, not the rank, unless the rank is a “first”.
