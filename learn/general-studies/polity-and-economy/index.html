Why it matters for the exam

This is the largest static block after ICT and environment. In 2025, six of the 30 GK questions came from it (Q23 and Q26 to Q30), and in 2026 six more (Q8, Q13, Q16, Q20, Q27, Q29). The questions are standard textbook facts: which article, which amendment, which list, which committee, which indicator. Several of the 12 asked an economic concept in sentence form (“identify the correct inference”), so the reasoning matters as much as the fact.

Core ideas

The making of the Constitution

  • The Constituent Assembly first met on 9 December 1946 (Rajendra Prasad later became its president).
  • The Drafting Committee was chaired by B. R. Ambedkar.
  • The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950.
  • It had 395 articles, 22 parts and 8 schedules at the start; it now has 12 schedules.
  • Sources borrowed: the UK (parliamentary system, rule of law), the US (fundamental rights, judicial review, Vice-President), Ireland (directive principles), Canada (federation with a strong Centre, residuary powers), Australia (concurrent list), Weimar Germany (emergency provisions), the USSR (fundamental duties), South Africa (amendment procedure).

The Preamble

It declares India a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic. The words “socialist”, “secular” and “integrity” were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976). It secures justice, liberty, equality and fraternity.

Fundamental rights (Part III, Articles 12-35)

Group Articles Content
Equality 14-18 Equality before law; no discrimination; equal opportunity in public jobs; abolition of untouchability (17) and titles (18)
Freedom 19-22 Six freedoms in 19; protection in conviction (20); life and personal liberty (21); arrest safeguards (22)
Against exploitation 23-24 No trafficking or forced labour; no child labour in hazardous work
Religion 25-28 Freedom of conscience and religion
Cultural and educational 29-30 Minority rights
Constitutional remedies 32 Right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement
  • Article 21A: the right to education for children aged 6 to 14, inserted by the 86th Amendment (2002); the Right to Education Act came in 2009 and was enforced from 1 April 2010. Article 45 is a directive principle (early childhood care and education below six), not the fundamental right.
  • The right to property was removed from Part III by the 44th Amendment (1978) and became a legal right under Article 300A.
  • Ambedkar called Article 32 “the heart and soul” of the Constitution.
  • Writs: habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto. The Supreme Court issues them under Article 32 and the High Courts under Article 226 (which also covers “any other purpose”).

Directive principles and fundamental duties

  • Directive principles: Part IV, Articles 36-51; not enforceable in court; they guide the State. Ireland is the source.
  • Fundamental duties: Part IVA, Article 51A, inserted by the 42nd Amendment (1976) on the Swaran Singh Committee’s recommendation; there were ten and the 86th Amendment (2002) added the eleventh (parents’ duty to educate children aged 6-14).

Union, States and the Seventh Schedule

List Examples
Union Defence, foreign affairs, railways, currency, banking, atomic energy
State Police, public order, public health and sanitation, agriculture, land, local government
Concurrent Education, forests, weights and measures, protection of wild animals and birds, administration of justice, criminal law, marriage and divorce, population control

The 42nd Amendment (1976) moved five subjects from the State to the Concurrent List: education, forests, weights and measures, protection of wild animals and birds, and administration of justice. Public health and sanitation stayed on the State List, which is why it is the odd one out in the 2025 question.

Parliament, executive and judiciary

  • President (Article 52), Vice-President (63), Prime Minister and council of ministers (74-75), Attorney General (76).
  • Council of ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (Article 75(3)).
  • Lok Sabha: directly elected, five-year term, maximum strength 550. Rajya Sabha: indirectly elected, maximum 250, a permanent house with one-third retiring every two years.
  • The Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Supreme Court: Article 124; retirement at 65. High Court judges retire at 62.
  • Independent bodies: Election Commission (324), UPSC (315), CAG (148), Finance Commission (280).
  • Emergencies: national (352), state (356, President’s rule), financial (360).

Judicial review

Judicial review is the courts’ power to test laws and executive action against the Constitution. Its sources are Articles 13, 32 and 226 (with 131, 132 and 136 supporting). Article 13 makes laws void to the extent they violate fundamental rights. It covers legislation and executive action, and, since Kesavananda Bharati (1973), amendments that damage the basic structure. So the statements “limited to constitutional amendments only” and “excludes review of executive actions” are false.

Important amendments

Amendment Year Subject
1st 1951 Ninth Schedule created
26th 1971 Privy purses abolished
42nd 1976 “Mini-Constitution”: Preamble words, fundamental duties, subjects shifted to Concurrent List
44th 1978 Right to property removed from fundamental rights
52nd 1985 Anti-defection law, Tenth Schedule
61st 1988 Voting age reduced from 21 to 18
73rd 1992 Panchayati Raj, Part IX, Eleventh Schedule
74th 1992 Municipalities, Part IXA, Twelfth Schedule
86th 2002 Article 21A; Article 45 changed; duty 51A(k)
97th 2011 Cooperative societies given constitutional status
99th 2014 National Judicial Appointments Commission (struck down in 2015)
101st 2016 GST: Article 246A, GST Council under Article 279A
103rd 2019 10 percent reservation for economically weaker sections
106th 2023 One-third reservation for women in the Lok Sabha and Assemblies

The 97th Amendment added the right to form cooperative societies to Article 19(1)(c), a directive principle in Article 43B and Part IXB. In 2021 the Supreme Court struck down Part IXB for cooperative societies within States, keeping it for multi-State societies.

Local government

The 73rd and 74th Amendments came into force in 1993. They give a three-tier Panchayati Raj (village, intermediate, district), regular five-year terms, State Election Commissions, State Finance Commissions every five years and one-third seats for women. The Eleventh Schedule lists 29 subjects and the Twelfth lists 18. The Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) proposed the three-tier model.

Federal finance

  • The Finance Commission (Article 280) is a constitutional body appointed by the President every five years. It recommends how the net tax proceeds are shared between the Union and States (vertical balance) and among States (horizontal balance), and the grants-in-aid under Article 275.
  • The first Finance Commission was constituted in 1951 (K. C. Neogy). The 14th Commission recommended a 42 percent State share and the 15th recommended 41 percent. The 16th Commission (chair Arvind Panagariya, period 2026-31) kept the share at 41 percent.
  • The Planning Commission (1950) was not constitutional; it was set up by a Cabinet resolution and prepared the Five-Year Plans (first Plan 1951-56). It was replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 January 2015, a policy think-tank chaired by the Prime Minister. NITI Aayog does not allocate tax devolution.
  • GST: introduced on 1 July 2017 after the 101st Amendment; the GST Council is chaired by the Union Finance Minister and includes State finance ministers.

Economy: indicators

Indicator Meaning
GDP Total value of final goods and services produced within the country in a year
GNP GDP plus net income from abroad
Fiscal deficit Gap between total government expenditure and total receipts excluding borrowing (the borrowing requirement)
Revenue deficit Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts
Primary deficit Fiscal deficit minus interest payments
HDI (UNDP) Composite of health (life expectancy), education (schooling) and income per head
Multidimensional Poverty Index Deprivation in health, education and living standards
Poverty line Minimum consumption expenditure threshold needed to meet basic needs

In the 2026 question the “multidimensional welfare indicator” is the Human Development Index. India’s poverty line has been set by expert committees (Lakdawala, Tendulkar, Rangarajan).

Unemployment

Type Meaning
Disguised More workers than needed; removing some does not change output (typical in agriculture)
Seasonal Work available only in some seasons
Structural Mismatch between skills and available jobs
Frictional Between jobs
Cyclical Falls in demand in a downturn
Underemployment Working fewer hours or below capacity

The 2025 question defines disguised unemployment: the person’s presence or absence makes no difference.

Inflation and monetary policy

  • Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level. Demand-pull inflation comes from excess demand; cost-push inflation comes from higher input costs. Indices: CPI (consumer) and WPI (wholesale).
  • RBI (established 1 April 1935, Mumbai, nationalised 1949) controls money supply with the repo rate, cash reserve ratio, statutory liquidity ratio and open market operations.
  • A tight (contractionary) monetary policy raises interest rates and limits money supply, which reduces demand and inflation. An expansionary policy does the opposite.
  • The inflation target is 4 percent with a tolerance band of 2 to 6 percent, set within a monetary policy committee framework.
  • Higher interest rates reduce, not raise, inflation. Large fiscal deficits can add to inflation; they do not “always eliminate” it.

Demographic dividend and Covid

  • The demographic dividend is the growth potential from a larger share of working-age people and a falling dependency ratio. It becomes real only with jobs, skills and health. It does not eliminate poverty automatically, and it does not mean a larger elderly population.
  • In 2020-21 the Covid shock hit services (trade, hotels, transport) hardest, then industry; agriculture kept growing. So the worst-to-least order is services, industry, agriculture.
  • The Union Budget 2025-26 (presented on 1 February 2025) proposed, for the first time, a social security scheme for online platform (gig) workers: identity cards, registration on the e-Shram portal and health cover.

Formulas and results

  • Constitution in force: 26 January 1950; adopted 26 November 1949; Drafting Committee chair Ambedkar.
  • Fundamental rights Articles 12-35; Article 21A education (86th Amendment, 2002); Article 32 remedies; Article 13 judicial review.
  • Directive principles Articles 36-51; duties Article 51A (42nd Amendment, 1976).
  • Concurrent List: education, forests, weights and measures, wild animals, administration of justice (all moved in 1976). State List: public health.
  • Finance Commission: Article 280, vertical plus horizontal balance. Planning Commission 1950 to 2014; NITI Aayog from 2015.
  • Amendment-to-subject: 97th cooperatives; 101st GST; 73rd and 74th local bodies; 42nd duties; 26th privy purses.
  • Fiscal deficit = total expenditure minus receipts excluding borrowing.
  • Disguised unemployment: zero marginal contribution. Tight monetary policy reduces inflation.

Worked examples

Example 1: Find the odd one out in the Concurrent List

Options: Education, Administration of justice, Weights and measures, Public health. Three of these were moved to the Concurrent List by the 42nd Amendment (1976). Public health and sanitation sits in the State List, so it is the answer.

Example 2: Article for the right to education

Options: Article 21(A), 45, 19, 32. Article 21A is the fundamental right (86th Amendment). Article 45 is a directive principle, Article 19 is freedoms, Article 32 is remedies. Answer: 21A.

Example 3: Chronological order

Order: Finance Commission, Privy Purses abolished, Fundamental Duties, GST Amendment. The first Finance Commission was set up in 1951, privy purses were abolished in 1971 (26th), duties came in 1976 (42nd) and GST in 2016 (101st). The order is b, c, a, d.

Example 4: Match the indicators

Poverty line: minimum consumption threshold. HDI: multidimensional welfare indicator. GDP: total value of goods and services. Fiscal deficit: gap between expenditure and revenue. Reading the options for these four pairs gives the pairing poverty line to IV, HDI to III, GDP to I, fiscal deficit to II.

Example 5: Sector order in the pandemic year

Sector growth in 2020-21: services and industry fell, agriculture grew. A question on “most affected first” therefore starts with services and industry, and agriculture is last. Pick the option in that order, and treat agriculture-first options as wrong.

Example 6: Evaluate the inflation statements

“Fiscal deficits always eliminate inflation” is false because “always” fails and deficits can add demand. “Expansionary policy reduces prices” is false. “Higher rates increase inflation” is false. Only “tight monetary policy reduces inflation by limiting money supply” is correct.

Common traps

  • Article 21A versus Article 45. One is a fundamental right; the other a directive principle.
  • Finance Commission versus Planning Commission. The first is constitutional and shares taxes; the second was an executive body that made plans.
  • NITI Aayog and devolution. NITI Aayog does not allocate tax revenue.
  • 96th, 97th, 99th, 101st. Co-operatives is the 97th, NJAC the 99th and GST the 101st. The 96th changed the name Oriya to Odia in the Eighth Schedule.
  • Education on the State List. It was on the State List until 1976, and it is now Concurrent.
  • Fiscal deficit versus revenue deficit. Fiscal deficit counts total spending against receipts without borrowing.
  • “Always”, “only”, “automatically”. Absolute words usually make an economics statement false.
  • Judicial review is limited to amendments. It is not; it also covers executive action and ordinary laws.

Quick revision

  • Part III Articles 12-35; Article 21A education (86th, 2002); Article 32 writs.
