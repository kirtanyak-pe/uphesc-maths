Eligibility

The conditions below are taken from the commission’s notices as repeated by several coaching sites. Check the current notification before you apply, because details change between advertisements.

  • Degree: a Master’s degree in the subject (or an allied subject as the notice allows) with at least 55 per cent marks or an equivalent grade. Reserved categories get a lower cut-off as the notice states.
  • Test or doctorate: NET (UGC or CSIR), SLET or SET qualification, or a PhD that the UGC regulations in force accept in place of it.
  • Age limit: sources disagree (one gives an upper limit of 62 years for this commission, while 21 to 40 appears for the different UP Public Service Commission). We could not confirm it, so read the age clause and the relaxations in the notification itself.
  • Where to check: the commission’s own website (upessc.up.gov.in) and the notification PDF.

Paper pattern and marking

The new pattern for Advt 04/2026 (exam on 19 and 20 November 2026):

Item Value
Questions 120, all multiple choice, four options
Parts 30 General Knowledge; 90 Mathematics
Marks 3 per correct answer, total 360
Negative marking 1 mark deducted per wrong answer
Time 2 hours
After the written test Interview

This pattern is reported the same way by several job and coaching sites (Testbook, Adda247, Dainik Jagran and others), but we could not read it in the official notification. The admit card (due from 15 November) and the instructions printed on it are the final word. Check there:

  1. the number of questions and the marks per question;
  2. whether there is negative marking and how much;
  3. your shift and exam centre.

With negative marking, do not guess blind. A blind guess among four options is worth \(\tfrac14\cdot 3 - \tfrac34\cdot 1 = 0\) marks on average. If you can rule out even one option, a guess among the remaining three is worth \(\tfrac13\cdot 3 - \tfrac23\cdot 1 = \tfrac13\), so attempt it. Leave a question blank only when you cannot rule out anything.

The older pattern (the 2025 and 2026 papers, verified from the papers):

Item Value
Questions 100, all multiple choice, four options
Parts Q1 to Q30 General Studies; Q31 to Q100 Mathematics (70)
Marks 2 per correct answer, total 200 (GK 60, Maths 140)
Negative marking None
Time 2 hours, pen and OMR

The past papers on this site follow the older pattern. The topics asked do not change with the new pattern; only the number of questions and the marking do.

What the maths papers asked

The 70 maths questions were spread across the ten maths units almost evenly. No unit can be skipped. The official syllabus sheet adds that at least 6 questions should be asked from each unit, so with 90 maths questions in the new pattern expect about 9 per unit (never fewer than 6).

What changed between the two full papers: 2025 questions were mostly direct, one-concept questions. In 2026 many maths questions were chained (“first find X, then compute Y”), and Linear Algebra (10), Topology and Differential Geometry (8) and Integral Equations (6 in 2026) rose, while Analysis fell to 3. Prepare for both styles.

Time strategy

The new pattern puts 120 questions in 2 hours: one minute per question on average.

Block Questions Time Per question
General Knowledge 30 about 15 minutes about 30 seconds
Mathematics 90 about 100 minutes about 67 seconds
Spare about 5 minutes

Under the older pattern (100 questions) the split was about 20 minutes for GK and 99 minutes for 70 maths questions.

  • Do GK first, quickly. GK is 90 of the 360 marks, and ICT, environment and polity facts take seconds if you know them. Never spend more than 45 seconds on one GK question; a fact you do not know is not recalled by staring.
  • In Maths, take a first pass of about 50 seconds per question: answer what you can, mark any question that needs a long computation, and come back in a second pass.
  • A chained question usually has one step that eliminates two options. Look for it before computing everything.
  • For numerical answers, test the options against one simple case instead of deriving a formula.
  • Mark the answer sheet as you go. With negative marking, do not fill unanswered questions at random in the last minute; fill only those where you ruled out at least one option.

How to use this site

  • Exam guide (this page): pattern, marking, weights and the timetable.
  • Unit page: what the unit covers, how it is asked, the study order, a formula sheet and the high-yield list. Read the formula sheet at the start and again at the end.
  • Topic page: the full notes, then formulas, worked examples, common traps and the real past questions on that topic. Each topic has a priority label and an estimated study time.
  • Practice questions: 10 to 15 questions per topic in the exam’s style, with solutions. Attempt them without looking at the notes, then read the solution of every wrong answer.
  • Previous-year questions: treat these as the model for what is asked. 2025 answers follow the official key. 2026 answers are worked answers, and where a question has a defect the topic page says so.

A good loop for each topic is: read the notes, close them, attempt the quiz, re-read only the parts you got wrong, and add one line per mistake to your error log.

The last week

If you are following the two-week table above, keep to it; this list is for students who finished the 12-week timetable.

  • Seven to five days before: one more pass over each unit’s formula sheet and the high-yield list. Redo only the questions in your error log.
  • Four days before: re-solve the 2026 paper and the 2025 paper questions you got wrong.
  • Three days before: GK day. Go through the fact sheet in Unit 11 and the current-affairs notes for the last 12 months, then the GK quizzes.
  • Two days before: one timed full set (30 GK in 15 minutes, then maths), and look only at the errors afterwards.
  • One day before: light revision of formulas for 2 hours, no new topics, and check the admit card, ID proof, exam centre and travel time. Read the exam-day instructions printed on the admit card.
  • Exam day: do GK first, then Maths with the first-pass rule above, and keep the last minutes for the answer sheet.
