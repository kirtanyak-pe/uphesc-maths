Roots of algebraic and transcendental equations

14 questions. Pick an answer to see if it is right and why.

0 of 14 answered · 0 correct

  1. Question 1Easy
    Using bisection on \([1,2]\) for a root of \(x^3-x-1=0\), the second midpoint computed is

    Why

    First midpoint \(1.5\) gives \(f(1.5)=0.875>0\), so the root is in \([1,1.5]\). The second midpoint is \(1.25\). (\(1.375\) is the third midpoint.)
  2. Question 2Easy
    The least number of bisection steps that guarantees an error below \(10^{-3}\) for a root known to lie in an interval of length \(1\) is

    Why

    Need \(2^{-n}<10^{-3}\), i.e. \(n>\log_2 1000=9.97\), so \(n=10\).
  3. Question 3Easy
    The order of convergence of the secant method is

    Why

    The error satisfies \(e_{n+1}\approx Ce_ne_{n-1}\), so \(p^2=p+1\) and \(p=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). Order \(2\) is Newton-Raphson; order \(1\) is bisection and regula falsi.
  4. Question 4Easy
    Applying Newton-Raphson to \(f(x)=\dfrac1x-N\) gives the division-free iteration for \(1/N\):

    Why

    \(f'=-1/x^2\), so \(x-f/f'=x+x^2(1/x-N)=2x-Nx^2=x(2-Nx)\). Option C is the \(\sqrt{\,}\)-type iteration.
  5. Question 5Medium
    Newton-Raphson applied to \(x^2-10=0\) with \(x_0=3\) gives \(x_1=\)

    Why

    \(x_1=\tfrac12\left(3+\tfrac{10}{3}\right)=\tfrac{19}{6}\). (\(10/3\) is only \(N/x_0\); \(\tfrac{17}{6}\) would come from the wrong sign \(x+f/f'\).)
  6. Question 6Medium
    To find the root \(x=2\) of \(x^2-x-2=0\) by fixed-point iteration \(x_{n+1}=g(x_n)\) with \(x_0\) near \(2\), which choice of \(g\) converges?

    Why

    Compute \(g'(2)\): \(2x=4\); \(-2/(x-1)^2=-2\); \(\frac1{2\sqrt{x+2}}=\frac14\); \(2-2x=-2\). Only option C has \(\lvert g'(2)\rvert<1\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    Consider: (I) regula falsi always keeps a bracket on the root; (II) the order of convergence of regula falsi is \(2\); (III) the secant method needs no derivative of \(f\). Which are correct?

    Why

    Regula falsi keeps the bracket (I true) but is linear, order \(1\) (II false). The secant method replaces \(f'\) by a divided difference (III true).
  8. Question 8Medium
    Newton-Raphson is applied to \(f(x)=(x-1)^2\). If the current error is \(e_n\), the next error is

    Why

    \(x_{n+1}=x_n-\dfrac{(x_n-1)^2}{2(x_n-1)}=\dfrac{x_n+1}2\), so \(e_{n+1}=e_n/2\). Double root: only linear convergence, ratio \(\tfrac12\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    Assertion (A): Newton-Raphson converges quadratically near a simple root \(\alpha\) of \(f\). Reason (R): For \(g(x)=x-f(x)/f'(x)\), \(g'(\alpha)=0\) when \(f'(\alpha)\ne0\).

    Why

    \(g'=ff''/(f')^2\) vanishes at a simple root, so \(e_{n+1}=\tfrac12g''(\xi)e_n^2\): quadratic. R is the reason for A.
  10. Question 10Medium
    Match the method with its order of convergence. I. Bisection II. Newton-Raphson III. Secant IV. Fixed-point iteration with \(g'(\alpha)\neq0\) P. \(1.618\) Q. \(2\) R. \(1\) (linear, constant \(\lvert g'(\alpha)\rvert\)) S. \(1\) (error halves)

    Why

    Bisection: ratio \(\tfrac12\) (S). Newton: \(2\) (Q). Secant: \(1.618\) (P). Fixed point: linear with constant \(\lvert g'(\alpha)\rvert\) (R).
  11. Question 11Medium
    Applying Graeffe’s root-squaring once to \(x^2-5x+6\) (roots \(2,3\)) gives the polynomial in \(y\)

    Why

    \((y+6)^2-25y=y^2-13y+36\), with roots \(4=2^2\) and \(9=3^2\). Squaring the coefficients (\(25\)) is the usual slip.
  12. Question 12Hard
    Newton’s method for \(\sqrt1\) is \(x_{n+1}=\tfrac12(x_n+1/x_n)\). If \(x_n=1.01\), the next error \(x_{n+1}-1\) is approximately

    Why

    \(e_{n+1}=e_n^2/(2x_n)=10^{-4}/2.02\approx4.95\times10^{-5}\). Direct: \(x_{n+1}=\tfrac12(1.01+0.990099)=1.0000495\).
  13. Question 13Hard
    Newton-Raphson applied to \(f(x)=x^3-N\) with \(N=8\) and \(x_0=3\) gives \(x_1=\)

    Why

    \(x_1=\dfrac13\left(2x_0+\dfrac{N}{x_0^2}\right)=\dfrac13\left(6+\dfrac89\right)=\dfrac{62}{27}\approx2.296\).
  14. Question 14Hard
    Newton-Raphson is applied to a function with a root \(\alpha\) of multiplicity \(m=3\). The convergence near \(\alpha\) is

    Why

    \(g'(\alpha)=1-1/m=2/3\), so the order is \(1\) with ratio \(2/3\). Replacing \(f/f'\) by \(3f/f'\) restores quadratic convergence.
