  1. Question 1Easy
    The degree of precision of Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) rule is

    Why

    Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) rule is exact for all polynomials of degree \(\le3\) (the cubic term integrates exactly by symmetry) but not for \(x^4\). Its error contains \(f^{(4)}\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) rule requires the number of sub-intervals to be

    Why

    It fits a parabola through each consecutive triple of points, so intervals come in pairs. \(3\mid n\) is for the \(\tfrac38\) rule and \(6\mid n\) for Weddle’s rule.
  3. Question 3Easy
    The two-point Gauss-Legendre rule on \([-1,1]\) uses nodes and weights

    Why

    Nodes are the roots of \(P_2(t)=\tfrac12(3t^2-1)\), i.e. \(\pm1/\sqrt3\), with weights \(1\). Nodes \(\pm1\) with weights \(1,1\) give the trapezoidal rule, nodes \(\pm\sqrt{3/5}\) belong to the three-point rule, and nodes \(0,\pm1\) with weights \(\tfrac13,\tfrac43,\tfrac13\) give Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) rule.
  4. Question 4Medium
    Two-point Gauss-Legendre quadrature applied to \(\displaystyle\int_1^3\frac{dx}{x}\) gives

    Why

    Put \(x=t+2\) (\(\tfrac{b-a}2=1\)): \(\displaystyle\int_{-1}^1\frac{dt}{t+2}\approx\frac1{2-1/\sqrt3}+\frac1{2+1/\sqrt3}=\frac{4}{4-1/3}=\frac{12}{11}\approx1.0909\). The exact value is \(\ln3\approx1.0986\); \(\tfrac{10}9\) is Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) rule on the same interval and \(1\) the one-point (midpoint) rule.
  5. Question 5Easy
    The degree of precision of the \(n\)-point Gauss-Legendre quadrature rule is

    Why

    Choosing \(n\) nodes and \(n\) weights (\(2n\) parameters) allows exactness for degree \(\le2n-1\); e.g. \(2\) points give \(3\), \(3\) points give \(5\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    From the table \(x=1,2,3,4\), \(y=x^3=1,8,27,64\), the forward formula \(f'(x_0)=\dfrac1h\big[\Delta y_0-\tfrac12\Delta^2y_0+\tfrac13\Delta^3y_0\big]\) gives \(f'(1)=\)

    Why

    \(\Delta y_0=7,\ \Delta^2y_0=12,\ \Delta^3y_0=6\), so \(f'(1)=7-6+2=3\), equal to \((x^3)'=3x^2\) at \(x=1\). The sign pattern alternates; adding all the terms gives \(15\), stopping after \(\Delta y_0\) gives \(7\).
  7. Question 7Hard
    Trapezoidal values for one integral are \(T(h)=0.8\), \(T(h/2)=0.7\), \(T(h/4)=0.68\). The second Romberg extrapolation \(R_{2,2}\) is

    Why

    \(R_{1,1}=\dfrac{4(0.7)-0.8}3=0.6667\) and \(R_{2,1}=\dfrac{4(0.68)-0.7}3=0.6733\). Then \(R_{2,2}=\dfrac{16R_{2,1}-R_{1,1}}{15}=\dfrac{10.7733-0.6667}{15}=0.6738\). \(R_{2,1}\) and \(R_{1,1}\) are the first extrapolations only.
  8. Question 8Medium
    Using the error bound \(\dfrac{(b-a)h^4}{180}\max\lvert f^{(4)}\rvert\), the least number of sub-intervals for Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) rule to evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^1e^x\,dx\) with error below \(10^{-6}\) is

    Why

    \(\dfrac{e}{180n^4}<10^{-6}\Rightarrow n^4>\dfrac{e\times10^6}{180}=15102\Rightarrow n>11.09\). So \(n\ge12\), and \(12\) is even as the rule requires; \(n=11\) is not allowed (odd) and \(n=10\) is too small.
  9. Question 9Easy
    The central difference formula \(f'(x)\approx\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h}\) has truncation error of order

    Why

    Taylor expansion: the \(h\) terms cancel, leaving \(-\tfrac{h^2}6f'''(\xi)\). Forward and backward differences are \(O(h)\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    Weddle’s rule with \(h=1\) on seven points (\(n=6\)) applied to \(\displaystyle\int_0^6x^5\,dx\) gives

    Why

    \(\tfrac{3}{10}[0+5\cdot1+32+6\cdot243+1024+5\cdot3125+7776]=\tfrac3{10}\cdot25920=7776=\tfrac{6^6}{6}\): exact, because Weddle’s rule has degree of precision \(5\). Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) gives \(7788\), Simpson’s \(\tfrac38\) gives \(7803\) and the trapezoid \(8313\).
  11. Question 11Medium
    Simpson’s \(\tfrac38\) rule with \(h=1\) applied to \(\displaystyle\int_0^3\frac{dx}{1+x}\) gives

    Why

    Values \(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\tfrac14\): \(\tfrac38\big[1+3\cdot\tfrac12+3\cdot\tfrac13+\tfrac14\big]=\tfrac38\cdot\tfrac{15}4=\tfrac{45}{32}\approx1.406\). The exact value is \(\ln4=1.386\) (not the rule’s output); \(\tfrac{35}{24}\) is the trapezoidal value with \(h=1\).
  12. Question 12Hard
    Consider: (I) Weddle’s rule has coefficients \(1,5,1,6,1,5,1\) and needs \(n\) a multiple of \(6\); (II) Simpson’s \(\tfrac38\) rule has degree of precision \(3\); (III) the second column of the Romberg table equals Simpson’s \(\tfrac38\) rule. Which are correct?

    Why

    I and II are true. III is false: the second column \(R_{k,1}\) equals Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) rule (and the third column is Boole’s rule).
  13. Question 13Medium
    Assertion (A): For a convex function the midpoint rule over-estimates the integral. Reason (R): The midpoint-rule error \(I-M\) equals \(+\dfrac{(b-a)h^2}{24}f''(\xi)\), which has the opposite sign to the trapezoidal error and half its size.

    Why

    For \(f''>0\), \(I-M=+\tfrac{(b-a)h^2}{24}f''>0\), so \(M<I\): the midpoint rule under-estimates a convex function (the trapezoid over-estimates it). Hence A is false while R is true. Example: \(\int_0^1x^2dx=\tfrac13\), midpoint \(\tfrac14\), trapezoid \(\tfrac12\).
  14. Question 14Medium
    Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) rule for double integrals with \(h=k=1\) on \([0,2]\times[0,2]\) gives, for \(\displaystyle\int_0^2\!\!\int_0^2x^2y^2\,dx\,dy\),

    Why

    The weights are \(\tfrac{hk}9(1,4,1)\otimes(1,4,1)\). Since \(f=x^2\cdot y^2\) is a product, \(\tfrac19\big(0+4\cdot1+1\cdot4\big)^2=\tfrac{64}9\), exact because Simpson is exact for cubics in each variable. The trapezoidal rule on the four unit cells gives \(9\), and on one cell of size \(2\times2\) gives \(16\).
  15. Question 15Medium
    The Cotes numbers \(\tfrac18,\tfrac38,\tfrac38,\tfrac18\) (the \(n=3\) rule) are applied on \(x=0,1,2,3\) to \(\displaystyle\int_0^3x^4\,dx\). The result is

    Why

    \(3\cdot\tfrac18\,[0+3\cdot1+3\cdot16+81]=\tfrac{3\cdot132}8=49.5\). The exact integral is \(\tfrac{3^5}5=48.6\), so the rule is not exact for \(x^4\) (degree of precision \(3\)); the trapezoid gives \(57.5\).
