Why it matters for the exam

No UPESSC question in the three recovered papers is on this topic yet, but the syllabus names it and the paper has repeatedly drawn two to four numerical-analysis questions. The likely forms are: the degree of precision of Simpson’s rule, the number of sub-intervals each rule needs, a short numerical evaluation with trapezoidal or Simpson’s rule, the nodes of two-point Gaussian quadrature, and a Romberg step.

Core ideas

Numerical differentiation from interpolation

Replace \(f\) by its interpolating polynomial (Newton or Lagrange) and differentiate the polynomial. With \(x=x_0+ph\), \(\dfrac{d}{dx}=\dfrac1h\dfrac d{dp}\).

From Newton’s forward formula (use near the start of the table):

\(f'(x)=\frac1h\Big[\Delta y_0+\frac{2p-1}2\Delta^2y_0+\frac{3p^2-6p+2}6\Delta^3y_0+\cdots\Big],\) \(f''(x)=\frac1{h^2}\Big[\Delta^2y_0+(p-1)\Delta^3y_0+\frac{6p^2-18p+11}{12}\Delta^4y_0+\cdots\Big].\)

At a tabular point \(x=x_0\) (\(p=0\)):

\(f'(x_0)=\frac1h\Big[\Delta y_0-\frac{\Delta^2y_0}2+\frac{\Delta^3y_0}3-\frac{\Delta^4y_0}4+\cdots\Big],\) \(f''(x_0)=\frac1{h^2}\Big[\Delta^2y_0-\Delta^3y_0+\frac{11}{12}\Delta^4y_0-\frac56\Delta^5y_0+\cdots\Big].\)

From Newton’s backward formula (use near the end), at \(x=x_n\):

\(f'(x_n)=\frac1h\Big[\nabla y_n+\frac{\nabla^2y_n}2+\frac{\nabla^3y_n}3+\frac{\nabla^4y_n}4+\cdots\Big],\) \(f''(x_n)=\frac1{h^2}\Big[\nabla^2y_n+\nabla^3y_n+\frac{11}{12}\nabla^4y_n+\cdots\Big].\)

These are the operator series \(hD=\ln(1+\Delta)=-\ln(1-\nabla)\) and \(h^2D^2=[\ln(1+\Delta)]^2\).

Maximum or minimum from a table: differentiate the interpolation polynomial, set \(f'(x)=0\), solve for \(p\), then check the sign of \(f''\).

Finite-difference formulas with error terms

Formula Expression Error
Forward (2-point) \(f'(x)\approx\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\) \(-\dfrac h2f''(\xi)\): \(O(h)\)
Backward (2-point) \(f'(x)\approx\dfrac{f(x)-f(x-h)}{h}\) \(+\dfrac h2f''(\xi)\): \(O(h)\)
Central (3-point) \(f'(x)\approx\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h}\) \(-\dfrac{h^2}6f'''(\xi)\): \(O(h^2)\)
Three-point forward \(f'(x_0)\approx\dfrac{-3f_0+4f_1-f_2}{2h}\) \(+\dfrac{h^2}3f'''(\xi)\)
Second derivative \(f''(x)\approx\dfrac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{h^2}\) \(-\dfrac{h^2}{12}f^{(4)}(\xi)\): \(O(h^2)\)

The central formula is a factor \(h\) better than the one-sided ones because the \(O(h)\) terms cancel. Round-off versus truncation: decreasing \(h\) reduces truncation error but increases round-off error (division by small \(h\)); there is an optimal \(h\). Numerical differentiation is intrinsically less stable than integration.

Richardson extrapolation: if \(D(h)=f'+c_2h^2+c_4h^4+\cdots\) (central), then \(\dfrac{4D(h/2)-D(h)}3\) has error \(O(h^4)\).

Numerical integration: Newton-Cotes rules

Approximate \(I=\int_a^bf(x)\,dx\) by integrating the interpolating polynomial through equally spaced nodes \(x_i=a+ih\), \(h=\dfrac{b-a}{n}\), \(y_i=f(x_i)\).

Degree of precision (exactness degree) of a rule: the largest \(m\) such that it integrates all polynomials of degree \(\le m\) exactly. A rule exact for degree \(m\) but not \(m+1\) has error proportional to \(f^{(m+1)}\).

Rule Intervals needed Formula Error (composite over \([a,b]\)) Degree of precision
Trapezoidal any \(n\) \(\dfrac h2\big[y_0+2(y_1+\cdots+y_{n-1})+y_n\big]\) \(-\dfrac{(b-a)h^2}{12}f''(\xi)\) \(1\)
Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) \(n\) even \(\dfrac h3\big[y_0+4(y_1+y_3+\cdots)+2(y_2+y_4+\cdots)+y_n\big]\) \(-\dfrac{(b-a)h^4}{180}f^{(4)}(\xi)\) \(3\)
Simpson’s \(\tfrac38\) \(n\) multiple of 3 \(\dfrac{3h}8\big[y_0+3y_1+3y_2+2y_3+3y_4+3y_5+2y_6+\cdots+y_n\big]\) \(-\dfrac{(b-a)h^4}{80}f^{(4)}(\xi)\) \(3\)
Boole \(n\) multiple of 4 \(\dfrac{2h}{45}\big[7y_0+32y_1+12y_2+32y_3+7y_4\big]\) per panel \(-\dfrac{8h^7}{945}f^{(6)}(\xi)\) per panel \(5\)
Weddle \(n\) multiple of 6 \(\dfrac{3h}{10}\big[y_0+5y_1+y_2+6y_3+y_4+5y_5+y_6\big]\) per panel \(-\dfrac{h^7}{140}f^{(6)}(\xi)\) per panel \(5\)

Single-panel errors: trapezoid \(-\dfrac{h^3}{12}f''(\xi)\); Simpson \(\tfrac13\) (\(2\) intervals) \(-\dfrac{h^5}{90}f^{(4)}(\xi)\); Simpson \(\tfrac38\) (\(3\) intervals) \(-\dfrac{3h^5}{80}f^{(4)}(\xi)\).

Facts and reading of the table:

  • The trapezoidal rule is exact for linear functions, error \(\propto h^2\). Halving \(h\) divides the error by about \(4\).
  • Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) rule fits a parabola through three points. By symmetry it is also exact for cubics, so its error involves \(f^{(4)}\) (not \(f'''\)). Halving \(h\) divides the error by about \(16\).
  • Simpson’s \(\tfrac38\) rule fits a cubic through four points; it is exact for cubics too (degree of precision \(3\)), and its error constant is larger than that of the \(\tfrac13\) rule (\(\tfrac1{80}\) vs \(\tfrac1{180}\)). Both are used together when the number of intervals is odd.
  • Newton-Cotes with \(n\) intervals has degree of precision \(n\) if \(n\) is odd, and \(n+1\) if \(n\) is even.
  • Weddle’s rule is derived from the seven-point Newton-Cotes rule by changing a coefficient so that it becomes simpler; it is exact up to degree \(5\) and its error is of order \(h^6\) when composite.
  • Composite Weddle for \(n=12\) has coefficients \(1,5,1,6,1,5,2,5,1,6,1,5,1\) (the shared point between panels gets \(1+1=2\)).

Error bounds to size \(n\). To make the trapezoidal error \(<\varepsilon\): \(\dfrac{(b-a)^3}{12n^2}M_2<\varepsilon\) with \(M_2=\max\lvert f''\rvert\). For Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\): \(\dfrac{(b-a)^5}{180n^4}M_4<\varepsilon\).

Gaussian quadrature

Newton-Cotes uses equally spaced nodes. Gauss-Legendre chooses nodes and weights together (more freedom, \(2n\) parameters) to maximise precision. The \(n\)-point rule

\(\int_{-1}^1f(t)\,dt\approx\sum_{i=1}^nw_if(t_i)\)

has nodes = roots of the Legendre polynomial \(P_n\) and has degree of precision \(2n-1\).

\(n\) Nodes \(t_i\) Weights \(w_i\) Exact up to degree
\(1\) \(0\) \(2\) \(1\) (midpoint rule)
\(2\) \(\pm\dfrac1{\sqrt3}\approx\pm0.57735\) \(1,\ 1\) \(3\)
\(3\) \(0,\ \pm\sqrt{\tfrac35}\) \(\tfrac89,\ \tfrac59\) \(5\)

Two-point rule: \(\displaystyle\int_{-1}^1f\,dt\approx f\!\Big(-\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\Big)+f\!\Big(\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\Big)\), error \(\dfrac{f^{(4)}(\xi)}{135}\).

General interval. Substitute \(x=\dfrac{b-a}2t+\dfrac{b+a}2\):

\(\int_a^bf(x)\,dx=\frac{b-a}2\int_{-1}^1f\Big(\frac{b-a}2t+\frac{b+a}2\Big)dt\approx\frac{b-a}2\sum w_if(x_i).\)

Two Gauss nodes beat Simpson’s three nodes in precision per evaluation (both exact for cubics), but Gauss nodes are irrational and cannot reuse earlier points.

Romberg integration (basic level)

Romberg combines trapezoidal values with Richardson extrapolation. The trapezoidal rule has the error expansion \(T(h)=I+c_1h^2+c_2h^4+c_3h^6+\cdots\). Let \(R_{k,0}=T\big(\tfrac{b-a}{2^k}\big)\) and

\(R_{k,j}=\frac{4^jR_{k,j-1}-R_{k-1,j-1}}{4^j-1}.\)

  • \(R_{k,1}=\dfrac{4R_{k,0}-R_{k-1,0}}{3}\) is exactly Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) rule with step \(h_k\) (error \(O(h^4)\)).
  • \(R_{k,2}=\dfrac{16R_{k,1}-R_{k-1,1}}{15}\) is Boole’s rule (error \(O(h^6)\)).
  • \(R_{k,3}\) uses \(\dfrac{64R_{k,2}-R_{k-1,2}}{63}\), error \(O(h^8)\).

Each column eliminates one more power of \(h^2\), using only function values already computed for the trapezoidal sequence (halving \(h\) reuses old points). Works well for smooth integrands.

Formulas and results

  • \(f'(x_0)=\frac1h[\Delta y_0-\frac12\Delta^2y_0+\frac13\Delta^3y_0-\cdots]\); \(\ f''(x_0)=\frac1{h^2}[\Delta^2y_0-\Delta^3y_0+\frac{11}{12}\Delta^4y_0-\cdots]\).
  • \(f'(x_n)=\frac1h[\nabla y_n+\frac12\nabla^2y_n+\frac13\nabla^3y_n+\cdots]\); \(\ f''(x_n)=\frac1{h^2}[\nabla^2y_n+\nabla^3y_n+\frac{11}{12}\nabla^4y_n+\cdots]\).
  • Central: \(f'\approx\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h}\), error \(-\frac{h^2}6f'''\); \(\ f''\approx\frac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{h^2}\), error \(-\frac{h^2}{12}f^{(4)}\).
  • Trapezoid: \(\frac h2[y_0+2\sum y_i+y_n]\); error \(-\frac{(b-a)h^2}{12}f''\); precision \(1\).
  • Simpson \(\frac13\): \(\frac h3[y_0+4\sum_{\text{odd}}+2\sum_{\text{even}}+y_n]\) (\(n\) even); error \(-\frac{(b-a)h^4}{180}f^{(4)}\); precision \(3\).
  • Simpson \(\frac38\): \(\frac{3h}8[y_0+3y_1+3y_2+2y_3+\cdots]\) (\(3\mid n\)); error \(-\frac{(b-a)h^4}{80}f^{(4)}\); precision \(3\).
  • Weddle: \(\frac{3h}{10}[1,5,1,6,1,5,1]\) (\(6\mid n\)); precision \(5\), error \(-\frac{h^7}{140}f^{(6)}\).
  • Gauss-Legendre \(n\) points: precision \(2n-1\); \(2\)-point nodes \(\pm\frac1{\sqrt3}\), weights \(1,1\); \(3\)-point nodes \(0,\pm\sqrt{3/5}\), weights \(\frac89,\frac59\).
  • Romberg: \(R_{k,j}=\dfrac{4^jR_{k,j-1}-R_{k-1,j-1}}{4^j-1}\); first extrapolation = Simpson \(\frac13\).

Worked examples

Example 1: derivatives from a difference table

\(x=1,2,3,4,5\) and \(y=x^4=1,16,81,256,625\). \(\Delta y_0=15,\ \Delta^2y_0=50,\ \Delta^3y_0=60,\ \Delta^4y_0=24\) (\(h=1\)).

\(f'(1)=15-\dfrac{50}2+\dfrac{60}3-\dfrac{24}4=15-25+20-6=4\) (exact: \(4x^3=4\)). \(f''(1)=50-60+\dfrac{11}{12}\cdot24=50-60+22=12\) (exact: \(12x^2=12\)).

At the end: \(\nabla y_5=369,\ \nabla^2y_5=194,\ \nabla^3y_5=84,\ \nabla^4y_5=24\). \(f'(5)=369+97+28+6=500\) (exact \(4\cdot125=500\)).

Example 2: central difference

For \(f=\sin x\) at \(x=1\), \(h=0.1\): \(\dfrac{\sin1.1-\sin0.9}{0.2}=0.53940\); true \(\cos1=0.54030\). The computed value is \(9\times10^{-4}\) below the truth. This matches the leading error term: \(D(h)=f'+\tfrac{h^2}6f'''+\cdots\) and \(f'''(1)=-\cos1\), so \(D-f'\approx-\tfrac{0.01}6(0.5403)=-9\times10^{-4}\). The error is \(O(h^2)\).

Example 3: one integral by four rules

\(I=\int_0^6\dfrac{dx}{1+x^2}=\arctan6=1.40565\). Take \(h=1\): \(y=1,\ 0.5,\ 0.2,\ 0.1,\ 0.05882,\ 0.03846,\ 0.02703\).

Rule Value
Trapezoidal \(1.41080\)
Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) \(1.36617\)
Simpson’s \(\tfrac38\) \(1.35708\)
Weddle \(1.37345\)

The step \(h=1\) is coarse for this function (large fourth and sixth derivatives near \(0\)), so even the higher-order rules are off; the point of the example is the method, not the accuracy. Weddle: \(\tfrac3{10}[1+5(0.5)+0.2+6(0.1)+0.05882+5(0.03846)+0.02703]=\tfrac3{10}(4.5782)=1.37345\).

Example 4: how many intervals

Trapezoidal rule for \(\int_0^1e^x\,dx\) with error \(<10^{-4}\): \(\dfrac{(1)h^2}{12}e<10^{-4}\), so \(h^2<4.4\times10^{-4}\), \(n>\sqrt{e/(12\times10^{-4})}=47.6\). Take \(n=48\). Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) for the same: \(\dfrac{e}{180n^4}<10^{-4}\Rightarrow n^4>151\), \(n>3.5\), so \(n=4\) (even). Simpson needs far fewer intervals.

Example 5: two-point Gauss

\(\int_0^2x^3\,dx\): \(x=t+1\), so \(\int_{-1}^1(t+1)^3dt\) with nodes \(\pm\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\) gives exactly \(4\) (degree \(3\) is integrated exactly). \(\int_0^1e^x\,dx\): \(x=\tfrac{t+1}2\), \(\tfrac12\big[e^{(1-0.57735)/2}+e^{(1+0.57735)/2}\big]=1.71790\) (true \(e-1=1.71828\)). Not exact at degree \(4\): \(\int_{-1}^1t^4dt=\tfrac25\) but the rule gives \(2\cdot\tfrac19=\tfrac29\), error \(\tfrac{24}{135}=0.1778\), equal to \(f^{(4)}/135\).

Example 6: Romberg

\(I=\int_0^1\dfrac4{1+x^2}dx=\pi\). \(T(1)=3\), \(T(\tfrac12)=3.1\), \(T(\tfrac14)=3.13118\).

\(R_{1,1}=\dfrac{4(3.1)-3}3=3.13333\) (this is Simpson with \(h=\tfrac12\)); \(R_{2,1}=\dfrac{4(3.13118)-3.1}3=3.14157\). \(R_{2,2}=\dfrac{16(3.14157)-3.13333}{15}=3.14212\), versus \(\pi=3.14159\).

Common traps

  • Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) is exact for cubics, not only quadratics; the error term has \(f^{(4)}\). Options saying “exact for degree 2 only” or “error \(\propto f'''\)” are wrong.
  • Degree of precision of \(\tfrac38\) is \(3\), of Weddle \(5\), of \(n\)-point Gauss \(2n-1\). Two-point Gauss is exact up to cubics, not quartics.
  • Simpson \(\tfrac13\) needs an even number of intervals (odd number of points); \(\tfrac38\) needs a multiple of \(3\); Weddle a multiple of \(6\). Trapezoid has no restriction.
  • Trapezoid error has \(h^2f''\) and a negative sign in \(I-T\) for convex \(f\): the trapezoid over-estimates convex functions.
  • Gauss-Legendre nodes are for \([-1,1]\): change the variable on \([a,b]\) and multiply by \(\dfrac{b-a}2\).
  • Forward-difference formulas for \(f'\) at \(x_0\) use \(\Delta y_0-\tfrac12\Delta^2y_0+\tfrac13\Delta^3y_0\): the alternating signs and \(1/k\) coefficients; backward has all plus signs. For \(f''\): \(\Delta^2-\Delta^3+\tfrac{11}{12}\Delta^4\) (forward), all plus (backward).
  • The central difference formula has error \(O(h^2)\), forward/backward \(O(h)\).
  • Romberg’s first column is the trapezoidal rule, the second column is Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\), not Simpson \(\tfrac38\).
  • Reducing \(h\) forever does not improve numerical differentiation (round-off).

Quick revision

  • Differentiate Newton’s formula: \(f'(x_0)=\frac1h[\Delta-\frac{\Delta^2}2+\frac{\Delta^3}3-\cdots]y_0\).
  • \(f''(x_0)=\frac1{h^2}[\Delta^2-\Delta^3+\frac{11}{12}\Delta^4]y_0\).
  • Central difference \(f'\): error \(O(h^2)\); forward/backward: \(O(h)\).
  • Trapezoid: precision \(1\), error \(-\frac{(b-a)h^2}{12}f''\).
  • Simpson \(\frac13\): even \(n\); precision \(3\); error \(-\frac{(b-a)h^4}{180}f^{(4)}\).
  • Simpson \(\frac38\): \(n\) multiple of \(3\); precision \(3\); error const \(\frac1{80}\).
  • Weddle: \(\frac{3h}{10}[1,5,1,6,1,5,1]\), \(n\) multiple of \(6\), precision \(5\).
  • Gauss-Legendre \(n\) points: precision \(2n-1\); \(2\)-point nodes \(\pm\frac1{\sqrt3}\), weights \(1\).
  • Change of interval: \(x=\frac{b-a}2t+\frac{b+a}2\), multiply by \(\frac{b-a}2\).
  • Romberg: \(R_{k,j}=\frac{4^jR_{k,j-1}-R_{k-1,j-1}}{4^j-1}\); \(R_{k,1}\) = Simpson \(\frac13\), \(R_{k,2}\) = Boole.
