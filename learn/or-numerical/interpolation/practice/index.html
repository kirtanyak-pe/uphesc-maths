  1. Question 1Easy
    The backward difference operator \(\nabla\) is related to the shift operator \(E\) by

    Why

    \(\nabla f(x)=f(x)-f(x-h)=(1-E^{-1})f(x)\). Option A is \(\Delta\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    Which relation between the operators is correct?

    Why

    \(\mu=\tfrac12(E^{1/2}+E^{-1/2})\), so \(\mu^2=\tfrac14(E+2+E^{-1})\) and \(\delta^2=E-2+E^{-1}\); hence \(\mu^2=1+\delta^2/4\). Numerical check for \(f=x^3\), \(x=2\), \(h=1\): \(\mu^2f=11=f+\delta^2f/4\).
  3. Question 3Easy
    For \(f(x)=2x^3-x+5\) with step \(h=0.5\), \(\Delta^3f(x)=\)

    Why

    \(\Delta^nf=a_n\,n!\,h^n=2\cdot6\cdot(0.5)^3=1.5\). (\(12\) forgets \(h^3\).)
  4. Question 4Medium
    A cubic polynomial takes the values \(1,\ 3,\ 9,\ y_3,\ 81\) at \(x=0,1,2,3,4\). Then \(y_3=\)

    Why

    Fourth differences vanish: \(y_4-4y_3+6y_2-4y_1+y_0=0\Rightarrow81-4y_3+54-12+1=0\Rightarrow y_3=31\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    A table gives \(f(0)=1,\ f(1)=2,\ f(2)=1,\ f(3)=10\). Newton’s forward formula gives \(f(1.5)=\)

    Why

    \(\Delta y_0=1,\ \Delta^2y_0=-2,\ \Delta^3y_0=12\), \(p=1.5\): \(1+1.5-0.75-0.75=1\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    For the same table (\(f(0..3)=1,2,1,10\)), Newton’s backward formula at \(x=2.5\) uses \(p=-0.5\) and gives

    Why

    \(\nabla y_3=9,\ \nabla^2y_3=10,\ \nabla^3y_3=12\): \(10-4.5-1.25-0.75=3.5\). (The interpolating cubic is unique, so forward and backward agree.)
  7. Question 7Medium
    The quadratic through \((0,1),\ (1,3),\ (3,13)\) takes the value ____ at \(x=2\).

    Why

    Lagrange: \(\ell_0(2)=\tfrac{(2-1)(2-3)}{(0-1)(0-3)}=-\tfrac13\), \(\ell_1(2)=\tfrac{(2-0)(2-3)}{(1-0)(1-3)}=1\), \(\ell_2(2)=\tfrac{(2-0)(2-1)}{(3-0)(3-1)}=\tfrac13\). \(P(2)=-\tfrac13+3+\tfrac{13}3=7\) (the data are from \(x^2+x+1\)).
  8. Question 8Medium
    The third divided difference of \(f(x)=2x^3+x\) at any four distinct points \(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3\) equals

    Why

    \(f[x_0,\dots,x_3]=f'''(\xi)/3!=12/6=2\), the leading coefficient, independent of the points. (Checked numerically for several node sets.)
  9. Question 9Medium
    Linear interpolation of \(e^x\) between the nodes \(0\) and \(1\) has error bounded for all \(x\in[0,1]\) by

    Why

    \(\lvert f-P_1\rvert\le\dfrac{M_2}{2!}\max\lvert x(x-1)\rvert=\dfrac e2\cdot\dfrac14=\dfrac e8\) with \(M_2=\max e^x=e\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    A Hermite interpolating polynomial that matches both \(f\) and \(f'\) at three distinct nodes has degree at most

    Why

    \(n+1=3\) nodes give \(2(n+1)=6\) conditions, so degree \(\le2n+1=5\).
  11. Question 11Hard
    The cubic Hermite polynomial with \(H(0)=1,\ H'(0)=0,\ H(1)=2,\ H'(1)=3\) has value ____ at \(x=\tfrac12\).

    Why

    Divided differences on nodes \(0,0,1,1\) give \(H=1+x^2+x^2(x-1)=1+x^3\); \(H(\tfrac12)=\tfrac98\).
  12. Question 12Hard
    The conditions that define a natural cubic spline through the data points \((x_i,y_i)\), \(i=0,\dots,n\), are \(S\in C^2\), \(S(x_i)=y_i\) and

    Why

    \(n\) cubics have \(4n\) coefficients; interpolation and \(C^2\) continuity give \(4n-2\) conditions; the natural end conditions \(S''=0\) at both ends supply the last two.
  13. Question 13Hard
    For the natural cubic spline through \((0,0),(1,1),(2,8),(3,27)\) the second derivative \(S''(1)\) is

    Why

    \(M_0=M_3=0\); \(4M_1+M_2=6(0-2+8)=36\) and \(M_1+4M_2=6(1-16+27)=72\). Solving: \(M_1=\tfrac{24}5\), \(M_2=\tfrac{84}5\).
  14. Question 14Hard
    Assertion (A): If \(y=f(x)\) is a polynomial of degree \(n\), then \(\Delta^{n+1}y=0\) for equally spaced data. Reason (R): Each application of \(\Delta\) lowers the degree of a polynomial by exactly one.

    Why

    \(\Delta^ny\) is a nonzero constant (\(a_nn!h^n\)), so one more \(\Delta\) gives \(0\). R is the mechanism.
