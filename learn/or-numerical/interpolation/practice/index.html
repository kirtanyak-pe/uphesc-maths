  1. Question 1Easy
    Which relation between the operators is correct?

    Why

    \(\mu=\tfrac12(E^{1/2}+E^{-1/2})\), so \(\mu^2=\tfrac14(E+2+E^{-1})\) and \(\delta^2=E-2+E^{-1}\); hence \(\mu^2=1+\delta^2/4\). Numerical check for \(f=x^3\), \(x=2\), \(h=1\): \(\mu^2f=11=f+\delta^2f/4\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    For \(f(x)=2x^3-x+5\) with step \(h=0.5\), \(\Delta^3f(x)=\)

    Why

    \(\Delta^nf=a_n\,n!\,h^n=2\cdot6\cdot(0.5)^3=1.5\). (\(12\) forgets \(h^3\).)
  3. Question 3Easy
    A cubic polynomial takes the values \(1,\ 2,\ 9,\ y_3,\ 65\) at \(x=0,1,2,3,4\). Then \(y_3=\)

    Why

    Fourth differences of a cubic vanish: \(y_4-4y_3+6y_2-4y_1+y_0=0\), so \(65-4y_3+54-8+1=0\) and \(y_3=28\). (The data are from \(x^3+1\).)
  4. Question 4Medium
    A table gives \(f(1)=3,\ f(2)=7,\ f(3)=13,\ f(4)=21\). Newton’s forward formula gives \(f(1.5)=\)

    Why

    \(\Delta y_0=4,\ \Delta^2y_0=2,\ \Delta^3y_0=0\) and \(p=0.5\): \(3+0.5\cdot4+\dfrac{0.5(-0.5)}2\cdot2=3+2-0.25=4.75\). \(5\) is the linear value (second difference ignored) and \(5.25\) has the wrong sign on the \(\Delta^2\) term.
  5. Question 5Medium
    A table with step \(h=2\) gives \(f(0)=1,\ f(2)=5,\ f(4)=17,\ f(6)=37\). Newton’s backward formula at \(x=5\) gives \(f(5)=\)

    Why

    Here \(p=\dfrac{5-6}{2}=-0.5\). \(\nabla y_3=20\) and \(\nabla^2y_3=8\), so \(37+(-0.5)(20)+\dfrac{(-0.5)(0.5)}2\cdot8=37-10-1=26\). \(17\) comes from using \(p=-1\) (forgetting to divide by \(h\)) and \(28\) from the wrong sign on the second-difference term.
  6. Question 6Medium
    The quadratic through \((0,1),\ (1,3),\ (3,13)\) takes the value ____ at \(x=2\).

    Why

    Lagrange: \(\ell_0(2)=\tfrac{(2-1)(2-3)}{(0-1)(0-3)}=-\tfrac13\), \(\ell_1(2)=\tfrac{(2-0)(2-3)}{(1-0)(1-3)}=1\), \(\ell_2(2)=\tfrac{(2-0)(2-1)}{(3-0)(3-1)}=\tfrac13\). \(P(2)=-\tfrac13+3+\tfrac{13}3=7\) (the data are from \(x^2+x+1\)).
  7. Question 7Medium
    The third divided difference of \(f(x)=2x^3+x\) at any four distinct points \(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3\) equals

    Why

    \(f[x_0,\dots,x_3]=f'''(\xi)/3!=12/6=2\), the leading coefficient, independent of the points.
  8. Question 8Easy
    The standard error bound \(\dfrac{M_2h^2}{8}\) for linear interpolation, with \(M_2=\max\lvert f''\rvert\) on the interval, for \(f(x)=e^x\) between the nodes \(0\) and \(1\) equals

    Why

    \(M_2=\max_{[0,1]}e^x=e\), \(h=1\), so \(\dfrac{M_2h^2}8=\dfrac e8\). It comes from \(\lvert f-P_1\rvert\le\dfrac{M_2}{2!}\max\lvert x(x-1)\rvert=\dfrac e2\cdot\dfrac14\). \(\dfrac e2\) forgets the factor \(\max\lvert x(x-1)\rvert=\tfrac14\) and \(\dfrac e{16}\) divides once too often.
  9. Question 9Medium
    A Hermite interpolating polynomial that matches both \(f\) and \(f'\) at three distinct nodes has degree at most

    Why

    \(n+1=3\) nodes give \(2(n+1)=6\) conditions, so degree \(\le2n+1=5\).
  10. Question 10Hard
    The cubic Hermite polynomial with \(H(0)=2,\ H'(0)=1,\ H(1)=3,\ H'(1)=0\) has value ____ at \(x=\tfrac12\).

    Why

    Divided differences on the nodes \(0,0,1,1\): \(f[0,0]=1\), \(f[0,1]=1\), \(f[1,1]=0\), \(f[0,0,1]=0\), \(f[0,1,1]=-1\), \(f[0,0,1,1]=-1\). So \(H=2+x+0\cdot x^2-x^2(x-1)=2+x+x^2-x^3\). Check: \(H(1)=3\), \(H'(0)=1\), \(H'(1)=0\). Then \(H(\tfrac12)=2+\tfrac12+\tfrac14-\tfrac18=\tfrac{21}8\).
  11. Question 11Hard
    For the natural cubic spline through \((0,0),(1,2),(2,0),(3,2)\) the second derivative \(S''(1)\) is

    Why

    \(M_0=M_3=0\) (natural), \(4M_1+M_2=6(0-4+0)=-24\) and \(M_1+4M_2=6(2-0+2)=24\). Solving: \(M_1=-8\), \(M_2=8\). \(-6\) comes from ignoring \(M_2\) in the first equation. The end conditions \(S''=0\) (not \(S'=0\)) are what make the system square.
  12. Question 12Medium
    The data \((x,y)=(1,2),(2,5),(3,10)\) come from \(y=x^2+1\). Inverse Lagrange interpolation (with \(y\) as the variable) of the value of \(x\) at which \(y=7\) gives

    Why

    Take the nodes \(y=2,5,10\) with values \(x=1,2,3\): \(\ell_0(7)=\dfrac{(7-5)(7-10)}{(2-5)(2-10)}=-\dfrac14\), \(\ell_1=\dfrac{(7-2)(7-10)}{(5-2)(5-10)}=1\), \(\ell_2=\dfrac{(7-2)(7-5)}{(10-2)(10-5)}=\dfrac14\). So \(x=-\tfrac14+2+\tfrac34=2.5\). The exact root is \(\sqrt6=2.449\); the \(y\) values are unequally spaced, so Newton’s equal-spacing formulas do not apply. \(2.4\) is the linear inverse interpolation between \(y=5\) and \(y=10\).
  13. Question 13Medium
    Neville’s method is used with the nodes \(x=1,2,4\) and values \(1,4,16\) (from \(y=x^2\)) to find the value at \(x=2.5\). The final value \(P_{012}(2.5)\) is

    Why

    \(P_{01}=\dfrac{(2.5-2)\cdot1-(2.5-1)\cdot4}{1-2}=5.5\), \(P_{12}=\dfrac{(2.5-4)\cdot4-(2.5-2)\cdot16}{2-4}=7\), \(P_{012}=\dfrac{(2.5-4)\cdot5.5-(2.5-1)\cdot7}{1-4}=6.25\). The quadratic through three points of \(x^2\) is \(x^2\) itself, and \(2.5^2=6.25\). \(5.5\) and \(7\) are the intermediate linear values.
  14. Question 14Easy
    For interpolation at a point \(x\) whose \(p=\dfrac{x-x_0}h\) is close to \(\dfrac12\), which central-difference formula is best suited?

    Why

    Bessel’s formula is built on the pair of central values \(y_0,y_1\) and is most accurate near \(p=\tfrac12\). Stirling’s formula is best for \(\lvert p\rvert\le\tfrac14\), near a tabular point. The Newton formulas are for the start or the end of the table.
  15. Question 15Hard
    Using \(\Delta^{-1}\) with factorial polynomials (\(h=1\), \(x^{(2)}=x(x-1)\), \(\Delta^{-1}x^{(2)}=\tfrac{x^{(3)}}3\)), the sum \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{10}k(k-1)\) equals

    Why

    \(F(x)=\dfrac{x^{(3)}}3\) and \(\sum_{k=1}^{10}f(k)=F(11)-F(1)=\dfrac{11\cdot10\cdot9}{3}-0=330\). \(990\) forgets the division by \(3\); \(385=\sum k^2\) and \(285=\sum_{k=1}^{9}k^2\) are different sums.
