Why it matters for the exam

Interpolation is the most frequently examined part of numerical analysis here: three questions in the two recent papers (divided difference of \(1/x\), a Lagrange polynomial from a table, Lagrange versus Hermite). Operator relations (\(E,\Delta,\nabla,\delta,\mu\)) and finite differences of polynomials are standard direct questions. Be able to compute a divided-difference table, a Lagrange polynomial and a Newton forward value quickly, and know the statements about Hermite and spline interpolation.

Core ideas

Finite difference operators

Let \(y_k=f(x_k)\) with equally spaced nodes \(x_k=x_0+kh\).

Operator Definition In terms of \(E\)
Shift \(E\) \(Ef(x)=f(x+h)\) \(E\)
Forward \(\Delta\) \(\Delta f(x)=f(x+h)-f(x)\) \(E-1\)
Backward \(\nabla\) \(\nabla f(x)=f(x)-f(x-h)\) \(1-E^{-1}\)
Central \(\delta\) \(\delta f(x)=f(x+\tfrac h2)-f(x-\tfrac h2)\) \(E^{1/2}-E^{-1/2}\)
Average \(\mu\) \(\mu f(x)=\tfrac12\big[f(x+\tfrac h2)+f(x-\tfrac h2)\big]\) \(\tfrac12(E^{1/2}+E^{-1/2})\)
Derivative \(D\) \(Df=f'\) \(E=e^{hD}\)

Relations to memorise.

  • \(\Delta=E-1\), so \(E=1+\Delta\); \(\ \nabla=1-E^{-1}\), so \(E^{-1}=1-\nabla\) and \(E=(1-\nabla)^{-1}\).
  • \(\nabla=\Delta E^{-1}=E^{-1}\Delta\), equivalently \(\Delta=\nabla E\).
  • \(\delta=E^{1/2}-E^{-1/2}\); \(\ \delta=\Delta E^{-1/2}=\nabla E^{1/2}\).
  • \(\Delta-\nabla=\Delta\nabla=\delta^2\). (Because \(\Delta\nabla=(E-1)(1-E^{-1})=E-2+E^{-1}=\delta^2\).)
  • \(\mu^2=1+\dfrac{\delta^2}{4}\), so \(\mu=\sqrt{1+\delta^2/4}\).
  • \(\mu\delta=\tfrac12(E-E^{-1})=\tfrac12(\Delta+\nabla)\).
  • \(E^{1/2}=\mu+\dfrac\delta2\).
  • With \(E=e^{hD}\): \(\ hD=\ln(1+\Delta)=-\ln(1-\nabla)=2\sinh^{-1}\dfrac\delta2\); \(\ \delta=2\sinh\dfrac{hD}2\); \(\ \mu=\cosh\dfrac{hD}2\); \(\ \mu\delta=\sinh(hD)\).

Series for derivatives: \(D=\dfrac1h\Big(\Delta-\dfrac{\Delta^2}2+\dfrac{\Delta^3}3-\cdots\Big)=\dfrac1h\Big(\nabla+\dfrac{\nabla^2}2+\dfrac{\nabla^3}3+\cdots\Big)\).

Higher differences: \(\Delta^nf(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^k\binom nk f\big(x+(n-k)h\big)\), and conversely \(y_n=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk\Delta^ky_0\). Also \(\nabla^ny_n=\Delta^ny_0\), and \(\delta^{2m}y_0=\Delta^{2m}y_{-m}\).

Product rule: \(\Delta(uv)=u\,\Delta v+Ev\,\Delta u\). Quotient: \(\Delta\dfrac uv=\dfrac{v\Delta u-u\Delta v}{v\,Ev}\).

Differences of polynomials

  • \(\Delta c=0\) for constants; \(\Delta\) lowers the degree of a polynomial by exactly one, so \(\Delta^k\) lowers it by \(k\).
  • If \(f\) is a polynomial of degree \(n\) with leading coefficient \(a_n\): \(\Delta^nf(x)=a_n\,n!\,h^n\ \text{(a constant)},\qquad\Delta^{n+1}f=0.\)
  • Conversely, if the \(n\)-th differences of a table are constant, the data come from a polynomial of degree \(n\) (assumption in “find the missing term” problems).
  • Factorial polynomial \(x^{(n)}=x(x-h)(x-2h)\cdots(x-(n-1)h)\) satisfies \(\Delta x^{(n)}=n\,h\,x^{(n-1)}\). This makes differencing as easy as differentiating.
  • \(\Delta a^x=a^x(a^h-1)\); \(\ \Delta\sin(ax+b)=2\sin\frac{ah}2\cos\big(ax+b+\frac{ah}2\big)\); \(\ \Delta e^{ax}=e^{ax}(e^{ah}-1)\).
  • With \(h=1\): \(\Delta^n\dfrac1x=\dfrac{(-1)^nn!}{x(x+1)\cdots(x+n)}\).

Difference tables and error detection

A forward difference table lists \(y_k\), then \(\Delta y_k\), \(\Delta^2y_k,\dots\) in columns. The same table read along the top diagonal gives forward differences \(\Delta^ky_0\), along the bottom diagonal gives backward differences \(\nabla^ky_n\), along the middle gives central differences.

Error propagation: an error \(\varepsilon\) in one entry spreads through the \(k\)-th differences as \(\varepsilon\cdot\binom{k}{j}(-1)^j\) (for \(k=3\): \(\varepsilon,-3\varepsilon,3\varepsilon,-\varepsilon\)). The column where noise dominates and alternates in sign shows the bad entry.

Missing term: if \(m\) values are known out of a polynomial table, use \(\Delta^{m}y=0\). For five values with one unknown, \(\Delta^4y_0=0\) gives \(y_4-4y_3+6y_2-4y_1+y_0=0\).

Newton’s forward difference formula

For \(x=x_0+ph\) (\(p=\frac{x-x_0}h\)), degree-\(n\) polynomial through \(x_0,\dots,x_n\):

\(y(x)=y_0+p\,\Delta y_0+\frac{p(p-1)}{2!}\Delta^2y_0+\frac{p(p-1)(p-2)}{3!}\Delta^3y_0+\cdots+\frac{p(p-1)\cdots(p-n+1)}{n!}\Delta^ny_0.\)

Use near the beginning of the table. Error: \(\ \dfrac{p(p-1)\cdots(p-n)}{(n+1)!}h^{n+1}f^{(n+1)}(\xi)\).

Newton’s backward difference formula

For \(x=x_n+ph\) (\(p=\frac{x-x_n}h\), negative inside the table):

\(y(x)=y_n+p\,\nabla y_n+\frac{p(p+1)}{2!}\nabla^2y_n+\frac{p(p+1)(p+2)}{3!}\nabla^3y_n+\cdots\)

Use near the end of the table. Compare signs: forward uses \(p(p-1)(p-2)\ldots\), backward uses \(p(p+1)(p+2)\ldots\).

Central difference formulas (statement level)

For interpolating near the middle of the table, with \(x=x_0+ph\):

  • Gauss forward: \(y_p=y_0+p\Delta y_0+\dfrac{p(p-1)}{2!}\Delta^2y_{-1}+\dfrac{(p+1)p(p-1)}{3!}\Delta^3y_{-1}+\cdots\)
  • Stirling (best for \(\lvert p\rvert\le\tfrac14\)): \(y_p=y_0+p\,\mu\delta y_0+\dfrac{p^2}{2!}\delta^2y_0+\dfrac{p(p^2-1)}{3!}\mu\delta^3y_0+\dfrac{p^2(p^2-1)}{4!}\delta^4y_0+\cdots\)
  • Bessel (best for \(p\) near \(\tfrac12\)): \(y_p=\dfrac{y_0+y_1}2+\big(p-\tfrac12\big)\Delta y_0+\dfrac{p(p-1)}{2!}\cdot\dfrac{\Delta^2y_{-1}+\Delta^2y_0}2+\cdots\)
  • Everett: an equivalent form using only even differences of \(y_0\) and \(y_1\).

Lagrange interpolation (unequal spacing allowed)

Given distinct nodes \(x_0,\dots,x_n\) and values \(y_i\), the unique polynomial of degree \(\le n\) through them is

\(P_n(x)=\sum_{i=0}^ny_i\,\ell_i(x),\qquad\ell_i(x)=\prod_{j\ne i}\frac{x-x_j}{x_i-x_j},\qquad\ell_i(x_k)=\delta_{ik}.\)

Also \(\sum\ell_i(x)=1\) (interpolating the constant \(1\)). Compact form: with \(\omega(x)=\prod(x-x_j)\), \(\ell_i=\dfrac{\omega(x)}{(x-x_i)\omega'(x_i)}\).

Drawback: adding one node forces recomputing everything. Newton’s divided-difference form avoids this.

Divided differences

\(f[x_i]=f(x_i),\qquad f[x_i,x_{i+1}]=\frac{f(x_{i+1})-f(x_i)}{x_{i+1}-x_i},\qquad f[x_0,\dots,x_k]=\frac{f[x_1,\dots,x_k]-f[x_0,\dots,x_{k-1}]}{x_k-x_0}.\)

Properties:

  • Symmetric in the nodes: any ordering gives the same value.
  • Explicit form: \(f[x_0,\dots,x_n]=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^n\frac{f(x_i)}{\prod_{j\ne i}(x_i-x_j)}\).
  • Mean value form: \(f[x_0,\dots,x_n]=\dfrac{f^{(n)}(\xi)}{n!}\) for some \(\xi\) in the node range. Hence an \(n\)-th divided difference of a polynomial of degree \(n\) equals its leading coefficient, and of degree \(<n\) equals \(0\).
  • Equal spacing: \(f[x_0,\dots,x_k]=\dfrac{\Delta^ky_0}{k!\,h^k}\).
  • Repeated nodes: \(f[x_0,x_0]=f'(x_0)\), \(f[x_0,x_0,x_0]=f''(x_0)/2\).

Newton’s divided-difference formula:

\(P_n(x)=f[x_0]+f[x_0,x_1](x-x_0)+f[x_0,x_1,x_2](x-x_0)(x-x_1)+\cdots+f[x_0,\dots,x_n]\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(x-x_j).\)

It is the same polynomial as Lagrange’s (uniqueness). Adding a node only adds one term.

Example: \(f(x)=1/x\). For nodes \(a,b,c,d\): \(f[a,b]=-\dfrac1{ab}\), \(f[a,b,c]=\dfrac1{abc}\), and in general \(f[x_0,\dots,x_n]=\dfrac{(-1)^n}{x_0x_1\cdots x_n}\). So the third divided difference is \(-\dfrac1{abcd}\).

Interpolation error

If \(f\in C^{n+1}[a,b]\) and \(P_n\) interpolates at \(n+1\) distinct nodes in \([a,b]\), then for \(x\in[a,b]\) there is \(\xi\in(a,b)\) with

\(f(x)-P_n(x)=\frac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}\,\prod_{i=0}^n(x-x_i).\)

Consequences: if \(f\) is itself a polynomial of degree \(\le n\) the interpolation is exact. Linear interpolation on \([x_0,x_1]\): \(\lvert f-P_1\rvert\le\dfrac{M_2}8h^2\), where \(M_2=\max\lvert f''\rvert\) (since \(\max\lvert(x-x_0)(x-x_1)\rvert=h^2/4\)). Error is small near the middle of the nodes and large outside (extrapolation). Equally spaced high-degree interpolation can oscillate (Runge phenomenon); Chebyshev nodes minimise the maximum of \(\lvert\prod(x-x_i)\rvert\).

Hermite interpolation

Given values and first derivatives at \(n+1\) distinct nodes (\(2n+2\) conditions), there is a unique polynomial of degree \(\le2n+1\) with \(H(x_i)=f(x_i)\), \(H'(x_i)=f'(x_i)\):

\(H(x)=\sum_{i=0}^n\big[h_i(x)f(x_i)+\hat h_i(x)f'(x_i)\big],\) \(h_i(x)=\big[1-2(x-x_i)\ell_i'(x_i)\big]\ell_i(x)^2,\qquad\hat h_i(x)=(x-x_i)\,\ell_i(x)^2.\)

Using divided differences: repeat each node twice (\(x_0,x_0,x_1,x_1,\dots\)) and use \(f[x_i,x_i]=f'(x_i)\) in Newton’s formula. The error is

\(f(x)-H(x)=\frac{f^{(2n+2)}(\xi)}{(2n+2)!}\prod_{i=0}^n(x-x_i)^2.\)

Hermite is an osculating polynomial: it touches \(f\) with matching slope, so for smooth functions it is more accurate than Lagrange interpolation on the same nodes (Lagrange uses values only and has degree \(\le n\) with error \(f^{(n+1)}\)).

Cubic splines

Polynomial interpolation of high degree can oscillate. A cubic spline \(S\) for knots \(x_0<\dots<x_n\) is piecewise cubic with

  1. \(S\) cubic on each \([x_i,x_{i+1}]\);
  2. \(S(x_i)=y_i\) (interpolation);
  3. \(S,\ S',\ S''\) continuous at the interior knots (\(S\in C^2\)).

Count: \(n\) cubics have \(4n\) coefficients; interpolation gives \(2n\) conditions; continuity of \(S'\) and \(S''\) at \(n-1\) interior knots gives \(2n-2\); total \(4n-2\). Two end conditions are still needed:

Type End conditions
Natural \(S''(x_0)=S''(x_n)=0\)
Clamped \(S'(x_0)=f'(x_0),\ S'(x_n)=f'(x_n)\)
Periodic \(S'(x_0)=S'(x_n)\), \(S''(x_0)=S''(x_n)\)

Moment form. Let \(M_i=S''(x_i)\) and let the knots be equally spaced with step \(h\). On \([x_i,x_{i+1}]\):

\(S(x)=\frac{M_i(x_{i+1}-x)^3+M_{i+1}(x-x_i)^3}{6h}+\Big(y_i-\frac{M_ih^2}6\Big)\frac{x_{i+1}-x}{h}+\Big(y_{i+1}-\frac{M_{i+1}h^2}6\Big)\frac{x-x_i}{h}.\)

Continuity of \(S'\) gives, for equal spacing \(h\), the tridiagonal system

\(M_{i-1}+4M_i+M_{i+1}=\frac6{h^2}\big(y_{i-1}-2y_i+y_{i+1}\big),\quad i=1,\dots,n-1,\)

with \(M_0=M_n=0\) for the natural spline. The system is diagonally dominant, so the spline exists and is unique.

Minimum curvature property: among all twice-differentiable functions through the data, the natural cubic spline minimises \(\int_{x_0}^{x_n}[g''(x)]^2dx\). For smooth \(f\) with clamped ends the error is \(O(h^4)\).

Formulas and results

  • \(\Delta=E-1\), \(\nabla=1-E^{-1}\), \(\delta=E^{1/2}-E^{-1/2}\), \(\mu=\tfrac12(E^{1/2}+E^{-1/2})\), \(E=e^{hD}\).
  • \(\Delta\nabla=\Delta-\nabla=\delta^2\); \(\ \mu^2=1+\delta^2/4\); \(\ \mu\delta=\tfrac12(\Delta+\nabla)\); \(\ \nabla=E^{-1}\Delta\).
  • \(hD=\ln(1+\Delta)=-\ln(1-\nabla)=2\sinh^{-1}(\delta/2)\).
  • Degree-\(n\) polynomial: \(\Delta^ny=a_nn!h^n\), \(\Delta^{n+1}y=0\). \(\Delta x^{(n)}=nhx^{(n-1)}\).
  • Newton forward: \(y_0+p\Delta y_0+\frac{p(p-1)}{2!}\Delta^2y_0+\cdots\), \(p=(x-x_0)/h\).
  • Newton backward: \(y_n+p\nabla y_n+\frac{p(p+1)}{2!}\nabla^2y_n+\cdots\), \(p=(x-x_n)/h\).
  • Lagrange: \(P=\sum y_i\prod_{j\ne i}\frac{x-x_j}{x_i-x_j}\).
  • Divided difference: \(f[x_0..x_n]=\sum\frac{f(x_i)}{\prod_{j\ne i}(x_i-x_j)}=\frac{f^{(n)}(\xi)}{n!}=\frac{\Delta^ny_0}{n!h^n}\).
  • Error: \(\dfrac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}\prod(x-x_i)\); Hermite: \(\dfrac{f^{(2n+2)}(\xi)}{(2n+2)!}\prod(x-x_i)^2\).
  • Hermite on \(n+1\) nodes has degree \(\le2n+1\).
  • Natural cubic spline: \(S\in C^2\), \(S''(x_0)=S''(x_n)=0\); \(M_{i-1}+4M_i+M_{i+1}=\frac6{h^2}\delta^2y_i\).

Worked examples

Example 1: missing term

A cubic polynomial takes values \(1,\ 3,\ 9,\ ?,\ 81\) at \(x=0,1,2,3,4\). Since the data are from a cubic, \(\Delta^4y_0=0\):

\(y_4-4y_3+6y_2-4y_1+y_0=0\), so \(81-4y_3+54-12+1=0\), i.e. \(4y_3=124\) and \(y_3=31\).

Check the table: \(1,3,9,31,81\to\Delta:2,6,22,50\to\Delta^2:4,16,28\to\Delta^3:12,12\to\Delta^4:0\). Consistent.

Example 2: Newton forward and backward

\(x=0,1,2,3\) and \(y=1,2,1,10\). Differences: \(\Delta y_0=1,\ \Delta^2y_0=-2,\ \Delta^3y_0=12\) (table: \(1,-1,9;\ -2,10;\ 12\)).

Forward at \(x=1.5\): \(p=1.5\). \(y=1+1.5(1)+\dfrac{1.5(0.5)}{2}(-2)+\dfrac{1.5(0.5)(-0.5)}{6}(12)=1+1.5-0.75-0.75=1\).

Backward at \(x=2.5\): \(p=-0.5\); \(\nabla y_3=9,\ \nabla^2y_3=10,\ \nabla^3y_3=12\). \(y=10+(-0.5)(9)+\dfrac{(-0.5)(0.5)}{2}(10)+\dfrac{(-0.5)(0.5)(1.5)}{6}(12)=10-4.5-1.25-0.75=3.5\).

Both are the same cubic \(y=1+x-x(x-1)+2x(x-1)(x-2)\).

Example 3: Lagrange, unequal spacing, and its error

Find \(f(3)\) from \((1,1),(2,8),(4,64)\) (data of \(x^3\)). \(\ell_0(3)=\dfrac{(3-2)(3-4)}{(1-2)(1-4)}=-\dfrac13,\ \ \ell_1(3)=\dfrac{(3-1)(3-4)}{(2-1)(2-4)}=1,\ \ \ell_2(3)=\dfrac{(3-1)(3-2)}{(4-1)(4-2)}=\dfrac13.\) \(P(3)=-\tfrac13+8+\tfrac{64}3=29\). True value \(27\). Error formula: \(f'''=6\), so \(f-P=\dfrac66(3-1)(3-2)(3-4)=-2\), matching \(27-29\).

Example 4: Newton divided differences, same data

\(f[1,2]=\dfrac{8-1}{1}=7,\quad f[2,4]=\dfrac{64-8}{2}=28,\quad f[1,2,4]=\dfrac{28-7}{4-1}=7.\) \(P(x)=1+7(x-1)+7(x-1)(x-2)\); \(P(3)=1+14+14=29\). Same as Lagrange.

Example 5: Hermite

Find the cubic with \(f(0)=1,\ f'(0)=0,\ f(1)=2,\ f'(1)=3\). Nodes \(0,0,1,1\): \(f[0,0]=0,\ f[0,1]=1,\ f[1,1]=3,\ f[0,0,1]=1,\ f[0,1,1]=2,\ f[0,0,1,1]=1.\)

\(H(x)=1+0\cdot x+1\cdot x^2+1\cdot x^2(x-1)=1+x^3.\)

Check: \(H(1)=2\), \(H'(x)=3x^2\), so \(H'(0)=0\), \(H'(1)=3\).

Example 6: natural cubic spline

Data \((0,0),(1,1),(2,0)\), \(h=1\). \(M_0=M_2=0\); \(M_0+4M_1+M_2=6(0-2+0)=-12\Rightarrow M_1=-3\).

On \([0,1]\): \(S(x)=-\dfrac{x^3}{2}+\dfrac{3x}{2}\). On \([1,2]\): \(S(x)=-\dfrac{(2-x)^3}{2}+\dfrac{3(2-x)}2\). Check: \(S(1)=1\) from both sides; \(S'(1^-)=-\tfrac32+\tfrac32=0\) and \(S'(1^+)=\tfrac32-\tfrac32=0\); \(S''(1)=-3\) from both sides.

For four points \((0,0),(1,1),(2,8),(3,27)\): \(4M_1+M_2=36\), \(M_1+4M_2=72\), so \(M_1=\tfrac{24}5\), \(M_2=\tfrac{84}5\).

Example 7: differences of a product

For \(f(x)=(1-x)(1-2x)(1-3x)\) the leading term is \(-6x^3\), so with \(h=1\): \(\Delta^3f=-6\cdot3!=-36\) (constant) and \(\Delta^4f=0\). Table check: \(f(0..4)=1,0,-15,-80,-231\), third differences \(-36,-36\).

Common traps

  • Backward formula has \(p(p+1)\ldots\); forward has \(p(p-1)\ldots\). Also \(p=(x-x_n)/h\) is negative for backward.
  • \(\mu^2=1+\delta^2/4\), not \(1+\delta^2\). \(\ \delta^2=\Delta\nabla\), not \(\Delta^2\).
  • \(\nabla=E^{-1}\Delta\) (not \(E\Delta\)). \(\ \Delta-\nabla=\delta^2\).
  • \(\Delta^ny\) for degree \(n\) is \(a_nn!h^n\): do not forget \(h^n\) (when \(h\ne1\)) or the factorial.
  • Divided differences are symmetric but not the same as finite differences: they carry \(1/(k!h^k)\).
  • A polynomial of degree \(\le n\) through \(n+1\) points is unique; Lagrange and Newton are different forms of the same polynomial.
  • Hermite with \(n+1\) nodes has degree \(2n+1\), not \(2n\) or \(n+1\).
  • For smooth functions Hermite (with derivative data) is more accurate than Lagrange on the same nodes. It is not “the same”.
  • Splines: continuity of \(S''\) (not just \(S'\)) is required; a natural spline has \(S''=0\) at the ends, not \(S'=0\).
  • Interpolation error is largest outside the nodes (extrapolation) and depends on \(\prod(x-x_i)\).
  • More nodes with equal spacing does not always reduce error (Runge).

2025-Q85 (official key: D)

Question. The third divided difference of the function \(f(x)=\dfrac1x\) for the points \(a,b,c,d\) is: (A) and (B) are expressions built from sums of terms such as \(\frac1{abc}\); (C) \(\dfrac1{abcd}\); (D) \(-\dfrac1{abcd}\). Answer: D. For \(f=1/x\): \(f[x_0,\dots,x_n]=\dfrac{(-1)^n}{x_0\cdots x_n}\). Induction: \(f[a,b]=\dfrac{1/b-1/a}{b-a}=-\dfrac1{ab}\), \(f[a,b,c]=\dfrac{-\frac1{bc}+\frac1{ab}}{c-a}=\dfrac1{abc}\), and similarly \(f[a,b,c,d]=-\dfrac1{abcd}\). Verified numerically for \((2,3,5,7)\): \(-\tfrac1{210}\).

Question. Applying Lagrange’s interpolation formula to the table \(x=0,1,2,5\), \(y=2,3,12,147\), the approximating polynomial for \(y(x)\) is: (A) \(x^3+x^2-x+2\) (C) \(x^3-x^2-x+2\); (B) and (D) are other cubics with different coefficients. 2026-Q88 (worked key: D; unofficial key)

Question. For uneven nodes, using Lagrange interpolation and Hermite interpolation that also uses derivative values, which gives higher accuracy for smooth functions? (A) Lagrange (B) Depends only on degree (C) Both the same (D) Hermite. Answer: D (Hermite). Hermite matches \(f\) and \(f'\) at each node, so with \(n+1\) nodes it has degree \(2n+1\) and error \(\dfrac{f^{(2n+2)}(\xi)}{(2n+2)!}\prod(x-x_i)^2\), versus \(\dfrac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}\prod(x-x_i)\) for Lagrange. The worked key marks D with high confidence.

Quick revision

  • \(\Delta=E-1\); \(\nabla=1-E^{-1}\); \(\delta=E^{1/2}-E^{-1/2}\); \(\mu=\tfrac12(E^{1/2}+E^{-1/2})\); \(E=e^{hD}\).
  • \(\Delta\nabla=\delta^2=\Delta-\nabla\); \(\mu^2=1+\delta^2/4\); \(\mu\delta=\tfrac12(\Delta+\nabla)\).
  • Degree-\(n\) polynomial: \(\Delta^ny=a_nn!h^n\), \(\Delta^{n+1}y=0\).
  • Newton forward: use near the start, \(p(p-1)\ldots\); backward: near the end, \(p(p+1)\ldots\).
  • Lagrange: unique polynomial of degree \(\le n\) through \(n+1\) points; unequal spacing allowed.
  • Divided difference \(f[x_0..x_n]=f^{(n)}(\xi)/n!\); symmetric; \(f[x_0,x_0]=f'(x_0)\).
  • \(f=1/x\): \(f[x_0..x_n]=(-1)^n/(x_0\cdots x_n)\).
  • Error \(=\dfrac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}\prod(x-x_i)\); linear interpolation error \(\le M_2h^2/8\).
  • Hermite: values and slopes, degree \(2n+1\), error uses \(\prod(x-x_i)^2\); more accurate than Lagrange for smooth \(f\).
  • Cubic spline: piecewise cubic, \(C^2\); natural: \(S''=0\) at both ends; \(4n-2\) conditions plus \(2\) end conditions.
