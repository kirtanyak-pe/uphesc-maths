  2. Question 2Easy
    For \(y'=x+y\), \(y(0)=1\), Euler’s method with \(h=0.1\) gives \(y(0.2)=\)

    Why

    \(y_1=1+0.1(0+1)=1.1\); \(y_2=1.1+0.1(0.1+1.1)=1.22\). (\(1.242\) is the exact value.)
  3. Question 3Easy
    The classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta formula is

    Why

    The weights are \(1,2,2,1\) (sum \(6\)). A is Kutta’s third-order rule; B and D have wrong weights.
  4. Question 4Easy
    The global error of the classical Runge-Kutta fourth-order method is of order

    Why

    A method of order \(p\) has global error \(O(h^p)\) and local error \(O(h^{p+1})\); for RK4, \(O(h^4)\) and \(O(h^5)\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The modified Euler (Heun) method for \(y'=x+y\), \(y(0)=1\), \(h=0.1\) gives \(y(0.1)=\)

    Why

    Predictor \(y^*=1+0.1(1)=1.1\). Corrector \(y_1=1+0.05[1+(0.1+1.1)]=1.11\). (\(1.1103\) is the exact value, \(1.1\) is plain Euler.)
  6. Question 6Medium
    The second Picard iterate \(y_2(x)\) for \(y'=x+y\), \(y(0)=1\) is

    Why

    \(y_1=1+\int_0^x(t+1)dt=1+x+\tfrac{x^2}2\). \(y_2=1+\int_0^x\big(t+1+t+\tfrac{t^2}2\big)dt=1+x+x^2+\tfrac{x^3}6\). (Option B is \(y_1\); C is the exact series to \(x^3\).)
  7. Question 7Medium
    Euler’s method applied to \(y'=-50y\) is absolutely stable (i.e. \(\lvert y_n\rvert\) does not grow) only for step size

    Why

    \(y_{n+1}=(1-50h)y_n\), stable iff \(\lvert1-50h\rvert<1\iff h<2/50=0.04\). (\(0.0557=2.785/50\) is the RK4 limit.)

  9. Question 9Medium
    For \(y'=x^2+y^2\), \(y(0)=1\), the Taylor series method with terms up to \(h^3\) gives \(y(0.1)\approx\)

    Why

    \(y'(0)=1\); \(y''=2x+2yy'\Rightarrow2\); \(y'''=2+2y'^2+2yy''\Rightarrow8\). \(y(0.1)\approx1+0.1+\tfrac{2}{2}(0.01)+\tfrac86(0.001)=1.11133\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    Match the method with the order of its global error. I. Euler II. Heun (modified Euler) III. Classical RK4 P. \(O(h)\) Q. \(O(h^2)\) R. \(O(h^4)\) S. \(O(h^5)\)

    Why

    Order \(p\) gives global error \(O(h^p)\): \(p=1,2,4\). (\(h^5\) is the local error of RK4.)
  11. Question 11Hard
    Milne’s predictor-corrector method is not self-starting because

    Why

    The predictor \(y_{n+1}=y_{n-3}+\tfrac{4h}3(\ldots)\) requires \(y_0,\dots,y_3\) before the first prediction of \(y_4\).
  12. Question 12Hard
    Assertion (A): For a stiff equation the explicit RK4 method still needs a small step size to remain stable. Reason (R): The region of absolute stability of RK4 on the negative real axis is bounded, about \(-2.785<h\lambda<0\).

    Why

    A fast-decaying mode with large \(\lvert\lambda\rvert\) forces \(h\lvert\lambda\rvert<2.785\), however smooth the solution is. Implicit methods (backward Euler) have unbounded stability regions on \(\lambda<0\).
  13. Question 13Hard
    Consider: (I) a method of order \(p\) has local truncation error \(O(h^{p+1})\); (II) Euler’s method has local error \(O(h)\); (III) halving \(h\) in RK4 reduces the global error by a factor of about \(16\). Which are correct?

    Why

    I is correct. II is false: Euler’s local error is \(O(h^2)\) and its global error is \(O(h)\). III is correct (\(2^4=16\)).
