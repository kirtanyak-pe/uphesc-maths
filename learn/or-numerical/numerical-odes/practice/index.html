  1. Question 1Easy
    Euler’s method for \(y'=f(x,y)\), \(y(x_0)=y_0\) with step \(h\) is

    Why

    Euler follows the tangent at the old point. Evaluating \(f\) at \((x_{n+1},y_{n+1})\) gives backward (implicit) Euler, the averaged form is the implicit trapezoid rule, and \(y_{n-1}+2hf(x_n,y_n)\) is the leapfrog (explicit midpoint) rule.
  2. Question 2Medium
    For \(y'=y-x\), \(y(0)=2\), Euler’s method with \(h=0.2\) gives \(y(0.4)=\)

    Why

    \(y_1=2+0.2(2-0)=2.4\); \(y_2=2.4+0.2(2.4-0.2)=2.84\). One step gives \(2.4\); using \(x_{n+1}\) in \(f\) gives \(2.752\); a single step of \(h=0.4\) gives \(2.8\).
  3. Question 3Easy
    The classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta formula is

    Why

    The weights are \(1,2,2,1\) (sum \(6\)). The form \(\tfrac h6(k_1+4k_2+k_3)\) is Kutta’s third-order rule; the other two have wrong weights or the wrong divisor.
  4. Question 4Easy
    The global error of the classical Runge-Kutta fourth-order method is of order

    Why

    A method of order \(p\) has global error \(O(h^p)\) and local error \(O(h^{p+1})\); for RK4, \(O(h^4)\) and \(O(h^5)\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The modified Euler (Heun) method for \(y'=y-x\), \(y(0)=2\), \(h=0.2\) gives \(y(0.2)=\)

    Why

    Predictor \(y^*=2+0.2(2)=2.4\). Corrector \(y_1=2+0.1\big[2+(2.4-0.2)\big]=2+0.1(4.2)=2.42\). Plain Euler gives \(2.4\); using only the slope at the predicted point gives \(2.44\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    The second Picard iterate for \(y'=x^2+y^2\), \(y(0)=0\) (starting from \(y_0=0\)) is

    Why

    \(y_1=\int_0^xt^2dt=\tfrac{x^3}3\). \(y_2=\int_0^x\big(t^2+(\tfrac{t^3}3)^2\big)dt=\tfrac{x^3}3+\tfrac{x^7}{63}\). The term \(\tfrac{x^7}{21}\) forgets to square the \(\tfrac13\); \(\tfrac{x^7}7\) forgets the factor \(\tfrac19\) altogether.
  7. Question 7Medium
    Euler’s method applied to \(y'=-20y\) is absolutely stable (i.e. \(\lvert y_n\rvert\) decreases) only for step size

    Why

    \(y_{n+1}=(1-20h)y_n\), so stability needs \(\lvert1-20h\rvert<1\iff h<\tfrac2{20}=0.1\). The value \(0.139=2.785/20\) is the RK4 limit.
  8. Question 8Medium
    Which of the following is Milne’s predictor formula?

    Why

    The Adams-Bashforth 4-step formula has weights \(55,-59,37,-9\) over \(24\); Milne’s corrector is Simpson’s rule; and \(y_n+\tfrac h2(3f_n-f_{n-1})\) is the two-step Adams-Bashforth formula. Milne’s predictor is the one built on \(y_{n-3}\) with \(\tfrac{4h}3(2f_n-f_{n-1}+2f_{n-2})\).
  9. Question 9Hard
    For \(y'=x^2+y^2\), \(y(0)=1\), the Taylor series method with terms up to \(h^3\) gives \(y(0.1)\approx\)

    Why

    \(y'(0)=1\); \(y''=2x+2yy'\Rightarrow2\); \(y'''=2+2y'^2+2yy''\Rightarrow8\). \(y(0.1)\approx1+0.1+\tfrac{2}{2}(0.01)+\tfrac86(0.001)=1.11133\).
  10. Question 10Easy
    Match the method with the order of its global error. I. Euler II. Heun (modified Euler) III. Classical RK4 P. \(O(h)\) Q. \(O(h^2)\) R. \(O(h^4)\) S. \(O(h^5)\)

    Why

    Order \(p\) gives global error \(O(h^p)\): \(p=1,2,4\). (\(h^5\) is the local error of RK4.)
  11. Question 11Medium
    Milne’s predictor-corrector method is not self-starting because

    Why

    The predictor \(y_{n+1}=y_{n-3}+\tfrac{4h}3(\ldots)\) requires \(y_0,\dots,y_3\) before the first prediction of \(y_4\).
  12. Question 12Medium
    Assertion (A): For a stiff equation the explicit RK4 method still needs a small step size to remain stable. Reason (R): The region of absolute stability of RK4 on the negative real axis is bounded, about \(-2.785<h\lambda<0\).

    Why

    A fast-decaying mode with large \(\lvert\lambda\rvert\) forces \(h\lvert\lambda\rvert<2.785\), however smooth the solution is. Implicit methods (backward Euler) have unbounded stability regions on \(\lambda<0\).
  13. Question 13Hard
    Consider: (I) a method of order \(p\) has local truncation error \(O(h^{p+1})\); (II) Euler’s method has local error \(O(h)\); (III) halving \(h\) in RK4 reduces the global error by a factor of about \(16\). Which are correct?

    Why

    I is correct. II is false: Euler’s local error is \(O(h^2)\) and its global error is \(O(h)\). III is correct (\(2^4=16\)).
  14. Question 14Hard
    For \(y''=y\), \(y(0)=1\), \(y'(0)=0\), one step of classical RK4 with \(h=0.5\) on the system \(y'=z\), \(z'=y\) gives \(y(0.5)\approx\)

    Why

    \(k_1,\dots,k_4=0,\ 0.125,\ 0.125,\ 0.265625\) and the \(z\)-slopes are \(l_1,\dots,l_4=0.5,\ 0.5,\ 0.53125,\ 0.5625\). Then \(y_1=1+\tfrac16(0+0.25+0.25+0.265625)=1.1276\) (exact \(\cosh0.5=1.12763\)). Euler gives \(1.0000\), Heun \(1.1250\), and the equal-weight average \(\tfrac14(k_1+k_2+k_3+k_4)\) gives \(1.1289\).
  15. Question 15Medium
    Which initial value problem is NOT covered by the Picard-Lindelof uniqueness theorem at the initial point?

    Why

    \(f=\sqrt{\lvert y\rvert}\) is continuous but not Lipschitz in \(y\) near \(y=0\) (its slope is unbounded there); the IVP has the solutions \(y=0\) and \(y=\tfrac{x^2}4\) (for \(x\ge0\)). The other three right-hand sides are Lipschitz in \(y\) on a neighbourhood of the initial point.
