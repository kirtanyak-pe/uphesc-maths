Why it matters for the exam

The syllabus names Picard, Euler, modified Euler and Runge-Kutta. The 2026 paper asked about the stability of modified Euler and Runge-Kutta 4 on a stiff equation (Q69). Expect direct questions: the Euler or RK4 formula, a one-step computation, the order of a method, and the meaning of local versus global error. Predictor-corrector methods (Milne, Adams) are asked at statement level.

Core ideas

The problem

Initial value problem (IVP):

\[\frac{dy}{dx}=f(x,y),\qquad y(x_0)=y_0.\]

Existence and uniqueness (Picard-Lindelof). If \(f\) is continuous on the rectangle \(R=\{\lvert x-x_0\rvert\le a,\ \lvert y-y_0\rvert\le b\}\) and satisfies a Lipschitz condition in \(y\), \(\lvert f(x,y_1)-f(x,y_2)\rvert\le L\lvert y_1-y_2\rvert\), then the IVP has a unique solution on \(\lvert x-x_0\rvert\le\min\big(a,b/M\big)\) where \(M=\max_R\lvert f\rvert\). Continuity alone gives existence only (Peano), not uniqueness.

Numerical methods find \(y_n\approx y(x_n)\) at \(x_n=x_0+nh\). One-step methods (Euler, Runge-Kutta) use only \(y_n\). Multistep methods (Milne, Adams) use several earlier values.

Picard’s method of successive approximations

Integrate the ODE: \(y(x)=y_0+\displaystyle\int_{x_0}^xf(t,y(t))\,dt\) and iterate:

\[y_{0}(x)=y_0,\qquad y_{n+1}(x)=y_0+\int_{x_0}^xf\big(t,y_n(t)\big)\,dt.\]

Under the Lipschitz condition the sequence converges uniformly to the solution on the interval above, and \(\lvert y(x)-y_n(x)\rvert\le\dfrac{ML^n h^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\) for \(\lvert x-x_0\rvert\le h\). Each iterate is a polynomial when \(f\) is polynomial. It is an analytic method: the output is a formula, mainly of theoretical use.

Nonlinear equations. For \(y'=f(x,y)\) with \(f\) nonlinear in \(y\), each iterate must be squared or otherwise substituted into \(f\), so the degree roughly doubles at every step. Example 7 below does \(y'=x^2+y^2\), \(y(0)=0\): \(y_1=\tfrac{x^3}3\), \(y_2=\tfrac{x^3}3+\tfrac{x^7}{63}\). In practice only two or three iterates are taken. Trap: integrate from \(x_0\) (the lower limit is the initial point) and use the previous iterate inside \(f\), not the one being computed.

Taylor series method

Expand about \(x_0\):

\[y(x)=y_0+(x-x_0)y_0'+\frac{(x-x_0)^2}{2!}y_0''+\frac{(x-x_0)^3}{3!}y_0'''+\cdots\]

Get \(y',y'',\dots\) by differentiating the ODE (chain rule):

\[y'=f,\quad y''=f_x+ff_y,\quad y'''=f_{xx}+2ff_{xy}+f^2f_{yy}+f_y(f_x+ff_y).\]

Taking terms up to \(h^p\) gives a method of order \(p\) (local error \(O(h^{p+1})\)). Needs analytic derivatives of \(f\), which is its drawback. Euler’s method is the Taylor method with \(p=1\). The method is restarted at \(x_1,x_2,\dots\) with step \(h\).

Euler’s method

Keep the first two terms of the Taylor series:

\[\boxed{y_{n+1}=y_n+h\,f(x_n,y_n)}\]

Geometric meaning: follow the tangent at \((x_n,y_n)\) for a step \(h\). Explicit, one function evaluation per step.

  • Local truncation error \(=\dfrac{h^2}2y''(\xi)=O(h^2)\).
  • Global error \(=O(h)\): first-order method.
  • Error accumulates; for \(y'=\lambda y\) with \(\lambda>0\) it consistently under-estimates convex solutions.

Modified Euler (Heun’s method)

Predict with Euler, then correct with the trapezoidal average of slopes:

\[y_{n+1}^{(0)}=y_n+hf(x_n,y_n),\qquad y_{n+1}^{(k+1)}=y_n+\frac h2\Big[f(x_n,y_n)+f\big(x_{n+1},y_{n+1}^{(k)}\big)\Big].\]

One pass of the corrector (the “improved Euler”/Heun form) is the second-order Runge-Kutta method; iterating the corrector to convergence solves the implicit trapezoid rule. Local error \(O(h^3)\), global error \(O(h^2)\).

Note: some books call the midpoint form \(y_{n+1}=y_n+hf\big(x_n+\tfrac h2,\,y_n+\tfrac h2f(x_n,y_n)\big)\) the “modified Euler” method. Both are second order. Read the formula in the question.

Runge-Kutta methods

Idea: match the Taylor series to order \(p\) using only evaluations of \(f\) (no derivatives).

Second order (general). \(k_1=f(x_n,y_n)\), \(k_2=f(x_n+\alpha h,\ y_n+\alpha hk_1)\),

\[y_{n+1}=y_n+h\Big[\Big(1-\frac1{2\alpha}\Big)k_1+\frac1{2\alpha}k_2\Big].\]

\(\alpha=1\) is Heun’s method; \(\alpha=\tfrac12\) is the midpoint method.

Third order (Kutta). \(k_1=f(x_n,y_n)\), \(k_2=f(x_n+\tfrac h2,\,y_n+\tfrac h2k_1)\), \(k_3=f(x_n+h,\,y_n-hk_1+2hk_2)\); \(\ y_{n+1}=y_n+\tfrac h6(k_1+4k_2+k_3)\).

Fourth order (classical RK4).

\[\begin{aligned}k_1&=f(x_n,y_n)\\k_2&=f\big(x_n+\tfrac h2,\ y_n+\tfrac h2k_1\big)\\k_3&=f\big(x_n+\tfrac h2,\ y_n+\tfrac h2k_2\big)\\k_4&=f\big(x_n+h,\ y_n+hk_3\big)\end{aligned}\qquad\boxed{y_{n+1}=y_n+\frac h6\big(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4\big)}\]

Local error \(O(h^5)\), global error \(O(h^4)\); four function evaluations per step. If \(f\) depends only on \(x\), RK4 reduces to Simpson’s \(\tfrac13\) rule. The weights \(1,2,2,1\) add to \(6\).

Systems and higher-order equations. A second-order IVP \(y''=g(x,y,y')\) is converted by \(z=y'\) into the system \(y'=z,\ z'=g(x,y,z)\). RK4 for the system \(y'=f(x,y,z),\ z'=g(x,y,z)\):

\[\begin{aligned}k_1&=hf(x,y,z), & l_1&=hg(x,y,z)\\k_2&=hf\big(x+\tfrac h2,y+\tfrac{k_1}2,z+\tfrac{l_1}2\big), & l_2&=hg\big(x+\tfrac h2,y+\tfrac{k_1}2,z+\tfrac{l_1}2\big)\\k_3&=hf\big(x+\tfrac h2,y+\tfrac{k_2}2,z+\tfrac{l_2}2\big), & l_3&=hg\big(x+\tfrac h2,y+\tfrac{k_2}2,z+\tfrac{l_2}2\big)\\k_4&=hf(x+h,y+k_3,z+l_3), & l_4&=hg(x+h,y+k_3,z+l_3)\end{aligned}\]
\[y_{n+1}=y_n+\tfrac16(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4),\qquad z_{n+1}=z_n+\tfrac16(l_1+2l_2+2l_3+l_4).\]

Euler’s method for a system. For \(y'=f(x,y,z),\ z'=g(x,y,z)\):

\[y_{n+1}=y_n+hf(x_n,y_n,z_n),\qquad z_{n+1}=z_n+hg(x_n,y_n,z_n).\]

Both updates use the old values \((y_n,z_n)\) (do not feed the new \(y_{n+1}\) into the \(z\) update). For \(y''=y\), \(y(0)=1\), \(y'(0)=0\), \(h=0.1\): \(y_1=1+0.1\cdot0=1\), \(z_1=0+0.1\cdot1=0.1\); \(y_2=1+0.1\cdot0.1=1.01\), \(z_2=0.1+0.1\cdot1=0.2\) (exact \(\cosh0.2=1.0201\)). The same recipe handles any higher-order IVP after reduction to first-order form.

Local and global error; order

  • Local truncation error: error made in one step, assuming the previous value is exact.
  • Global error: error in \(y_n\) at a fixed \(x\) after \(n=(x-x_0)/h\) steps, accumulated.
  • For a method of order \(p\): local error \(O(h^{p+1})\), global error \(O(h^p)\) (one power lost because about \(1/h\) steps accumulate errors).
Method Order \(p\) Local error Global error \(f\)-evaluations per step
Euler \(1\) \(O(h^2)\) \(O(h)\) \(1\)
Heun / midpoint (RK2) \(2\) \(O(h^3)\) \(O(h^2)\) \(2\)
Kutta RK3 \(3\) \(O(h^4)\) \(O(h^3)\) \(3\)
Classical RK4 \(4\) \(O(h^5)\) \(O(h^4)\) \(4\)
Taylor (to \(h^p\)) \(p\) \(O(h^{p+1})\) \(O(h^p)\) needs derivatives

Halving \(h\) divides the global error by \(2\) (Euler), \(4\) (RK2), \(16\) (RK4). Convergence follows from consistency and stability (Dahlquist, statement level): a consistent one-step method that is Lipschitz-stable converges.

Stability (basic level)

Test equation \(y'=\lambda y\), \(\operatorname{Re}\lambda<0\) (the true solution decays). A one-step method gives \(y_{n+1}=R(z)\,y_n\) with \(z=h\lambda\). It is absolutely stable for those \(z\) where \(\lvert R(z)\rvert<1\).

Method \(R(z)\) Stable real interval for \(z=h\lambda<0\)
Euler \(1+z\) \(-2<z<0\), so \(h<\dfrac2{\lvert\lambda\rvert}\)
Heun (RK2) \(1+z+\dfrac{z^2}2\) \(-2<z<0\)
RK4 \(1+z+\dfrac{z^2}2+\dfrac{z^3}6+\dfrac{z^4}{24}\) \(-2.785<z<0\)
Implicit (backward) Euler \(\dfrac1{1-z}\) all \(z<0\) (A-stable)

Stiff equations have a solution with components decaying at very different rates (for example \(y'=-1000(y-\cos x)-\sin x\), or systems with eigenvalues \(-1\) and \(-10^4\)). A fast-decaying component forces \(h\lvert\lambda\rvert\) below the stability bound even after that component has vanished. Explicit methods then need tiny steps; they become unstable for larger \(h\) (oscillating, growing errors). RK4 has a larger stability region than Euler but still a bounded one, so for stiff problems the remedy is a small step size with explicit methods, or an implicit A-stable method (backward Euler, trapezoid rule).

Predictor-corrector (multistep) methods: statement level

These need \(y_0,\dots,y_n\) (several starting values, found by Taylor series or RK4). Each step: Predict \(y_{n+1}^{(p)}\) (explicit), Evaluate \(f\), Correct (implicit formula using \(f_{n+1}\)), Evaluate again.

Milne’s method (fourth order; needs \(y_0,y_1,y_2,y_3\)):

\[\text{Predictor: } y_{n+1}^{(p)}=y_{n-3}+\frac{4h}3\big(2f_n-f_{n-1}+2f_{n-2}\big),\]
\[\text{Corrector: } y_{n+1}=y_{n-1}+\frac h3\big(f_{n+1}+4f_n+f_{n-1}\big)\quad(\text{Simpson's rule}).\]

Local errors: predictor \(\tfrac{14}{45}h^5y^{(5)}\), corrector \(-\tfrac1{90}h^5y^{(5)}\). The corrector can be iterated. Milne’s corrector can show a weak (parasitic) instability for some equations.

Adams-Bashforth-Moulton (fourth order):

\[\text{Adams-Bashforth: } y_{n+1}=y_n+\frac h{24}\big(55f_n-59f_{n-1}+37f_{n-2}-9f_{n-3}\big),\]
\[\text{Adams-Moulton: } y_{n+1}=y_n+\frac h{24}\big(9f_{n+1}+19f_n-5f_{n-1}+f_{n-2}\big).\]

Local errors: \(\tfrac{251}{720}h^5y^{(5)}\) (AB4) and \(-\tfrac{19}{720}h^5y^{(5)}\) (AM4). Two-step version: \(y_{n+1}=y_n+\tfrac h2(3f_n-f_{n-1})\). Compared with RK4, multistep methods need only one new \(f\) value per step (with a corrector, two) but are not self-starting.

Formulas and results

  • Picard: \(y_{n+1}(x)=y_0+\int_{x_0}^xf(t,y_n(t))dt\).
  • Taylor: \(y=y_0+hy_0'+\frac{h^2}{2!}y_0''+\cdots\); \(y''=f_x+ff_y\).
  • Euler: \(y_{n+1}=y_n+hf(x_n,y_n)\); local \(O(h^2)\), global \(O(h)\).
  • Heun: \(y_{n+1}=y_n+\frac h2[f(x_n,y_n)+f(x_{n+1},y_n+hf(x_n,y_n))]\); local \(O(h^3)\), global \(O(h^2)\).
  • RK4: \(y_{n+1}=y_n+\frac h6(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4)\); local \(O(h^5)\), global \(O(h^4)\).
  • Order \(p\) \(\Rightarrow\) local \(O(h^{p+1})\), global \(O(h^p)\).
  • Stability: Euler \(h<2/\lvert\lambda\rvert\); RK4 \(h\lvert\lambda\rvert<2.785\); backward Euler unconditionally stable on \(y'=\lambda y\), \(\lambda<0\).
  • Milne P: \(y_{n-3}+\frac{4h}3(2f_n-f_{n-1}+2f_{n-2})\); C: \(y_{n-1}+\frac h3(f_{n+1}+4f_n+f_{n-1})\).
  • Adams-Bashforth 4: \(\frac h{24}(55f_n-59f_{n-1}+37f_{n-2}-9f_{n-3})\).
  • System: \(y''=g(x,y,y')\) becomes \(y'=z,\ z'=g\); Euler and RK4 update \(y\) and \(z\) together from the old values.
  • Nonlinear Picard (\(y'=x^2+y^2\), \(y(0)=0\)): \(y_1=\frac{x^3}3\), \(y_2=\frac{x^3}3+\frac{x^7}{63}\).

Worked examples

Examples 1 to 6 use \(y'=x+y,\ y(0)=1\), whose exact solution is \(y=2e^x-x-1\). Examples 7 and 8 use other equations.

Example 1: Picard

\(y_0=1\). \(y_1=1+\int_0^x(t+1)dt=1+x+\dfrac{x^2}2\). \(y_2=1+\int_0^x\big(t+1+t+\tfrac{t^2}2\big)dt=1+x+x^2+\dfrac{x^3}6\). Exact series: \(1+x+x^2+\dfrac{x^3}3+\dots\), so \(y_2\) is correct through \(x^2\); each iteration adds one correct power.

Example 2: Taylor, \(y(0.1)\)

\(y'=x+y\Rightarrow y'(0)=1\); \(y''=1+y'\Rightarrow2\); \(y'''=y''\Rightarrow2\); \(y^{(4)}=2\). \(y(0.1)=1+0.1+\dfrac{2(0.01)}{2}+\dfrac{2(0.001)}6+\dfrac{2(10^{-4})}{24}=1.110342\) (exact \(1.1103418\)).

Example 3: Euler, Heun, RK4 with \(h=0.1\)

\(x\) Euler Heun RK4 Exact
\(0.1\) \(1.1000\) \(1.1100\) \(1.110342\) \(1.110342\)
\(0.2\) \(1.2200\) \(1.24205\) \(1.242805\) \(1.242806\)
\(0.3\) \(1.3620\) \(1.39847\) \(1.399717\) \(1.399718\)

Euler step 1: \(y_1=1+0.1(0+1)=1.1\); step 2: \(y_2=1.1+0.1(0.1+1.1)=1.22\). Heun step 1: predictor \(y^*=1.1\); \(y_1=1+0.05\big[1+(0.1+1.1)\big]=1.11\). The errors at \(x=0.3\) are about \(3.8\times10^{-2}\) (Euler), \(1.3\times10^{-3}\) (Heun) and \(6\times10^{-7}\) (RK4), in line with orders \(1,\ 2,\ 4\).

Example 4: one RK4 step

\(h=0.1\), \((x_0,y_0)=(0,1)\): \(k_1=f(0,1)=1\); \(k_2=f(0.05,\,1.05)=1.1\); \(k_3=f(0.05,\,1.055)=1.105\); \(k_4=f(0.1,\,1.1105)=1.2105\). \(y_1=1+\dfrac{0.1}6(1+2.2+2.21+1.2105)=1+\dfrac{0.1}{6}(6.6205)=1.110342.\)

Example 5: stability

\(y'=-50y\). Euler: \(y_{n+1}=(1-50h)y_n\). Stable iff \(\lvert1-50h\rvert<1\), i.e. \(h<0.04\). \(h=0.05\): factor \(-1.5\), so \(\lvert y_n\rvert\) grows like \(1.5^n\) although the true solution decays. \(h=0.02\): factor \(0\). RK4 allows \(h<2.785/50=0.0557\).

Example 6: Adams-Bashforth-Moulton step

Starting values (exact) at \(x=0,0.1,0.2,0.3\): \(y=1,\ 1.110342,\ 1.242806,\ 1.399718\); \(f=1,\ 1.210342,\ 1.442806,\ 1.699718\). Predictor (AB4): \(y_4^{(p)}=1.399718+\dfrac{0.1}{24}\big(55\cdot1.699718-59\cdot1.442806+37\cdot1.210342-9\cdot1\big)=1.583641\). Corrector (AM4) with \(f_4=0.4+1.583641=1.983641\): \(y_4=1.399718+\dfrac{0.1}{24}\big(9\cdot1.983641+19\cdot1.699718-5\cdot1.442806+1.210342\big)=1.583650\). Exact \(1.583649\).

Example 7: Picard for a nonlinear equation

\(y'=x^2+y^2\), \(y(0)=0\). Start with \(y_0=0\). \(y_1=\displaystyle\int_0^xt^2\,dt=\frac{x^3}3\). \(y_2=\displaystyle\int_0^x\Big(t^2+\frac{t^6}9\Big)dt=\frac{x^3}3+\frac{x^7}{63}\). \(y_3=\displaystyle\int_0^x\Big(t^2+\Big(\frac{t^3}3+\frac{t^7}{63}\Big)^2\Big)dt=\frac{x^3}3+\frac{x^7}{63}+\frac{2x^{11}}{2079}+\frac{x^{15}}{59535}.\) The true series is \(\tfrac{x^3}3+\tfrac{x^7}{63}+\tfrac{2x^{11}}{2079}+\tfrac{13x^{15}}{218295}+\cdots\), so \(y_3\) is correct up to \(x^{11}\) (the \(x^{15}\) term is not yet right). For \(x=0.5\) the first three terms give \(0.041667+0.000124+0.0000005=0.04179\).

Example 8: RK4 for a second-order IVP

\(y''=y\), \(y(0)=1\), \(y'(0)=0\) (exact \(y=\cosh x\)). Put \(z=y'\): \(y'=z\), \(z'=y\). Take one step \(h=0.5\) from \((y,z)=(1,0)\):

\(k\) (for \(y\)) \(l\) (for \(z\))
1 \(0.5\cdot0=0\) \(0.5\cdot1=0.5\)
2 \(0.5\cdot(0+0.25)=0.125\) \(0.5\cdot(1+0)=0.5\)
3 \(0.5\cdot(0+0.25)=0.125\) \(0.5\cdot(1+0.0625)=0.53125\)
4 \(0.5\cdot(0+0.53125)=0.265625\) \(0.5\cdot(1+0.125)=0.5625\)

\(y_1=1+\tfrac16(0+0.25+0.25+0.265625)=1.127604\) (exact \(\cosh0.5=1.127626\)) and \(z_1=\tfrac16(0.5+1+1.0625+0.5625)=0.520833\) (exact \(\sinh0.5=0.521095\)). Euler with the same \(h\) gives only \(y_1=1\), \(z_1=0.5\). Trap: \(k_2\) and \(k_3\) use the half-step values of both \(y\) and \(z\) from the previous \(k,l\), and the \(l\)’s are evaluated with the \(y\)-increments \(k\) (not with \(z\) alone).

Common traps

  • Euler formula: \(y_{n+1}=y_n+hf(x_n,y_n)\) with \(f\) at the old point. Evaluating \(f\) at \((x_{n+1},y_{n+1})\) is the implicit (backward) Euler method.
  • Order vs error: order \(p\) means global error \(O(h^p)\) and local error \(O(h^{p+1})\). Euler’s local error is \(O(h^2)\) although the method is “first order”.
  • RK4 weights are \(1,2,2,1\) over \(6\), and \(k_2,k_3\) are at the midpoint. \(\tfrac16(k_1+k_2+k_3+k_4)\) is wrong.
  • Modified Euler / Heun uses the average of two slopes with the predicted \(y\); it is second order, not first.
  • RK4 does not remove stiffness; its stability region is bounded (\(\approx2.78\)). The fix is a smaller step or an implicit method.
  • Higher-order IVPs are converted to first-order systems; one RK4 pass updates both \(y\) and \(z\).
  • Picard needs a Lipschitz condition for uniqueness; continuity of \(f\) alone is not enough (e.g. \(y'=\sqrt{\lvert y\rvert},\ y(0)=0\) has several solutions).
  • Milne/Adams methods are not self-starting and need starting values from RK4 or a Taylor series.
  • Predictor-corrector methods: the predictor is explicit; the corrector contains \(f_{n+1}\) (implicit).
  • Smaller step is not always better: round-off grows and stiff stability limits apply only for explicit methods.

2026-Q69 (worked key: D; unofficial key, flagged “high” confidence)

Question. The modified Euler method applied to a stiff equation shows instability. If Runge-Kutta 4 is used, determine the condition for improvement in stability. (A) Large step size (B) Linear system (C) No dependence (D) Small step size. Answer: D (small step size). RK4 is explicit, so its region of absolute stability is bounded: for \(y'=\lambda y\) it needs \(h\lvert\lambda\rvert\lesssim2.78\). For a stiff problem (\(\lvert\lambda\rvert\) large) this forces a small \(h\); a large step makes RK4 unstable too.

Quick revision

  • Euler: \(y_{n+1}=y_n+hf(x_n,y_n)\); local \(O(h^2)\), global \(O(h)\).
  • Heun/modified Euler: predict with Euler, correct with average of slopes; global \(O(h^2)\).
  • RK4: \(y_{n+1}=y_n+\frac h6(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4)\); global \(O(h^4)\); \(4\) evaluations.
  • Method of order \(p\): local \(O(h^{p+1})\), global \(O(h^p)\).
  • Picard: \(y_{n+1}=y_0+\int f(t,y_n)dt\); converges under a Lipschitz condition.
  • Taylor method: differentiate the ODE, use terms up to \(h^p\).
  • Euler stable on \(y'=\lambda y\) iff \(h<2/\lvert\lambda\rvert\); RK4 iff \(h\lvert\lambda\rvert<2.785\).
  • Stiff problem: explicit methods need small \(h\); implicit methods (backward Euler) are A-stable.
  • Milne: P \(y_{n-3}+\frac{4h}3(2f_n-f_{n-1}+2f_{n-2})\), C Simpson \(y_{n-1}+\frac h3(f_{n+1}+4f_n+f_{n-1})\).
  • Adams-Bashforth 4: \(\frac h{24}(55,-59,37,-9)\); Adams-Moulton 4: \(\frac h{24}(9,19,-5,1)\).
  • Multistep methods are not self-starting.
  • Second-order IVP: set \(z=y'\), then apply Euler or RK4 to \((y,z)\) together, all slopes from old values.
  • Nonlinear Picard: substitute the previous iterate into \(f\); degrees double each step.
