  1. Question 1Easy
    A linear programming problem in standard form has \(3\) independent equations in \(6\) variables (slack variables included). The maximum number of basic solutions is

    Why

    Choose \(3\) basic variables out of \(6\): \(\binom{6}{3}=20\). The value \(15=\binom62\) uses the wrong subset size; \(120=6\cdot5\cdot4\) counts ordered choices.
  2. Question 2Easy
    Which of the following sets in \(\mathbb R^2\) is not convex?

    Why

    \(y\ge x^2\) is the region above a convex function, so it is convex. \(\lvert x\rvert+\lvert y\rvert\le1\) is a square, convex. \(xy\ge1\) with \(x,y>0\) lies above the convex curve \(y=1/x\), so it is convex. \(y\le x^2\) is below a convex curve: \((-1,1)\) and \((1,1)\) belong to it but their midpoint \((0,1)\) does not.
  3. Question 3Easy
    The maximum value of \(Z=3x+4y\) subject to \(x+y\le4\), \(x+3y\le6\), \(x,y\ge0\) is

    Why

    Corners: \((0,0)\) gives \(0\), \((4,0)\) gives \(12\), \((3,1)\) gives \(13\), \((0,2)\) gives \(8\). The maximum is \(13\) at \((3,1)\). \(16\) is the value at the infeasible point \((0,4)\) (it violates \(x+3y\le6\)).
  4. Question 4Medium
    The minimum value of \(Z=2x+3y\) subject to \(x+y\ge4\), \(x+3y\ge6\), \(x,y\ge0\) is

    Why

    The region is unbounded, with corners \((6,0)\), \((3,1)\), \((0,4)\) where \(Z=12,\ 9,\ 12\). Since \(c\ge0\) the minimum exists and lies at a corner: \(9\) at \((3,1)\). \(12\) is the value at the other two corners.
  5. Question 5Easy
    In the simplex method for a maximisation problem, the problem is declared unbounded when

    Why

    The entering variable could be increased without limit because no row blocks it (no positive entry for the ratio test), and \(Z\) improves along it. The other options describe infeasibility (Big-M), degeneracy and alternative optima.
  6. Question 6Medium
    At an optimal tableau of a maximisation LPP, consider: (i) all nonbasic \(Z_j-C_j>0\) implies the optimal solution is unique; (ii) some nonbasic \(Z_j-C_j=0\) with a positive entry in its column implies another optimal BFS exists; (iii) a basic variable equal to \(0\) implies the optimal solution is not unique. Which statements are correct?

    Why

    (i) and (ii) are the standard optimality tests. (iii) is false: \(\max 3x_1+9x_2\) s.t. \(x_1+4x_2\le8\), \(x_1+2x_2\le4\) ends at the degenerate BFS \((0,2)\) with a basic variable at \(0\), yet every nonbasic \(Z_j-C_j=\tfrac32>0\), so the optimum is unique. Degeneracy and non-uniqueness are independent.
  7. Question 7Medium
    The Big-M method ends with all \(Z_j-C_j\) of the correct sign, but the artificial variable \(R_1\) is still in the basis with value \(4\). Then the original problem

    Why

    An artificial variable positive at the end means the constraints cannot all be satisfied with it equal to \(0\). The problem is infeasible. If the value were \(0\), the solution would be feasible but degenerate.
  8. Question 8Easy
    For the constraints \(x_1+x_2\le10\), \(2x_1+x_2\ge4\), \(x_1-x_2=2\), \(x_1+3x_2\ge6\), the number of artificial variables needed is

    Why

    \(\le\) takes a slack only. Each \(\ge\) takes a surplus and an artificial variable (two of them) and \(=\) takes an artificial variable. Total artificial: \(2+1=3\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    In the two-phase method Phase I ends with \(\min W=\tfrac52\) where \(W\) is the sum of the artificial variables. Then

    Why

    \(W=0\) is needed for feasibility. A positive minimum of \(W\) shows the original constraints cannot be satisfied with all artificials at zero.
  10. Question 10Medium
    Assertion (A): a tie in the minimum ratio test leads to a degenerate basic feasible solution at the next iteration. Reason (R): one of the tied rows has its right-hand side reduced to \(0\), and its basic variable stays basic at level \(0\).

    Why

    With ratios \(\theta=b_p/a_{pk}=b_q/a_{qk}\), after pivoting row \(q\) has RHS \(b_q-a_{qk}\theta=0\) but its variable remains basic, which is exactly a degenerate BFS.
  11. Question 11Medium
    Consider: (i) the intersection of two convex sets is convex; (ii) the union of two convex sets is convex; (iii) the set of all optimal solutions of an LPP is convex; (iv) every convex set in \(\mathbb R^2\) has finitely many extreme points. Which are true?

    Why

    (ii) fails for two disjoint discs. (iv) fails for a disc, which has infinitely many extreme points (its whole boundary circle). (i) and (iii) are true; (iii) because a convex combination of two optimal points has the same objective value.
  12. Question 12Hard
    For \(\max Z=2x+4y\) subject to \(x+2y\le5\), \(x+y\le4\), \(x,y\ge0\), the number of optimal basic feasible solutions is

    Why

    The corners are \((0,0)\), \((4,0)\), \((3,1)\), \((0,\tfrac52)\) with \(Z=0,\ 8,\ 10,\ 10\). Two corners are optimal, so there are \(2\) optimal BFS, although the optimal solutions are infinitely many (the whole edge). The question asks for basic solutions.
  13. Question 13Hard
    The LPP \(\max Z=x+y\) subject to \(x-y\le1\), \(-x+y\le1\), \(x,y\ge0\) has

    Why

    The region is the strip \(\lvert x-y\rvert\le1\) in the first quadrant, which contains \((t,t)\) for every \(t\ge0\), with \(Z=2t\to\infty\). It is nonempty (so not infeasible) and \(Z\) has no maximum.
  14. Question 14Hard
    The number of extreme points of the set \(x_1+x_2\le3\), \(x_1\le2\), \(x_2\le2\), \(x_1,x_2\ge0\) is

    Why

    Corners: \((0,0)\), \((2,0)\), \((2,1)\), \((1,2)\), \((0,2)\), five in all. The point \((2,2)\) violates \(x_1+x_2\le3\).
