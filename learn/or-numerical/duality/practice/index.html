  1. Question 1Easy
    The dual of \(\max Z=4x_1+3x_2\) subject to \(2x_1+x_2\le8\), \(x_1+2x_2\le6\), \(x_1,x_2\ge0\) is

    Why

    Dual objective uses the right-hand sides \(8,6\), the dual right-hand sides are the primal costs \(4,3\), and a canonical maximisation gives a minimisation with \(\ge\) constraints. The second option swaps \(b\) and \(c\); the last two keep a wrong direction.
  2. Question 2Medium
    The dual of the problem \(\min Z=c^{T}x\) subject to \(Ax=b\), \(x\ge0\) is

    Why

    An equality constraint gives a free dual variable. A minimisation primal has a maximisation dual, and its non-negative variables give \(\le\) dual constraints. Using \(y\ge0\) forgets the equality.
  3. Question 3Medium
    A maximisation problem has three constraints, the first of type \(\le\), the second of type \(\ge\) and the third of type \(=\). In its dual, the three variables \(y_1,y_2,y_3\) are respectively

    Why

    In a maximisation primal, \(\le\) is the natural direction and gives \(y\ge0\); the opposite inequality \(\ge\) gives \(y\le0\); an equality gives a free variable.
  4. Question 4Medium
    In \(\max Z=2x_1+3x_2+x_3\) the variables satisfy \(x_1\ge0\), \(x_2\le0\), \(x_3\) unrestricted. The three corresponding dual constraints are respectively

    Why

    For a maximisation primal: \(x_j\ge0\) gives a \(\ge\) dual constraint, \(x_j\le0\) gives a \(\le\) constraint, and a free \(x_j\) gives an equality.
  5. Question 5Easy
    For a maximisation LPP, a feasible solution has \(Z=18\) and a feasible solution of its dual has \(W=20\). The optimal value \(Z^{*}\) of the primal satisfies

    Why

    Weak duality: every primal-feasible value is at most every dual-feasible value. So \(18\le Z^{*}=W^{*}\le20\); nothing more can be concluded.
  6. Question 6Medium
    Which of the following combinations cannot occur for a primal LPP and its dual?

    Why

    If one problem has a finite optimum, so does the other (strong duality), so a finite primal optimum and an unbounded dual cannot coexist. The other three are all possible: the unbounded primal forces an infeasible dual, and both can be infeasible.
  7. Question 7Medium
    The LPP \(\max Z=3x_1+5x_2\) s.t. \(x_1\le4\), \(2x_2\le12\), \(3x_1+2x_2\le18\), \(x\ge0\) has optimum \(x=(2,6)\) with \(Z=36\). The optimal value of the dual variable \(y_2\) (for the constraint \(2x_2\le12\)) is

    Why

    Constraint 1 is slack at \((2,6)\) (\(2<4\)), so \(y_1=0\). Constraints 2 and 3 are tight and \(x_1,x_2>0\), so both dual constraints are equalities: \(3y_3=3\) gives \(y_3=1\), and \(2y_2+2y_3=5\) gives \(y_2=\tfrac32\). Check \(W=12\cdot\tfrac32+18\cdot1=36\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    In the problem \(\max Z=3x_1+5x_2\) s.t. \(x_1\le4\), \(2x_2\le12\), \(3x_1+2x_2\le18\), the optimum is \((2,6)\). By complementary slackness, which dual variable must be zero?

    Why

    At \((2,6)\): \(x_1=2<4\), so constraint 1 is slack and \(y_1=0\). Constraints 2 (\(12=12\)) and 3 (\(6+12=18\)) are tight, and \(x_1,x_2>0\) forces both dual constraints to be tight, so \(y_2,y_3\) are in general positive (indeed \(y=(0,\tfrac32,1)\)).
  9. Question 9Medium
    In the optimal tableau of a maximisation problem, the entry of the \(Z_j-C_j\) row under the slack variable \(s_2\) of constraint 2 is \(\tfrac32\). Then

    Why

    \(y^{T}=c_B^{T}B^{-1}\) and the slack column of row \(i\) is the \(i\)th identity column with cost \(0\), so \(Z_j-C_j=y_i\) there. Slack in a constraint is the RHS entry of \(s_2\) if \(s_2\) is basic; it is not the \(Z_j-C_j\) entry (and a nonbasic \(s_2\) has slack \(0\)).
  10. Question 10Hard
    A dual simplex tableau for \(\max Z'=-2x_1-x_2\) has rows \(s_1:\ (-3,-1,1,0,0\,|\,-3)\), \(s_2:\ (-4,-3,0,1,0\,|\,-6)\), \(s_3:\ (-1,-2,0,0,1\,|\,-3)\) in columns \(x_1,x_2,s_1,s_2,s_3\) and \(Z_j-C_j=(2,1,0,0,0)\). The leaving and entering variables in the first iteration are

    Why

    The most negative RHS is \(-6\) (row \(s_2\)), so \(s_2\) leaves. Over the negative entries \(-4,-3\) the ratios \(\lvert(Z_j-C_j)/a_{rj}\rvert\) are \(\tfrac24=\tfrac12\) for \(x_1\) and \(\tfrac13\) for \(x_2\). The minimum is \(\tfrac13\), so \(x_2\) enters. Choosing \(x_1\) would make \(Z_j-C_j\) of \(x_2\) negative and lose dual feasibility.
  11. Question 11Hard
    Assertion (A): if a maximisation primal is unbounded, its dual is infeasible. Reason (R): any dual-feasible \(y\) would give the upper bound \(b^{T}y\) on every primal-feasible value \(c^{T}x\).

    Why

    Weak duality gives \(c^{T}x\le b^{T}y\) for all feasible pairs. If a dual-feasible \(y\) existed, \(c^{T}x\) would be bounded above, contradicting unboundedness. So R is true and is the reason for A.
  12. Question 12Hard
    A maximisation LPP has alternative optimal solutions. Which statement about its dual must be true?

    Why

    Strong duality gives the dual a finite optimum, which rules out infeasible and unbounded. A non-degenerate dual optimal BFS would force a unique primal optimum (complementary slackness gives a square nonsingular system for the primal variables), so alternative primal optima make every dual optimal BFS degenerate. Example: \(\max2x_1+4x_2\) s.t. \(x_1+2x_2\le5\), \(x_1+x_2\le4\) has dual optimum \((2,0)\), with three constraints active in two dimensions.
  13. Question 13Easy
    A primal LPP has \(3\) constraints and \(5\) variables. Its dual has

    Why

    One dual variable per primal constraint and one dual constraint per primal variable.
