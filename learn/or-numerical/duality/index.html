Why it matters for the exam

Duality appears about once per paper: the 2019 paper asked for the dual of an equality-constrained LPP, and 2026 Q65 asked what a primal with alternative optima implies for the dual. The questions test three things: the sign rules for writing the dual, the outcome table (which combinations of optimal, unbounded and infeasible can occur), and complementary slackness and degeneracy facts. All are rules, so they can be memorised precisely.

Core ideas

The dual of a canonical problem

Primal (P), canonical maximisation form, with \(A\) an \(m\times n\) matrix:

\(\max\ Z=c^{T}x\quad\text{s.t. }Ax\le b,\ x\ge 0.\)

Dual (D):

\(\min\ W=b^{T}y\quad\text{s.t. }A^{T}y\ge c,\ y\ge 0.\)

  • The dual has one variable \(y_i\) for each primal constraint and one constraint for each primal variable.
  • The cost vector of one problem is the right-hand side of the other.
  • The dual of the dual is the primal.
  • A primal with \(m\) constraints and \(n\) variables has a dual with \(m\) variables and \(n\) constraints.

For a minimisation primal in canonical form, \(\min c^{T}x\) s.t. \(Ax\ge b\), \(x\ge0\), the dual is \(\max b^{T}y\) s.t. \(A^{T}y\le c\), \(y\ge0\).

General rules (all sign cases)

Maximisation problem Minimisation problem
constraint \(\le\) dual variable \(\ge 0\)
constraint \(\ge\) dual variable \(\le 0\)
constraint \(=\) dual variable free (unrestricted)
variable \(\ge 0\) dual constraint \(\ge\)
variable \(\le 0\) dual constraint \(\le\)
variable free dual constraint \(=\)

Read this table in one direction when the primal is a maximisation. For a minimisation primal, the dual is the maximisation problem and the rules reverse:

Primal (min) Dual (max)
constraint \(\ge\) variable \(\ge 0\)
constraint \(\le\) variable \(\le 0\)
constraint \(=\) variable free
variable \(\ge 0\) constraint \(\le\)
variable \(\le 0\) constraint \(\ge\)
variable free constraint \(=\)

Easy way to remember: in the “natural” pairing (max with \(\le\), min with \(\ge\)) everything is non-negative. An unnatural inequality gives a non-positive variable, and an equality gives a free one.

Equality constraint: \(Ax=b\) is the same as \(Ax\le b\) and \(-Ax\le -b\), giving two non-negative dual variables \(y^{+},y^{-}\) whose difference \(y=y^{+}-y^{-}\) is free. Special case:

\(\max c^{T}x\ \text{s.t. }Ax=b,\ x\ge0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \min b^{T}y\ \text{s.t. }A^{T}y\ge c,\ y\ \text{free}.\)

Likewise \(\min c^{T}x\) s.t. \(Ax=b\), \(x\ge 0\) has dual \(\max b^{T}y\) s.t. \(A^{T}y\le c\), \(y\) free.

Procedure: (1) Note whether the primal is a maximisation or a minimisation and use the matching table. (2) Write the dual objective with the right-hand sides. (3) Write one dual constraint per primal variable using the columns of \(A\). (4) Attach the sign restriction to each dual variable from the constraint type.

Weak duality

If \(x\) is feasible for the maximisation primal and \(y\) is feasible for its dual, then

\(c^{T}x\le b^{T}y.\)

Proof: \(c^{T}x\le (A^{T}y)^{T}x=y^{T}(Ax)\le y^{T}b\) using \(x\ge0\), \(A^{T}y\ge c\), \(y\ge0\) and \(Ax\le b\).

Consequences:

  • Every dual-feasible value is an upper bound on the primal optimum, and every primal-feasible value a lower bound on the dual optimum.
  • If \(c^{T}x^{*}=b^{T}y^{*}\) for feasible \(x^{*},y^{*}\), both are optimal.
  • If the primal is unbounded the dual is infeasible; if the dual is unbounded the primal is infeasible.

Strong duality

If either problem has a finite optimal solution, so does the other, and the optimal values are equal: \(\max c^{T}x=\min b^{T}y\).

In the optimal simplex tableau \(y^{T}=c_B^{T}B^{-1}\), so the dual solution comes for free.

Outcome table

Primal \(\backslash\) Dual Optimal Unbounded Infeasible
Optimal possible (equal values) impossible impossible
Unbounded impossible impossible possible
Infeasible impossible possible possible

Both infeasible really happens. Example: \(\max x_1+x_2\) s.t. \(x_1-x_2\le-1\), \(-x_1+x_2\le-1\) is infeasible (adding gives \(0\le-2\)), and its dual \(\min -y_1-y_2\) s.t. \(y_1-y_2\ge1\), \(-y_1+y_2\ge1\), \(y\ge0\) is infeasible too. Primal unbounded, dual infeasible: \(\max x_1+x_2\) s.t. \(x_1-x_2\le1\) has dual \(\min y\) s.t. \(y\ge1\), \(-y\ge1\), infeasible.

Complementary slackness

Let \(x\) and \(y\) be feasible for P and D. They are both optimal if and only if

\(y_i\,(b_i-a_i^{T}x)=0\ \text{for all }i,\qquad x_j\,(a_j^{T}y-c_j)=0\ \text{for all }j,\)

where \(a_i^{T}\) is row \(i\) of \(A\) and \(a_j\) is column \(j\) of \(A\). In words: if a constraint is slack in one problem, the matching variable of the other problem is zero; if a variable is positive in one problem, the matching constraint of the other is tight.

Uses:

  • Check a proposed solution for optimality without running simplex: find the matching dual solution and test the conditions.
  • Find the dual optimum from the primal optimum: set up the equations for variables \(x_j>0\) and for tight constraints and solve.
  • Strict complementary slackness: there exist optimal \(x,y\) such that exactly one of each pair is positive (never both zero).

Economic meaning

\(y_i^{*}=\partial Z^{*}/\partial b_i\) is the shadow price of resource \(i\): the extra profit per extra unit of right-hand side, valid while the optimal basis does not change. A slack (unused) resource has shadow price \(0\).

Reading the dual from the optimal tableau

Since \(y^{T}=c_B^{T}B^{-1}\), look at the columns that formed the identity matrix in the starting tableau. For the initial identity column of row \(i\) with original cost \(C\), the tableau entry is \(Z_j-C_j=y_i-C\). So \(y_i=(Z_j-C_j)+C\).

Starting basic variable in row \(i\) (max problem) \(y_i\) equals
slack (cost 0) \(Z_j-C_j\) under the slack column
artificial (cost \(-M\)) \(Z_j-C_j\) under the artificial column, with the \(M\) removed
surplus (column \(-e_i\), cost 0) \(-(Z_j-C_j)\) under the surplus column

For a minimisation problem solved as \(\max(-Z)\), the dual variables read this way belong to the \(\max(-Z)\) form; the dual variables of the original minimisation are their negatives (see Example 3).

The optimal value is the same in the \(Z\)-row RHS: \(Z^{*}=b^{T}y^{*}\).

Dual simplex method (basic level)

Use it when a starting tableau is dual feasible (all \(Z_j-C_j\ge0\) for maximisation) but primal infeasible (some RHS negative). This happens when a minimisation problem with \(\ge\) constraints and non-negative costs is multiplied through by \(-1\) to get slacks.

  1. Leaving variable: the row with the most negative RHS. If all RHS \(\ge0\), stop: optimal.
  2. Entering variable: among the negative entries \(a_{rj}<0\) of that row, minimise \(\left\lvert\dfrac{Z_j-C_j}{a_{rj}}\right\rvert\). If the row has no negative entry, the problem is infeasible.
  3. Pivot as usual. All \(Z_j-C_j\) stay \(\ge0\).

It is the simplex method applied to the dual. It is useful for adding a constraint to an optimal tableau (the new tableau is dual feasible but primal infeasible) and for sensitivity analysis.

Degeneracy and duality (2026 Q65 type)

  • If the primal optimal BFS is nondegenerate, the dual optimal solution is unique.
  • If the dual has a nondegenerate optimal BFS, the primal optimum is unique.
  • Equivalent form: if the primal has alternative optima, every dual optimal BFS is degenerate. Symmetrically, if the dual has alternative optima, every primal optimal BFS is degenerate.
  • The converse fails: a degenerate primal optimum need not give alternative dual optima.

Example: \(\max2x_1+4x_2\) s.t. \(x_1+2x_2\le5\), \(x_1+x_2\le4\) has alternative optima (the edge from \((0,\tfrac52)\) to \((3,1)\)). Its dual \(\min5y_1+4y_2\) s.t. \(y_1+y_2\ge2\), \(2y_1+y_2\ge4\), \(y\ge0\) has the unique optimum \(y=(2,0)\), which is degenerate: three constraints meet there (\(y_2=0\) and both dual constraints tight).

Formulas and results

  • (P) \(\max c^{T}x\), \(Ax\le b\), \(x\ge0\); (D) \(\min b^{T}y\), \(A^{T}y\ge c\), \(y\ge0\).
  • Max primal: \(\le\to y\ge0\); \(\ge\to y\le0\); \(=\to y\) free; \(x\ge0\to\ge\); \(x\le0\to\le\); \(x\) free \(\to=\).
  • \(\max c^{T}x\), \(Ax=b\), \(x\ge0\) has dual \(\min b^{T}y\), \(A^{T}y\ge c\), \(y\) free.
  • Dual of the dual is the primal.
  • Weak duality: \(c^{T}x\le b^{T}y\) for feasible \(x,y\).
  • Strong duality: optimal values are equal.
  • Complementary slackness: \(y_i(b_i-a_i^{T}x)=0\) and \(x_j(a_j^{T}y-c_j)=0\).
  • Dual solution from tableau: \(y^{T}=c_B^{T}B^{-1}\).
  • Shadow price: \(y_i^{*}=\partial Z^{*}/\partial b_i\).
  • Primal unbounded \(\Rightarrow\) dual infeasible; both can be infeasible; primal optimal \(\Leftrightarrow\) dual optimal.
  • Primal alternative optima \(\Rightarrow\) dual optimum degenerate. Nondegenerate primal optimum \(\Rightarrow\) dual optimum unique.
  • Dual simplex: leave on the most negative RHS, enter on the minimum \(\left\lvert (Z_j-C_j)/a_{rj}\right\rvert\) over \(a_{rj}<0\).

Worked examples

Example 1: dual with mixed constraints

Primal: \(\max Z=2x_1+3x_2+x_3\) s.t. \(x_1+x_2+x_3\le10\), \(2x_1-x_2\ge4\), \(x_1+2x_3=8\), with \(x_1\ge0\), \(x_2\le0\), \(x_3\) free.

Dual variables: \(y_1\ge0\) (constraint \(\le\)), \(y_2\le0\) (constraint \(\ge\)), \(y_3\) free (constraint \(=\)). Dual objective: \(\min W=10y_1+4y_2+8y_3\). Dual constraints, one per primal variable, from the columns of \(A\):

  • \(x_1\ge0\): \(y_1+2y_2+y_3\ge2\)
  • \(x_2\le0\): \(y_1-y_2\le3\)
  • \(x_3\) free: \(y_1+2y_3=1\)

Check by the table rule: the primal and this dual both have optimal value \(22\). A primal optimum is \((12,0,-2)\) (value \(24-2=22\)) and a dual optimum is \(y=(3,0,-1)\) (value \(30-8=22\)).

Example 2: weak duality, dual and complementary slackness (paint problem)

Primal: \(\max5x_1+4x_2\) s.t. \(6x_1+4x_2\le24\), \(x_1+2x_2\le6\), \(-x_1+x_2\le1\), \(x_2\le2\), \(x\ge0\) (see Linear programming, Example 1; optimum \((3,\tfrac32)\), \(Z=21\)).

Dual: \(\min W=24y_1+6y_2+y_3+2y_4\) s.t. \(6y_1+y_2-y_3\ge5\), \(4y_1+2y_2+y_3+y_4\ge4\), \(y\ge0\).

From the optimal tableau, the entries under \(s_1,\dots,s_4\) are \(Z_j-C_j=(\tfrac34,\tfrac12,0,0)\), so \(y^{*}=(\tfrac34,\tfrac12,0,0)\). Check: \(6\cdot\tfrac34+\tfrac12=5\) and \(4\cdot\tfrac34+2\cdot\tfrac12=4\), both tight; \(W=24\cdot\tfrac34+6\cdot\tfrac12=18+3=21=Z^{*}\).

Weak duality: any feasible \(y\) gives an upper bound; for instance \(y=(1,0,0,0)\) is feasible (\(6\ge5\), \(4\ge4\)) with \(W=24\ge21\).

Complementary slackness: constraints 3 and 4 are slack at \((3,\tfrac32)\) (values \(-\tfrac32<1\) and \(\tfrac32<2\)), so \(y_3=y_4=0\) as found. Since \(x_1,x_2>0\) both dual constraints are tight. Shadow prices: one more unit of raw material M1 earns \(\tfrac34\), of M2 earns \(\tfrac12\).

Example 3: dual from a Big-M tableau

Take \(\min Z=4x_1+x_2\) s.t. \(3x_1+x_2=3\), \(4x_1+3x_2\ge6\), \(x_1+2x_2\le4\) (Linear programming, Example 2). Its dual is

\(\max W=3y_1+6y_2+4y_3\ \text{s.t. }3y_1+4y_2+y_3\le4,\ y_1+3y_2+2y_3\le1,\ y_1\ \text{free},\ y_2\ge0,\ y_3\le0.\)

The final Big-M tableau (for \(\max Z'=-Z\)) has \(Z_j-C_j\) equal to \(-\tfrac75+M\) under \(R_1\), \(M\) under \(R_2\) and \(\tfrac15\) under \(s_3\) (slack of constraint 3). Remove the \(M\) terms: \(y'=(-\tfrac75,0,\tfrac15)\) for the \(\max Z'\) form. The dual variables of the original minimisation are the negatives, \(y=(\tfrac75,0,-\tfrac15)\).

Check: \(3\cdot\tfrac75-\tfrac15=4\) (tight), \(\tfrac75-\tfrac25=1\) (tight), signs \(y_2=0\ge0\), \(y_3<0\le0\), and \(W=\tfrac{21}{5}-\tfrac45=\tfrac{17}{5}=\min Z\).

Example 4: dual simplex

\(\min Z=2x_1+x_2\) s.t. \(3x_1+x_2\ge3\), \(4x_1+3x_2\ge6\), \(x_1+2x_2\ge3\), \(x\ge0\).

Maximise \(Z'=-Z\) and multiply each constraint by \(-1\), adding slacks \(s_1,s_2,s_3\). The starting tableau has \(Z_j-C_j=(2,1,0,0,0)\ge0\) but negative RHS.

Basis \(x_1\) \(x_2\) \(s_1\) \(s_2\) \(s_3\) RHS
\(s_1\) -3 -1 1 0 0 -3
\(s_2\) -4 -3 0 1 0 -6
\(s_3\) -1 -2 0 0 1 -3
\(Z_j-C_j\) 2 1 0 0 0 0

Iteration 1: leaving row \(s_2\) (RHS \(-6\)). Ratios \(\lvert 2/(-4)\rvert=\tfrac12\) and \(\lvert1/(-3)\rvert=\tfrac13\), so \(x_2\) enters. New RHS: \(s_1=-1\), \(x_2=2\), \(s_3=1\).

Iteration 2: leaving row \(s_1\) (RHS \(-1\)), row entries \(x_1:-\tfrac53\), \(s_2:-\tfrac13\); ratios \(\tfrac{2/3}{5/3}=\tfrac25\) and \(\tfrac{1/3}{1/3}=1\), so \(x_1\) enters. Final tableau:

Basis \(x_1\) \(x_2\) \(s_1\) \(s_2\) \(s_3\) RHS
\(x_1\) 1 0 -3/5 1/5 0 3/5
\(x_2\) 0 1 4/5 -3/5 0 6/5
\(s_3\) 0 0 1 -1 1 0
\(Z_j-C_j\) 0 0 2/5 1/5 0 \(-\tfrac{12}{5}\)

All RHS are \(\ge0\). \(x_1=\tfrac35\), \(x_2=\tfrac65\), \(\min Z=\tfrac{12}{5}\). The dual solution reads \(y=(\tfrac25,\tfrac15,0)\) from the slack columns. The basic variable \(s_3=0\) shows the primal optimum is degenerate (all three constraints are tight at the vertex, one more than the two needed in two variables), and correspondingly the dual \(\max3y_1+6y_2+3y_3\) s.t. \(3y_1+4y_2+y_3\le2\), \(y_1+3y_2+2y_3\le1\) has a second optimal solution \((\tfrac35,0,\tfrac15)\) with the same value \(\tfrac{12}{5}\).

Example 5: verifying optimality with complementary slackness

Check that \(x=(12,0,-2)\) is optimal in Example 1. Take \(y=(3,0,-1)\).

  • Dual feasibility: \(y_1+2y_2+y_3=2\ge2\) (tight); \(y_1-y_2=3\le3\) (tight); \(y_1+2y_3=1\) (equality holds); \(y_1\ge0\), \(y_2\le0\).
  • Primal feasibility: \(12+0-2=10\le10\) (tight); \(24-0=24\ge4\) (slack); \(12+2(-2)=8\).
  • Complementary slackness: \(y_1=3>0\) and primal constraint 1 is tight; primal constraint 2 is slack and \(y_2=0\); \(x_1=12>0\) and dual constraint 1 is tight; \(x_3=-2\ne0\) and dual constraint 3 is an equality; \(x_2=0\) imposes nothing.
  • Values: \(Z=24+0-2=22\) and \(W=30+0-8=22\). Equal, so both are optimal.

Common traps

  • Sign of the dual variable for a \(\ge\) constraint in a maximisation problem is \(\le0\), not \(\ge0\). The rule flips with the problem type.
  • Equality constraint gives a free variable; a free variable gives an equality constraint.
  • Dual objective uses the right-hand sides \(b\); dual right-hand sides are the primal costs \(c\). Dual constraints use \(A^{T}\), so each dual constraint is one primal column.
  • Dual of a maximisation is a minimisation. The dual constraints of a canonical max problem are \(\ge\).
  • Primal unbounded gives dual infeasible, not dual unbounded. Both infeasible is possible.
  • Primal infeasible does not force the dual unbounded; it might also be infeasible.
  • Weak duality gives \(c^{T}x\le b^{T}y\) for a max primal; reversing the inequality is a common distractor.
  • The value of \(y_i\) is read under the identity column of the starting tableau, not under the final basic columns. Surplus columns give \(-(Z_j-C_j)\).
  • In a minimisation solved as \(\max(-Z)\), the sign of the dual values flips.
  • Complementary slackness needs both feasibilities; checking the products alone is not enough.
  • Alternative optima in the primal imply a degenerate dual optimum, not a unique non-degenerate one. Nondegenerate primal optimum implies a unique dual optimum, not the other way round.
  • Dual simplex chooses the leaving row first (most negative RHS) and then the entering column; ordinary simplex is the opposite order.

2026 Q65

The primal LPP has alternative optimal solutions, and the dual region is non-degenerate. What is the implication for the dual? The worked key gives A: the dual has a unique optimal solution. The options were only partly recovered. The worked key rules out two options because the dual is feasible and bounded (the primal has an optimum), and chooses between “unique” and “multiple” optimal solutions.

Defect note: the premises contradict each other. A primal with alternative optima forces every dual optimal BFS to be degenerate, so a non-degenerate dual cannot occur. Both solves of the key picked A. The usable facts: a finite primal optimum means the dual is feasible and bounded with the same value, and a non-degenerate dual optimum would force a unique primal optimum.

Quick revision

  • Dual of \(\max c^{T}x\), \(Ax\le b\), \(x\ge0\) is \(\min b^{T}y\), \(A^{T}y\ge c\), \(y\ge0\).
  • Max problem: \(\le\) gives \(y\ge0\), \(\ge\) gives \(y\le0\), \(=\) gives \(y\) free. Variable \(\ge0\) gives \(\ge\), \(\le0\) gives \(\le\), free gives \(=\).
  • \(\max c^{T}x\), \(Ax=b\), \(x\ge0\): dual \(\min b^{T}y\), \(A^{T}y\ge c\), \(y\) free.
  • Dual of the dual is the primal; \(m\) constraints and \(n\) variables give \(m\) dual variables and \(n\) dual constraints.
  • Weak duality: \(c^{T}x\le b^{T}y\). Strong duality: equal optimal values.
  • Primal unbounded gives dual infeasible; both infeasible can occur; optimal and unbounded never pair.
  • Complementary slackness: \(y_i(b_i-a_i^{T}x)=0\), \(x_j(a_j^{T}y-c_j)=0\).
  • \(y=c_B^{T}B^{-1}\): read \(Z_j-C_j\) under slack columns; \(y_i\) is the shadow price.
  • Dual simplex: start with \(Z_j-C_j\ge0\), RHS negative; leave on the most negative RHS, enter on the minimum ratio over negative entries.
  • Primal alternative optima means a degenerate dual optimum; nondegenerate primal optimum means a unique dual optimum.
