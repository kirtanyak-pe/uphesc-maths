Connectedness and compactness

15 questions. Pick an answer to see if it is right and why.

0 of 15 answered · 0 correct

  1. Question 1Easy
    Which of the following is NOT necessarily preserved when a continuous map \(f:X\to Y\) is applied?

    Why

    Continuous images of compact, connected and path-connected sets are compact, connected and path-connected. The image of a closed set need not be closed: \(x\mapsto\tfrac1{1+x^2}\) maps the closed set \(\mathbb R\) onto \((0,1]\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    The connected subsets of \(\mathbb R\) (usual topology) are exactly

    Why

    A subset of \(\mathbb R\) is connected iff it is convex, that is, an interval of any type (bounded or unbounded, open, closed or half-open, possibly a point).
  3. Question 3Medium
    Which of these subsets of \(\mathbb R\) is compact?

    Why

    Compact in \(\mathbb R\) means closed and bounded. \(\{1/n\}\) is not closed (missing \(0\)), \(\mathbb Z\) is not bounded, \((0,1]\) is not closed.
  4. Question 4Medium
    Which of the following metric spaces (usual metric) has the property that every sequence in it has a Cauchy subsequence?

    Why

    This property is equivalent to total boundedness. \((0,1)\) is totally bounded. \(\mathbb R\), \(\mathbb Z\) and \([0,\infty)\) are unbounded, so the sequence \(x_n=n\) has no Cauchy subsequence.
  5. Question 5Medium
    Consider: (I) a continuous bijection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism; (II) every closed and bounded subset of every metric space is compact; (III) a compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed. Which are true?

    Why

    (I) and (III) are standard theorems. (II) is false: the closed unit ball of \(\ell^2\), or an infinite set with the discrete metric \(d=1\), is closed and bounded but not compact.
  6. Question 6Medium
    The number of connected components of the orthogonal group \(O(3)\) is

    Why

    \(\det:O(3)\to\{\pm1\}\) is continuous and onto, so there are at least two pieces. \(SO(3)\) is connected and \(O(3)=SO(3)\cup(-I)SO(3)\), so there are exactly \(2\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    The topologist’s sine curve \(S=\{(x,\sin\tfrac1x):0<x\le1\}\cup(\{0\}\times[-1,1])\) is

    Why

    \(S\) is the closure of a connected graph, so it is connected. No path can join a point of the vertical segment to a point of the graph, because \(\sin(1/x)\) oscillates near \(x=0\).
  8. Question 8Hard
    The number of connected components of \(\mathbb R^2\setminus\{(x,y):xy=1\}\) is

    Why

    The set \(xy>1\) has two pieces (\(x,y>0\) and \(x,y<0\)). The set \(xy<1\) contains the origin and is connected. So \(3\) components (grid flood-fill check agrees).
  9. Question 9Hard
    The one-point compactification of the disjoint union \((0,1)\sqcup(2,3)\) is homeomorphic to

    Why

    Adding one point \(\infty\) closes up each interval into a circle, and both circles pass through the single point \(\infty\).
  10. Question 10Hard
    Assertion (A): \([0,1]\) is not compact as a subspace of the lower limit line \(\mathbb R_\ell\). Reason (R): \([0,1]\) is not closed in \(\mathbb R_\ell\).

    Why

    The cover \(\{[0,1-\tfrac1n):n\ge2\}\cup\{[1,2)\}\) has no finite subcover, so A is true. But \(\mathbb R\setminus[0,1]=(-\infty,0)\cup(1,\infty)\) is a union of sets \([a,b)\), so it is open and \([0,1]\) is closed; R is false.
  11. Question 11Medium
    A continuous bijection \(f:X\to Y\) is guaranteed to be a homeomorphism when

    Why

    A continuous map from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is closed, so a continuous bijection is also a closed map, hence its inverse is continuous. The other conditions fail: \([0,2\pi)\to S^1\) has Hausdorff domain and compact codomain.
  12. Question 12Easy
    The Heine-Borel theorem (closed and bounded implies compact) holds for subsets of

    Why

    In general metric spaces compactness needs total boundedness plus completeness. In \(\mathbb R^n\) boundedness is equivalent to total boundedness.
  13. Question 13Medium
    Let \(X=\{1,2,3,4\}\) carry the topology whose open sets are the unions of \(\{1\},\{2\},\{1,2\},\{3,4\}\) (and \(\emptyset\)). The number of connected components of \(X\) is

    Why

    \(\{1\}\), \(\{2\}\) and \(\{3,4\}\) are clopen. The subspace \(\{3,4\}\) has only \(\emptyset,\{3,4\}\) as open sets, so it is connected. Components: \(\{1\},\{2\},\{3,4\}\).
  14. Question 14Medium
    By the Baire category theorem, exactly one of the following is impossible. Which one?

    Why

    If \(\mathbb Q\) were a \(G_\delta\), then \(\mathbb Q\) and the \(G_\delta\) set \(\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q=\bigcap_q(\mathbb R\setminus\{q\})\) would be disjoint dense \(G_\delta\) sets, contradicting Baire. The other three are possible: \(\mathbb Q\) is the union of its points (closed, empty interior), \(\mathbb Z\) is the union of its points (nowhere dense), and \(\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q\) is the intersection of the open sets \(\mathbb R\setminus\{q\}\).
  15. Question 15Hard
    Consider: (I) every compact space is limit point compact; (II) a limit point compact metric space is compact; (III) the ordinal space \([0,\omega_1)\) with the order topology is compact. Which are true?

    Why

    (I) is true: an infinite subset of a compact space has a limit point. (II) is true for metric spaces (compact, sequentially compact and limit point compact coincide). (III) is false: the open cover by the sets \([0,\alpha)\), \(\alpha<\omega_1\), has no finite subcover; \([0,\omega_1)\) is sequentially compact but not compact. So I and II only.
