Why it matters for the exam

This topic is the toolbox for the next two: every Gaussian-curvature and geodesic question starts by writing \(E,F,G\) and \(L,M,N\). By itself it is asked as “write the first fundamental form of …”, “find the angle between two curves on a surface” or “find an area / length on a surface”. Speed in computing \(E,F,G,L,M,N\) for the sphere, cylinder, cone, paraboloid, surface of revolution and helicoid decides several marks.

Core ideas

Parametrised surfaces

A parametrised surface is a smooth map \(\mathbf r(u,v):U\subset\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^3\). It is regular if \(\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v\ne0\) everywhere, i.e. \(\mathbf r_u,\mathbf r_v\) are linearly independent. Then:

  • the tangent plane at a point is spanned by \(\mathbf r_u,\mathbf r_v\);
  • the unit normal is \(\mathbf n=\dfrac{\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v}{\lVert\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v\rVert}\) (the opposite choice \(-\mathbf n\) is the other orientation);
  • the tangent plane at \(P\) is \((\mathbf R-\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf n=0\).

A surface given as a graph \(z=f(x,y)\) is parametrised by \(\mathbf r(x,y)=(x,y,f(x,y))\) (the Monge patch), and then \(\mathbf n=\dfrac{(-f_x,-f_y,1)}{\sqrt{1+f_x^2+f_y^2}}\). For an implicit surface \(\Phi(x,y,z)=c\), \(\mathbf n=\nabla\Phi/\lVert\nabla\Phi\rVert\).

First fundamental form

A curve on the surface is \(\mathbf r(u(t),v(t))\) and its velocity is \(\mathbf r_uu'+\mathbf r_vv'\). Its squared speed is

\(ds^2=E\,du^2+2F\,du\,dv+G\,dv^2=:\mathrm I,\) \(E=\mathbf r_u\cdot\mathbf r_u,\qquad F=\mathbf r_u\cdot\mathbf r_v,\qquad G=\mathbf r_v\cdot\mathbf r_v .\)

\(\mathrm I\) is the first fundamental form. It is positive definite: \(EG-F^2=\lVert\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v\rVert^2>0\) and \(E,G>0\). It is the metric of the surface: everything measured inside the surface comes from \(E,F,G\) (this is what “intrinsic” means).

  • Arc length of \(u(t),v(t)\), \(a\le t\le b\): \(s=\displaystyle\int_a^b\sqrt{E u'^2+2Fu'v'+Gv'^2}\,dt\).
  • Angle between two curves through a point with tangent directions \((du,dv)\) and \((\delta u,\delta v)\): \(\cos\theta=\frac{E\,du\,\delta u+F(du\,\delta v+dv\,\delta u)+G\,dv\,\delta v}{\sqrt{E\,du^2+2F\,du\,dv+G\,dv^2}\ \sqrt{E\,\delta u^2+2F\,\delta u\,\delta v+G\,\delta v^2}}.\)
  • The angle \(\omega\) between the parametric curves (\(v=\) const and \(u=\) const; directions \(\mathbf r_u,\mathbf r_v\)) satisfies \(\cos\omega=\dfrac{F}{\sqrt{EG}}\), \(\sin\omega=\dfrac{\sqrt{EG-F^2}}{\sqrt{EG}}\). So the parametric curves are orthogonal iff \(F=0\).
  • Orthogonality condition for two directions: \(E\,du\,\delta u+F(du\,\delta v+dv\,\delta u)+G\,dv\,\delta v=0\).
  • Area element \(dA=\sqrt{EG-F^2}\,du\,dv\); area of a region \(R\) is \(\displaystyle\iint_R\sqrt{EG-F^2}\,du\,dv\).

Isometries. Two surfaces are (locally) isometric if there are parametrisations with the same \(E,F,G\). An isometry preserves lengths, angles, areas, geodesics and (by Gauss) Gaussian curvature. A plane and a cylinder are locally isometric (unroll the cylinder), and so are a plane and a cone, and the catenoid and the helicoid. A sphere is not isometric to any piece of a plane (see topic 08). A map is conformal if it preserves angles but not necessarily lengths: \(E:F:G\) equal to the target’s; it is equiareal if \(EG-F^2\) is preserved.

Second fundamental form

The second fundamental form measures how the surface bends away from its tangent plane. With \(\mathbf n\) the unit normal,

\(\mathrm{II}=L\,du^2+2M\,du\,dv+N\,dv^2,\) \(L=\mathbf r_{uu}\cdot\mathbf n,\qquad M=\mathbf r_{uv}\cdot\mathbf n,\qquad N=\mathbf r_{vv}\cdot\mathbf n .\)

Equivalent forms: \(L=-\mathbf r_u\cdot\mathbf n_u\), \(M=-\mathbf r_u\cdot\mathbf n_v=-\mathbf r_v\cdot\mathbf n_u\), \(N=-\mathbf r_v\cdot\mathbf n_v\); and \(L=\dfrac{[\mathbf r_{uu},\mathbf r_u,\mathbf r_v]}{\sqrt{EG-F^2}}\), \(M=\dfrac{[\mathbf r_{uv},\mathbf r_u,\mathbf r_v]}{\sqrt{EG-F^2}}\), \(N=\dfrac{[\mathbf r_{vv},\mathbf r_u,\mathbf r_v]}{\sqrt{EG-F^2}}\).

For a Monge patch \(z=f(x,y)\) write \(p=f_x\), \(q=f_y\), \(r=f_{xx}\), \(s=f_{xy}\), \(t=f_{yy}\), \(W=\sqrt{1+p^2+q^2}\): \(E=1+p^2,\quad F=pq,\quad G=1+q^2,\qquad L=\frac rW,\quad M=\frac sW,\quad N=\frac tW .\)

Changing \(\mathbf n\) to \(-\mathbf n\) changes the sign of \(L,M,N\) but not of \(E,F,G\). Both forms are unchanged by a reparametrisation (as quadratic forms of the tangent vector), and by a rigid motion.

Normal curvature

For a curve \(\gamma\) on the surface, the part of its curvature vector \(\kappa N_\gamma\) along the surface normal is the normal curvature \(\kappa_n=\kappa\,(N_\gamma\cdot\mathbf n)\). It depends only on the point and on the tangent direction \((du:dv)\):

\(\kappa_n=\frac{\mathrm{II}}{\mathrm I}=\frac{L\,du^2+2M\,du\,dv+N\,dv^2}{E\,du^2+2F\,du\,dv+G\,dv^2}.\)

\(\kappa_n\) is the curvature of the normal section, the plane curve cut by the plane through \(\mathbf n\) and the chosen direction (up to sign). The remaining part of \(\kappa N_\gamma\) is tangent to the surface; its size is the geodesic curvature \(\kappa_g\) (topic 09), and \(\kappa^2=\kappa_n^2+\kappa_g^2\).

Meusnier’s theorem. All curves on a surface through a point \(P\) with the same tangent line there have the same normal curvature, and \(\kappa_n=\kappa\cos\theta,\) where \(\theta\) is the angle between the principal normal \(N_\gamma\) of the curve and the surface normal \(\mathbf n\). Consequently the curvature of an oblique section is the curvature of the normal section divided by \(\cos\theta\), and the centres of curvature of all curves with a common tangent at \(P\) lie on a circle (the Meusnier circle) whose diameter is the radius of the normal section.

Standard surfaces

Throughout, \(\mathbf n\) is the normal given by \(\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v\) with the parametrisation shown (so signs of \(L,M,N\) follow it).

Surface Parametrisation \(E,F,G\) \(L,M,N\)
Plane \((u,v,0)\) \(1,0,1\) \(0,0,0\)
Sphere radius \(a\) \((a\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\ a\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\ a\cos\theta)\) \(a^2,\ 0,\ a^2\sin^2\theta\) \(-a,\ 0,\ -a\sin^2\theta\)
Cylinder radius \(a\) \((a\cos\theta,\ a\sin\theta,\ z)\) \(a^2,\ 0,\ 1\) \(-a,\ 0,\ 0\)
Cone, semi-angle \(\alpha\) \((v\sin\alpha\cos u,\ v\sin\alpha\sin u,\ v\cos\alpha)\) \(v^2\sin^2\alpha,\ 0,\ 1\) \(-v\sin\alpha\cos\alpha,\ 0,\ 0\)
Paraboloid \(z=x^2+y^2\) \((x,y,x^2+y^2)\) \(1+4x^2,\ 4xy,\ 1+4y^2\) \(\dfrac{2}{W},\ 0,\ \dfrac2W\), \(W=\sqrt{1+4x^2+4y^2}\)
Hyperbolic paraboloid \(z=xy\) \((x,y,xy)\) \(1+y^2,\ xy,\ 1+x^2\) \(0,\ \dfrac1W,\ 0\), \(W=\sqrt{1+x^2+y^2}\)
Helicoid \((u\cos v,\ u\sin v,\ cv)\) \(1,\ 0,\ u^2+c^2\) \(0,\ \dfrac{-c}{\sqrt{u^2+c^2}},\ 0\)
Catenoid \((a\cosh\tfrac ua\cos v,\ a\cosh\tfrac ua\sin v,\ u)\) \(\cosh^2\tfrac ua,\ 0,\ a^2\cosh^2\tfrac ua\) \(-\dfrac1a,\ 0,\ a\)

(The sign pattern in the sphere row corresponds to the outward normal; with the inward normal the signs flip.) The sphere row can be rederived in seconds: \(\mathbf r_{\theta\theta}=-\mathbf r\), so \(L=-\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf n=-a\).

Surfaces of revolution

Rotate the profile curve \((f(u),0,g(u))\) about the \(z\)-axis: \(\mathbf r=(f(u)\cos v,\ f(u)\sin v,\ g(u))\), \(f>0\).

\(E=f'^2+g'^2,\quad F=0,\quad G=f^2,\) \(L=\frac{f'g''-f''g'}{\sqrt{f'^2+g'^2}},\quad M=0,\quad N=\frac{fg'}{\sqrt{f'^2+g'^2}} .\)

The curves \(u=\) const are the parallels (circles of radius \(f\)) and the curves \(v=\) const are the meridians. They are orthogonal (\(F=0\)). If the profile is parametrised by arc length, \(f'^2+g'^2=1\), so \(\mathrm I=du^2+f(u)^2dv^2\) and \(K=-f''/f\) (topic 08). Special cases: the sphere (\(f=a\sin(u/a)\), \(g=a\cos(u/a)\)), the torus (\(f=b+a\cos u\), \(g=a\sin u\), \(E=a^2\), \(G=(b+a\cos u)^2\)), the cylinder (\(f=a\)), the cone (\(f=u\sin\alpha\)), and the pseudosphere (tractrix profile).

Using the forms: worked patterns

  • Length of a curve on a sphere. A latitude circle \(\theta=\theta_0\): \(ds^2=a^2\sin^2\theta_0\,d\varphi^2\), length \(2\pi a\sin\theta_0\).
  • Area of a region. Sphere zone: \(dA=a^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi\), so the cap \(0\le\theta\le\theta_0\) has area \(2\pi a^2(1-\cos\theta_0)\) and the whole sphere \(4\pi a^2\). Torus: \(dA=a(b+a\cos u)\,du\,dv\), total \(4\pi^2ab\).
  • Cylinder. The helix \(\theta=t\), \(z=ct\) has \(ds^2=(a^2+c^2)dt^2\), length per turn \(2\pi\sqrt{a^2+c^2}\) – the same as unrolling.
  • Cone. \(\mathrm I=v^2\sin^2\alpha\,du^2+dv^2\). Writing \(\phi=u\sin\alpha\) turns this into polar coordinates \(dv^2+v^2d\phi^2\), so the cone unrolls onto a sector of angle \(2\pi\sin\alpha\).

Formulas and results

  • \(E=\mathbf r_u\cdot\mathbf r_u\), \(F=\mathbf r_u\cdot\mathbf r_v\), \(G=\mathbf r_v\cdot\mathbf r_v\); \(EG-F^2=\lVert\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v\rVert^2>0\).
  • \(ds^2=E\,du^2+2F\,du\,dv+G\,dv^2\); \(dA=\sqrt{EG-F^2}\,du\,dv\).
  • \(\cos\omega=F/\sqrt{EG}\) between parametric curves; \(F=0\) iff they are orthogonal.
  • Angle between directions \((du,dv)\), \((\delta u,\delta v)\): \(\dfrac{E\,du\,\delta u+F(du\,\delta v+dv\,\delta u)+G\,dv\,\delta v}{\sqrt{\mathrm I}\sqrt{\mathrm I'}}\).
  • \(\mathbf n=\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v/\lVert\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v\rVert\); \(L=\mathbf r_{uu}\cdot\mathbf n\), \(M=\mathbf r_{uv}\cdot\mathbf n\), \(N=\mathbf r_{vv}\cdot\mathbf n\).
  • \(\kappa_n=\mathrm{II}/\mathrm I\); Meusnier: \(\kappa_n=\kappa\cos\theta\).
  • Monge patch \(z=f\): \(E=1+p^2\), \(F=pq\), \(G=1+q^2\), \((L,M,N)=(r,s,t)/\sqrt{1+p^2+q^2}\).
  • Surface of revolution \((f\cos v,f\sin v,g)\): \(E=f'^2+g'^2\), \(F=0\), \(G=f^2\).
  • Sphere: \(\mathrm I=a^2d\theta^2+a^2\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2\); zone area \(2\pi a^2(1-\cos\theta_0)\); helicoid \(\mathrm I=du^2+(u^2+c^2)dv^2\).
  • Reversing \(\mathbf n\): \(L,M,N\to-L,-M,-N\); \(E,F,G\) unchanged.
  • Isometry keeps \(E,F,G\), hence lengths, angles, areas, geodesics and \(K\).

Worked examples

Example 1: first fundamental form and length on the helicoid

For \(\mathbf r=(u\cos v,\ u\sin v,\ 2v)\): \(\mathbf r_u=(\cos v,\sin v,0)\), \(\mathbf r_v=(-u\sin v,u\cos v,2)\). \(E=1\), \(F=0\), \(G=u^2+4\). So \(\mathrm I=du^2+(u^2+4)dv^2\). At \(u=1\), \(G=5\). Length of the curve \(u=1\), \(0\le v\le\pi\): \(\int_0^\pi\sqrt5\,dv=\pi\sqrt5\) (a helix on the cylinder \(u=1\) with pitch parameter \(2\)).

Example 2: angle between two curves

On the helicoid with \(c=1\), \(\mathrm I=du^2+(1+u^2)dv^2\), the curves \(v=u\) and \(v=2-u\) meet at \((u,v)=(1,1)\). Tangent directions \((1,1)\) and \((1,-1)\); there \(E=1\), \(G=2\). Then \(\cos\theta=\frac{1\cdot1\cdot1+2\cdot1\cdot(-1)}{\sqrt{1+2}\sqrt{1+2}}=\frac{-1}{3}.\) The angle is \(\arccos(-1/3)\approx109.5^\circ\) (or its supplement, \(70.5^\circ\), as an unoriented angle). Note: in the plane’s \((u,v)\) these lines look perpendicular, but on the surface they are not – angles on the surface need \(E,F,G\).

Example 3: area of a sphere cap and of a helicoid strip

Cap \(0\le\theta\le\pi/3\) on a sphere of radius \(2\): \(A=2\pi\cdot4\,(1-\cos60^\circ)=4\pi\). Helicoid piece \(0\le u\le1\), \(0\le v\le2\pi\), \(c=1\): \(\sqrt{EG-F^2}=\sqrt{1+u^2}\), so \(A=2\pi\int_0^1\sqrt{1+u^2}\,du=2\pi\Big[\tfrac u2\sqrt{1+u^2}+\tfrac12\sinh^{-1}u\Big]_0^1=\pi\big(\sqrt2+\ln(1+\sqrt2)\big)\approx7.21.\)

Example 4: second fundamental form of \(z=xy\)

\(f=xy\): \(p=y\), \(q=x\), \(r=t=0\), \(s=1\). At the origin \(W=1\): \(E=G=1\), \(F=0\), \(L=N=0\), \(M=1\). So \(\mathrm I=du^2+dv^2\), \(\mathrm{II}=2\,du\,dv\) and \(\kappa_n=\dfrac{2\,du\,dv}{du^2+dv^2}\). In the direction at angle \(\alpha\) from the \(x\)-axis (\(du=\cos\alpha\), \(dv=\sin\alpha\)) this is \(\sin2\alpha\). It ranges over \([-1,1]\) with extreme values at \(\alpha=\pm45^\circ\).

Example 5: Meusnier on a sphere

A sphere has radius \(5\). A plane at distance \(3\) from the centre cuts it in a circle of radius \(4\), curvature \(\kappa=1/4\). The sphere’s normal curvature in every direction is \(1/5\). Meusnier: \(\cos\theta=\kappa_n/\kappa=(1/5)/(1/4)=4/5\). So the principal normal of the small circle makes an angle \(\arccos(4/5)\) with the surface normal, and indeed \(1/4=(1/5)/(4/5)\): the small circle’s curvature is the great circle’s curvature divided by \(\cos\theta\).

Example 6: normal curvature on a cylinder

Cylinder of radius \(a=2\), outward normal. \(\mathrm I=4\,d\theta^2+dz^2\), \(\mathrm{II}=-2\,d\theta^2\). A unit direction at angle \(\varphi\) from the axis has \(2\,d\theta=\sin\varphi\), \(dz=\cos\varphi\), so \(\kappa_n=-2\cdot\dfrac{\sin^2\varphi}{4}=-\dfrac{\sin^2\varphi}{2}\). At \(\varphi=30^\circ\), \(\lvert\kappa_n\rvert=\dfrac18\). Along the axis (ruling) \(\kappa_n=0\); along the circle \(\lvert\kappa_n\rvert=1/2=1/a\).

Common traps

  • \(F=0\) is not automatic. Only for orthogonal parametrisations (sphere, surfaces of revolution, cylinder, helicoid). On the paraboloid and \(z=xy\), \(F=pq\ne0\).
  • Area element is \(\sqrt{EG-F^2}\), not \(EG-F^2\) and not \(\sqrt{EG}\) unless \(F=0\).
  • Wrong \(G\) on the sphere. \(G=a^2\sin^2\theta\), not \(a^2\); \(\theta\) is the colatitude. If latitude \(\lambda\) is used instead, \(G=a^2\cos^2\lambda\).
  • Sign of \(L,M,N\) depends on the chosen normal. Never quote the sign of \(L\) for a sphere without saying which normal; \(K\) does not depend on it, and \(H\) does.
  • Angle between curves. Use the full formula with \(F\); the “angle between the directions” can be \(\theta\) or \(\pi-\theta\).
  • Normal curvature is not curvature of the curve. Only for a normal section (or geodesic) are they equal. Meusnier: \(\kappa_n=\kappa\cos\theta\), so \(\lvert\kappa_n\rvert\le\kappa\).
  • Intrinsic vs extrinsic. \(\mathrm I\) (and \(K\)) are intrinsic; \(\mathrm{II}\) and \(H\) are not. A cylinder and a plane have the same \(\mathrm I\) but different \(\mathrm{II}\).
  • Cone: \(G=1\) and \(E=v^2\sin^2\alpha\) when \(v\) measures distance from the vertex along a generator. If the cone is written \(z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\) with \(x=u\cos t\), then \(E=2\) for \(u\).
  • A reparametrisation changes the coefficients; the quadratic forms stay the same.

Quick revision

  • \(E=\mathbf r_u^2\), \(F=\mathbf r_u\cdot\mathbf r_v\), \(G=\mathbf r_v^2\); \(ds^2=E\,du^2+2F\,du\,dv+G\,dv^2\).
  • \(dA=\sqrt{EG-F^2}\,du\,dv\); \(\cos\omega=F/\sqrt{EG}\); orthogonal net iff \(F=0\).
  • \(L,M,N=\mathbf r_{uu},\mathbf r_{uv},\mathbf r_{vv}\) dotted with the unit normal.
  • Monge \(z=f\): \((E,F,G)=(1+p^2,pq,1+q^2)\) and \((L,M,N)=(r,s,t)/\sqrt{1+p^2+q^2}\).
  • \(\kappa_n=\mathrm{II}/\mathrm I\); Meusnier: \(\kappa_n=\kappa\cos\theta\).
  • Sphere: \(E=a^2\), \(F=0\), \(G=a^2\sin^2\theta\); \(L=-a\), \(N=-a\sin^2\theta\) (outward normal).
  • Cylinder: \(E=a^2\), \(G=1\); \(L=-a\), \(M=N=0\). Cone: \(E=v^2\sin^2\alpha\), \(G=1\), \(L=-v\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\).
  • Helicoid: \(\mathrm I=du^2+(u^2+c^2)dv^2\); \(M=-c/\sqrt{u^2+c^2}\), \(L=N=0\).
  • Surface of revolution: \(E=f'^2+g'^2\), \(F=0\), \(G=f^2\).
  • Sphere area \(4\pi a^2\); cap \(2\pi a^2(1-\cos\theta_0)\); torus \(4\pi^2ab\).
  • Isometry preserves \(E,F,G\), lengths, angles, areas, \(K\) (not \(H\)).
