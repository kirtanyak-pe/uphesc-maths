Surfaces and fundamental forms

  1. Question 1Easy
    For the sphere of radius \(a\) parametrised by colatitude \(\theta\) and longitude \(\varphi\), the first fundamental form is

    Why

    \(\mathbf r_\theta=a(\cos\theta\cos\varphi,\cos\theta\sin\varphi,-\sin\theta)\) has \(E=a^2\); \(\mathbf r_\varphi=a\sin\theta(-\sin\varphi,\cos\varphi,0)\) has \(G=a^2\sin^2\theta\); \(F=0\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    For a parametrised surface with first fundamental form coefficients \(E,F,G\), the element of area is

    Why

    \(\lVert\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v\rVert^2=EG-F^2\), so \(dA=\lVert\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v\rVert\,du\,dv=\sqrt{EG-F^2}\,du\,dv\). (\(\sqrt{EG}\) is correct only when \(F=0\).)
  3. Question 3Easy
    The parametric curves \(u=\text{const}\) and \(v=\text{const}\) of a surface are orthogonal at every point if and only if

    Why

    The angle \(\omega\) between them has \(\cos\omega=F/\sqrt{EG}\), so they are orthogonal iff \(F=0\). (\(M=0\) concerns the second fundamental form; \(EG=F^2\) is impossible for a regular surface.)
  4. Question 4Medium
    The area of the spherical cap \(0\le\theta\le\pi/3\) on a sphere of radius \(2\) is

    Why

    \(dA=a^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi\), so \(A=2\pi a^2(1-\cos\theta_0)=2\pi\cdot4\cdot\tfrac12=4\pi\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    On the cylinder \(\mathbf r=(2\cos\theta,\,2\sin\theta,\,z)\), the length of the curve \(\theta=t\), \(z=t\), \(0\le t\le\pi\) is

    Why

    \(E=4\), \(F=0\), \(G=1\), so \(ds^2=4\,d\theta^2+dz^2=(4+1)dt^2\) and the length is \(\sqrt5\,\pi\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    On the helicoid \(\mathbf r=(u\cos v,\,u\sin v,\,v)\) the curves \(v=u\) and \(v=2-u\) meet at \((u,v)=(1,1)\). The cosine of the angle between their tangent directions \((du,dv)=(1,1)\) and \((1,-1)\) is

    Why

    \(E=1\), \(F=0\), \(G=u^2+1=2\) at \(u=1\). \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{1\cdot1\cdot1+2\cdot1\cdot(-1)}{\sqrt{1+2}\,\sqrt{1+2}}=-\dfrac13\). The answer \(0\) is what the flat \((u,v)\)-plane would suggest.
  7. Question 7Medium
    On the cylinder of radius \(2\), the magnitude of the normal curvature in a direction making an angle \(30^\circ\) with the axis is

    Why

    \(\mathrm I=4\,d\theta^2+dz^2\), \(\mathrm{II}=-2\,d\theta^2\) (outward normal). For a unit direction at angle \(\varphi\) from the axis, \(2\,d\theta=\sin\varphi\), so \(\lvert\kappa_n\rvert=\dfrac{2\,d\theta^2}{1}=\dfrac{\sin^2\varphi}{2}=\dfrac18\) at \(\varphi=30^\circ\). (\(\tfrac38\) would be \(\cos^2\)-type, measured from the circle.)
  8. Question 8Medium
    A sphere of radius \(5\) is cut by a plane at distance \(3\) from its centre. By Meusnier’s theorem, the cosine of the angle between the principal normal of the section circle and the normal of the sphere is

    Why

    The section circle has radius \(\sqrt{25-9}=4\), so \(\kappa=\tfrac14\); the sphere’s normal curvature is \(\kappa_n=\tfrac15\) in every direction. \(\kappa_n=\kappa\cos\theta\) gives \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{1/5}{1/4}=\dfrac45\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    Which statements are correct? (i) The first fundamental form is positive definite. (ii) Replacing the normal \(\mathbf n\) by \(-\mathbf n\) leaves the second fundamental form unchanged. (iii) Two locally isometric surfaces have the same second fundamental form. (iv) \(F=0\) means the parametric curves are orthogonal.

    Why

    (i) \(EG-F^2>0\), \(E>0\). (ii) is false: \(L,M,N\) change sign. (iii) is false: a plane and a cylinder are isometric, but \(\mathrm{II}=0\) for one and not for the other. (iv) is true.
  10. Question 10Medium
    For the helicoid \(\mathbf r=(u\cos v,\,u\sin v,\,3v)\), the coefficient \(G\) of the first fundamental form at \(u=4\) is

    Why

    \(\mathbf r_v=(-u\sin v,u\cos v,3)\), so \(G=u^2+9=25\) at \(u=4\). Option \(16\) forgets the \(3^2\); \(9\) forgets \(u^2\).
  11. Question 11Hard
    The cone \(x^2+y^2=z^2\) is cut along a generator and rolled out flat. The angle of the resulting circular sector is

    Why

    Semi-vertical angle \(\alpha=45^\circ\). With \(v\) the distance from the vertex along a generator, \(\mathrm I=dv^2+v^2\sin^2\alpha\,du^2\). Putting \(\phi=u\sin\alpha\) gives the polar metric \(dv^2+v^2d\phi^2\), and \(u\in[0,2\pi)\) gives \(\phi\in[0,2\pi\sin\alpha)\). So the sector angle is \(2\pi\sin45^\circ=\pi\sqrt2\).
  12. Question 12Hard
    The area of the helicoid \(\mathbf r=(u\cos v,\,u\sin v,\,v)\), \(0\le u\le1\), \(0\le v\le2\pi\), is

    Why

    \(E=1\), \(F=0\), \(G=1+u^2\), so \(dA=\sqrt{1+u^2}\,du\,dv\) and \(A=2\pi\int_0^1\sqrt{1+u^2}\,du=2\pi\Big[\tfrac u2\sqrt{1+u^2}+\tfrac12\sinh^{-1}u\Big]_0^1=\pi\big(\sqrt2+\ln(1+\sqrt2)\big)\approx7.21\).
  13. Question 13Hard
    Assertion (A): A circular cylinder is locally isometric to a plane. Reason (R): In the coordinates \((\theta,z)\) the first fundamental form of the cylinder of radius \(a\) is \(a^2d\theta^2+dz^2=du^2+dz^2\) with \(u=a\theta\). Choose the correct option.

    Why

    Having the same \(E,F,G\) in suitable coordinates (\(E=G=1\), \(F=0\), the plane’s form) is exactly the definition of local isometry, so R explains A.
