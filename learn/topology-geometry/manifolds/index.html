Why it matters for the exam

Manifolds are the last phrase of the official Unit 5 text, and they surfaced in 2026 twice (2026-Q96 on components of a compact connected manifold, and 2026-Q42, covered in the previous topic, on Urysohn metrization of a manifold). Expect definition-level questions: the three conditions in the definition, dimension of standard examples, charts and transition maps, which spaces are manifolds, and the dimension of a tangent space.

Core ideas

Topological manifolds

A topological manifold of dimension \(n\) is a topological space \(M\) that is

  1. Hausdorff,
  2. second countable,
  3. locally Euclidean of dimension \(n\): every point has an open neighbourhood homeomorphic to an open subset of \(\mathbb R^n\).

All three conditions are needed.

Space Locally Euclidean Hausdorff Second countable Manifold?
line with two origins yes no yes no
long line yes yes no no
uncountable disjoint union of copies of \(\mathbb R\) yes yes no no
the cone \(x^2+y^2=z^2\) in \(\mathbb R^3\) no (at the vertex) yes yes no
figure eight (two circles meeting at a point) no (at the wedge point) yes yes no
union of the two coordinate axes in \(\mathbb R^2\) no (at the crossing) yes yes no
\(\mathbb R^n\), any open subset of \(\mathbb R^n\) yes yes yes yes

A point in a manifold is not special: near any point the space looks like \(\mathbb R^n\).

Consequences of the definition.

  • A manifold is locally compact, locally connected and locally path-connected; it is regular, and by Urysohn’s theorem metrizable. It is also paracompact.
  • Components are open. Each component is a manifold, and there are at most countably many components (second countability). A connected manifold is path-connected.
  • A compact connected manifold has exactly one connected component (that is the meaning of connected; 2026-Q96). A compact manifold has finitely many components.
  • Dimension is well defined: \(\mathbb R^n\not\cong\mathbb R^m\) for \(n\ne m\) (invariance of domain), so a nonempty manifold cannot have two different dimensions near the same point. A \(0\)-manifold is a countable discrete space.
  • Open subsets of manifolds are manifolds of the same dimension. Products: \(\dim(M\times N)=\dim M+\dim N\).
  • Classification in low dimension. Connected \(1\)-manifolds: \(\mathbb R\) and \(S^1\) (compact one is \(S^1\)). Compact connected surfaces without boundary: the sphere, a sphere with \(g\) handles (orientable, Euler characteristic \(2-2g\)), or a connected sum of \(k\) projective planes (non-orientable, Euler characteristic \(2-k\)).

Charts, atlases and smooth manifolds

A chart is a pair \((U,\varphi)\) with \(U\subseteq M\) open and \(\varphi:U\to\varphi(U)\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) a homeomorphism onto an open set. An atlas is a family of charts whose domains cover \(M\). For two charts \((U,\varphi)\) and \((V,\psi)\) with \(U\cap V\ne\emptyset\) the transition map is

\[\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}:\varphi(U\cap V)\to\psi(U\cap V).\]

It is always a homeomorphism between open subsets of \(\mathbb R^n\).

An atlas is smooth (\(C^\infty\)) if all transition maps are \(C^\infty\) (then they are diffeomorphisms, since the inverse is also a transition map). Two smooth atlases are compatible if their union is smooth; a smooth structure is a maximal smooth atlas. A smooth manifold is a topological manifold with a smooth structure.

A map \(f:M\to N\) is smooth if in charts it is smooth: \(\psi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1}\) is \(C^\infty\). A diffeomorphism is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse.

Points to know.

  • Different smooth structures on the same topological manifold may give diffeomorphic manifolds. On \(\mathbb R\) the chart \(x\mapsto x^3\) is not compatible with the identity chart (its inverse \(\sqrt[3]{x}\) is not smooth at \(0\)), but the two structures give diffeomorphic manifolds.
  • Every topological manifold of dimension \(\le3\) has a unique smooth structure up to diffeomorphism. \(\mathbb R^n\) has a unique one for \(n\ne4\), uncountably many for \(n=4\). The sphere \(S^7\) has \(28\) smooth structures up to orientation-preserving diffeomorphism (\(15\) up to diffeomorphism) (Milnor’s exotic spheres). There are topological manifolds with no smooth structure.
  • \(C^k\) manifolds and analytic manifolds are defined in the same way.

Special cases: \(SO(2)\cong S^1\) (dimension \(1\)), \(SO(3)\) has dimension \(3\), \(SU(2)\cong S^3\). \(O(n)\) has two components, \(SO(n)\) is connected, and \(O(n)\), \(SO(n)\) are compact.

Atlas for \(S^n\). With \(N=(0,\dots,0,1)\) and \(S=-N\):

\[\varphi_N(x)=\frac{(x_1,\dots,x_n)}{1-x_{n+1}}\ \text{on }S^n\setminus\{N\},\qquad\varphi_S(x)=\frac{(x_1,\dots,x_n)}{1+x_{n+1}}\ \text{on }S^n\setminus\{S\}.\]

Transition: \(\varphi_S\circ\varphi_N^{-1}(u)=u/\lvert u\rvert^2\) for \(u\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}\), which is \(C^\infty\). So \(S^n\) is a smooth \(n\)-manifold.

Atlas for \(\mathbb{RP}^n\). Points are \([x_0:\dots:x_n]\). On \(U_i=\{x_i\ne0\}\), \(\varphi_i[x]=(x_0/x_i,\dots,\widehat{x_i/x_i},\dots,x_n/x_i)\in\mathbb R^n\). For \(\mathbb{RP}^1\) the transition between the two charts is \(u\mapsto1/u\) on \(\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\).

Regular value theorem (preimage theorem). Let \(F:M^m\to N^n\) be smooth and \(c\in N\) a regular value (\(dF_p\) surjective for every \(p\in F^{-1}(c)\)). Then \(F^{-1}(c)\) is a smooth submanifold of \(M\) of dimension \(m-n\) (if nonempty). Use it as follows:

  • \(S^n=f^{-1}(1)\) for \(f(x)=\lvert x\rvert^2\), \(\dim=n+1-1=n\).
  • \(SL_n=\det^{-1}(1)\), \(\dim=n^2-1\).
  • \(O(n)=F^{-1}(I)\) for \(F(A)=A^TA\) into the space of symmetric matrices, which has dimension \(n(n+1)/2\). So \(\dim O(n)=n^2-\tfrac{n(n+1)}2=\tfrac{n(n-1)}2\).

Not manifolds: the cone (vertex), the union of two lines, a figure eight, \([0,1]\) (it is a manifold with boundary), any space with a point where removal of a small neighbourhood gives the wrong number of pieces. Point-removal test: in a \(1\)-manifold, removing a point from a small connected chart neighbourhood leaves \(2\) pieces; at a crossing of two lines it leaves \(4\).

Manifolds with boundary

Replace \(\mathbb R^n\) by the half space \(\mathbb H^n=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:x_n\ge0\}\). Each point has a neighbourhood homeomorphic to an open subset of \(\mathbb H^n\). The boundary \(\partial M\) is the set of points sent to \(\{x_n=0\}\) by some chart; this is independent of the chart (invariance of domain). The rest is the interior.

  • \(\partial M\) is an \((n-1)\)-manifold without boundary, and \(\partial(\partial M)=\emptyset\).
  • Examples: \([0,1]\) (\(\partial=\{0,1\}\)); the closed disc \(D^n\) (\(\partial=S^{n-1}\)); the cylinder \(S^1\times[0,1]\) (two boundary circles); the Möbius band (one boundary circle); \([0,1)\).
  • \(M\times[0,1]\) has boundary \(M\times\{0,1\}\) for a manifold \(M\) without boundary.
  • “Compact manifold” in many texts means compact without boundary (a closed manifold).

Tangent spaces

For a smooth manifold \(M\) and \(p\in M\), the tangent space \(T_pM\) is an \(n\)-dimensional real vector space. Equivalent descriptions:

  • Curves: equivalence classes of smooth curves \(\gamma\) with \(\gamma(0)=p\), two curves being equivalent if they have the same velocity in a chart.
  • Derivations: linear maps \(v:C^\infty(M)\to\mathbb R\) with \(v(fg)=f(p)v(g)+g(p)v(f)\).
  • Chart basis: in a chart \((x^1,\dots,x^n)\) the vectors \(\partial/\partial x^i\big|_p\) form a basis; \(\dim T_pM=n\).

For a submanifold \(M\subseteq\mathbb R^N\), \(T_pM\) is the set of velocities \(\gamma'(0)\) of smooth curves in \(M\) through \(p\), a linear subspace of \(\mathbb R^N\).

The differential. A smooth map \(F:M\to N\) has a linear map \(dF_p:T_pM\to T_{F(p)}N\), given in charts by the Jacobian matrix. Chain rule: \(d(G\circ F)_p=dG_{F(p)}\circ dF_p\). \(F\) is an immersion if every \(dF_p\) is injective, a submersion if surjective, and an embedding if it is an immersion and a homeomorphism onto its image. Inverse function theorem: if \(dF_p\) is an isomorphism, \(F\) is a diffeomorphism near \(p\).

Standard tangent spaces.

Manifold \(T_pM\)
open subset of \(\mathbb R^n\) \(\mathbb R^n\)
\(S^n\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}\) at \(p\) \(\{v:v\cdot p=0\}\) (\(n\)-dimensional)
level set \(F^{-1}(c)\) \(\ker dF_p\)
\(GL_n(\mathbb R)\) at any point \(M_n(\mathbb R)\)
\(SL_n(\mathbb R)\) at \(I\) matrices with trace \(0\)

Derivation for \(O(n)\): if \(\gamma(t)^T\gamma(t)=I\) and \(\gamma(0)=I\), differentiating at \(0\) gives \(\gamma'(0)^T+\gamma'(0)=0\).

Other results worth knowing (not usually asked in detail). Sard’s theorem: the set of critical values of a smooth map has measure zero. Whitney: every smooth \(n\)-manifold embeds in \(\mathbb R^{2n}\). Partitions of unity exist on smooth manifolds because of Hausdorff and second countable. Orientable means the atlas can be chosen with positive-determinant transition maps; \(S^n\), \(T^n\), \(\mathbb{RP}^n\) for odd \(n\) are orientable, while the Möbius band, Klein bottle and \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) are not.

Lie groups

A Lie group is a smooth manifold \(G\) that is also a group such that multiplication \(G\times G\to G\) and inversion \(G\to G\) are smooth. Examples: \((\mathbb R^n,+)\), \((\mathbb R_{>0},\cdot)\), \(S^1\), the torus \(T^n\), \(GL_n(\mathbb R)\), \(GL_n(\mathbb C)\), \(SL_n\), \(O(n)\), \(SO(n)\), \(U(n)\), \(SU(n)\), the unit quaternions \(S^3\cong SU(2)\), and \(Sp(n)\).

Translations. For \(g\in G\) the left translation \(L_g(h)=gh\) is a diffeomorphism of \(G\) (its inverse is \(L_{g^{-1}}\)). So \(G\) looks the same near every point, all tangent spaces \(T_gG\) are isomorphic to \(T_eG\) via \(dL_g\), and every Lie group is parallelizable (\(TG\cong G\times T_eG\)) and orientable.

Lie algebra. \(T_eG=\mathfrak g\), with \(\dim\mathfrak g=\dim G\). For matrix groups the bracket is \([A,B]=AB-BA\).

Group Lie algebra Dimension Components Compact
\(GL_n(\mathbb R)\) all \(n\times n\) real matrices \(n^2\) \(2\) no
\(SL_n(\mathbb R)\) trace \(0\) \(n^2-1\) \(1\) no
\(O(n)\) skew-symmetric \(\tfrac{n(n-1)}2\) \(2\) yes
\(SO(n)\) skew-symmetric \(\tfrac{n(n-1)}2\) \(1\) yes
\(U(n)\) skew-Hermitian (\(A^*=-A\)) \(n^2\) \(1\) yes
\(SU(n)\) skew-Hermitian, trace \(0\) \(n^2-1\) \(1\) yes

\(GL_n(\mathbb C)\) all complex matrices \(2n^2\) (real) \(1\) no

Which spheres are Lie groups? Only \(S^0=\{\pm1\}\), \(S^1\) (unit complex numbers) and \(S^3\) (unit quaternions). \(S^2\) is not a Lie group: a compact Lie group is parallelizable, but \(S^{2k}\) has no nowhere-vanishing vector field (hairy ball theorem). \(S^7\) is parallelizable (unit octonions) but is not a group.

Further facts: \(SO(3)\cong\mathbb{RP}^3\) as manifolds, and \(SU(2)=S^3\) is a double cover of \(SO(3)\). \(O(n)\) is two copies of \(SO(n)\) (determinant \(\pm1\)). A Lie group is a manifold, hence Hausdorff and second countable; a connected Lie group is generated by any neighbourhood of \(e\).

Example. Dimension of \(U(2)\): it is \(\{A^*A=I\}\) in \(M_2(\mathbb C)=\mathbb R^8\), and \(A^*A\) lies in the \(4\)-dimensional space of Hermitian matrices, so \(\dim U(2)=8-4=4=n^2\). \(SU(2)\) adds the condition \(\det A=1\) (one real condition on the modulus-one determinant), giving dimension \(3\).

Trap. \(\dim U(n)=n^2\) and \(\dim SU(n)=n^2-1\); do not give \(2n^2\) (that is \(GL_n(\mathbb C)\)). \(T_eO(n)\) and \(T_eSO(n)\) coincide (both skew-symmetric matrices) because \(SO(n)\) is the identity component.

Structures on manifolds: orientation, almost complex, symplectic, contact (statement level)

  • Orientable: there is an atlas whose transition maps have positive Jacobian determinant, equivalently a nowhere-vanishing top-degree form. Orientable: \(S^n\), \(T^n\), every Lie group, every complex manifold, \(\mathbb{CP}^n\), \(\mathbb{RP}^n\) for \(n\) odd. Not orientable: the Möbius band, the Klein bottle, \(\mathbb{RP}^n\) for \(n\) even. A product is orientable iff both factors are; a simply connected manifold is orientable.
  • Almost complex structure: a smooth bundle map \(J:TM\to TM\) with \(J^2=-1\). It forces \(\dim M\) to be even and \(M\) to be orientable. Every complex manifold has one (\(J\) = multiplication by \(i\)). Among spheres only \(S^2\) and \(S^6\) have an almost complex structure; \(S^4\) does not. An almost complex structure comes from a complex structure only if it is integrable (vanishing Nijenhuis tensor).
  • Symplectic: a closed, nondegenerate \(2\)-form \(\omega\); again \(\dim M\) is even. \(\mathbb R^{2n}\), every orientable surface, \(\mathbb{CP}^n\) and every cotangent bundle \(T^*N\) are symplectic. Among spheres only \(S^2\) is symplectic (for \(n>2\) the form \(\omega\) would be exact, since \(H^2(S^n)=0\), but \(\omega^{n/2}\) must be a volume form and cannot be exact on a closed manifold).
  • Contact: an odd-dimensional manifold \(M^{2n+1}\) with a \(1\)-form \(\alpha\) such that \(\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n\) is nowhere zero. The odd spheres \(S^{2n+1}\) and \(\mathbb R^{2n+1}\) are contact manifolds.
  • Parity rules to remember: almost complex and symplectic need an even dimension, contact needs an odd dimension. A \(1\)-dimensional manifold is never almost complex, and \(\mathbb R^3\) is contact and not symplectic.

Trap. “Almost complex” is not “complex”: integrability is an extra condition. And “orientable” is a topological property of the manifold, not of the chosen atlas.

Formulas and results

  • Manifold = Hausdorff + second countable + locally Euclidean. Drop any one and it fails (line with two origins, long line).
  • Manifolds are locally compact, regular, metrizable, locally path-connected; connected implies path-connected.
  • Compact connected manifold: exactly \(1\) component.
  • Transition map \(\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}\); smooth atlas means smooth transitions; maximal atlas = smooth structure.
  • \(S^n\): two stereographic charts, transition \(u\mapsto u/\lvert u\rvert^2\). \(\mathbb{RP}^n\): \(n+1\) charts, transition \(u\mapsto1/u\) for \(n=1\).
  • Dimensions: \(\dim S^n=n\), \(\dim T^n=n\), \(\dim\mathbb{RP}^n=n\), \(\dim\mathbb{CP}^n=2n\), \(\dim GL_n(\mathbb R)=n^2\), \(\dim SL_n=n^2-1\), \(\dim O(n)=\dim SO(n)=n(n-1)/2\), \(\dim U(n)=n^2\), \(\dim G(k,n)=k(n-k)\), \(\dim TM=2\dim M\).
  • Regular value theorem: \(\dim F^{-1}(c)=m-n\).
  • \(\dim T_pM=\dim M\). \(T_pS^n=p^\perp\). \(T_IO(n)=\) skew-symmetric matrices. \(T_ISL_n=\{\operatorname{tr}=0\}\).
  • Boundary: \(\partial M\) is an \((n-1)\)-manifold with no boundary. \(\partial D^n=S^{n-1}\).
  • Euler characteristic of closed surfaces: \(2-2g\) (orientable genus \(g\)), \(2-k\) (\(k\) projective planes).
  • Lie group: manifold with smooth multiplication and inversion; left translations are diffeomorphisms; \(T_eG=\mathfrak g\), \(\dim\mathfrak g=\dim G\). \(\dim U(n)=n^2\), \(\dim SU(n)=n^2-1\), \(\dim Sp(n)=n(2n+1)\). Spheres that are Lie groups: \(S^0,S^1,S^3\) only.
  • Almost complex and symplectic need even dimension; contact needs odd dimension. Spheres with an almost complex structure: \(S^2\), \(S^6\) only. \(\mathbb{RP}^n\) orientable iff \(n\) odd.

Worked examples

Example 1. Transition map on the circle

\(S^1\) with \(N=(0,1)\) and \(S=(0,-1)\). \(\varphi_N(x,y)=x/(1-y)\) and \(\varphi_S(x,y)=x/(1+y)\). For \(u\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\) the point with \(\varphi_N=u\) is \(\bigl(\tfrac{2u}{1+u^2},\tfrac{u^2-1}{u^2+1}\bigr)\), and then

\[\varphi_S=\frac{2u/(1+u^2)}{1+(u^2-1)/(u^2+1)}=\frac{2u}{2u^2}=\frac1u.\]

So \(\varphi_S\circ\varphi_N^{-1}(u)=1/u\) (matching the general formula \(u/\lvert u\rvert^2\) for \(n=1\)), a smooth map on \(\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\). Hence \(S^1\) is a smooth \(1\)-manifold.

Example 2. Dimension of \(O(n)\)

\(F:GL_n(\mathbb R)\to\mathrm{Sym}_n\), \(F(A)=A^TA\). At \(A=I\) the differential is \(H\mapsto H^T+H\), which is surjective onto \(\mathrm{Sym}_n\) (given a symmetric \(S\) take \(H=S/2\)). For any \(A\in O(n)\), \(dF_A(H)=A^TH+H^TA\) is also onto (take \(H=AS/2\)). So \(I\) is a regular value and \(\dim O(n)=n^2-\tfrac{n(n+1)}2=\tfrac{n(n-1)}2\). Check: \(n=2\) gives \(1\) (a circle’s worth of rotations plus a reflection component); \(n=3\) gives \(3\). The rank of \(dF_I\) is \(n(n+1)/2\) (a short computation for \(n=2,\dots,5\) gives \(3,6,10,15\)).

Example 3. Is it a manifold?

(a) \(\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:xy=0\}\). Near the origin remove the origin from a small ball: \(4\) pieces remain, but a \(1\)-manifold would leave \(2\). Not a manifold.

(b) \(\{(x,y):x^2+y^2=1\}\). Level set of a function with \(dF=(2x,2y)\ne0\). Manifold, dimension \(1\).

(c) The cone \(x^2+y^2=z^2\). Minus the vertex the cone is two open surfaces; near the vertex, removing the vertex from a small connected neighbourhood leaves \(2\) pieces, whereas a \(2\)-manifold leaves a connected set. Not a manifold.

(d) \(\{x^2+y^2-z^2=1\}\) (one-sheet hyperboloid): \(dF=(2x,2y,-2z)\ne0\) on the surface, so it is a smooth \(2\)-manifold.

Example 4. Tangent space of a sphere

Find \(T_pS^2\) at \(p=(1,0,0)\). \(S^2=f^{-1}(1)\), \(f=x^2+y^2+z^2\), \(df_p=(2,0,0)\), so \(T_pS^2=\ker df_p=\{(0,b,c)\}\), the plane \(x=0\), of dimension \(2\). In general \(T_pS^n=p^\perp\).

Example 5. Charts of \(\mathbb{RP}^n\) and dimension

\(U_i=\{[x]:x_i\ne0\}\), and \(\varphi_i[x]=(x_j/x_i)_{j\ne i}\in\mathbb R^n\) is a homeomorphism onto \(\mathbb R^n\). There are \(n+1\) charts covering \(\mathbb{RP}^n\), so \(\dim\mathbb{RP}^n=n\). For \(n=1\), \(\varphi_0[x_0:x_1]=x_1/x_0\) and \(\varphi_1=x_0/x_1\), with transition \(u\mapsto1/u\). \(\mathbb{RP}^1\) is compact, connected and a \(1\)-manifold, hence \(\cong S^1\).

Example 6. Boundary of a product

\(M=S^1\times[0,1]\) (cylinder). It is a manifold with boundary \(S^1\times\{0,1\}\), two circles. A closed disc \(D^2\) has boundary \(S^1\). The Möbius band has boundary one circle. In each case the boundary is a \(1\)-manifold without boundary.

Common traps

  • Dropping a condition. Locally Euclidean alone is not enough: the line with two origins (not Hausdorff) and the long line (not second countable) are locally Euclidean.
  • Manifold versus manifold with boundary. \([0,1]\) and \(D^n\) are not manifolds (without boundary) but are manifolds with boundary. “Compact manifold” usually means without boundary.
  • Compact connected means one component. Connected already means a single component; compactness adds nothing (2026-Q96).
  • Dimension of matrix groups. \(\dim O(n)=n(n-1)/2\) not \(n^2\) or \(n(n+1)/2\). \(\dim SL_n=n^2-1\). \(\dim U(n)=n^2\) (real dimension). \(\dim GL_n(\mathbb C)=2n^2\) as a real manifold.
  • Projective space. \(\dim\mathbb{RP}^n=n\) (not \(n+1\)); \(\dim\mathbb{CP}^n=2n\) real, \(n\) complex.
  • Tangent space dimension equals \(\dim M\), not the dimension of the ambient space.
  • Smooth structure. A chart with a non-smooth transition (like \(x^3\) against the identity) gives a different atlas, not a different manifold up to diffeomorphism. A topological manifold may carry several smooth structures.
  • Cones, crossings. Spaces with a singular point (cone vertex, crossing) are not manifolds; test by removing the point.
  • Transition maps are homeomorphisms in general and diffeomorphisms for a smooth atlas.
  • Orientation. \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) is not orientable but \(\mathbb{RP}^3\) is. \(S^n\) and \(T^n\) are always orientable.
  • Lie groups and spheres. \(S^2\) is not a Lie group (hairy ball); \(S^1\) and \(S^3\) are. \(\dim U(n)=n^2\), not \(2n^2\).
  • Even versus odd dimension. Almost complex and symplectic structures live in even dimensions only; contact structures in odd dimensions. \(S^4\) has no almost complex structure, \(S^2\) and \(S^6\) do.
  • Level set requires a regular value. \(F^{-1}(c)\) may not be a manifold if \(c\) is a critical value (the cone is \(F^{-1}(0)\) for \(F=x^2+y^2-z^2\), and \(0\) is a critical value).

Previous-year questions

2026-Q96 (worked key D; unofficial)

A compact connected manifold is analysed using connectedness and component decomposition. Determine the number of connected components. (A) \(0\) (B) infinite (C) finite (D) \(1\).

Answer: D. Connected means exactly one component. Compactness plays no role (a compact manifold may have finitely many components, but this one is connected). The answer “finite” is true in a weaker sense but D is the exact value.

Related: 2026-Q42 (see the previous topic)

A Hausdorff, second countable, regular manifold is metrizable by Urysohn’s theorem, with infinitely many compatible metrics (worked key D).

Quick revision

  • Manifold = Hausdorff + second countable + locally Euclidean.
  • Line with two origins: not Hausdorff. Long line: not second countable.
  • Manifolds are locally compact, metrizable; connected implies path-connected.
  • Compact connected manifold: exactly one component.
  • Chart \((U,\varphi)\), atlas, transition \(\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}\); smooth atlas means \(C^\infty\) transitions.
  • \(S^n\): two stereographic charts, transition \(u/\lvert u\rvert^2\). \(\mathbb{RP}^n\): \(n+1\) charts.
  • \(\dim GL_n=n^2\), \(\dim SL_n=n^2-1\), \(\dim O(n)=n(n-1)/2\), \(\dim U(n)=n^2\), \(\dim G(k,n)=k(n-k)\).
  • Regular value theorem: preimage of a regular value has dimension \(m-n\).
  • \(\dim T_pM=\dim M\); \(T_pS^n=p^\perp\); \(T_IO(n)=\) skew-symmetric matrices.
  • Manifold with boundary: charts into the half space; \(\partial M\) has dimension \(n-1\) and no boundary.
  • Not manifolds: cone, crossing lines, figure eight (test by removing a point).
  • \(\mathbb{RP}^n\) orientable iff \(n\) odd.
  • Lie group: left translations are diffeomorphisms, \(T_eG\) is the Lie algebra, \(\dim G=\dim\mathfrak g\); only \(S^0,S^1,S^3\) among spheres are Lie groups.
  • Almost complex or symplectic: even dimension; contact: odd dimension. Almost complex spheres: \(S^2,S^6\).
