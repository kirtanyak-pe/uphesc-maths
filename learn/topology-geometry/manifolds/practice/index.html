  1. Question 1Easy
    Which of the following is NOT part of the definition of a topological manifold?

    Why

    A topological manifold is Hausdorff, second countable and locally Euclidean. Compactness is not required: \(\mathbb R^n\) is a manifold and is not compact.
  2. Question 2Easy
    The line with two origins fails to be a manifold because it is

    Why

    It is locally Euclidean and second countable. The two origins cannot be separated by disjoint open sets, so it is not Hausdorff.
  3. Question 3Medium
    The long line is locally Euclidean and Hausdorff. Which condition for a manifold does it fail?

    Why

    The long line is locally Euclidean, Hausdorff, locally compact and regular, but it has no countable basis (it is not Lindelof and not metrizable). Hence it is not a manifold.
  4. Question 4Easy
    The real dimension of the complex projective plane \(\mathbb{CP}^2\) is

    Why

    \(\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathbb{CP}^n=2n\), so \(\mathbb{CP}^2\) has real dimension \(4\) (complex dimension \(2\)).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The dimension of the orthogonal group \(O(4)\) as a manifold is

    Why

    \(O(n)=F^{-1}(I)\) for \(F(A)=A^TA\), whose differential at \(I\) has rank \(n(n+1)/2\) (a script computed ranks \(3,6,10,15\) for \(n=2,\dots,5\)). So \(\dim O(n)=n^2-n(n+1)/2=n(n-1)/2\), giving \(6\) for \(n=4\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    The dimension of \(SL_3(\mathbb R)\) is

    Why

    \(SL_3(\mathbb R)=\det^{-1}(1)\) and \(d\det_I=\operatorname{tr}\) is onto, so the dimension is \(9-1=8\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    Let \(S^1\) have the stereographic charts \(\varphi_N\) (from \(N=(0,1)\)) and \(\varphi_S\) (from \(S=(0,-1)\)). The transition map \(\varphi_S\circ\varphi_N^{-1}\) on \(\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\) is

    Why

    Writing \(\varphi_N^{-1}(u)=\bigl(\tfrac{2u}{1+u^2},\tfrac{u^2-1}{u^2+1}\bigr)\) and applying \(\varphi_S(x,y)=x/(1+y)\) gives \(1/u\) (checked numerically). The general formula is \(u/\lvert u\rvert^2\).
  8. Question 8Easy
    Which of the following subsets of \(\mathbb R^2\) is a \(1\)-dimensional manifold?

    Why

    The circle is a level set with nonzero gradient. The other three are all pairs of crossing lines (two of them are the same set), and at the crossing point removing the point from a small neighbourhood leaves four pieces rather than two.
  9. Question 9Medium
    Which statement about a topological manifold \(M\) is FALSE?

    Why

    A manifold is locally connected, so components are open; it is regular and second countable, so metrizable; connected and locally path-connected give path-connected. Uncountably many components would give an uncountable family of disjoint nonempty open sets, impossible in a second countable space.
  10. Question 10Easy
    The tangent plane of the unit sphere \(S^2\subset\mathbb R^3\) at \(p=(0,0,1)\) (viewed as a subspace of \(\mathbb R^3\)) contains the vector

    Why

    \(T_pS^2=p^\perp\), i.e. vectors with third coordinate \(0\). Only \((1,1,0)\) qualifies.
  11. Question 11Medium
    The dimension of the Grassmannian \(G(2,5)\) of \(2\)-planes in \(\mathbb R^5\) is

    Why

    \(\dim G(k,n)=k(n-k)\); a chart near a plane is given by the \(2\times3\) matrices expressing it as a graph over a coordinate \(2\)-plane. So \(2\cdot3=6\).
  12. Question 12Medium
    Which matrix lies in the tangent space \(T_IO(3)\) of the orthogonal group at the identity?

    Why

    Differentiating \(\gamma(t)^T\gamma(t)=I\) at \(t=0\) gives \(\gamma'(0)^T+\gamma'(0)=0\), so \(T_IO(n)\) is the space of skew-symmetric matrices. Only the first matrix is skew-symmetric.
  13. Question 13Easy
    The boundary of the closed ball \(D^3\subset\mathbb R^3\), viewed as a manifold with boundary, is

    Why

    \(\partial D^n=S^{n-1}\), so \(\partial D^3=S^2\). A boundary is itself a manifold without boundary, so \(D^2\) cannot be the answer.
  14. Question 14Hard
    Assertion (A): The interval \([0,1]\) is not a topological manifold without boundary. Reason (R): The point \(0\) has no neighbourhood in \([0,1]\) homeomorphic to an open subset of \(\mathbb R\).

    Why

    A small connected neighbourhood \([0,\varepsilon)\) of \(0\) stays connected when \(0\) is removed, whereas removing a point from an interval in \(\mathbb R\) disconnects it; a homeomorphism would preserve this. So R is true and is exactly why A holds (it is a manifold with boundary).
