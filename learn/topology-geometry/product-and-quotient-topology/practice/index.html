Product and quotient topology

  1. Question 1Easy
    In which topology on \(\prod_\alpha X_\alpha\) can the product fail to be compact even though every \(X_\alpha\) is compact?

    Why

    Tychonoff’s theorem is for the product topology (which is the coarsest topology making the projections continuous, i.e. pointwise convergence). In the box topology \(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\) is an uncountable discrete space, so it is not compact.
  2. Question 2Easy
    The projection \(\pi_1:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\), \((x,y)\mapsto x\), is

    Why

    Projections are continuous and open. It is not closed: the closed hyperbola \(\{xy=1\}\) maps onto \(\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\), which is not closed.
  3. Question 3Medium
    In \(\mathbb R^{\omega}=\prod_{n\ge1}\mathbb R\) with the box topology, which statement is true?

    Why

    The set of bounded sequences is open (a box \(\prod(x_n-1,x_n+1)\) around a bounded sequence consists of bounded sequences) and so is its complement, so the space is disconnected. In the product topology \(\mathbb R^\omega\) is connected.
  4. Question 4Easy
    The square \([0,1]^2\) with the identification \((0,y)\sim(1,1-y)\) is homeomorphic to

    Why

    Gluing the left and right edges with a flip gives a Möbius band. Without the flip it would be a cylinder; the torus and the Klein bottle need the top and bottom edges glued as well.
  5. Question 5Medium
    Reading round the boundary of a square, the word \(abab^{-1}\) describes which surface?

    Why

    The torus is \(aba^{-1}b^{-1}\), the projective plane is \(aa\) (or \(abab\)), the sphere is \(aa^{-1}\). The word \(abab^{-1}\) has one pair of edges in the same direction and one pair opposed, which is the Klein bottle (Euler characteristic \(0\), non-orientable).
  6. Question 6Medium
    The Euler characteristic of the real projective plane \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) is

    Why

    From the word \(aa\): one vertex class, one edge pair, one face give \(V-E+F=1\) (checked from the polygon words; the sphere gives \(2\), the torus and Klein bottle \(0\)).
  7. Question 7Medium
    The real projective space \(\mathbb{RP}^3\) is

    Why

    \(\mathbb{RP}^n=S^n/\pm1\) is a compact connected \(n\)-manifold, orientable exactly when \(n\) is odd. So \(\mathbb{RP}^3\) is orientable; \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) is not.
  8. Question 8Medium
    Consider: (I) a quotient of a Hausdorff space is Hausdorff; (II) a quotient of a compact space is compact; (III) every quotient map is an open map. Which are true?

    Why

    (II) holds because a quotient map is a continuous surjection. (I) fails (\(\mathbb R/\mathbb Q\) is indiscrete; the line with two origins). (III) fails (\([0,1]\to[0,1]/\{0,1\}\cong S^1\) sends the open set \([0,\tfrac14)\) to a non-open set).
  9. Question 9Hard
    Let \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Q\) be the set of cosets \(x+\mathbb Q\) with the quotient topology. This topology is

    Why

    A nonempty open set of \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Q\) pulls back to a nonempty saturated open subset of \(\mathbb R\). Such a set contains a whole coset \(x+\mathbb Q\), which is dense, and being a union of cosets and open it must meet every coset, hence it is everything. So only \(\emptyset\) and the whole space are open.
  10. Question 10Hard
    Identify the three points \(0,\tfrac12,1\) of \([0,1]\) to a single point. The quotient space is homeomorphic to

    Why

    The two halves \([0,\tfrac12]\) and \([\tfrac12,1]\) each have both end points identified to the same point, so each becomes a circle, and the two circles meet only at the identified point.
  11. Question 11Hard
    The line with two origins (the quotient of \(\mathbb R\sqcup\mathbb R\) identifying \(x\) with \(x\) for \(x\ne0\)) fails which property?

    Why

    Every neighbourhood of one origin meets every neighbourhood of the other in punctured intervals, so the two origins cannot be separated. It is locally homeomorphic to \(\mathbb R\), second countable and connected.
  12. Question 12Medium
    In \(\mathbb R^{X}\) with the product topology, a net or sequence \(f_k\to f\) if and only if

    Why

    The product topology is the topology of pointwise convergence. Uniform convergence needs a finer topology (the uniform topology).
  13. Question 13Hard
    Which of the following properties is NOT inherited by an arbitrary product (in the product topology) of spaces that have it?

    Why

    Hausdorff and connected pass to arbitrary products, compactness passes by Tychonoff’s theorem. Normality does not: \(\mathbb R_\ell\times\mathbb R_\ell\) is not normal although \(\mathbb R_\ell\) is.
  14. Question 14Easy
    A map \(f:Z\to\prod_\alpha X_\alpha\) is continuous (product topology) if and only if

    Why

    This is the universal property of the product topology. The box topology version is false: \(t\mapsto(t,t,\dots)\) is continuous into \(\mathbb R^\omega\) with the product topology but not with the box topology.
