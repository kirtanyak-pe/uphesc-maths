What this unit covers

Nine topics in two halves.

Topology (five topics). Topological spaces, bases, closure and interior, subspaces, continuity and homeomorphism; connectedness and compactness (with the metric-space equivalences); countability axioms and the separation chain \(T_0\) to \(T_4\) with Urysohn’s lemma, Tietze and Urysohn metrization; product and quotient topologies with Tychonoff’s theorem; and, marked [A+] in the syllabus, manifolds, charts, atlases and tangent spaces.

Differential geometry of curves and surfaces (four topics). Space curves with curvature, torsion and the Serret-Frenet equations; the first and second fundamental forms; Gaussian, mean and principal curvatures; and geodesics, with Clairaut’s relation and the Gauss-Bonnet theorem.

The unit is worth 3 questions in the 2019 paper (only 35 of 70 recovered), 7 in 2025 and 8 in 2026. Expect about 7 to 8 questions in a full paper, so it is one of the best-paying units per hour of study.

How it is asked

Every question in the three papers on record is a single-fact multiple choice. Almost none needs a long computation.

Paper Topology questions Geometry questions

Patterns worth knowing.

  • Curves are the most repeated geometry theme. The helix, the twisted cubic and “constant curvature and constant torsion means a circular helix” came up in both 2025 and 2026. A plane curve has \(\tau=0\); a curve with \(\kappa=1\) and \(\tau=0\) is a unit circle.
  • Gaussian curvature of a sphere is \(1/r^2\) (2025 twice). The paraboloid \(z=x^2+y^2\) has \(K=4\) at the origin.
  • Topology questions test definitions and counterexamples, not proofs: the separation chain, “which of compact, totally bounded, complete”, what a continuous map preserves, what Tychonoff does and does not say, and manifold conditions.
  • Look-alike options. Options often differ by one word: product versus box topology, closed versus open map, countable versus uncountable, “separable” versus “second countable”, Hausdorff versus regular.
  • Topology questions can be answered from a handful of standard counterexamples (cofinite topology, Sorgenfrey line, discrete space, topologist’s sine curve, \(\mathbb Q\), the line with two origins). Learn those.

High-yield list

  1. Gaussian curvature of a sphere, \(1/r^2\). Asked twice in 2025. Cylinder and cone: \(K=0\).
  2. Curvature and torsion of a helix: \(\kappa=a/(a^2+b^2)\), \(\tau=b/(a^2+b^2)\). Constant \(\kappa\) and constant \(\tau\) gives a circular helix; \(\tau=0\) gives a plane curve. Asked in 2025 and 2026.
  3. Compute \(\kappa\) and \(\tau\) from \(\mathbf r',\mathbf r'',\mathbf r'''\) with the cross-product formulas. The twisted cubic at \(t=0\) gives \(\kappa=2\), \(\tau=3\).
  4. Gaussian curvature from the fundamental forms: \(K=(LN-M^2)/(EG-F^2)\); for \(z=f(x,y)\) use the Monge formula. The paraboloid gives \(4\).
  5. Geodesics on the sphere are great circles; antipodal points are joined by infinitely many.
  6. Separation chain \(T_4\Rightarrow T_3\Rightarrow T_2\Rightarrow T_1\Rightarrow T_0\) and the counterexamples at each step; metric spaces satisfy all of them.
  7. Totally bounded, complete, compact: a metric space is compact iff complete and totally bounded; totally bounded implies separable; “every sequence has a Cauchy subsequence” means totally bounded.
  8. Properties preserved by continuous maps: compactness, connectedness. Not openness.
  9. Tychonoff’s theorem: true for the product topology, false for the box topology; needs the axiom of choice.
  10. Discrete topology on an infinite set: not compact, not connected, every function out of it is continuous, Hausdorff (so \(T_1\)).
  11. Manifold: Hausdorff, second countable, locally Euclidean; compact connected manifold has one component; metrizable by Urysohn, with infinitely many compatible metrics.
  12. Urysohn metrization, Urysohn lemma, Tietze: know the hypotheses (regular, \(T_1\), second countable; normal; normal and closed set).
  13. Connected versus path-connected: the topologist’s sine curve is connected but not path-connected.
  14. Compact sets: closed and bounded in \(\mathbb R^n\) only; closed subsets of compact are compact; compact subsets of Hausdorff are closed.
  15. Quotient surfaces: polygon words and Euler characteristic (\(2,0,0,1\) for sphere, torus, Klein bottle, \(\mathbb{RP}^2\)).
  16. Normal curvature and principal curvatures: \(\kappa_n=\kappa_1\cos^2\theta+\kappa_2\sin^2\theta\); \(K=\kappa_1\kappa_2\), \(H=(\kappa_1+\kappa_2)/2\); minimal surfaces have \(H=0\).

Official syllabus

topology basics, homeomorphism, connectedness, compactness, countability, separation axioms, subspaces, product, quotient, Tychonoff, Urysohn metrization; space curves (curvature, torsion, Serret-Frenet), first and second fundamental forms, Gaussian curvature, principal curvatures, geodesics; [A+] manifolds.
