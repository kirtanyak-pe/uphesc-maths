Formula sheet

Every formula and result of the unit. Each topic page explains where they come from.

Topology

  • Topology axioms: \(\emptyset,X\in\tau\); arbitrary unions and finite intersections of members lie in \(\tau\).
  • Basis: covers \(X\), and for \(B_1,B_2\) and \(x\in B_1\cap B_2\) there is \(B_3\ni x\) with \(B_3\subseteq B_1\cap B_2\). Standard topology of \(\mathbb R\) has basis of open intervals; the lower limit topology has \([a,b)\); the \(K\)-topology has the intervals and the sets \((a,b)\setminus K\) with \(K=\{1/n\}\). Finer than standard: lower limit and \(K\)-topology; they are not comparable with each other.
  • Closure, interior: \(\bar A=A\cup A'\); \(\operatorname{int}A=X\setminus\overline{X\setminus A}\); \(\partial A=\bar A\setminus\operatorname{int}A\); \(\overline{A\cup B}=\bar A\cup\bar B\), but \(\overline{A\cap B}\subseteq\bar A\cap\bar B\) only.
  • Subspace: open sets are \(U\cap Y\). Open sets of \(Y\) need not be open in \(X\) unless \(Y\) is open.
  • Continuity: preimage of open is open, equivalently preimage of closed is closed, equivalently \(f(\bar A)\subseteq\overline{f(A)}\). Homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse.
  • Number of topologies on \(n\) points: \(1,4,29,355,6942\) for \(n=1,\dots,5\); \(T_0\) ones: \(1,3,19,219,4231\).
  • Connectedness: continuous image of a connected set is connected; closure of a connected set is connected; product of connected spaces is connected; \([a,b]\) and intervals are the connected subsets of \(\mathbb R\); connected implies path-connected fails (topologist’s sine curve); path-connected implies connected.
  • Compactness: continuous image of compact is compact; closed subset of compact is compact; compact subset of Hausdorff is closed; continuous bijection from compact to Hausdorff is a homeomorphism; in \(\mathbb R^n\) compact iff closed and bounded; metric space: compact iff sequentially compact iff complete and totally bounded; Lebesgue number lemma; extreme value theorem.
  • Preservation: continuous maps preserve connectedness, path-connectedness, compactness, separability. They do not preserve openness, closedness, boundedness, completeness, Hausdorffness.
  • Countability: second countable implies first countable, separable and Lindelof; for metric spaces second countable iff separable iff Lindelof; subspaces of second countable are second countable; \(\mathbb R_\ell\) is first countable, separable, Lindelof, not second countable.
  • Separation: \(T_4\Rightarrow T_3\Rightarrow T_{2}\Rightarrow T_1\Rightarrow T_0\) (with \(T_1\) built into \(T_3\) and \(T_4\)); metric spaces are \(T_4\) (perfectly normal); compact Hausdorff spaces are normal; regular plus Lindelof implies normal; \(\mathbb R_\ell\) is normal, \(\mathbb R_\ell^2\) is not.
  • Urysohn lemma: \(X\) normal, \(A,B\) disjoint closed, then there is continuous \(f:X\to[0,1]\) with \(f|_A=0\), \(f|_B=1\). Tietze: \(X\) normal, \(A\) closed, \(f:A\to\mathbb R\) continuous extends to \(X\). Urysohn metrization: regular, \(T_1\), second countable implies metrizable.
  • Product: basis \(\prod U_\alpha\) with \(U_\alpha=X_\alpha\) for all but finitely many \(\alpha\) (box allows all). Tychonoff: arbitrary product of compact spaces is compact in the product topology. Product topology is the coarsest making projections continuous; projections are open and continuous, not necessarily closed.
  • Quotient: \(V\) is open in \(X/\!\sim\) iff \(q^{-1}(V)\) is open. \(f\) continuous on the quotient iff \(f\circ q\) is continuous. Quotients of compact, connected spaces are compact, connected; Hausdorffness is not preserved.
  • Surfaces from polygon words: sphere \(aa^{-1}\), torus \(aba^{-1}b^{-1}\), Klein bottle \(abab^{-1}\) or \(aabb\), projective plane \(aa\). Euler characteristic \(V-E+F\): sphere \(2\), torus \(0\), Klein bottle \(0\), \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) \(1\). Orientable genus \(g\): \(2-2g\). Connected sum of \(k\) projective planes: \(2-k\).
  • Manifold: Hausdorff, second countable, locally Euclidean. Compact connected manifold has exactly one component. \(\dim S^n=n\), \(\dim\mathbb{RP}^n=n\), \(\dim\mathbb{CP}^n=2n\), \(\dim GL_n=n^2\), \(\dim SL_n=n^2-1\), \(\dim O(n)=n(n-1)/2\), \(\dim G(k,n)=k(n-k)\). \(T_pS^n=p^\perp\). \(T_IO(n)\) is the skew-symmetric matrices. Stereographic transition \(u\mapsto u/\lvert u\rvert^2\).

Space curves

For \(\mathbf r(t)\) with derivatives \(\mathbf r',\mathbf r'',\mathbf r'''\) in an arbitrary parameter:

  • Arc length: \(s=\int_a^t\lvert\mathbf r'(u)\rvert\,du\). Unit tangent \(\mathbf T=\mathbf r'/\lvert\mathbf r'\rvert\).
  • Curvature: \(\kappa=\lvert\mathbf T'(s)\rvert=\dfrac{\lvert\mathbf r'\times\mathbf r''\rvert}{\lvert\mathbf r'\rvert^3}\). For a plane graph \(y=f(x)\): \(\kappa=\dfrac{\lvert f''\rvert}{(1+f'^2)^{3/2}}\).
  • Torsion: \(\tau=\dfrac{(\mathbf r'\times\mathbf r'')\cdot\mathbf r'''}{\lvert\mathbf r'\times\mathbf r''\rvert^2}\).
  • Frenet frame: \(\mathbf N=\mathbf T'/\kappa\), \(\mathbf B=\mathbf T\times\mathbf N\). Equations (derivatives with respect to \(s\)): \(\mathbf T'=\kappa\mathbf N,\qquad\mathbf N'=-\kappa\mathbf T+\tau\mathbf B,\qquad\mathbf B'=-\tau\mathbf N.\)
  • Darboux vector: \(\boldsymbol\omega=\tau\mathbf T+\kappa\mathbf B\).
  • Planes: osculating \((\mathbf T,\mathbf N)\), normal \((\mathbf N,\mathbf B)\), rectifying \((\mathbf T,\mathbf B)\). Osculating circle has radius \(1/\kappa\).
  • Characterisations: \(\tau\equiv0\) iff the curve is planar; \(\kappa\equiv0\) iff a straight line; \(\kappa\) and \(\tau\) both nonzero constants iff a circular helix; \(\tau/\kappa\) constant iff a general helix (constant angle with a fixed direction); spherical curve: \(\rho^2+(\rho'\sigma)^2=a^2\) with \(\rho=1/\kappa\), \(\sigma=1/\tau\).
  • Fundamental theorem: \(\kappa>0\) and \(\tau\) given as smooth functions of \(s\) determine the curve up to a rigid motion.
  • Standard values: circle of radius \(a\): \(\kappa=1/a\), \(\tau=0\). Helix \((a\cos t,a\sin t,bt)\): \(\kappa=\dfrac{a}{a^2+b^2}\), \(\tau=\dfrac{b}{a^2+b^2}\). Twisted cubic \((t,t^2,t^3)\) at \(t=0\): \(\kappa=2\), \(\tau=3\).

Surfaces

For \(\mathbf r(u,v)\) with \(\mathbf N=\dfrac{\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v}{\lvert\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v\rvert}\):

  • First fundamental form: \(I=E\,du^2+2F\,du\,dv+G\,dv^2\) with \(E=\mathbf r_u\cdot\mathbf r_u\), \(F=\mathbf r_u\cdot\mathbf r_v\), \(G=\mathbf r_v\cdot\mathbf r_v\). Always \(EG-F^2=\lvert\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v\rvert^2>0\).
  • Arc length and area: \(ds^2=I\), \(dA=\sqrt{EG-F^2}\,du\,dv\). Angle between curves \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{E\,du\,\delta u+F(du\,\delta v+dv\,\delta u)+G\,dv\,\delta v}{\sqrt{I(d)}\sqrt{I(\delta)}}\).
  • Second fundamental form: \(II=L\,du^2+2M\,du\,dv+N\,dv^2\) with \(L=\mathbf r_{uu}\cdot\mathbf N\), \(M=\mathbf r_{uv}\cdot\mathbf N\), \(N=\mathbf r_{vv}\cdot\mathbf N\).
  • Normal curvature: \(\kappa_n=\dfrac{II}{I}\) in the direction \(du:dv\). Meusnier: \(\kappa_n=\kappa\cos\varphi\) (\(\varphi\) is the angle between the principal normal and the surface normal).
  • Principal curvatures \(\kappa_1,\kappa_2\) are the extreme values of \(\kappa_n\), the roots of \((EG-F^2)\kappa^2-(EN-2FM+GL)\kappa+(LN-M^2)=0\). Euler: \(\kappa_n=\kappa_1\cos^2\theta+\kappa_2\sin^2\theta\). Principal directions are orthogonal (unless the point is umbilic).
  • Gaussian and mean curvature: \(K=\kappa_1\kappa_2=\frac{LN-M^2}{EG-F^2},\qquad H=\frac{\kappa_1+\kappa_2}2=\frac{EN-2FM+GL}{2(EG-F^2)}.\) Then \(\kappa_{1,2}=H\pm\sqrt{H^2-K}\).
  • Monge patch \(z=f(x,y)\): \(K=\dfrac{f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2}{(1+f_x^2+f_y^2)^2}\).
  • Theorema Egregium: \(K\) depends only on \(E,F,G\) and their derivatives, so it is preserved by isometries. \(K\) does not change sign under \(\mathbf N\to-\mathbf N\); \(H\) does.
  • Types of point: elliptic \(K>0\), hyperbolic \(K<0\), parabolic \(K=0\) with one zero principal curvature, planar \(\kappa_1=\kappa_2=0\), umbilic \(\kappa_1=\kappa_2\). Minimal surface: \(H=0\) (then \(K\le0\)). Developable: \(K=0\).
  • Standard values: sphere radius \(r\): \(K=1/r^2\), \(\lvert H\rvert=1/r\), every point umbilic. Cylinder radius \(r\): \(K=0\), \(\lvert H\rvert=1/(2r)\). Plane: \(K=H=0\). Cone: \(K=0\). Torus (major radius \(R\), minor radius \(r\), \(v\) the angle around the tube): \(K=\dfrac{\cos v}{r(R+r\cos v)}\). Catenoid \(x=a\cosh(v/a)\cos u\) and so on: \(H=0\), \(K=-\dfrac1{a^2\cosh^4(v/a)}\). Pseudosphere: \(K=-1\). Paraboloid \(z=x^2+y^2\) at the origin: \(K=4\), \(\lvert H\rvert=2\).

Geodesics

  • Geodesic: a curve with zero geodesic curvature, equivalently acceleration normal to the surface, equivalently a local length-minimiser (locally shortest). A straight line in the plane; a great circle on the sphere; a helix, circle or line on a cylinder; a straight line after unrolling on a cone.
  • Equations: \(\ddot u^k+\Gamma^k_{ij}\dot u^i\dot u^j=0\). Christoffel symbols depend only on \(E,F,G\).
  • Euler-Lagrange for length: a geodesic extremises \(\int\sqrt{E\dot u^2+2F\dot u\dot v+G\dot v^2}\,dt\).
  • Clairaut: on a surface of revolution with radius function \(\rho\) at a point of a geodesic, \(\rho\sin\psi\) is constant, where \(\psi\) is the angle between the geodesic and the meridian. Meridians are always geodesics; a parallel is a geodesic iff \(\rho'=0\) there.
  • Geodesic curvature: \(\kappa_g=\kappa\sin\varphi\); \(\kappa^2=\kappa_n^2+\kappa_g^2\).
  • Gauss-Bonnet: for a simple region, \(\iint K\,dA+\oint\kappa_g\,ds+\sum\text{exterior angles}=2\pi\chi\). Closed surface: \(\iint K\,dA=2\pi\chi\) (sphere \(4\pi\), torus \(0\)). Geodesic triangle: angle sum \(=\pi+\iint K\,dA\).
  • Facts: through a point in a given direction there is a unique geodesic. Isometries map geodesics to geodesics. Two antipodal points of a sphere are joined by infinitely many shortest geodesics (all the half great circles); two non-antipodal points lie on a unique great circle, whose shorter arc is the unique shortest geodesic.
