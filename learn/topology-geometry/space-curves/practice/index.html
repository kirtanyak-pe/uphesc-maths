  1. Question 1Easy
    In the Serret-Frenet formulas for a unit-speed curve with \(\kappa>0\), the derivative of the principal normal is

    Why

    \(\dot N\cdot T=-N\cdot\dot T=-\kappa\), \(\dot N\cdot N=0\), and \(\dot N\cdot B=-N\cdot\dot B=\tau\) (because \(\dot B=-\tau N\)). So \(\dot N=-\kappa T+\tau B\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    The curvature of the helix \(\gamma(t)=(\cos t,\sin t,t)\) is

    Why

    With \(a=b=1\): \(\kappa=\dfrac{a}{a^2+b^2}=\dfrac12\). Direct check: \(\lVert\gamma'\times\gamma''\rVert=\sqrt2\) and \(\lVert\gamma'\rVert^3=2\sqrt2\), so \(\kappa=\dfrac12\).
  3. Question 3Easy
    A space curve has constant curvature \(\kappa=\dfrac13\) and torsion \(\tau=0\). It is

    Why

    \(\tau=0\) with \(\kappa\ne0\) means a plane curve; a plane curve of constant curvature \(\kappa\) is a circle of radius \(1/\kappa=3\).
  4. Question 4Medium
    The torsion of the curve \(\gamma(t)=(3\cos t,\,3\sin t,\,4t)\) is

    Why

    For \((a\cos t,a\sin t,bt)\), \(\tau=\dfrac{b}{a^2+b^2}=\dfrac4{25}\). The distractor \(3/25\) is \(\kappa\); \(4/5\) divides by \(\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\) instead of \(a^2+b^2\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The torsion of the twisted cubic \(\gamma(t)=(t,t^2,t^3)\) at \(t=1\) is

    Why

    \(\gamma'\times\gamma''=(6t^2,-6t,2)\), \(\lVert\gamma'\times\gamma''\rVert^2=4(9t^4+9t^2+1)\) and \([\gamma',\gamma'',\gamma''']=12\), so \(\tau=\dfrac{3}{9t^4+9t^2+1}\). At \(t=1\) this is \(\dfrac3{19}\). The value \(3\) is \(\tau(0)\); \(14=\lVert\gamma'(1)\rVert^2\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    Consider: (i) a curve with \(\kappa\equiv0\) is a straight line; (ii) a curve with \(\kappa\ne0\) and \(\tau\equiv0\) lies in a plane; (iii) a curve with constant \(\kappa>0\) and constant \(\tau\) is a circle. Which statements are correct?

    Why

    (i) and (ii) are standard. (iii) is false: constant \(\kappa>0\) and constant \(\tau\ne0\) gives a circular helix; only \(\tau=0\) gives a circle.
  7. Question 7Medium
    The osculating plane of the curve \((t,t^2,t^3)\) at \(t=1\) is

    Why

    The normal is \(\gamma'\times\gamma''=(6,-6,2)\parallel(3,-3,1)\) at \(t=1\) and the plane passes through \((1,1,1)\): \(3(x-1)-3(y-1)+(z-1)=0\), i.e. \(3x-3y+z=1\). Option D has the right normal direction but misses the point \((1,1,1)\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    The curve \(\gamma(t)=(3t-t^3,\,3t^2,\,3t+t^3)\) has \(\kappa=\tau\) at every point. The constant angle between its tangent and the \(z\)-axis is

    Why

    Lancret: \(\tau/\kappa=\cot\theta=1\), so \(\theta=45^\circ\). Direct check: \(\lVert\gamma'\rVert=3\sqrt2(1+t^2)\) and \(\gamma'_z=3(1+t^2)\), so \(\cos\theta=1/\sqrt2\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    The radius of curvature of the ellipse \(\dfrac{x^2}{9}+\dfrac{y^2}{4}=1\) at the point \((0,2)\) is

    Why

    With \(x=3\cos t\), \(y=2\sin t\) at \(t=\pi/2\): \(x'=-3\), \(y'=0\), \(x''=0\), \(y''=-2\), so \(\kappa=\dfrac{\lvert x'y''-y'x''\rvert}{(x'^2+y'^2)^{3/2}}=\dfrac{6}{27}=\dfrac29\) and \(\rho=\dfrac92=\dfrac{a^2}{b}\). (At \((3,0)\) the radius would be \(b^2/a=4/3\); \(2/9\) is \(\kappa\).)
  10. Question 10Hard
    A curve has constant curvature \(\kappa=3\) and constant torsion \(\tau=4\). It lies on a circular cylinder of radius

    Why

    It is a circular helix \((a\cos t,a\sin t,bt)\) with \(\kappa=\dfrac{a}{a^2+b^2}\), \(\tau=\dfrac{b}{a^2+b^2}\). Solving, \(a=\dfrac{\kappa}{\kappa^2+\tau^2}=\dfrac{3}{25}\) and \(b=\dfrac{\tau}{\kappa^2+\tau^2}=\dfrac4{25}\) (check: \(\dfrac{a}{a^2+b^2}=\dfrac{3/25}{1/25}=3\)). Note \(a\ne1/\kappa\): the radius of curvature \(1/3\) is not the cylinder radius.
  11. Question 11Hard
    The involute of the circle of radius \(3\) is \(\bar\gamma(t)=3(\cos t+t\sin t,\ \sin t-t\cos t)\). Its curvature at \(t=2\) is

    Why

    \(\bar\gamma'=3t(\cos t,\sin t)\), speed \(3t\); the involute of a plane curve has curvature \(1/\lvert c-s\rvert\) and here \(\kappa=\dfrac1{at}=\dfrac1{3t}\). At \(t=2\): \(\dfrac16\). (The circle itself has curvature \(\tfrac13\).)
  12. Question 12Hard
    Assertion (A): A curve with constant curvature \(\kappa=2\) and constant torsion \(\tau=3\) is a plane curve. Reason (R): A curve with \(\kappa\ne0\) is a plane curve if and only if \(\tau\equiv0\). Choose the correct option.

    Why

    R is the standard criterion and is true. With \(\tau=3\ne0\) the curve is not planar, so A is false (it is a circular helix).
  13. Question 13Medium
    Match the curve with its \((\kappa,\tau)\). Curves: P: circle of radius \(2\); Q: helix \((\cos t,\sin t,t)\); R: twisted cubic \((t,t^2,t^3)\) at \(t=0\). Values: 1: \((\tfrac12,0)\); 2: \((\tfrac12,\tfrac12)\); 3: \((2,3)\).

    Why

    Circle of radius \(2\): \(\kappa=\tfrac12\), \(\tau=0\). Helix with \(a=b=1\): \(\kappa=\tau=\tfrac12\). Twisted cubic at \(t=0\): \(\kappa=2\), \(\tau=3\).
  14. Question 14Medium
    A circular helix of radius \(a\) winds on a cylinder so that its tangent makes the angle \(\alpha\) with the plane of the base circle. Its radius of curvature is

    Why

    For \(\gamma=(a\cos t,a\sin t,bt)\), \(\tan\alpha=b/a\) and \(\kappa=a/(a^2+b^2)=\dfrac{\cos^2\alpha}{a}\), so \(\rho=1/\kappa=a\sec^2\alpha\) (larger than \(a\)).
  15. Question 15Medium
    For a unit-speed space curve with curvature \(\kappa\) and torsion \(\tau\), the angle between the principal normals at two nearby points a distance \(\Delta s\) apart along the curve is approximately

    Why

    Since \(\dot N=-\kappa T+\tau B\), \(\lVert\dot N\rVert=\sqrt{\kappa^2+\tau^2}\), so the principal normal turns through \(\sqrt{\kappa^2+\tau^2}\,\Delta s\). The angle between consecutive tangents is \(\kappa\,\Delta s\) and between consecutive binormals \(\lvert\tau\rvert\,\Delta s\).
