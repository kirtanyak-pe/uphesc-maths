  1. Question 1Easy
    For \(Q=(x-y)^2+(y-z)^2+(z-x)^2\) on \(\mathbb R^3\), the pair (rank, signature) is

    Why

    \(Q=2x^2+2y^2+2z^2-2xy-2yz-2zx\) has matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&-1\\-1&2&-1\\-1&-1&2\end{pmatrix}\) with eigenvalues \(3,3,0\). So \(p=2\), \(q=0\), rank \(2\), signature \(p-q=2\). \(Q\) vanishes on \((1,1,1)\), so it cannot be definite.
  2. Question 2Easy
    The symmetric matrix of the quadratic form \(Q=2x^2+6xy-4y^2\) is

    Why

    The cross term \(6xy=a_{12}xy+a_{21}yx\) with \(a_{12}=a_{21}\), so each is \(3\). Check: \((x,y)A(x,y)^T=2x^2+6xy-4y^2\). Option B is not symmetric, and D is not symmetric.
  3. Question 3Easy
    The number of congruence classes of real symmetric \(3\times3\) matrices is

    Why

    A class is determined by \((p,q)\) with \(p+q\le3\): that is \(4+3+2+1=10\) pairs (\(=\tfrac{4\cdot5}{2}\)). The number \(4\) counts only the invertible classes.
  4. Question 4Medium
    The quadratic form with matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&k\end{pmatrix}\) is positive definite if and only if

    Why

    Leading minors: \(2\), \(4-1=3\), and \(\det=2(2k-1)-(k)=3k-2\). All positive iff \(k>\tfrac23\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The form \(x^2+y^2+z^2+2a(xy+yz+zx)\) is positive definite if and only if

    Why

    The matrix \((1-a)I+aJ\) has eigenvalues \(1+2a\) (once) and \(1-a\) (twice). Both positive iff \(a>-\tfrac12\) and \(a<1\). Minors check: \(\Delta_2=1-a^2\), \(\Delta_3=(1-a)^2(1+2a)\).
  6. Question 6Easy
    Which of the following is NOT preserved when a real symmetric matrix \(A\) is replaced by a congruent matrix \(P^TAP\) (\(P\) invertible)?

    Why

    Congruence preserves rank, the numbers \((p,q)\) and hence the signature and definiteness (Sylvester). Eigenvalues change: \(A=I\) and \(P=2I\) give \(P^TAP=4I\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    \(A\) is a real symmetric \(n\times n\) matrix. Which statement is true?

    C is the correct PSD test. A fails: \(\operatorname{diag}(0,-1)\) has leading minors \(0,0\). B fails: \(\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}\) has determinant \(-3\). D fails: \(-I_2\) has \(\det=1\) but is negative definite.
  8. Question 8Medium
    A real symmetric matrix has leading principal minors \(\Delta_1=1\), \(\Delta_2=-3\), \(\Delta_3=9\) (for example \(\begin{pmatrix}1&2&0\\2&1&0\\0&0&-3\end{pmatrix}\)). The signature of its quadratic form is

    Why

    Sequence \(1,1,-3,9\) has \(2\) sign changes, so \(q=2\) and \(p=1\) (Jacobi’s rule; the eigenvalues are \(3,-1,-3\)). Signature \(p-q=-1\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    For \(Q=xy+yz\) on \(\mathbb R^3\), the pair (rank, signature) is

    Why

    \(Q=y(x+z)\). With \(u=x+z\), \(yu=\tfrac14\big((y+u)^2-(y-u)^2\big)\), so \(p=q=1\). Rank \(2\), signature \(0\). The matrix has eigenvalues \(\pm\tfrac1{\sqrt2},0\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    The conic \(2x^2+xy+2y^2+5=0\) represents

    Why

    \(\delta=ab-h^2=4-\tfrac14>0\), \(\Delta=c\delta=\tfrac{75}4\ne0\). \((a+b)\Delta>0\), so the ellipse is imaginary. Directly: \(2x^2+xy+2y^2\) is positive definite and equals \(-5\), impossible for real \(x,y\).
  11. Question 11Medium
    The surface \(2x^2+3y^2-z^2=-4\) is

    Why

    Rewrite as \(z^2-2x^2-3y^2=4\): signs \((1,2)\) with right side positive, i.e. \(\tfrac{z^2}{4}-\tfrac{x^2}{2}-\tfrac{y^2}{4/3}=1\), a hyperboloid of two sheets (it has no points with \(\lvert z\rvert<2\)).
  12. Question 12Hard
    The conic \(x^2+2xy+y^2=4\) represents

    Why

    \(\delta=1-1=0\) but \(\Delta=0\) as well (the matrix has two equal rows), so the conic is degenerate, not a parabola. Indeed \((x+y)^2=4\) gives \(x+y=2\) and \(x+y=-2\), two parallel lines.
  13. Question 13Hard
    The maximum value of \(2xy+2yz+2zx\) on the sphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2=1\) is

    Why

    The matrix \(J-I\) has characteristic polynomial \(x^3-3x-2=(x-2)(x+1)^2\), so the eigenvalues are \(2,-1,-1\). By the Rayleigh bound the maximum is \(\lambda_{\max}=2\), at \(x=y=z=\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\). Option C confuses this matrix with the all-ones matrix \(J\), whose largest eigenvalue is \(3\).
  14. Question 14Hard
    The form \(x^2+y^2+z^2+2a(xy+yz+zx)\) is positive semidefinite but not positive definite exactly when

    Why

    Eigenvalues are \(1+2a,\,1-a,\,1-a\). All are \(\ge0\) iff \(-\tfrac12\le a\le1\), and one vanishes only at the endpoints \(a=-\tfrac12\) (eigenvalues \(0,\tfrac32,\tfrac32\)) and \(a=1\) (eigenvalues \(3,0,0\)). The open interval is positive definite; option D includes it.
  15. Question 15Easy
    Assertion (A): if a real quadratic form is reduced to \(\sum d_iy_i^2\) by two different invertible substitutions, the number of positive \(d_i\) is the same in both. Reason (R): Sylvester’s law of inertia. Which is correct?

    Why

    A is a statement of the invariance of the index; Sylvester’s law of inertia is exactly that theorem, so R explains A. (The coefficients \(d_i\) themselves are not invariant, only the numbers of positive and negative ones.)
