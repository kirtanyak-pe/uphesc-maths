Why it matters for the exam

Quadratic forms were asked twice in 2026 (Q84 rank and index, Q87 signature of a symmetric matrix) and the topic is named in the syllabus. The questions are short calculations: write the matrix of a form, reduce to a sum of squares, read off rank, index and signature, or test definiteness. Conics and quadrics classification belongs here too and is a standard application.

Core ideas

Matrix of a quadratic form

A quadratic form on \(\mathbb R^n\) is \(Q(x)=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}x_ix_j=x^TAx\). The matrix \(A\) can be chosen symmetric and then it is unique:

  • \(a_{ii}\) is the coefficient of \(x_i^2\);
  • \(a_{ij}=a_{ji}\) is half the coefficient of \(x_ix_j\) (\(i\ne j\)).

Example: \(Q=2x^2+6xy-4y^2\) has \(A=\begin{pmatrix}2&3\\3&-4\end{pmatrix}\) (the \(3\), not \(6\)).

Over \(\mathbb C\) the same applies to \(x^TAx\) (complex symmetric \(A\)), and a Hermitian form is \(x^*Ax\) with \(A\) Hermitian; everything below also holds for Hermitian forms.

Congruence

\(A\) and \(B\) are congruent if \(B=P^TAP\) for some invertible \(P\) (the substitution \(x=Py\) turns \(x^TAx\) into \(y^TBy\)). Congruence is an equivalence relation. It preserves: symmetry, rank, the signature and index (Sylvester), definiteness. It does not preserve eigenvalues, trace or determinant (the determinant is multiplied by \((\det P)^2\), so its sign is preserved).

Similarity (\(P^{-1}AP\)) and congruence (\(P^TAP\)) coincide when \(P\) is orthogonal (\(P^T=P^{-1}\)). That is why orthogonal diagonalization gives a reduction of a form with the eigenvalues as coefficients.

Reducing to a sum of squares

There are three standard methods. All give \(Q=\sum d_iy_i^2\) with the same number of positive and negative \(d_i\).

  1. Orthogonal diagonalization (principal axes). Find orthonormal eigenvectors \(q_i\) of \(A\) with eigenvalues \(\lambda_i\); with \(x=Qy\), \(Q(x)=\sum\lambda_iy_i^2\). Metric information (lengths, angles) is kept.
  2. Lagrange’s completing the square. If some \(a_{ii}\ne0\), collect all terms with \(x_i\) and complete the square, then repeat on the remaining variables. If every \(a_{ii}=0\) but some \(a_{ij}\ne0\), first substitute \(x_i=u+v\), \(x_j=u-v\), which creates squares.
  3. Symmetric elimination (congruence). Apply a row operation, then the same column operation, until the matrix is diagonal.

Rank, index, signature, canonical form

Let \(A\) be real symmetric with \(p\) positive and \(q\) negative diagonal entries after diagonalization, and \(r=p+q\).

  • Rank \(r=\operatorname{rank}A\) (number of non-zero squares).
  • Index \(p\) = number of positive squares.
  • Signature \(s=p-q=2p-r\).
  • The normal form over \(\mathbb R\) is \(y_1^2+\dots+y_p^2-y_{p+1}^2-\dots-y_r^2\).
  • Over \(\mathbb C\) the normal form is \(z_1^2+\dots+z_r^2\), so rank is the only invariant.

Conventions vary: most Indian texts (and the 2026 key) define index as the number of positive squares, and a few call \(q\) the index, or write signature as the pair \((p,q)\). Read the options: if they are pairs, the pair \((p,q)\) or (rank, index) is meant.

Counting: the congruence classes of real symmetric \(n\times n\) matrices are given by \((p,q)\) with \(p+q\le n\), which is \(\tfrac{(n+1)(n+2)}2\) classes (for \(n=3\): \(10\)). The non-degenerate ones (\(r=n\)) give \(n+1\) classes. Over \(\mathbb C\) there are \(n+1\) classes (ranks \(0,\dots,n\)).

Eigenvalue reading. \(p\) = number of positive eigenvalues of \(A\), \(q\) = number of negative eigenvalues, \(n-r\) = multiplicity of eigenvalue \(0\) (all with multiplicity). Example: \(Q=(x+y+z)^2\) has matrix of all ones, eigenvalues \(3,0,0\), so \(r=1,p=1,q=0\).

Sylvester’s law of inertia. For a real quadratic form, \(p\) and \(q\) do not depend on the diagonalizing substitution. (The individual coefficients \(d_i\) do: scaling \(y_i\) changes them.) Equivalent statement: congruent symmetric matrices have the same \((p,q)\), and conversely real symmetric matrices with the same \((p,q)\) are congruent.

Jacobi’s rule. If all leading principal minors \(\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_n\) of \(A\) are non-zero, then \(q\) equals the number of sign changes in the sequence \(1,\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\dots,\Delta_n\).

Definiteness

For real symmetric \(A\) and \(Q(x)=x^TAx\):

Type Condition Eigenvalues
Positive definite (PD) \(Q(x)>0\) for all \(x\ne0\) all \(>0\)
Positive semidefinite (PSD) \(Q(x)\ge0\) for all \(x\) all \(\ge0\)
Negative definite (ND) \(Q(x)<0\) for all \(x\ne0\) all \(<0\)
Negative semidefinite \(Q(x)\le0\) for all \(x\) all \(\le0\)
Indefinite takes both signs both signs occur

Tests for PD (any one is equivalent to the others):

  • all eigenvalues are positive;
  • Sylvester’s criterion: all leading principal minors are positive, \(\Delta_k>0\) for \(k=1,\dots,n\);
  • all pivots in Gaussian elimination (without row exchanges) are positive;
  • \(A=B^TB\) for an invertible \(B\) (Cholesky: \(A=LL^T\), \(L\) lower triangular with positive diagonal);
  • \(p=n\).

ND: \((-1)^k\Delta_k>0\) for all \(k\) (signs alternate, starting negative).

PSD needs all principal minors (every choice of rows \(=\) columns, not only the leading ones) to be \(\ge0\). Leading minors \(\ge0\) are not enough: \(\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}\) has \(\Delta_1=\Delta_2=0\) but is negative semidefinite. Equivalent: \(A=B^TB\) for some \(B\).

Consequences for PD matrices: \(a_{ii}>0\); \(a_{ij}^2<a_{ii}a_{jj}\); \(\det A>0\); \(A^{-1}\) and \(A^k\) are PD; \(C^TAC\) is PD for invertible \(C\); sums of PD matrices are PD. A strictly diagonally dominant symmetric matrix with positive diagonal is PD. A necessary condition only: positive diagonal entries (not sufficient), \(\det>0\) (not sufficient).

Rayleigh quotient. For real symmetric \(A\) with eigenvalues \(\lambda_{\min}\le\dots\le\lambda_{\max}\), on \(\lVert x\rVert=1\) the form \(x^TAx\) ranges over \([\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\max}]\). The extremes are attained at the corresponding unit eigenvectors.

Simultaneous diagonalization. If \(B\) is PD and \(A\) is symmetric, there is an invertible \(P\) with \(P^TBP=I\) and \(P^TAP\) diagonal (solve \(\det(A-\lambda B)=0\)). Two arbitrary forms cannot always be diagonalized together.

Conics

For \(S:\ ax^2+2hxy+by^2+2gx+2fy+c=0\) put

\(\delta=ab-h^2,\qquad\Delta=\det\begin{pmatrix}a&h&g\\h&b&f\\g&f&c\end{pmatrix}.\)

\(\Delta\) \(\delta\) Conic
\(\ne0\) \(>0\) ellipse: real if \((a+b)\Delta<0\), imaginary (no real points) if \((a+b)\Delta>0\)
\(\ne0\) \(=0\) parabola
\(\ne0\) \(<0\) hyperbola (rectangular iff \(a+b=0\))
\(=0\) \(>0\) a single point (two imaginary lines)
\(=0\) \(<0\) pair of distinct intersecting lines
\(=0\) \(=0\) pair of parallel (or coincident, or imaginary) lines

The sign test is \(\delta\) = product of the eigenvalues of the form’s matrix: same sign means elliptic, opposite signs hyperbolic, one zero parabolic. To remove the \(xy\) term rotate by \(\theta\) with \(\tan2\theta=\dfrac{2h}{a-b}\) (\(\theta=\pi/4\) if \(a=b\)). For a central conic (\(\delta\ne0\)) the centre solves \(ax+hy+g=0\), \(hx+by+f=0\).

Quadrics

For \(x^TAx+2b^Tx+c=0\) in \(\mathbb R^3\), diagonalize \(A\) orthogonally and translate.

If \(A\) is invertible, the centre is \(x_0=-A^{-1}b\) and the equation becomes \(\lambda_1u^2+\lambda_2v^2+\lambda_3w^2=d\) with \(d=b^TA^{-1}b-c\). If \(d\ne0\) multiply by \(\operatorname{sign}d\) so \(d>0\) (this may swap \(p\) and \(q\)), then use the signs \((p,q)\) of the eigenvalues:

\((p,q)\) of \(A\) (after \(d>0\)) Surface
\((3,0)\) ellipsoid
\((2,1)\) hyperboloid of one sheet
\((1,2)\) hyperboloid of two sheets
\((0,3)\) imaginary ellipsoid

If \(d=0\): signs \((3,0)\) or \((0,3)\) give a single point; mixed signs give a real cone.

If \(\operatorname{rank}A=2\) (one zero eigenvalue): with a linear term along the null direction, \(\lambda_1u^2+\lambda_2v^2=2cw\) is a paraboloid, elliptic if \(\lambda_1\lambda_2>0\), hyperbolic if \(\lambda_1\lambda_2<0\) (here \(\operatorname{rank}\bar A=4\) for the \(4\times4\) bordered matrix). Without it, \(\lambda_1u^2+\lambda_2v^2=d\) is a cylinder: elliptic (same signs), hyperbolic (opposite signs, \(d\ne0\)); with \(d=0\) a line or a pair of intersecting planes.

If \(\operatorname{rank}A=1\): \(\lambda u^2=2cv\) is a parabolic cylinder; \(\lambda u^2=d\) is a pair of parallel planes (or coincident, or imaginary).

Worked examples

Example 1: Lagrange reduction

Reduce \(Q=x_1^2+2x_2^2-7x_3^2-4x_1x_2+8x_1x_3\) and find rank, index, signature.

Collect \(x_1\): \(x_1^2-4x_1x_2+8x_1x_3=(x_1-2x_2+4x_3)^2-4x_2^2-16x_3^2+16x_2x_3\). So \(Q=(x_1-2x_2+4x_3)^2-2x_2^2+16x_2x_3-23x_3^2\). Then \(-2x_2^2+16x_2x_3=-2(x_2-4x_3)^2+32x_3^2\), giving

\(Q=y_1^2-2y_2^2+9y_3^2,\quad y_1=x_1-2x_2+4x_3,\ y_2=x_2-4x_3,\ y_3=x_3.\)

Rank \(3\), index \(p=2\), \(q=1\), signature \(1\). (Identity checked at random integer points, and the congruence reduction of the matrix gives \((2,1)\).)

Example 2: no square terms

\(Q=2(xy+yz+zx)\). Put \(x=u+v\), \(y=u-v\): \(xy=u^2-v^2\) and \(z(x+y)=2uz\), so \(Q=2u^2-2v^2+4uz=2(u+z)^2-2z^2-2v^2\). Hence \(p=1\), \(q=2\), rank \(3\), signature \(-1\). Check with eigenvalues: the matrix \(J-I\) has eigenvalues \(2,-1,-1\).

Example 3: definiteness with a parameter

For which \(k\) is \(A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&k\end{pmatrix}\) positive definite?

\(\Delta_1=2\), \(\Delta_2=4-1=3\), \(\Delta_3=\det A=3k-2\) (checked at several values of \(k\)). All positive iff \(k>\tfrac23\). At \(k=\tfrac23\) the determinant is \(0\) and \(A\) is positive semidefinite (inertia \((2,0)\) with one zero). For \(k<\tfrac23\) it is indefinite with \(p=2,q=1\) (when \(\Delta_3<0\)).

Example 4: classify conics

(a) \(5x^2-6xy+5y^2-8=0\): \(a=5,h=-3,b=5,c=-8\). \(\delta=25-9=16>0\), \(\Delta=-8\cdot16=-128\), \((a+b)\Delta<0\), real ellipse. Eigenvalues of \(\begin{pmatrix}5&-3\\-3&5\end{pmatrix}\) are \(8,2\), so in rotated coordinates \(8u^2+2v^2=8\), i.e. \(u^2+v^2/4=1\) with semi-axes \(1\) and \(2\).

(b) \(x^2-2xy+y^2-4x-4y=0\): \(\delta=1-1=0\), \(\Delta=-16\ne0\), parabola.

(c) \(x^2+2xy+y^2=4\): \(\delta=0\), \(\Delta=0\), so a pair of parallel lines: \((x+y)^2=4\) gives \(x+y=\pm2\).

(d) \(2x^2+xy+2y^2+5=0\): \(\delta=\tfrac{15}4>0\), \(\Delta=\tfrac{75}4>0\), \((a+b)\Delta>0\): imaginary ellipse (the form is positive definite and the right side is negative, so no real point).

Example 5: classify a quadric

\(x^2+y^2-z^2-2x+2y-2z+2=0\). Complete squares: \((x-1)^2+(y+1)^2-(z+1)^2+1=0\), i.e. \((x-1)^2+(y+1)^2-(z+1)^2=-1\). Here \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1)\), \(d=-1<0\). Multiply by \(-1\): \(-(x-1)^2-(y+1)^2+(z+1)^2=1\), signs \((1,2)\): hyperboloid of two sheets, centre \((1,-1,-1)\).

Example 6: maximum of a form on the unit circle

Maximum and minimum of \(x^2+4xy+y^2\) on \(x^2+y^2=1\). Matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}\) has eigenvalues \(3,-1\) (eigenvectors \((1,1)\), \((1,-1)\)). By the Rayleigh bound the range is \([-1,3]\). Maximum \(3\) at \(\pm\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)\) (numerically confirmed by scanning the circle), minimum \(-1\).

Common traps

  • Off-diagonal entries are half the coefficient. \(6xy\) gives \(a_{12}=3\).
  • Congruence is not similarity. Eigenvalues change under congruence; only the signs (and the zero count) are invariant. Orthogonal \(P\) is the one case where both hold.
  • Sylvester’s law is about the counts \(p,q\), not the coefficients. Different reductions give different coefficients but the same numbers of positive and negative terms.
  • Convention of “index”. Index \(=p\) (positive squares) in most exam keys; signature \(=p-q\). If options are pairs, check which pair is asked: (rank, index), \((p,q)\) or (rank, signature).
  • Leading minors \(\ge0\) do not give PSD. PSD needs all principal minors \(\ge0\). For PD, leading minors \(>0\) suffice.
  • Positive diagonal does not give PD. \(\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}\) has positive diagonal and is indefinite (\(\det=-3\)).
  • \(\det A>0\) does not give PD. \(-I_2\) has \(\det=1\) but is negative definite.
  • ND test. The signs of \(\Delta_k\) alternate \(-,+,-,\dots\); “all minors negative” is wrong.
  • Conic with \(\Delta=0\) is degenerate, whatever \(\delta\) says. Check \(\Delta\) first.
  • Real vs imaginary ellipse. \(\delta>0\) and \(\Delta\ne0\) does not guarantee a real ellipse; check the sign of \((a+b)\Delta\).
  • Quadric with \(d<0\). Flip the sign of the whole equation before counting positive and negative eigenvalues (hyperboloid of one sheet versus two sheets swap).
  • Rank of the form is the rank of the symmetric matrix, not the number of variables appearing.

Previous-year questions

2026, Q84 (rank and index of a form)

For \(Q=x^2+y^2+z^2+2xy+2yz+2zx\), express it in matrix form, diagonalize by an orthogonal transformation, and give (rank, index). Options: (A) \((1,0)\) (B) \((1,1)\) (C) \((2,1)\) (D) \((3,0)\).

Answer: B, \((1,1)\) (worked key, unofficial; flagged as medium confidence).

\(Q=(x+y+z)^2\). The matrix is the all-ones matrix with eigenvalues \(3,0,0\), so \(Q=3u^2\) in suitable orthonormal coordinates. Rank \(1\); number of positive squares \(1\). With index \(=p\) the answer is \((1,1)\). If a paper counted negative squares as the index it would be \((1,0)\), option A; the key uses the positive count.

2026, Q87 (symmetric matrix and signature)

\(A\) is a \(3\times3\) real symmetric matrix with characteristic polynomial \((x-1)^2(x-3)\). Decide whether \(A\) is diagonalizable, then give the signature of the associated quadratic form. Options are pairs such as \((2,1)\), \((1,2)\), \((3,0)\), \((0,3)\) (the printed order is not fully legible).

Answer: C, \((3,0)\) (worked key, unofficial). The matrix is real symmetric, hence orthogonally diagonalizable with eigenvalues \(1,1,3\). All are positive, so the form is positive definite with \(p=3\), \(q=0\): signature pair \((3,0)\).

Quick revision

  • Matrix of \(Q\): squares on the diagonal, cross-term coefficient halved off the diagonal.
  • Congruence \(B=P^TAP\) keeps rank, \((p,q)\) and definiteness; eigenvalues are not kept.
  • Rank \(=p+q\); index \(=p\); signature \(=p-q\).
  • Sylvester: \(p\) and \(q\) are invariants of the form.
  • \(p\), \(q\) = numbers of positive and negative eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix.
  • Real classes of \(n\times n\) forms: \(\tfrac{(n+1)(n+2)}2\); over \(\mathbb C\): \(n+1\).
  • PD \(\iff\) all eigenvalues \(>0\) \(\iff\) all leading minors \(>0\) \(\iff\) \(A=B^TB\), \(B\) invertible.
  • ND \(\iff\) \((-1)^k\Delta_k>0\); PSD needs all principal minors \(\ge0\).
  • Jacobi: \(q\) = sign changes in \(1,\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_n\) (all \(\Delta_k\ne0\)).
  • Conic: \(\delta=ab-h^2\) and \(\Delta\); \(\Delta=0\) degenerate; \(\delta>0\) ellipse, \(=0\) parabola, \(<0\) hyperbola.
  • Quadric with \(A\) invertible: \((3,0)\) ellipsoid, \((2,1)\) one sheet, \((1,2)\) two sheets after making \(d>0\).
  • Rayleigh: \(\lambda_{\min}\le x^TAx\le\lambda_{\max}\) on the unit sphere.
