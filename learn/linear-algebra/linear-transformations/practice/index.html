Linear transformations, rank-nullity and change of basis

  1. Question 1Easy
    A linear map \(T:V\to W\) has \(\dim V=7\) and \(\dim\ker T=3\). Then \(\dim\operatorname{range}T\) is

    Why

    Rank-nullity: \(\operatorname{rank}=7-3=4\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    Which of the following linear maps exists?

    Why

    An onto map needs \(\dim\) domain \(\ge\dim\) codomain, a one-one map needs \(\dim\) domain \(\le\dim\) codomain; the inclusion \((x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y,z,0,0)\) is one-one. On equal dimensions one-one forces onto.
  3. Question 3Medium
    The matrix of differentiation \(D:P_2\to P_2\) with respect to the basis \(\{1,x,x^2\}\) (columns are images of basis vectors) is

    Why

    \(D1=0\), \(Dx=1\), \(Dx^2=2x\), so the columns are \((0,0,0)^T\), \((1,0,0)^T\), \((0,2,0)^T\). The transpose is the option from reading rows; the entry \(2\) must appear, not \(1\).
  4. Question 4Medium
    Let \(T:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2\) have standard matrix \(A=\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\2&4\end{pmatrix}\). The matrix of \(T\) relative to the basis \(\{(1,1),(0,1)\}\) is

    Why

    With \(P=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\) (new basis as columns) the matrix is \(P^{-1}AP=\begin{pmatrix}4&3\\2&1\end{pmatrix}\). Directly: \(T(1,1)=(4,6)=4(1,1)+2(0,1)\) and \(T(0,1)=(3,4)=3(1,1)+1(0,1)\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    For similar \(n\times n\) matrices \(A\) and \(B\), which of the following need NOT be equal?

    Why

    Trace, determinant, rank and all polynomial invariants are similarity invariants. Row spaces are subspaces of \(F^n\) that change: \(A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\) and \(B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\) are similar (swap the basis vectors) but have different row spaces.
  6. Question 6Medium
    Let \(A,B\) be \(4\times4\) matrices with \(\operatorname{rank}A=\operatorname{rank}B=3\). The least possible value of \(\operatorname{rank}(AB)\) is

    Why

    Sylvester’s inequality: \(\operatorname{rank}AB\ge3+3-4=2\), and the example \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,0)\) with \(B\) a shift \(e_1\to e_2\to e_3\to e_4\to0\) gives exactly \(2\) (rank computed).
  7. Question 7Medium
    \(P\) is a \(5\times5\) real matrix with \(P^2=P\) and \(\operatorname{rank}P=3\). Then \(\operatorname{rank}(I-P)\) equals

    Why

    \(P\) is diagonalisable with eigenvalues \(1\) (\(3\) times) and \(0\) (\(2\) times). \(I-P\) has eigenvalue \(0\) three times and \(1\) twice, so its rank is \(2\). (Equivalently \(\operatorname{range}(I-P)=\ker P\) has dimension \(5-3\).)
  8. Question 8Medium
    If \(N\) is a \(5\times5\) matrix with \(N^2=0\), the largest possible rank of \(N\) is

    Why

    \(N^2=0\) gives \(\operatorname{range}N\subseteq\ker N\), so \(r\le5-r\) and \(r\le2\). Rank \(2\) occurs, e.g. \(N=E_{13}+E_{24}\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    If \(N^3=0\), then \((I-N)^{-1}\) equals

    Why

    \((I-N)(I+N+N^2)=I-N^3=I\). The alternating signs belong to \((I+N)^{-1}=I-N+N^2\). (Checked on a numerical example.)
  10. Question 10Hard
    Let \(U\) be a fixed \(2\)-dimensional subspace of \(\mathbb{R}^5\). The dimension of the space of all linear maps \(T:\mathbb{R}^5\to\mathbb{R}^3\) with \(T(u)=0\) for every \(u\in U\) is

    Why

    Such maps are the same as linear maps \(\mathbb{R}^5/U\to\mathbb{R}^3\), and \(\dim\mathbb{R}^5/U=3\). So the dimension is \(3\times3=9\). (Without the condition it would be \(15\).)
  11. Question 11Hard
    Let \(N\) be a nilpotent \(n\times n\) matrix over \(\mathbb{R}\). Consider: I. \(N^n=0\). II. \(I+N\) is invertible. III. If \(N'\) is another nilpotent \(n\times n\) matrix, then \(N+N'\) is nilpotent. IV. \(\operatorname{tr}N=0\). Which are always true?

    Why

    I: the minimal polynomial is \(x^k\) with \(k\le n\). II: \((I+N)^{-1}=I-N+N^2-\cdots\). IV: all eigenvalues are \(0\). III fails: \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\) has square \(I\).
  12. Question 12Hard
    Let \(M=\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\0&B\end{pmatrix}\) where \(A\) is \(3\times3\) invertible, \(B\) is \(2\times2\) of rank \(1\), and \(C\) is any \(3\times2\) matrix. The nullity of \(M\) is

    Why

    Right-multiply by \(\begin{pmatrix}I&-A^{-1}C\\0&I\end{pmatrix}\) (invertible) to get \(\operatorname{diag}(A,B)\) of rank \(3+1=4\). Nullity \(5-4=1\), independent of \(C\).
  13. Question 13Easy
    Assertion A: A linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space is one-one if and only if it is onto. Reason R: By the rank-nullity theorem, \(\operatorname{nullity}T=0\) holds exactly when \(\operatorname{rank}T=\dim V\).

    Why

    For \(T:V\to V\), \(\dim V=\operatorname{nullity}+\operatorname{rank}\); nullity \(0\) means one-one and rank \(\dim V\) means onto. R is the proof of A.
  14. Question 14Easy
    The kernel of \(T:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^3\), \(T(x,y,z)=(x+y,\ y+z,\ x+2y+z)\) is spanned by

    Why

    \(x+y=0\) and \(y+z=0\) give \((x,y,z)=t(1,-1,1)\), and the third equation \(x+2y+z=0\) then holds automatically (row 3 = row 1 + row 2). So \(T\) has rank \(2\) and nullity \(1\).
