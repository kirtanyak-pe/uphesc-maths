Why it matters for the exam

This is the most frequently tested part of the unit: four questions in the 2025 paper (Q46, Q50, Q51, Q53) and one in 2026 (Q80) are rank, nullity, injective/surjective or idempotent problems, plus three from 2019. They are quick calculations once rank-nullity and the matrix-of-a-map convention are automatic.

Core ideas

Linear maps and their algebra

A map \(T:V\to W\) over a field \(F\) is linear if \(T(au+bv)=aT(u)+bT(v)\). Standard examples: \(x\mapsto Ax\); differentiation \(D\) on polynomials; integration; \(A\mapsto A^T\); \(A\mapsto\operatorname{tr}A\); evaluation \(p\mapsto p(a)\); shifts of sequences; projections.

A linear map is determined by its values on a basis, and the values can be chosen freely: if \(v_1,\dots,v_n\) is a basis of \(V\) and \(w_1,\dots,w_n\in W\), there is exactly one linear \(T\) with \(T(v_i)=w_i\).

The set \(L(V,W)\) of linear maps is a vector space with \(\dim L(V,W)=\dim V\cdot\dim W\). The set \(L(V)=L(V,V)\) is also closed under composition and so is an algebra of dimension \(n^2\):

  • composition is associative and bilinear but not commutative;
  • \(L(V)\) has zero divisors (\(T\ne0\), \(S\ne0\) with \(TS=0\));
  • the maps commuting with every element of \(L(V)\) are exactly the scalar multiples of \(I\);
  • \(T\) is invertible iff some \(S\) has \(ST=TS=I\); then \((ST)^{-1}=T^{-1}S^{-1}\);
  • for any polynomial \(p\), \(p(T)\) is defined, and \(p(T)q(T)=q(T)p(T)\).

Kernel and range

\[\ker T=\{v: Tv=0\},\qquad \operatorname{range}T=T(V)=\{Tv\}.\]

\(\ker T\le V\) and \(\operatorname{range}T\le W\) are subspaces. \(\operatorname{nullity}T=\dim\ker T\), \(\operatorname{rank}T=\dim\operatorname{range}T\).

  • \(T\) is one-one (injective) iff \(\ker T=\{0\}\).
  • \(T\) is onto (surjective) iff \(\operatorname{range}T=W\).
  • A linear map takes a spanning set of \(V\) to a spanning set of the range; it takes independent sets to independent sets iff it is injective on their span.
  • For \(S:W\to U\) and \(T:V\to W\): \(\ker T\subseteq\ker(ST)\) and \(\operatorname{range}(ST)\subseteq\operatorname{range}S\).

Rank-Nullity Theorem

If \(V\) is finite-dimensional and \(T:V\to W\) is linear then

\[\dim V=\operatorname{nullity}T+\operatorname{rank}T.\]

Proof idea: extend a basis \(u_1,\dots,u_k\) of \(\ker T\) to a basis \(u_1,\dots,u_k,v_1,\dots,v_r\) of \(V\). Then \(Tv_1,\dots,Tv_r\) is a basis of the range. (Equivalently, \(V/\ker T\cong\operatorname{range}T\), the first isomorphism theorem for vector spaces.)

Consequences, for \(\dim V=n\) and \(\dim W=m\):

Situation Conclusion
\(n>m\) \(T\) is never injective (\(\operatorname{nullity}\ge n-m>0\))
\(n<m\) \(T\) is never onto
\(n=m\) injective \(\iff\) onto \(\iff\) bijective
\(T:V\to V\) injective \(\iff\) onto \(\iff\) invertible
any \(\operatorname{rank}T\le\min(n,m)\)

The “injective \(\Leftrightarrow\) onto” statements fail in infinite dimension: the right shift \((a_1,a_2,\dots)\mapsto(0,a_1,a_2,\dots)\) is injective, not onto. Also in finite dimension \(ST=I\Rightarrow TS=I\) for operators; this too fails for the shifts.

A map with prescribed data exists iff the dimensions allow it. For example, \(T:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^n\) with \(\ker T=\operatorname{range}T\) exists iff \(n\) is even (rank \(=\) nullity forces \(n=2\operatorname{rank}\)).

Matrix of a linear map

Let \(B=(b_1,\dots,b_n)\) be a basis of \(V\) and \(C=(c_1,\dots,c_m)\) a basis of \(W\). The matrix \([T]_{B,C}\) is \(m\times n\) and its \(j\)-th column is the coordinate vector \([T(b_j)]_C\). Then \([Tv]_C=[T]_{B,C}[v]_B\).

  • \([ST]=[S][T]\) (with matching bases); \([T^{-1}]=[T]^{-1}\).
  • \(\operatorname{rank}T=\operatorname{rank}[T]\); \(\ker T\) corresponds to the null space of \([T]\) and \(\operatorname{range}T\) to the column space.
  • For \(T(x,y)=(x-2y,\,y)\): \(T(e_1)=(1,0)\), \(T(e_2)=(-2,1)\), so \([T]=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\) (columns, not rows).
  • For differentiation on \(P_2\) with basis \(1,x,x^2\): \(D1=0\), \(Dx=1\), \(Dx^2=2x\) give \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&2\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\).

Change of basis and similarity

Let \(P\) be the matrix whose columns are the new basis vectors \(B'\) written in the old basis \(B\). Then

\[[v]_B=P[v]_{B'},\qquad [T]_{B'}=P^{-1}[T]_BP.\]

Two square matrices \(A,A'\) are similar if \(A'=P^{-1}AP\) for some invertible \(P\): they represent the same operator in different bases. For a map \(T:V\to W\) with two independent changes of basis, the matrices are only equivalent: \(A'=Q^{-1}AP\).

Similarity invariants (equal for similar matrices): determinant, trace, rank, nullity, characteristic polynomial, minimal polynomial, eigenvalues with their algebraic and geometric multiplicities, diagonalisability, Jordan form. Not invariants: entries, eigenvectors, row space, column space, symmetry.

Similar \(\Rightarrow\) same characteristic polynomial; the converse is false. The matrices \(0\) and \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\) have the same characteristic polynomial \(x^2\) but different ranks, so they are not similar.

Equivalence is completely decided by rank: every \(m\times n\) matrix of rank \(r\) is equivalent to \(\begin{pmatrix}I_r&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\).

Invertibility

For a square matrix \(A\) (or operator \(T\) on a finite-dimensional space) the following are equivalent: \(A\) is invertible; \(\det A\ne0\); rank \(n\); \(\ker A=0\); the columns (or the rows) are independent; \(A\) is onto; \(0\) is not an eigenvalue; \(Ax=b\) has a unique solution for every \(b\).

For a block upper triangular matrix, \(\det\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\0&B\end{pmatrix}=\det A\cdot\det B\) when \(A,B\) are square. So if \(A\) and \(B\) are invertible the whole matrix is invertible, whatever \(C\) is, and its nullity is \(0\). In general \(\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\0&B\end{pmatrix}\ge\operatorname{rank}A+\operatorname{rank}B\), and \(\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\0&B\end{pmatrix}=\operatorname{rank}A+\operatorname{rank}B\).

“Non-singular linear map \(V\to W\)” means an isomorphism in most Indian textbooks (and “injective” in Hoffman and Kunze). For \(\dim V\ne\dim W\) there is no isomorphism, so the count of non-singular maps is \(0\) in either reading when \(\dim V>\dim W\).

Idempotent maps (projections)

\(P^2=P\). Then:

  • \(V=\ker P\oplus\operatorname{range}P\), and \(\operatorname{range}P=\ker(I-P)=\{v:Pv=v\}\). Proof: \(v=(v-Pv)+Pv\) and \(P(v-Pv)=0\); if \(Pv=w\) and \(Pw=0\) then \(w=Pv=P^2v=Pw=0\).
  • The minimal polynomial divides \(x^2-x\), so the only eigenvalues are \(0\) and \(1\), and \(P\) is diagonalisable.
  • \(\operatorname{tr}P=\operatorname{rank}P\) (in characteristic \(0\)), and \(\operatorname{nullity}P=n-\operatorname{rank}P\).
  • \(I-P\) is also idempotent with kernel \(\operatorname{range}P\) and range \(\ker P\); \(\operatorname{rank}(I-P)=n-\operatorname{rank}P\).
  • \(I+P\) is invertible with inverse \(I-\tfrac12P\); and \(I-P\) is singular unless \(P=0\).
  • \(P\) is an orthogonal projection iff \(P=P^*\) (see the inner-product topic). The matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\) is idempotent but not symmetric.
  • If \(P\ne0\) and \(P\ne I\), then \(\ker P\ne0\) and \(\operatorname{range}P\ne V\).

Nilpotent maps

\(N^k=0\) for some \(k\); the least such \(k\) is the index.

  • The only eigenvalue is \(0\), so the characteristic polynomial is \(x^n\) and the minimal polynomial is \(x^k\) with \(k\le n\). Hence \(N^n=0\) on an \(n\)-dimensional space.
  • \(\operatorname{tr}N=0\) and \(\det N=0\).
  • \(I-N\) and \(I+N\) are invertible: \((I-N)^{-1}=I+N+\cdots+N^{k-1}\).
  • The kernels strictly increase until they reach \(V\): \(\ker N\subsetneq\ker N^2\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq\ker N^k=V\).
  • If \(N^2=0\) then \(\operatorname{range}N\subseteq\ker N\), so \(\operatorname{rank}N\le n/2\).
  • A strictly upper triangular matrix is nilpotent. \(N\) has index \(n\) iff \(\operatorname{rank}N=n-1\) and it is nilpotent (a single Jordan block).
  • The sum or product of two nilpotent matrices need not be nilpotent: \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\) squares to \(I\). Commuting nilpotents do have a nilpotent sum.

Rank of sums and products

For \(A\) of size \(m\times n\) and \(B\) of size \(n\times p\):

  • \(\operatorname{rank}(AB)\le\min(\operatorname{rank}A,\operatorname{rank}B)\).
  • Sylvester: \(\operatorname{rank}A+\operatorname{rank}B-n\le\operatorname{rank}(AB)\).
  • \(\operatorname{nullity}(AB)\le\operatorname{nullity}A+\operatorname{nullity}B\).
  • \(\operatorname{rank}(A+B)\le\operatorname{rank}A+\operatorname{rank}B\) and \(\lvert\operatorname{rank}A-\operatorname{rank}B\rvert\le\operatorname{rank}(A\pm B)\).
  • \(\operatorname{rank}A=\operatorname{rank}A^T=\operatorname{rank}A^*\); multiplying by invertible matrices does not change rank.
  • For real \(A\): \(\operatorname{rank}(A^TA)=\operatorname{rank}A\). For complex \(A\) use \(A^*A\): the matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\i&-1\end{pmatrix}\) has rank \(1\) but its square is \(0\).
  • \(\operatorname{rank}(A)\ge\operatorname{rank}(A^2)\ge\operatorname{rank}(A^3)\ge\cdots\), and the sequence stabilises.

Fixing the domain: maps vanishing on a subspace

Maps \(T:V\to W\) with \(T(U)=0\) correspond to maps \(V/U\to W\), so they form a space of dimension \((\dim V-\dim U)\dim W\).

Formulas and results

  • \(\dim V=\operatorname{nullity}T+\operatorname{rank}T\) (\(V\) finite-dimensional, nullity measured on the domain).
  • \(\dim L(V,W)=\dim V\cdot\dim W\); \(\dim L(V)=n^2\).
  • Column \(j\) of \([T]_{B,C}\) is \([T(b_j)]_C\).
  • \([T]_{B'}=P^{-1}[T]_BP\) with \(P\) = new basis in old coordinates.
  • Similar matrices share trace, determinant, rank, characteristic and minimal polynomials.
  • \(\operatorname{rank}A+\operatorname{rank}B-n\le\operatorname{rank}AB\le\min(\operatorname{rank}A,\operatorname{rank}B)\).
  • Idempotent: \(P^2=P\Rightarrow V=\ker P\oplus\operatorname{range}P\), eigenvalues in \(\{0,1\}\), \(\operatorname{tr}P=\operatorname{rank}P\).
  • Nilpotent of index \(k\): \(N^n=0\), \(\operatorname{tr}N=0\), \((I-N)^{-1}=\sum_{j<k}N^j\).
  • \(N^2=0\Rightarrow\operatorname{rank}N\le n/2\).
  • \(\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\0&B\end{pmatrix}\) with \(A,B\) square invertible is invertible.
  • \(\{T:T(U)=0\}\) has dimension \((\dim V-\dim U)\dim W\).

Worked examples

Example 1: kernel, range, nullity

Let \(T:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^3\), \(T(x,y,z)=(x+y,\ y+z,\ x+2y+z)\). Find \(\ker T\), \(\operatorname{range}T\).

The matrix has rows \((1,1,0),(0,1,1),(1,2,1)\). Row 3 is row 1 plus row 2, so the rank is \(2\) and the nullity is \(3-2=1\). Solving \(x+y=0\), \(y+z=0\) gives \(\ker T=\operatorname{span}\{(1,-1,1)\}\). The range is the column space, spanned by \(T(e_1)=(1,0,1)\) and \(T(e_2)=(1,1,2)\); it is the plane \(z=x+y\).

Example 2: change of basis

Let \(T(x,y)=(2x+y,\ x+2y)\) and \(B'=\{(1,0),(1,1)\}\). Find \([T]_{B'}\).

Directly: \(T(1,0)=(2,1)=1\cdot(1,0)+1\cdot(1,1)\) and \(T(1,1)=(3,3)=0\cdot(1,0)+3\cdot(1,1)\). So \([T]_{B'}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&3\end{pmatrix}\).

By formula: \(P=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\), \(A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}\), and \(P^{-1}AP\) gives the same matrix. Its trace \(4\) and determinant \(3\) equal those of \(A\), as similarity requires.

Example 3: an idempotent that is not a projection onto an orthogonal complement

\(P=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\) satisfies \(P^2=P\). \(\operatorname{range}P=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)\}\) and \(\ker P=\operatorname{span}\{(1,-1)\}\). These are not orthogonal, so \(P\) is a projection along a non-perpendicular direction. \(\operatorname{tr}P=1=\operatorname{rank}P\), eigenvalues \(1,0\).

Example 4: nilpotent matrix

\(N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&2\\0&0&3\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\). Then \(N^2=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&3\\0&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\) and \(N^3=0\), so the index is \(3\). Hence \((I-N)^{-1}=I+N+N^2=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&5\\0&1&3\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\); indeed \((I-N)(I+N+N^2)=I-N^3=I\).

Example 5: bounds for the rank of a product

\(A\) and \(B\) are \(4\times4\) of rank \(3\) each. Then \(2\le\operatorname{rank}(AB)\le3\) by Sylvester. The lower bound is attained: take \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,0)\) and \(B\) the shift with \(Be_1=e_2\), \(Be_2=e_3\), \(Be_3=e_4\), \(Be_4=0\). Then \(\operatorname{range}B=\operatorname{span}\{e_2,e_3,e_4\}\) and \(A\) kills \(e_4\), so \(\operatorname{rank}AB=2\).

Example 6: dimension counts

(a) Maps \(T:\mathbb{R}^5\to\mathbb{R}^3\) with \(T=0\) on a fixed \(2\)-dimensional subspace \(U\) form a space of dimension \((5-2)\cdot3=9\).

(b) Does a linear \(T\) on \(\mathbb{R}^3\) exist with \(\ker T=\operatorname{range}T\)? No: it would need \(3=2\operatorname{rank}T\). On \(\mathbb{R}^4\) one exists, e.g. \(T(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=(x_3,x_4,0,0)\).

Example 7: block matrix

\(M=\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\0&B\end{pmatrix}\) with \(A\) \(3\times3\) invertible, \(B\) \(2\times2\) of rank \(1\) and any \(C\) (\(3\times2\)). Then \(\det M=\det A\cdot\det B=0\), and the rank is \(3+\operatorname{rank}B=4\) (eliminate \(C\) using the invertible \(A\)). So the nullity is \(5-4=1\). A numerical instance (rank computed by script) confirms this.

Common traps

  • Reading the matrix by rows: the columns of \([T]\) are the images \(T(b_j)\). The transpose is the usual wrong option.
  • Confusing \(P\) with \(P^{-1}\) in \([T]_{B'}=P^{-1}[T]_BP\). Check with \(\operatorname{tr}\) and \(\det\), which must not change.
  • Nullity is measured on the domain: for \(T:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^3\), \(\operatorname{nullity}=2-\operatorname{rank}\), never \(3-\operatorname{rank}\).
  • For a non-square map, rank is at most \(\min(m,n)\): a map \(\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^3\) cannot have rank \(3\) or be onto; \(\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^2\) cannot be injective.
  • To test one-one and onto for \(T:V_3\to V_3\) given only \(T(e_i)\), look for a relation among the three images; a relation means singular, so neither one-one nor onto.
  • Same characteristic polynomial does not imply similar.
  • \(\operatorname{tr}P=\operatorname{rank}P\) holds for idempotents only; and idempotent does not mean symmetric or orthogonal projection.
  • \(A^2=0\) does not give \(A=0\); \(AB=0\) does not give \(A=0\) or \(B=0\).
  • \(\operatorname{rank}(AB)\) is not \(\operatorname{rank}A\cdot\operatorname{rank}B\) and not \(\min\) in general; only an upper bound.
  • Direction of inclusion: \(\ker T\subseteq\ker T^2\), and \(\operatorname{range}T^2\subseteq\operatorname{range}T\).
  • A sum of nilpotents need not be nilpotent: \(E_{12}+E_{21}\) has eigenvalues \(\pm1\). If the two nilpotents commute, the sum (and the product) is nilpotent; non-commuting nilpotents such as \(E_{12},E_{23}\) may still have a nilpotent sum.

Previous-year questions

2025 Q46 (answer A)

Nullity of \(T:V_3(F)\to V_2(F)\), \(T(a_1,a_2,a_3)=(a_2,a_3)\): (A) \(1\) (B) \(2\) (C) \(3\) (D) \(0\).

Solution: the kernel is \(\{a_2=a_3=0\}=\{(a_1,0,0)\}\), dimension \(1\). Equivalently \(T\) is onto, rank \(2\), nullity \(3-2=1\). Official key: A.

2025 Q50 (answer A)

The number of non-singular linear transformations from \(\mathbb{R}^5\) to \(\mathbb{R}^3\) over \(\mathbb{R}\): (A) \(0\) (B) \(8\) (C) \(15\) (D) \(2\).

Solution: an invertible map needs equal dimensions, and even injectivity is impossible since \(5>3\) (nullity \(\ge2\)). So there are none. Official key: A.

2025 Q51 (answer D)

\(T:V_3\to V_3\) with \(T(e_1)=e_1-e_2\), \(T(e_2)=2e_2+e_3\), \(T(e_3)=e_1+e_2+e_3\). The map is: (A) one-one and onto (B) one-one and into (C) many-one and onto (D) neither one-one nor onto.

Solution: \(T(e_1)+T(e_2)=e_1+e_2+e_3=T(e_3)\), so \(T(e_1+e_2-e_3)=0\) with \(e_1+e_2-e_3\ne0\). The kernel is non-trivial, so \(T\) is not one-one, and (finite dimension, same space) not onto. The matrix has determinant \(0\). Official key: D.

2025 Q53 (answer D)

Rank and nullity of \(T:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^3\), \(T(x_1,x_2)=(x_1+x_2,\ x_1-x_2,\ x_2)\): (A) \(3,0\) (B) \(0,3\) (C) \(0,2\) (D) \(2,0\).

Solution: the range is spanned by \(T(e_1)=(1,1,0)\) and \(T(e_2)=(1,-1,1)\), independent, so rank \(2\); nullity \(2-2=0\). Rank \(3\) is impossible from a \(2\)-dimensional domain. Official key: D.

2026 Q80 (answer A, worked key)

\(T:\mathbb{R}^4\to\mathbb{R}^4\) satisfies \(T^2=T\) and \(\operatorname{rank}T=2\). Find the minimal polynomial, then the eigenvalues with multiplicities, then the trace and nullity. (A) trace \(2\), nullity \(2\) (B) trace \(4\), nullity \(0\) (C) trace \(2\), nullity \(1\) (D) trace \(3\), nullity \(1\).

Solution: \(T\ne0\) and \(T\ne I\) (rank \(2\)), so the minimal polynomial is \(x^2-x\). \(T\) is diagonalisable with eigenvalue \(1\) of multiplicity \(\operatorname{rank}T=2\) and eigenvalue \(0\) of multiplicity \(2\). Trace \(=2\), nullity \(=4-2=2\). This answer comes from the unofficial worked key.

2019 (unnumbered, reconstructed from a video) idempotent operator (answer D, all three)

\(A^2=A\), \(A\ne0\), \(A\ne I\). Statements: (i) \(\ker A\ne\{0\}\); (ii) \(V=\ker A\oplus\operatorname{range}A\); (iii) \(I+A\) is invertible. Which hold?

Solution: all three. (i) Since \(A\ne I\), some \(v\) has \(Av\ne v\), and \(v-Av\) is a non-zero vector of the kernel. (ii) is the projection decomposition above. (iii) the eigenvalues are \(0,1\), so \(I+A\) has eigenvalues \(1,2\); indeed \((I+A)(I-\tfrac12A)=I\). The on-screen mark in the video is ambiguous; this answer is from the mathematics, with the option letter taken from the re-typed copy.

2019 (unnumbered, reconstructed) nullity of a block matrix (answer A, nullity 0)

Nullity of \(\begin{pmatrix}A_n&C\\0&B_m\end{pmatrix}\) with \(A_n\), \(B_m\) invertible of sizes \(n\) and \(m\): \(0\).

Solution: the determinant is \(\det A\det B\ne0\), so the \((n+m)\times(n+m)\) matrix is invertible and its null space is \(\{0\}\). The presenter’s answer was not seen on screen; this is the mathematically forced answer.

Quick revision

  • Rank-nullity: \(\dim(\text{domain})=\operatorname{nullity}+\operatorname{rank}\).
  • \(\dim V>\dim W\Rightarrow\) never injective; \(\dim V<\dim W\Rightarrow\) never onto.
  • Equal dimensions: injective \(\iff\) onto \(\iff\) invertible; a dependency among the \(T(e_i)\) means singular.
  • Matrix of \(T\): column \(j\) is the coordinates of \(T(b_j)\).
  • New basis: \([T]_{B'}=P^{-1}[T]_BP\); trace, determinant, rank, characteristic and minimal polynomial survive.
  • Same characteristic polynomial does not mean similar.
  • \(\operatorname{rank}A+\operatorname{rank}B-n\le\operatorname{rank}AB\le\min\).
  • Idempotent: \(V=\ker\oplus\operatorname{range}\), eigenvalues \(0,1\), trace \(=\) rank, \(I+P\) invertible.
  • Nilpotent: \(N^n=0\), trace \(0\), \((I-N)^{-1}=I+N+\dots\); \(N^2=0\Rightarrow\operatorname{rank}\le n/2\).
  • Block triangular with invertible square diagonal blocks is invertible.
  • \(\dim L(V,W)=\dim V\dim W\).
