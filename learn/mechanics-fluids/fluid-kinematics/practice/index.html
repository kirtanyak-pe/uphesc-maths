  1. Question 1Easy
    In the Eulerian description of fluid motion one

    Why

    Eulerian variables are \((\mathbf r,t)\) with \(\mathbf r\) a fixed point in space. Following a labelled particle with independent variables \((a,b,c,t)\) is the Lagrangian description.
  2. Question 2Easy
    Streamlines, pathlines and streaklines coincide when the flow is

    Why

    In steady flow the velocity field does not change with time, so a particle follows a streamline, and every particle through a point follows the same curve. Irrotational, incompressible or uniform flows need not be steady, so the three curves can differ.
  3. Question 3Easy
    A flow has \(u=x+t\), \(v=y-t\), \(w=0\). The \(x\)-component of acceleration at \((1,2,0)\) at \(t=1\) is

    Why

    \(f_x=u_t+uu_x+vu_y=1+(x+t)\cdot1+0=1+2=3\). Option D is the local part alone, option A the convective part alone.
  4. Question 4Easy
    Which velocity field can represent an incompressible fluid?

    Why

    Need \(u_x+v_y=0\). \(u=x,v=y\) has divergence \(2\) (irrotational but not incompressible); \(u=x^2y,v=-xy^2\) has \(2xy-2xy=0\); \(u=xy,v=xy\) gives \(y+x\); \(u=x^2,v=y^2\) gives \(2x+2y\). Only the second has zero divergence.
  5. Question 5Medium
    The field \(u=2x+y,\ v=-3y+z,\ w=kz+x\) is a possible incompressible flow only if \(k\) equals

    Why

    \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=2-3+k=0\), so \(k=1\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    Steady incompressible purely radial flow in three dimensions, \(q_r=f(r)\) only, has \(f(r)\) proportional to

    Why

    Spherical continuity gives \(\dfrac1{r^2}\dfrac{d(r^2f)}{dr}=0\), so \(r^2f=\text{const}\). The \(1/r\) law belongs to a 2-D line source, \(1/r^3\) to the doublet speed.
  7. Question 7Medium
    Assertion (A): A velocity potential exists for every incompressible flow. Reason (R): For an incompressible fluid \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\).

    Why

    R is true. A is false: a velocity potential needs \(\nabla\times\mathbf q=0\) (irrotational flow). \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\) only guarantees that a stream function exists in 2-D, for example rigid rotation is incompressible but has no potential.
  8. Question 8Medium
    For \(\mathbf q=(-\Omega y,\Omega x,0)\) with \(\Omega=3\), the circulation around the circle \(x^2+y^2=4\) is

    Why

    \(\omega_z=2\Omega=6\) and the area is \(4\pi\), so \(\Gamma=24\pi\). Directly, \(q_\theta=\Omega a=6\) and \(\Gamma=6\cdot2\pi\cdot2=24\pi\). Option B uses \(\Omega\) instead of \(2\Omega\) for the vorticity.
  9. Question 9Medium
    The \(z\)-component of vorticity of \(u=y^2,\ v=x^2\) at the point \((2,1)\) is

    Why

    \(\omega_z=v_x-u_y=2x-2y=4-2=2\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    Consider: (i) For an inviscid barotropic fluid under conservative body forces, circulation about a closed material curve does not change with time. (ii) Circulation about any closed curve fixed in space does not change with time. (iii) Under the conditions of (i), a flow that is irrotational at one instant stays irrotational. (iv) Kelvin’s theorem holds unchanged for viscous fluids. Which are correct?

    Why

    Kelvin’s theorem concerns a curve moving with the fluid, so (ii) is false, and it requires inviscid flow, so (iv) is false. (iii) follows because zero circulation on every material curve means zero vorticity.
  11. Question 11Hard
    The field \(u=2x+4y,\ v=bx+cy\) is a possible incompressible flow and is irrotational. Then \(b+c\) equals

    Why

    Incompressible: \(2+c=0\), so \(c=-2\). Irrotational: \(v_x-u_y=b-4=0\), so \(b=4\). Hence \(b+c=2\).
  12. Question 12Hard
    For \(u=1,\ v=t\), dye is released continuously from the origin. At \(t=2\) the streakline passes through \(x=1\) at \(y=\)

    Why

    A particle released at time \(\tau\) is at \(x=2-\tau\), \(y=(4-\tau^2)/2\) at \(t=2\). For \(x=1\), \(\tau=1\) and \(y=3/2\). The pathline from \(t=0\) gives \(\tfrac12\) and the streamline \(y=2x\) gives \(2\).
