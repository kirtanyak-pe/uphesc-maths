Equations of motion and Bernoulli's equation

12 questions. Pick an answer to see if it is right and why.

  1. Question 1Easy
    Euler’s equation of motion for a perfect fluid with body force \(\mathbf F\) per unit mass is

    Why

    Net pressure force per unit volume is \(-\nabla p\); dividing by \(\rho\) gives acceleration \(-\nabla p/\rho\). The option \(\mathbf F-\nabla p\) drops \(1/\rho\); the option with \(\partial\mathbf q/\partial t\) alone omits the convective acceleration.
  2. Question 2Easy
    The result \(p/\rho+\tfrac12q^2+gz=\text{const}\) along a streamline does NOT require that the flow be

    Why

    Along one streamline Bernoulli holds for steady inviscid incompressible flow under gravity; irrotationality is needed only to make the constant the same on every streamline.
  3. Question 3Easy
    Water leaves a small hole \(5\) m below the free surface of a large tank. Taking \(g=9.8\) m/s\(^2\) the outflow speed is closest to

    Why

    Torricelli: \(v=\sqrt{2gh}=\sqrt{98}\approx9.9\). Option A is \(\sqrt{gh}\).
  4. Question 4Medium
    A horizontal pipe narrows with \(A_1=4A_2\). For water (\(\rho=1000\) kg/m\(^3\)) the pressure drop between the sections is \(15000\) Pa. The speed \(v_1\) in the wide section is

    Why

    \(v_2=4v_1\), so \(15000=\tfrac12(1000)(16-1)v_1^2\) and \(v_1^2=2\). Option A ignores \(v_1\) and the area ratio (\(\sqrt{30}\)).
  5. Question 5Medium
    For incompressible irrotational flow with \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\) and conservative force potential \(\Omega\), the pressure equation is

    Why

    Substituting \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\) in Lamb’s form gives \(\nabla(-\phi_t+\tfrac12q^2+\Omega+p/\rho)=0\). Option C is the form for \(\mathbf q=+\nabla\phi\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    Air of density \(1.2\) kg/m\(^3\) flows at \(20\) m/s onto a Pitot tube. The stagnation pressure exceeds the static pressure by

    Why

    \(\tfrac12\rho U^2=\tfrac12(1.2)(400)=240\) Pa. Option A is \(\rho U^2\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    Consider: (i) In steady rotational flow, \(p/\rho+\tfrac12q^2+\Omega\) is constant along a streamline but may differ between streamlines. (ii) In steady irrotational flow it has the same value throughout the fluid. (iii) In unsteady irrotational flow with \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\), \(-\phi_t+\tfrac12q^2+p/\rho+\Omega\) is a function of time only. (iv) For any unsteady flow, \(p/\rho+\tfrac12q^2+\Omega\) is constant along a streamline. Which are correct?

    Why

    (iv) is false: unsteady rotational flow has no such integral along a streamline, and in unsteady irrotational flow the \(\phi_t\) term and \(F(t)\) appear.
  8. Question 8Medium
    Liquid rotates rigidly with \(\Omega=4\) rad/s in a vertical cylinder. The free surface at radius \(R=0.1\) m is higher than at the axis by (take \(g=9.8\) m/s\(^2\))

    Why

    \(z=\Omega^2R^2/(2g)=16(0.01)/19.6=0.00816\) m \(=0.82\) cm. Option D forgets the factor 2.
  9. Question 9Medium
    Liquid of density \(\rho=1000\) kg/m\(^3\) rotates rigidly at \(\Omega=10\) rad/s in a cylinder of radius \(0.3\) m. The pressure on the bottom at the wall exceeds that on the axis by

    Why

    From \(\partial p/\partial R=\rho\Omega^2R\): \(\Delta p=\tfrac12\rho\Omega^2a^2=\tfrac12(1000)(100)(0.09)=4500\) Pa. Option B forgets the \(\tfrac12\).
  10. Question 10Hard
    A spherical cavity (zero internal pressure) in an infinite liquid of density \(\rho\) collapses from rest at radius \(R_0\) under far pressure \(p_\infty\). Its wall speed satisfies \(\dot R^2=\dfrac{2p_\infty}{3\rho}\left(\dfrac{R_0^3}{R^3}-1\right)\). At \(R=R_0/2\), \(\dot R^2=\)

    Why

    \(R_0^3/R^3=8\), so \(\dot R^2=\dfrac{2p_\infty}{3\rho}(8-1)=\dfrac{14p_\infty}{3\rho}\). Option D uses \(R_0/R=2\) without cubing; the expression was itself obtained from \(R\ddot R+\tfrac32\dot R^2=-p_\infty/\rho\).
  11. Question 11Medium
    A piston suddenly sets an incompressible liquid column of density \(1000\) kg/m\(^3\) and length \(2\) m, in a uniform tube open at the far end, moving at \(0.4\) m/s. The impulsive pressure at the piston is

    Why

    \(\rho\mathbf q=-\nabla\varpi\) gives a linear fall of \(\varpi\) by \(\rho Vl=1000(0.4)(2)=800\) along the tube, ending at \(0\) at the open end.
  12. Question 12Hard
    A liquid at rest is set in motion by impulsive pressures applied to its boundary. The motion produced

    Why

    Integrating Euler’s equation over the short interval gives \(\rho(\mathbf q-0)=-\nabla\varpi\), a gradient, so \(\nabla\times\mathbf q=0\). Finite body forces do not contribute in the limit.
