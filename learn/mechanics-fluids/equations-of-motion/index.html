Why it matters for the exam

No 2019, 2025 or 2026 question has been taken purely from Euler’s equation or Bernoulli’s theorem, but the syllabus names Euler’s equation and every later topic uses it: the pressure on a cylinder or sphere, the added-mass force and the Navier-Stokes equations all start here. Questions, when they come, are short: the correct form of Bernoulli’s equation, a pressure calculation from a velocity potential, or a Venturi or Torricelli number.

Core ideas

Perfect fluid and body forces

A perfect (inviscid) fluid exerts only normal stress: across any surface the force per unit area is \(-p\mathbf n\), and the pressure \(p\) at a point is the same in all directions (Pascal’s law). The body force per unit mass is \(\mathbf F\); it is conservative if \(\mathbf F=-\nabla\Omega\) (gravity: \(\Omega=gz\) with \(z\) upward). A fluid is barotropic if \(\rho\) depends on \(p\) alone; then \(P=\int dp/\rho\) is a function of position and \(\nabla P=\nabla p/\rho\). An incompressible fluid is barotropic with \(P=p/\rho\).

Euler’s equation of motion

Apply Newton’s second law to a material volume \(V\): \(\int_V\rho\dfrac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}dV=\int_V\rho\mathbf F\,dV-\oint_S p\mathbf n\,dS\). The surface integral equals \(\int_V\nabla p\,dV\), and \(V\) is arbitrary, so \(\boxed{\ \frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}=\frac{\partial\mathbf q}{\partial t}+(\mathbf q\cdot\nabla)\mathbf q=\mathbf F-\frac1\rho\nabla p\ }\) In Cartesian components, for example \(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}+u\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}+v\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial y}+w\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial z}=F_x-\dfrac1\rho\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial x}\).

Cylindrical components \((R,\theta,z)\) with velocity \((q_R,q_\theta,q_z)\): \(\frac{Dq_R}{Dt}-\frac{q_\theta^2}{R}=F_R-\frac1\rho\frac{\partial p}{\partial R},\qquad\frac{Dq_\theta}{Dt}+\frac{q_Rq_\theta}{R}=F_\theta-\frac1{\rho R}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\theta},\qquad\frac{Dq_z}{Dt}=F_z-\frac1\rho\frac{\partial p}{\partial z},\) where \(\dfrac{D}{Dt}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}+q_R\dfrac{\partial}{\partial R}+\dfrac{q_\theta}{R}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+q_z\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\). The \(-q_\theta^2/R\) term is the centripetal term.

Lamb’s form (use \((\mathbf q\cdot\nabla)\mathbf q=\nabla(\tfrac12q^2)-\mathbf q\times\boldsymbol\omega\) and \(\mathbf F=-\nabla\Omega\)): \(\frac{\partial\mathbf q}{\partial t}-\mathbf q\times\boldsymbol\omega=-\nabla\!\left(\frac{q^2}{2}+\Omega+P\right).\) This is the form used to derive every Bernoulli-type integral.

Fluid at rest. \(\nabla p=\rho\mathbf F\). Surfaces of constant pressure are perpendicular to \(\mathbf F\). For gravity and constant \(\rho\): \(p=p_0+\rho gh\) at depth \(h\).

The closed problem. For an incompressible fluid there are four unknowns (\(u,v,w,p\)) and four equations (three Euler components and \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\)), together with boundary conditions (normal velocity continuous; \(p\) given on a free surface).

Bernoulli’s equation, steady flow

Take the dot product of Lamb’s form with \(\mathbf q\) (steady flow). Since \(\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf q\times\boldsymbol\omega)=0\), \(\mathbf q\cdot\nabla H=0,\qquad H=\frac{q^2}{2}+\Omega+P .\) So along a streamline (and along a vortex line, since \(\boldsymbol\omega\cdot(\mathbf q\times\boldsymbol\omega)=0\) too) \(\frac{p}{\rho}+\frac12q^2+\Omega=\text{const}\qquad(\text{incompressible, conservative forces}).\) The constant may be different on different streamlines when the flow is rotational. If the flow is also irrotational, then \(\nabla H=0\) and \(H\) takes the same constant everywhere in the fluid.

Conditions to remember: inviscid, steady, conservative body force, barotropic. Irrotationality is not needed along one streamline; it is needed to get one constant for the whole flow. (Special case: if \(\mathbf q\parallel\boldsymbol\omega\), called Beltrami flow, \(H\) is also constant everywhere.)

Bernoulli’s equation, unsteady irrotational flow

If \(\boldsymbol\omega=0\) then \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\), and \(\partial\mathbf q/\partial t=-\nabla(\partial\phi/\partial t)\). Lamb’s form becomes \(\nabla\!\left(-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\frac{q^2}{2}+\Omega+P\right)=0,\) so, with the pressure equation written out, \(\boxed{\ -\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\frac{q^2}{2}+\frac p\rho+\Omega=F(t)\ }\) (\(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\), incompressible). The function \(F(t)\) depends on time only, and can be absorbed into \(\phi\) by \(\phi\to\phi-\int F\,dt\). With \(\mathbf q=+\nabla\phi\) the sign of \(\partial\phi/\partial t\) becomes plus: \(\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\dfrac{q^2}2+\dfrac p\rho+\Omega=F(t)\).

Situation Statement Constant
Steady, rotational \(p/\rho+\tfrac12q^2+\Omega\) constant along a streamline varies between streamlines
Steady, irrotational same expression constant one value throughout
Unsteady, irrotational \(-\phi_t+\tfrac12q^2+p/\rho+\Omega=F(t)\) function of \(t\) only
Unsteady, rotational no simple integral along a streamline not available

Pressure equation

The relation above, solved for the pressure, is called the pressure equation: \(\frac p\rho=F(t)-\Omega+\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}-\frac12q^2\qquad(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi).\) For incompressible flow, taking the divergence of Euler’s equation also gives a Poisson equation for pressure: \(\nabla^2p=-\rho\,\dfrac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}\dfrac{\partial u_j}{\partial x_i}\) when \(\nabla^2\Omega=0\) (as for gravity).

Standard applications of Bernoulli.

  • Torricelli. Outflow from a small hole at depth \(h\) below the free surface: \(v=\sqrt{2gh}\).
  • Pitot / stagnation pressure. At a stagnation point \(p_0=p+\tfrac12\rho U^2\) (take \(\Omega\) constant).
  • Venturi. Pipe areas \(A_1>A_2\): \(A_1v_1=A_2v_2\) and \(p_1-p_2=\tfrac12\rho(v_2^2-v_1^2)\).
  • Pulsating spherical bubble of radius \(R(t)\) in an infinite liquid with pressure \(p_\infty\) at infinity (Rayleigh): \(R\ddot R+\tfrac32\dot R^2=\frac{p_R-p_\infty}{\rho}.\) (Worked below.)

Impulsive motion

An impulsive force acts for a very short time \(\tau\) but produces a finite change of momentum. Integrate Euler’s equation over \(\tau\to0\): finite terms (body force, \(\tfrac12q^2\) terms) contribute nothing, while the pressure becomes infinite with finite integral \(\varpi=\lim\int_0^\tau p\,dt\), the impulsive pressure: \(\rho(\mathbf q_2-\mathbf q_1)=-\nabla\varpi .\) Properties:

  • Incompressible fluid: \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf q_2=\nabla\cdot\mathbf q_1=0\), so \(\nabla^2\varpi=0\).
  • Fluid initially at rest: \(\rho\mathbf q=-\nabla\varpi\), so the motion generated is irrotational, with \(\varpi=\rho\phi\) when \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\) (the arbitrary function of time is dropped). This is why impulsive motion of a body in fluid at rest is described by a velocity potential.
  • At a free surface (pressure constant) \(\varpi=0\). At a rigid boundary the normal velocity equals that of the boundary.
  • If the initial motion is irrotational the impulsive motion stays irrotational; if the fluid initially has vorticity, \(\nabla\times\mathbf q_2=\nabla\times\mathbf q_1\) (the impulse does not alter vorticity).
  • The impulse of the pressure on a body surface \(S\) is \(-\oint\varpi\,\mathbf n\,dS\) (\(\mathbf n\) outward from the body).

Formulas and results

  • Euler: \(D\mathbf q/Dt=\mathbf F-\nabla p/\rho\).
  • Lamb form: \(\partial\mathbf q/\partial t-\mathbf q\times\boldsymbol\omega=-\nabla(\tfrac12q^2+\Omega+P)\).
  • Hydrostatics: \(\nabla p=\rho\mathbf F\); \(p=p_0+\rho gh\).
  • Steady Bernoulli: \(\dfrac p\rho+\dfrac{q^2}{2}+\Omega=\text{const}\) along a streamline; one constant everywhere if irrotational.
  • Barotropic: replace \(p/\rho\) by \(\int dp/\rho\).
  • Unsteady irrotational: \(-\phi_t+\tfrac12q^2+p/\rho+\Omega=F(t)\) for \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\).
  • Torricelli \(v=\sqrt{2gh}\); stagnation pressure \(p+\tfrac12\rho U^2\).
  • Venturi: \(p_1-p_2=\tfrac12\rho(v_2^2-v_1^2)\).
  • Rayleigh bubble: \(R\ddot R+\tfrac32\dot R^2=(p_R-p_\infty)/\rho\); empty-cavity collapse: \(\dot R^2=\dfrac{2p_\infty}{3\rho}\left(\dfrac{R_0^3}{R^3}-1\right)\).
  • Impulsive: \(\rho(\mathbf q_2-\mathbf q_1)=-\nabla\varpi\); from rest, \(\varpi=\rho\phi\).

Worked examples

Example 1: Venturi meter

A horizontal pipe narrows so that \(A_1=4A_2\). Water (\(\rho=1000\) kg/m\(^3\)) shows \(p_1-p_2=15000\) Pa. Find \(v_1\).

Continuity: \(v_2=4v_1\). Bernoulli on the axis (same height): \(p_1-p_2=\tfrac12\rho(v_2^2-v_1^2)=\tfrac12\rho\cdot15v_1^2\). So \(15000=7500v_1^2\), \(v_1^2=2\), \(v_1=\sqrt2\approx1.41\) m/s (and \(v_2\approx5.66\) m/s).

Example 2: unsteady potential flow and its pressure

The incompressible flow \(\mathbf q=(kx,-ky,0)\) with \(k=k(t)\) has \(\phi=-\tfrac12k(x^2-y^2)\) for \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\). With no body force, find \(p\).

\(-\phi_t=\tfrac12k'(x^2-y^2)\) and \(q^2=k^2(x^2+y^2)\). So \(\frac p\rho=F(t)-\tfrac12k'(x^2-y^2)-\tfrac12k^2(x^2+y^2).\) Check with Euler’s \(x\)-equation: \(u_t+uu_x=k'x+k^2x\), and \(-p_x/\rho=k'x+k^2x\). For steady flow (\(k'=0\)) this reduces to \(p+\tfrac12\rho q^2=\text{const}\).

Example 3: Rayleigh equation for a bubble

A spherical bubble of radius \(R(t)\) in an infinite incompressible liquid. Radial flow: \(q=\dot RR^2/r^2\) from continuity (\(r^2q\) is independent of \(r\) and equals \(R^2\dot R\) at \(r=R\)). Then \(\phi=R^2\dot R/r\) (with \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\)). At fixed \(r\), \(\partial\phi/\partial t=(2R\dot R^2+R^2\ddot R)/r\).

Apply the unsteady Bernoulli equation at \(r=R\) and at \(r=\infty\) (where \(\phi_t=0,\ q=0\), pressure \(p_\infty\)): \(-(2\dot R^2+R\ddot R)+\tfrac12\dot R^2+\frac{p_R}\rho=\frac{p_\infty}\rho\ \Longrightarrow\ R\ddot R+\tfrac32\dot R^2=\frac{p_R-p_\infty}\rho .\)

Empty cavity collapse (\(p_R=0\)): multiply by \(2R^2\dot R\) to get \(\dfrac d{dt}(R^3\dot R^2)=-\dfrac{2p_\infty}{\rho}R^2\dot R\). Integrating from rest at \(R_0\): \(R^3\dot R^2=\dfrac{2p_\infty}{3\rho}(R_0^3-R^3)\). At \(R=R_0/2\): \(\dot R^2=\dfrac{2p_\infty}{3\rho}(8-1)=\dfrac{14p_\infty}{3\rho}\).

Example 4: rotating liquid in a cylinder

Liquid rotates rigidly with angular speed \(\Omega\) about the vertical axis: \(q_\theta=\Omega R\), \(q_R=q_z=0\), steady. Cylindrical Euler with \(F_z=-g\): \(-\Omega^2R=-\frac1\rho\frac{\partial p}{\partial R},\qquad0=-g-\frac1\rho\frac{\partial p}{\partial z}.\) So \(p=\rho\left(\tfrac12\Omega^2R^2-gz\right)+C\). The free surface \(p=\text{const}\) is the paraboloid \(z=\Omega^2R^2/(2g)\). For \(\Omega=4\) rad/s and \(R=0.1\) m the surface is higher at the wall than the axis by \(16\times0.01/(2\times9.8)\approx0.0082\) m. On the bottom, pressure at radius \(a\) exceeds that on the axis by \(\tfrac12\rho\Omega^2a^2\).

Example 5: impulsive motion of a cylinder

An infinite circular cylinder of radius \(a\) in liquid at rest is suddenly given velocity \(U\) along \(x\). The resulting potential (for \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\)) is \(\phi=Ua^2\cos\theta/r\) (this is a 2-D doublet, shown in the next topic). Then the impulsive pressure on the surface is \(\varpi=\rho\phi=\rho Ua\cos\theta\), and the impulse of the pressure on the cylinder per unit length in the \(x\)-direction is \(-\int_0^{2\pi}\varpi\cos\theta\,a\,d\theta=-\pi\rho a^2U .\) The fluid resists with impulse \(\pi\rho a^2U\): the cylinder behaves as if carrying an extra mass \(\pi\rho a^2\) per unit length, equal to the mass of liquid displaced (the added mass).

Example 6: column of liquid in a tube

A piston suddenly drives an incompressible liquid column of length \(l\) in a uniform tube, from rest to speed \(V\), the far end open to atmospheric pressure. Then \(\rho V=-\partial\varpi/\partial x\) with uniform \(\partial\varpi/\partial x\), so \(\varpi\) falls linearly by \(\rho Vl\) along the column. The piston must supply impulsive pressure \(\rho Vl\). For \(\rho=1000\), \(V=0.4\), \(l=2\) that is \(800\) Pa s.

Common traps

  • The sign in unsteady Bernoulli depends on the convention. With \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\) it is \(-\partial\phi/\partial t\); with \(\mathbf q=+\nabla\phi\) it is \(+\partial\phi/\partial t\). Read the problem’s convention.
  • Along a streamline does not require irrotational flow; the same constant everywhere does.
  • Unsteady flow along a streamline has no Bernoulli integral unless the flow is irrotational (then use the \(F(t)\) form).
  • Euler’s equation has \(-\nabla p/\rho\), not \(-\nabla p\). Forgetting \(1/\rho\) breaks dimensions.
  • Torricelli’s speed is \(\sqrt{2gh}\), not \(\sqrt{gh}\) and not \(gh/2\).
  • Stagnation pressure is \(\tfrac12\rho U^2\) above static, not \(\rho U^2\).
  • Rayleigh equation coefficients: \(\tfrac32\) on \(\dot R^2\), not \(2\) or \(1\); the \(\dot R^2\) coefficient arises as \(2-\tfrac12\).
  • Impulsive motion from rest is irrotational and \(\varpi=\rho\phi\); body force and \(q^2\) terms drop out because they are finite.
  • Centripetal term: in the radial equation it is \(-q_\theta^2/R\) on the left side (so the pressure gradient is \(+\rho\Omega^2R\) for rigid rotation).
