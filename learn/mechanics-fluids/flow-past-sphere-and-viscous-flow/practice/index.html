Flow past a sphere and viscous flow

15 questions. Pick an answer to see if it is right and why.

0 of 15 answered · 0 correct

  1. Question 1Easy
    The Stokes stream function is used for flows that are

    Why

    The Stokes stream function \(\psi(r,\theta)\) satisfies the axisymmetric incompressible continuity equation identically. It is not restricted to irrotational flow, and without axial symmetry it does not exist in this form.
  2. Question 2Easy
    The velocity potential of a three-dimensional doublet varies with distance \(r\) as

    Why

    A doublet is the limit of a source and a sink, \(\phi=\mu\cos\theta/r^2\). The \(1/r\) potential is the point source; the speed (not the potential) of the doublet falls as \(1/r^3\).
  3. Question 3Easy
    A sphere of radius \(1\) mm moves at \(0.01\) m/s through a liquid of viscosity \(0.1\) Pa s at low Reynolds number. The drag is closest to

    Why

    Stokes: \(6\pi\mu aU=6\pi(0.1)(10^{-3})(10^{-2})=1.89\times10^{-5}\) N. The other options correspond to \(2\pi\), \(4\pi\) and \(12\pi\).
  4. Question 4Easy
    The added mass of a sphere of radius \(a\) moving in a perfect liquid of density \(\rho\) is

    Why

    \(T=\tfrac\pi3\rho a^3U^2=\tfrac12M'U^2\) gives \(M'=\tfrac23\pi\rho a^3\), half the displaced mass \(\tfrac43\pi a^3\rho\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    Liquid of speed \(U\) at infinity streams past a fixed sphere. The maximum speed on the surface of the sphere is

    Why

    \(q_\theta=-\tfrac32U\sin\theta\) at \(r=a\), maximum at the equator. \(2U\) is the cylinder value.
  6. Question 6Medium
    In the same flow, how much lower is the pressure at the equator of the fixed sphere than \(p_\infty\)?

    Why

    \(p-p_\infty=\tfrac12\rho(U^2-\tfrac94U^2)=-\tfrac58\rho U^2\). The value \(\tfrac98\rho U^2\) is the difference between the stagnation point and the equator; \(\tfrac32\rho U^2\) is the cylinder.
  7. Question 7Medium
    A sphere of radius \(0.1\) m moves at \(2\) m/s through water (\(1000\) kg/m\(^3\)) that is otherwise at rest and perfect. The kinetic energy of the water is closest to

    Why

    \(T=\tfrac\pi3\rho a^3U^2=\tfrac\pi3(1000)(10^{-3})(4)=4.19\) J. The value \(8.38\) J is \(\tfrac12M_{\text{disp}}U^2\), which uses the full displaced mass instead of the added mass.
  8. Question 8Medium
    A sphere of density \(3\rho\) is released from rest in a large body of perfect liquid of density \(\rho\). Counting the added mass, its initial downward acceleration is

    Why

    \((\sigma+\tfrac12\rho)\dot U=(\sigma-\rho)g\) with \(\sigma=3\rho\) gives \(\dot U=\dfrac{2}{3.5}g=\dfrac47g\). The value \(\tfrac23g\) ignores the added mass.
  9. Question 9Medium
    A fluid of viscosity \(0.002\) Pa s is sheared between a fixed plate and a plate moving at \(3\) m/s, \(1\) mm apart (plane Couette flow, no pressure gradient). The shear stress is

    Why

    \(u=Uy/h\), so \(\tau=\mu U/h=0.002(3)/0.001=6\) Pa.
  10. Question 10Medium
    Water (\(\nu=10^{-6}\) m\(^2\)/s) flows at mean speed \(0.2\) m/s in a pipe of diameter \(0.05\) m. The Reynolds number based on diameter is

    Why

    \(\mathrm{Re}=\bar wD/\nu=0.2(0.05)/10^{-6}=10^4\). Using the radius would give \(5\times10^3\).
  11. Question 11Medium
    For an incompressible fluid of constant viscosity \(\mu\) and density \(\rho\), the Navier-Stokes equation in terms of force per unit volume is

    Why

    The viscous force per unit volume is \(+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf q\); \(\nu\nabla^2\mathbf q\) is per unit mass, so the second option has inconsistent units.
  12. Question 12Medium
    Viscous liquid (\(\mu=0.1\) Pa s) is driven between parallel fixed plates \(0.01\) m apart by a pressure gradient \(-dp/dx=800\) Pa/m. The mean velocity is

    Why

    \(\bar u=\dfrac{Gd^2}{12\mu}=\dfrac{800(10^{-4})}{1.2}=0.0667\) m/s. The maximum is \(0.1\) m/s; \(0.05\) would be half the maximum (the pipe ratio).
  13. Question 13Hard
    Oil of viscosity \(0.1\) Pa s flows steadily at \(10^{-5}\) m\(^3\)/s through a circular pipe of radius \(0.01\) m and length \(5\) m. The pressure drop is closest to

    Why

    Hagen-Poiseuille: \(\Delta p=\dfrac{8\mu LQ}{\pi a^4}=\dfrac{8(0.1)(5)(10^{-5})}{\pi(10^{-8})}=1273\) Pa.
  14. Question 14Hard
    Consider: (i) For incompressible irrotational flow the viscous term \(\mu\nabla^2\mathbf q\) vanishes. (ii) Potential flow past a solid body satisfies the no-slip condition. (iii) \(\mathrm{Re}=\rho UL/\mu\) is the ratio of inertial to viscous forces. (iv) In plane Poiseuille flow the convective acceleration is zero. Which are correct?

    Why

    (ii) is false: potential flow allows slip at the wall (only the normal velocity vanishes). The others are true: \(\nabla^2\mathbf q=-\nabla\times\boldsymbol\omega=0\); \(u=u(y)\) gives \(u\,\partial u/\partial x=0\).
  15. Question 15Hard
    The flow rate through a circular pipe under a fixed pressure gradient changes by what factor if the radius is halved (laminar flow)?

    Why

    \(Q=\pi Ga^4/(8\mu)\propto a^4\), so \(Q\) becomes \(\left(\tfrac12\right)^4=\tfrac1{16}\) of its value.
