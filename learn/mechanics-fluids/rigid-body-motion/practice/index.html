Rigid body motion and Euler's equations

  1. Question 1Easy
    Match the body (mass \(M\), radius \(R\) or length \(l\)) with its moment of inertia about the stated axis. List I: (a) thin rod about its centre, perpendicular; (b) uniform disc about its symmetry axis; (c) solid sphere about a diameter; (d) thin spherical shell about a diameter. List II: (1) \(\tfrac23MR^2\) (2) \(\tfrac12MR^2\) (3) \(\tfrac25MR^2\) (4) \(\tfrac1{12}Ml^2\).

    Why

    Rod \(Ml^2/12\); disc \(\tfrac12MR^2\); solid sphere \(\tfrac25MR^2\); shell \(\tfrac23MR^2\). The common slip is to swap the sphere and the shell.
  2. Question 2Easy
    A uniform thin rod of mass \(3\) kg and length \(2\) m rotates about an axis through one end, perpendicular to the rod. Its moment of inertia is (in kg m\(^2\))

    Why

    \(I=\tfrac13Ml^2=\tfrac13\cdot3\cdot4=4\). (About the centre it would be \(1\).)
  3. Question 3Easy
    In the Euler angles \((\varphi,\theta,\psi)\) of a rigid body, the angle \(\theta\) between the body symmetry axis and the space \(z\)-axis is called

    Why

    \(\varphi\) is precession, \(\theta\) nutation (tilt), \(\psi\) spin.
  4. Question 4Medium
    A uniform square lamina of mass \(M\) and side \(a\) rotates about one of its diagonals. The moment of inertia is

    Why

    Perpendicular axis through the centre: \(I_z=M(a^2+a^2)/12=Ma^2/6\). The two diagonals are equivalent by symmetry, so \(I_{\rm diag}=\tfrac12I_z=Ma^2/12\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    A uniform disc of mass \(2\) kg and radius \(0.5\) m rotates about an axis perpendicular to its plane through a point on its rim. Its moment of inertia is (in kg m\(^2\))

    Why

    Parallel axis: \(I=\tfrac12MR^2+MR^2=\tfrac32MR^2=\tfrac32\cdot2\cdot0.25=0.75\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    Euler’s equation of motion for the first principal axis of a rigid body about its fixed point (torque component \(N_1\)) is

    Why

    From \((d\mathbf L/dt)_{\rm body}+\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf L=\mathbf N\): \(I_1\dot\omega_1+(I_3-I_2)\omega_2\omega_3=N_1\), which is option A.
  7. Question 7Medium
    A torque-free symmetric top has \(I_1=I_2=4\) and \(I_3=6\) (kg m\(^2\)) and spins with \(\omega_3=8\) rad/s. In the body frame the angular velocity vector precesses about the symmetry axis at (rad/s)

    Why

    \(\Omega=\dfrac{I_3-I_1}{I_1}\omega_3=\dfrac{6-4}{4}\cdot8=4\) rad/s.
  8. Question 8Medium
    A torque-free rigid body has \(I_1<I_2<I_3\). Small perturbations of steady rotation about which principal axes remain bounded (stable)?

    Why

    Rotation about the axes of smallest and largest moment is stable; the intermediate axis is unstable, as the linearised Euler equations show.
  9. Question 9Medium
    Consider: (i) The inertia tensor of a rigid body about a point is symmetric. (ii) Its principal axes are mutually orthogonal. (iii) The angular momentum \(\mathbf L\) is always parallel to the angular velocity \(\boldsymbol\omega\). (iv) \(T=\tfrac12\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\mathbf L\) for rotation about a fixed point. Which are correct?

    Why

    (iii) is false: \(\mathbf L=\mathsf I\boldsymbol\omega\) is parallel to \(\boldsymbol\omega\) only along a principal axis (or for a spherical top).
  10. Question 10Medium
    A solid cylinder rolls without slipping down a \(30^\circ\) incline. Its acceleration of the centre is

    Why

    \(a=\dfrac{g\sin\alpha}{1+I/MR^2}=\dfrac{g/2}{1+1/2}=\dfrac g3\). Option \(5g/14\) is the solid sphere.
  11. Question 11Hard
    A uniform solid cube of mass \(6\) kg and side \(1\) m is rotated about its body diagonal through a corner. Its moment of inertia about this axis is (in kg m\(^2\))

    Why

    About the corner, \(I_{ii}=\tfrac23Ma^2=4\) and \(I_{ij}=-\tfrac14Ma^2=-1.5\). For \(\mathbf n=(1,1,1)/\sqrt3\): \(I=4+2(-1.5)=1=\tfrac16Ma^2\). The perpendicular principal moments are \(\tfrac{11}{12}Ma^2=5.5\).
  12. Question 12Hard
    A torque-free body with \(I_1=1\), \(I_2=2\), \(I_3=3\) (kg m\(^2\)) rotates at \(\Omega=2\) rad/s about the intermediate axis. A small perturbation grows as \(e^{\lambda t}\) with \(\lambda\) equal to

    Why

    \(\lambda=\Omega\sqrt{\dfrac{(I_3-I_2)(I_2-I_1)}{I_1I_3}}=2\sqrt{\dfrac13}=\dfrac2{\sqrt3}\approx1.155\) s\(^{-1}\).
  13. Question 13Hard
    Which of the following sets of numbers cannot be the principal moments of inertia of a rigid body?

    Why

    Principal moments satisfy \(I_i\le I_j+I_k\). The set \(1,1,3\) has \(3>1+1\). The set \(1,2,3\) has equality, which is possible for a planar lamina.
