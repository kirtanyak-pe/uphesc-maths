  1. Question 1Easy
    With the convention \([u,v]=\sum_j\left(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial q_j}\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial p_j}-\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial p_j}\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial q_j}\right)\), the fundamental Poisson brackets are

    Why

    \([q_i,p_j]=\sum_k\delta_{ik}\delta_{jk}=\delta_{ij}\); the brackets of two coordinates or two momenta vanish.
  2. Question 2Easy
    For \(L=3\dot x^2-5x^2\) the Hamiltonian is

    Why

    \(p=6\dot x\), \(H=p\dot x-L=3\dot x^2+5x^2=\dfrac{p^2}{12}+5x^2\).
  3. Question 3Easy
    For one degree of freedom, \([q,p^2]\) equals

    Why

    \([q,F]=\partial F/\partial p\), so \([q,p^2]=2p\).
  4. Question 4Medium
    For \(\mathbf L=\mathbf r\times\mathbf p\), the Poisson bracket \([L_x,L_y]\) equals

    Why

    \([L_i,L_j]=\epsilon_{ijk}L_k\), so \([L_x,L_y]=L_z\). Reversing the order gives \(-L_z\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    With \(L_z=xp_y-yp_x\), the bracket \([L_z,p_x]\) equals

    Why

    \([L_z,p_x]=\dfrac{\partial L_z}{\partial x}\cdot1=p_y\) (rule \([F,p_x]=\partial F/\partial x\)).
  6. Question 6Medium
    The Hamiltonian equals \(T+V\) when: (i) the constraints are time independent, (ii) the potential is independent of velocities, (iii) the Hamiltonian is conserved, (iv) \(T\) is a homogeneous quadratic function of the generalized velocities. Which are the correct conditions?

    Why

    (i) makes \(T\) homogeneous quadratic (so (iv) follows from (i)) and \(\sum p\dot q=2T\); with (ii), \(H=2T-(T-V)=T+V\). Conservation (iii) is neither needed nor sufficient (a rotating wire gives a conserved \(H\ne T+V\)).
  7. Question 7Medium

    Canonical iff \([Q,P]=1\). (D): \(2q\cdot\dfrac1{2q}=1\). The others give \(4\), \(-2\) and \(2\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    The generating function \(F_1(q,Q)=qQ\) produces the transformation

    Why

    \(p=\partial F_1/\partial q=Q\) and \(P=-\partial F_1/\partial Q=-q\). So \(Q=p\), \(P=-q\), with \([Q,P]=1\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    A canonical transformation has \(F_2(q,P)=qP+q^2\). At the point \(q=1\), \(p=5\) the new momentum \(P\) is

    Why

    \(p=\partial F_2/\partial q=P+2q\), so \(P=p-2q=5-2=3\). Also \(Q=\partial F_2/\partial P=q\).
  10. Question 10Hard
    Assertion (A): A function \(u(q,p)\) with no explicit time dependence is a constant of motion if \([u,H]=0\). Reason (R): For any \(u(q,p,t)\), \(\dfrac{du}{dt}=[u,H]+\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}\). Choose the correct option.

    Why

    Put \(\partial u/\partial t=0\) in (R): \(\dot u=[u,H]=0\). So (R) is the reason.
  11. Question 11Hard
    A bead of unit mass slides on a smooth horizontal wire rotating at \(\omega=1\) (units with \(m=1\)). Taking \(r\) as coordinate, the Hamiltonian evaluated at \(r=2\), \(p_r=3\) is

    Why

    \(L=\tfrac12(\dot r^2+r^2)\), \(p=\dot r\), \(H=\tfrac12p^2-\tfrac12r^2=4.5-2=2.5\). The value \(6.5\) is the kinetic energy \(T=\tfrac12(\dot r^2+\omega^2r^2)\), not \(H\).
  12. Question 12Medium
    If \(A\) and \(B\) are two constants of motion of a system with Hamiltonian \(H\), then by Poisson’s theorem \([A,B]\) is

    Why

    From the Jacobi identity, \(\dfrac{d}{dt}[A,B]=[[A,B],H]=0\) (time-independent \(A,B\)). The result may be a constant (even zero) or a new integral.
  13. Question 13Easy
    In Hamilton’s principle \(\delta\int_{t_1}^{t_2}L\,dt=0\), the varied paths are required to satisfy

    Why

    The end-point configurations are held fixed. Velocities at the ends are not fixed.
  14. Question 14Hard
    For \(H=\dfrac{p^2}{2m}+\tfrac12m\omega^2q^2\) with \(m=1\), \(\omega=2\) and energy \(E=6\), the action variable \(J=\dfrac1{2\pi}\oint p\,dq\) is

    Why

    The orbit is an ellipse of area \(\pi\sqrt{2mE}\sqrt{2E/m\omega^2}=2\pi E/\omega\). So \(J=E/\omega=3\) and \(H=\omega J\).
