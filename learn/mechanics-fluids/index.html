What this unit covers

Unit 8 has two halves, and each half is a small, closed body of theory.

Classical mechanics (topics 1 to 4). Constraints and generalised coordinates, Lagrange’s equations, the Hamiltonian and Hamilton’s canonical equations, Hamilton’s variational principle, Poisson brackets, canonical transformations (generating functions, symplectic condition, action-angle variables), Euler’s equations for a rigid body, and the theory of small oscillations (normal modes, normal coordinates, coupled systems, the linear triatomic molecule).

Fluid dynamics (topics 5 to 8). The equation of continuity, streamlines and vorticity, Euler’s equation and Bernoulli’s equation for a perfect fluid, two-dimensional irrotational motion with the complex potential, sources, sinks and doublets, flow past a cylinder, motion of a sphere in a perfect fluid, and the Navier-Stokes equations with Stokes flow past a sphere.

Weight in the exam: 7 of the 70 mathematics questions in 2025 (Q67 to Q73) and 7 in 2026. No unit-8 question was recovered from the partial 2019 paper (35 of 70 questions recovered), so its 2019 weight is recorded as 0, not as “never asked”. Seven marks per paper makes this a small unit that is cheap to master: the whole syllabus is a short list of formulas and about a dozen standard problems.

Split of the 14 past questions: Hamiltonian side 5 (Poisson bracket, canonical transformation, action-angle variables, Hamiltonian from a Lagrangian), small oscillations 3, Lagrangian and DOF 1, rigid body 1, fluids 4 (continuity twice, velocity potential versus stream function, viscous drag on a sphere).

Patterns that repeat:

  • Poisson brackets and canonical transformations appear in both years. Know the algebra cold: antisymmetry, Jacobi identity, \([q,p]=1\), \([A,H]=\dot A\), the angular-momentum brackets.
  • Small oscillations is asked every year, once as a computation (find the modes) and once as a concept (what makes modes orthogonal, what a coupling term means).
  • Fluid questions test definitions, not integrals: continuity, incompressibility, the Cauchy-Riemann link between \(\phi\) and \(\psi\).
  • Options differ by one word or one sign. Read the convention given in the question (for example \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\) versus \(+\nabla\phi\)).

High-yield list

Learn these first. Each has appeared in the exam or is the standard form of an item that has.

  1. DOF counting. \(3N-k\). Rigid body: 6 free, 3 with a point fixed, 1 with a fixed axis.
  2. Hamiltonian from Lagrangian. Compute \(p=\partial L/\partial\dot q\), invert for \(\dot q\), then \(H=p\dot q-L\). Do not just add \(T+V\) without checking that \(T\) is expressed in terms of \(p\).
  3. Poisson bracket algebra. Antisymmetry, Jacobi, \([q,p]=1\), \([A,H]=\dot A\) when \(A\) has no explicit time dependence, \([L_i,L_j]=\varepsilon_{ijk}L_k\). Know which “identities” are false (for example \([u,[v,w]]=[[u,v],w]\)).
  4. Canonical transformations. The test \([Q,P]=1\), the symplectic condition, the four generating functions with their sign pattern, invariance of Poisson brackets and phase-space volume.
  5. Action-angle variables. Requires bounded periodic (libration or rotation) motion with \(H\) independent of time. \(J=\oint p\,dq\).
  6. Normal modes. Set up \(T\) and \(V\) matrices and solve \(\det(V-\omega^2T)=0\). Orthogonality is with respect to the kinetic matrix \(T\).
  7. Standard oscillator systems. Equal double pendulum \((2\mp\sqrt2)g/l\); coupled pendulums \(g/l\) and \(g/l+2k/m\); XYX molecule \(0\), \(k/m\), \(\frac km(1+\frac{2m}M)\).
  8. Euler’s equations and the intermediate axis. Rotation about the intermediate axis is unstable, about the largest and smallest axes stable. Symmetric top: \(\Omega=(I_3-I_1)\omega_3/I_1\).
  9. Continuity. General form, incompressible form \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\), the spherically symmetric form.
  10. \(\phi\) and \(\psi\). Cauchy-Riemann relations, orthogonality of the two families, \(w=\phi+i\psi\) analytic, \(\nabla^2\phi=\nabla^2\psi=0\).
  11. Bernoulli’s equation and when it holds (steady along a streamline; everywhere if irrotational).
  12. Source, sink, doublet, cylinder and sphere potentials. Cylinder \(-U(z+a^2/z)\); sphere \(-U(r+a^3/2r^2)\cos\theta\); d’Alembert’s paradox for both.
  13. Navier-Stokes and Stokes drag. Form of the equation, role of \(Re\), drag \(6\pi\mu aU\), and the qualitative fact that viscosity produces a drag the perfect-fluid solution lacks.
