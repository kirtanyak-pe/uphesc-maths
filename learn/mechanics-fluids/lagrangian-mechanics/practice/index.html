  1. Question 1Easy
    Two particles in space are joined by a rigid massless rod. The number of degrees of freedom of the system is

    Why

    Two free particles have \(6\) coordinates and the rod gives one holonomic constraint \(\lvert\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2\rvert=l\): \(6-1=5\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    Which of the following constraints is non-holonomic?

    Why

    An inequality constraint cannot be written as \(f(\mathbf r,t)=0\), so it is non-holonomic. The other three are equations between coordinates.
  3. Question 3Easy
    A bead slides on a smooth straight wire that rotates with constant angular velocity \(\omega\) about a vertical axis. The constraint is

    Why

    The constraint \(\varphi=\omega t\) is an equation between coordinates and time: holonomic. It contains \(t\) explicitly: rheonomic.
  4. Question 4Medium
    Masses \(7\) kg and \(5\) kg hang over a light frictionless pulley (Atwood machine). The acceleration of the masses is

    Why

    \(a=\dfrac{m_1-m_2}{m_1+m_2}g=\dfrac{2}{12}g=\dfrac g6\). Option \(g/3\) uses \(m_1-m_2\) over \(m_1\) only.
  5. Question 5Medium
    For \(L=\tfrac12m(\dot r^2+r^2\dot\theta^2)-V(r)\) the conserved quantity associated with the cyclic coordinate \(\theta\) is

    Why

    \(p_\theta=\partial L/\partial\dot\theta=mr^2\dot\theta\) is the angular momentum. Since \(\partial L/\partial\theta=0\), \(\dot p_\theta=0\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    A bead on a smooth vertical hoop of radius \(R\) rotates with the hoop about a vertical diameter at \(\omega\), where \(\omega^2=\dfrac{4g}{3R}\). With \(\theta\) measured from the downward vertical, the off-axis equilibrium has \(\cos\theta_0\) equal to

    Why

    \(\ddot\theta=\sin\theta(\omega^2\cos\theta-g/R)\), so \(\cos\theta_0=\dfrac{g}{R\omega^2}=\dfrac34\). The inverse \(\tfrac43\) is impossible (\(>1\)).
  7. Question 7Medium
    Consider: (i) For a system of \(n\) degrees of freedom, Lagrange’s equations are \(n\) second-order equations. (ii) For ideal constraints the constraint forces do not appear in them. (iii) The Lagrangian of a system is unique. (iv) \(L\) and \(L+dF(q,t)/dt\) lead to the same equations of motion. Which are correct?

    Why

    (iii) is false: \(L\) is not unique, since \(L\to L+dF/dt\) (and \(L\to cL\)) leave the equations unchanged. The rest are true.
  8. Question 8Medium
    A particle of mass \(2\) moves in the potential \(V=-4/r\) with angular momentum \(\ell=4\) (consistent units). The radius of the circular orbit is

    Why

    Circular orbit: \(\ell^2/(mr^3)=k/r^2\), so \(r_0=\ell^2/(mk)=16/(2\cdot4)=2\).
  9. Question 9Hard
    Assertion (A): Adding a total time derivative \(dF(q,t)/dt\) to the Lagrangian does not change the equations of motion. Reason (R): The action changes only by \(F(q_2,t_2)-F(q_1,t_1)\), which has zero variation when the end points are fixed. Choose the correct option.

    Why

    Fixed end points give \(\delta F=0\) at both ends, so \(\delta S\) is unchanged, which is why the Euler-Lagrange equations do not change.
  10. Question 10Hard
    A bead of mass \(m\) moves on a smooth horizontal straight wire rotating at constant \(\omega\) about a vertical axis through one point of the wire, \(r\) measured from that point. The conserved energy function \(h=\dot r\,\partial L/\partial\dot r-L\) is

    Why

    \(L=\tfrac12m(\dot r^2+r^2\omega^2)\), so \(h=m\dot r^2-L=\tfrac12m(\dot r^2-\omega^2r^2)\). It is conserved (\(\partial L/\partial t=0\)) but is not \(T\): the wire does work.
  11. Question 11Medium
    A particle of charge \(q\) and mass \(m\) moves along \(x\) in a vector potential \(A(x,t)\) with \(L=\tfrac12m\dot x^2+qA\dot x\). The canonical momentum is

    Why

    \(p=\partial L/\partial\dot x=m\dot x+qA\). Generalized momentum differs from \(m\dot x\) when the potential depends on velocity.
  12. Question 12Hard
    Masses \(4\) kg and \(2\) kg hang over a pulley that is a uniform disc of mass \(4\) kg (no slipping of the string). The acceleration of the masses is

    Why

    Pulley disc: \(I/r^2=\tfrac12M=2\) kg. \(a=\dfrac{(m_1-m_2)g}{m_1+m_2+I/r^2}=\dfrac{2g}{8}=\dfrac g4\). \(g/3\) is the answer for a massless pulley.
  13. Question 13Easy
    A spherical pendulum (bob on a rigid rod of fixed length, free to swing in any direction) has how many degrees of freedom?

    Why

    Three coordinates minus one constraint \(\lvert\mathbf r\rvert=l\) gives \(2\) (the polar angles \(\theta,\varphi\)).
