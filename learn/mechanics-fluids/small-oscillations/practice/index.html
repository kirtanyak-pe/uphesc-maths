Small oscillations and normal modes

  1. Question 1Easy
    For small oscillations about equilibrium with kinetic matrix \(T\) and potential matrix \(V\) (both symmetric), the normal-mode frequencies are the roots of

    Why

    \(T\ddot\eta+V\eta=0\) with \(\eta=\mathbf a e^{i\omega t}\) gives \((V-\omega^2T)\mathbf a=0\). Option A gives \(1/\omega^2\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    A configuration \(q^0\) of a conservative system with \(n\) degrees of freedom is a stable equilibrium (small oscillations) when

    Why

    Equilibrium needs zero generalized forces; stability needs all Hessian eigenvalues positive (a minimum of \(V\)). Positive diagonal entries alone are not enough (a saddle can have them).
  3. Question 3Easy
    Two equal masses \(m=1\) are joined to walls by springs \(k=3\) and to each other by a spring \(\kappa=2\). The larger of the two normal-mode \(\omega^2\) values is

    Why

    \(\omega_1^2=k/m=3\) (in phase) and \(\omega_2^2=(k+2\kappa)/m=7\) (out of phase).
  4. Question 4Medium
    The potential \(V=x^2+4xy+y^2\) has an equilibrium at the origin. This equilibrium is

    Why

    Hessian \(\begin{pmatrix}2&4\\4&2\end{pmatrix}\) has eigenvalues \(6\) and \(-2\). Along \(y=-x\), \(V=-2x^2<0\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    A system has \(T=\tfrac12(2\dot q_1^2+\dot q_2^2)\) and \(V=\tfrac12(3q_1^2-2q_1q_2+2q_2^2)\). The larger squared normal frequency is

    Why

    \(V=\begin{pmatrix}3&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}\), \(T=\mathrm{diag}(2,1)\). \(\det(V-\lambda T)=(3-2\lambda)(2-\lambda)-1=2\lambda^2-7\lambda+5=0\), so \(\lambda=1,\ \tfrac52\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    For a planar double pendulum with equal masses and equal lengths, the ratio of the larger to the smaller squared normal frequency is

    Why

    \(\omega^2=(2\mp\sqrt2)g/l\). Ratio \(\dfrac{2+\sqrt2}{2-\sqrt2}=\dfrac{(2+\sqrt2)^2}{2}=3+2\sqrt2\approx5.83\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    If \(\zeta_k\) are the normal coordinates of a stable system with all \(\omega_k^2>0\), then the kinetic and potential energies are

    Why

    With \(A^TTA=1\) and \(A^TVA=\mathrm{diag}(\omega_k^2)\), each \(\zeta_k\) obeys \(\ddot\zeta_k+\omega_k^2\zeta_k=0\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    A linear symmetric molecule \(X\,Y\,X\) has masses \(m=1\), \(M=2\) and two identical springs \(k=4\) (longitudinal motion only). The \(\omega^2\) of the antisymmetric stretching mode is

    Why

    Mode \((1,-2m/M,1)=(1,-1,1)\): \(\omega^2=\dfrac km\left(1+\dfrac{2m}M\right)=4\cdot2=8\). The symmetric stretch has \(\omega^2=k/m=4\), and the translation has \(0\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    For a stable system with a symmetric positive-definite kinetic matrix \(T\): (i) all \(\omega_k^2\) are real, (ii) a zero root \(\omega^2=0\) corresponds to a free translation or rotation mode, (iii) eigenvectors of different frequencies satisfy \(\mathbf a_k^T\mathbf a_l=0\) for the ordinary dot product in all cases, (iv) eigenvectors satisfy \(\mathbf a_k^TT\mathbf a_l=0\). Which are correct?

    Why

    (iii) is false: orthogonality holds with respect to \(T\), not the dot product, unless \(T\) is proportional to the identity.
  10. Question 10Hard
    Two identical pendulums with \(g/l=4\) s\(^{-2}\) are coupled by a spring with \(k/m=0.5\) s\(^{-2}\). Pendulum 1 is released from \(\theta_0\) with pendulum 2 at rest. The time to the first complete transfer of oscillation to pendulum 2 is (to \(0.1\) s)

    Why

    \(\omega_1=2\), \(\omega_2=\sqrt{4+2(0.5)}=\sqrt5\). \(\theta_1\propto\cos\frac{\Delta t}2\), so the first transfer takes \(t=\pi/\Delta=\pi/(\sqrt5-2)\approx13.3\) s. The full beat period is twice this.
  11. Question 11Medium
    The number of vibrational normal modes of the water molecule (non-linear, three atoms) is

    Why

    Non-linear \(N\)-atom molecule: \(3N-6=3\) vibrational modes. (A linear triatomic would have \(3N-5=4\).)
  12. Question 12Hard
    A system has \(L=\tfrac12(\dot x^2+\dot y^2)+\tfrac12\dot x\dot y-\tfrac32(x^2+y^2)\). The squared frequency of the mode with \(x=-y\) is

    Why

    \(T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&1\end{pmatrix}\), \(V=3\cdot\mathbf 1\). \(\det(V-\lambda T)=(3-\lambda)^2-\lambda^2/4=0\) gives \(\lambda=2\) (\(x=y\)) and \(\lambda=6\) (\(x=-y\)).
  13. Question 13Hard
    Assertion (A): For a stable equilibrium all squared normal frequencies are real and positive. Reason (R): Both the kinetic matrix \(T\) and the potential matrix \(V\) are symmetric and positive definite. Choose the correct option.

    Why

    \(\omega_k^2=\mathbf a_k^TV\mathbf a_k/\mathbf a_k^TT\mathbf a_k\), a ratio of positive numbers when both matrices are positive definite.
  14. Question 14Medium
    A particle moves in the central potential \(V=\tfrac12kr^2\) (isotropic oscillator) on a nearly circular orbit. The ratio of the frequency of radial oscillation to the orbital angular frequency is

    Why

    Circular orbit at \(r_0\): \(V_{\rm eff}'(r_0)=0\) gives \(\ell^2/(mr_0^4)=k\). Then \(V_{\rm eff}''=3\ell^2/(mr_0^4)+k=4k\), so \(\omega_r^2=4k/m\), while \(\dot\theta^2=\ell^2/(m^2r_0^4)=k/m\). Ratio \(=2\) (closed orbits are ellipses centred on the origin).
