Why it matters for the exam

Three questions in two years: a coupled-oscillator Lagrangian with several statements about momenta and normal modes (2025-Q71), orthogonality of eigenvectors with respect to the mass matrix (2026-Q46), and the meaning of a zero eigenfrequency (2026-Q67). The method is the same each time: write \(T\) and \(V\) as quadratic forms, solve \(\det(V-\omega^2T)=0\), and interpret the eigenvectors.

Core ideas

Equilibrium and stability

For a scleronomic system with potential \(V(q_1,\dots,q_n)\), a configuration \(q^0\) is an equilibrium if all generalized forces vanish:

\(\left.\frac{\partial V}{\partial q_j}\right|_{q^0}=0\quad(j=1,\dots,n).\)

Put \(\eta_j=q_j-q_j^0\) and expand \(V\) to second order:

\(V\approx V(q^0)+\tfrac12\sum_{jk}V_{jk}\,\eta_j\eta_k,\qquad V_{jk}=\left.\frac{\partial^2V}{\partial q_j\partial q_k}\right|_{q^0}.\)

The constant is dropped. Stability:

Hessian \(V_{jk}\) Equilibrium
Positive definite (all eigenvalues \(>0\)) stable (a minimum of \(V\))
At least one negative eigenvalue unstable (maximum or saddle)
Positive semi-definite, some zero eigenvalues neutral in those directions: higher-order terms or a free mode decide

For one degree of freedom: \(V'(q_0)=0\), stable if \(V''(q_0)>0\), unstable if \(V''(q_0)<0\). If \(V''=0\), look at the first non-zero derivative: even order and positive means stable, odd order means unstable (Lagrange-Dirichlet gives stability when \(V\) has a strict minimum).

Quadratic \(T\)

For \(\mathbf r_i=\mathbf r_i(q)\) (scleronomic), \(T=\tfrac12\sum m_{jk}(q)\dot q_j\dot q_k\). At the equilibrium, evaluate the coefficients at \(q^0\) and call them \(T_{jk}\):

\(T\approx\tfrac12\sum_{jk}T_{jk}\,\dot\eta_j\dot\eta_k.\)

The matrix \(T=(T_{jk})\) is symmetric and positive definite (the mass or kinetic matrix); \(V=(V_{jk})\) is symmetric (the stiffness or potential matrix). The Lagrangian is \(L=\tfrac12\dot\eta^TT\dot\eta-\tfrac12\eta^TV\eta\). Lagrange’s equations give

\(\boxed{T\ddot\eta+V\eta=0.}\)

If \(T\) is not diagonal there is kinetic coupling and if \(V\) is not diagonal potential coupling. A system can have either or both.

Normal modes and frequencies

Try \(\eta=\mathbf a\,e^{i\omega t}\):

\((V-\omega^2T)\,\mathbf a=0.\)

A non-trivial amplitude vector exists only if

\(\boxed{\det(V-\omega^2T)=0.}\)

This secular (characteristic) equation is a polynomial of degree \(n\) in \(\omega^2\). Its roots \(\omega_1^2,\dots,\omega_n^2\) are the squared normal-mode frequencies. For each root, the eigenvector \(\mathbf a_k\) solves \((V-\omega_k^2T)\mathbf a_k=0\). A normal mode is a motion in which every coordinate oscillates at the same frequency \(\omega_k\) with a fixed amplitude ratio \(\mathbf a_k\).

Properties (hypotheses matter):

  1. Since \(T\) is positive definite and \(V\) symmetric, all \(\omega_k^2\) are real.
  2. If \(V\) is positive definite, all \(\omega_k^2>0\): stable oscillation.
  3. If some \(\omega_k^2<0\), the mode grows exponentially: unstable equilibrium.
  4. If \(\omega_k^2=0\), the mode has no restoring force: a free-motion mode. Typical cause: a symmetry such as translation or rotation of the whole system. The coordinate changes linearly in time, \(\zeta=c_1+c_2t\).
  5. Orthogonality with respect to \(T\). For distinct frequencies \(\omega_k^2\ne\omega_l^2\),

\(\mathbf a_k^T\,T\,\mathbf a_l=0\quad(k\ne l),\qquad \mathbf a_k^T\,V\,\mathbf a_l=0.\)

Proof: \(V\mathbf a_k=\omega_k^2T\mathbf a_k\) and \(V\mathbf a_l=\omega_l^2T\mathbf a_l\); take the inner products with \(\mathbf a_l^T\) and \(\mathbf a_k^T\) and subtract using symmetry: \((\omega_k^2-\omega_l^2)\mathbf a_l^TT\mathbf a_k=0\). In general the eigenvectors are not orthogonal under the ordinary dot product; they are dot-orthogonal only in special cases, such as when \(T\) and \(V\) commute (for instance \(T\) a multiple of the identity). They are always \(T\)-orthogonal. The matrix that supplies orthogonality is the mass (kinetic) matrix (2026-Q46). For degenerate frequencies one can still choose \(T\)-orthogonal vectors.

  1. Normalise: \(\mathbf a_k^TT\mathbf a_k=1\).

Normal coordinates

Form the modal matrix \(A=(\mathbf a_1|\dots|\mathbf a_n)\) with \(\mathbf a_k^TT\mathbf a_l=\delta_{kl}\). Then \(A^TTA=1\) and \(A^TVA=\mathrm{diag}(\omega_k^2)\). Define normal coordinates by \(\eta=A\zeta\). Then

\(T=\tfrac12\sum_k\dot\zeta_k^2,\qquad V=\tfrac12\sum_k\omega_k^2\zeta_k^2,\qquad\ddot\zeta_k+\omega_k^2\zeta_k=0.\)

The system is a set of \(n\) independent harmonic oscillators:

\(\zeta_k=C_k\cos(\omega_kt+\delta_k),\qquad\eta=\sum_k\zeta_k(t)\,\mathbf a_k.\)

The energy of each normal mode is separately conserved. The \(2n\) constants \(C_k,\delta_k\) are fixed by the initial conditions. With one mode excited, all coordinates move in step; with two nearly equal frequencies excited, one sees beats at the difference frequency.

Standard solution routine:

  1. Find \(q^0\) from \(\partial V/\partial q=0\).
  2. Write \(T\) and \(V\) to second order; read off the matrices.
  3. Solve \(\det(V-\omega^2T)=0\).
  4. Substitute each \(\omega^2\) to get \(\mathbf a_k\) (amplitude ratio only needs one row).
  5. Describe each mode (in phase, out of phase, translation).

Standard systems

Two equal masses and three springs (wall, \(k\), mass \(m\), spring \(\kappa\), mass \(m\), \(k\), wall). With \(x_1,x_2\) the displacements,

\(T=\tfrac12m(\dot x_1^2+\dot x_2^2),\qquad V=\tfrac12kx_1^2+\tfrac12\kappa(x_1-x_2)^2+\tfrac12kx_2^2.\)

\(V=\begin{pmatrix}k+\kappa&-\kappa\\-\kappa&k+\kappa\end{pmatrix}\), \(T=m\mathbf 1\).

\(\omega_1^2=\frac km\ \ (x_1=x_2,\ \text{in phase; middle spring not stretched}),\qquad\omega_2^2=\frac{k+2\kappa}m\ \ (x_1=-x_2,\ \text{out of phase}).\)

The normal coordinates are \(\zeta_1\propto x_1+x_2\) and \(\zeta_2\propto x_1-x_2\). This is the model behind the question 2025-Q71.

Two equal coupled pendulums (length \(l\), mass \(m\), coupling spring \(k\)): \(\theta_1,\theta_2\), \(T=\tfrac12ml^2(\dot\theta_1^2+\dot\theta_2^2)\), \(V=\tfrac12mgl(\theta_1^2+\theta_2^2)+\tfrac12kl^2(\theta_1-\theta_2)^2\) (horizontal spring attached at the bobs, small angles).

\(\omega_1^2=\frac gl\ (\theta_1=\theta_2),\qquad\omega_2^2=\frac gl+\frac{2k}m\ (\theta_1=-\theta_2).\)

For weak coupling the energy passes back and forth with beat frequency \(\omega_2-\omega_1\approx k/(m\omega_0)\).

Double pendulum (masses \(m_1,m_2\), lengths \(l_1,l_2\), angles from the downward vertical):

\(T=\tfrac12(m_1+m_2)l_1^2\dot\theta_1^2+\tfrac12m_2l_2^2\dot\theta_2^2+m_2l_1l_2\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2),\) \(V=-(m_1+m_2)gl_1\cos\theta_1-m_2gl_2\cos\theta_2.\)

Equilibrium \(\theta_1=\theta_2=0\) (stable). To second order \(\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\to1\), \(\cos\theta\to1-\theta^2/2\):

\(T=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}\dot\theta_1&\dot\theta_2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}(m_1+m_2)l_1^2&m_2l_1l_2\\m_2l_1l_2&m_2l_2^2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\dot\theta_1\\\dot\theta_2\end{pmatrix},\qquad V=\tfrac12\left[(m_1+m_2)gl_1\theta_1^2+m_2gl_2\theta_2^2\right].\)

For \(m_1=m_2=m\) and \(l_1=l_2=l\), with \(\lambda=\omega^2l/g\):

\(\det\begin{pmatrix}2-2\lambda&-\lambda\\-\lambda&1-\lambda\end{pmatrix}=0\ \Rightarrow\ \lambda^2-4\lambda+2=0\ \Rightarrow\ \omega^2=(2\mp\sqrt2)\frac gl.\)

Slow mode, \(\omega^2=(2-\sqrt2)g/l\), amplitudes \(\theta_2/\theta_1=+\sqrt2\) (in phase). Fast mode, \(\omega^2=(2+\sqrt2)g/l\), \(\theta_2/\theta_1=-\sqrt2\) (out of phase). The equilibrium \(\theta_1=\theta_2=\pi\) (both up) would have negative \(\omega^2\): unstable.

Linear symmetric triatomic molecule \(X\,Y\,X\) (masses \(m,M,m\) on a line, equal springs \(k\), longitudinal motion only; this models CO\(_2\) stretching). With \(x_1,x_2,x_3\) the displacements:

\(m\ddot x_1=k(x_2-x_1),\quad M\ddot x_2=-k(x_2-x_1)+k(x_3-x_2),\quad m\ddot x_3=-k(x_3-x_2).\)

There are three modes:

Mode Amplitudes \((x_1:x_2:x_3)\) \(\omega^2\)
Translation of the whole molecule \(1:1:1\) \(0\)
Symmetric stretch (central atom at rest) \(1:0:-1\) \(\dfrac km\)
Antisymmetric stretch \(1:-\dfrac{2m}{M}:1\) \(\dfrac km\left(1+\dfrac{2m}M\right)=\dfrac{k(M+2m)}{mM}\)

The zero-frequency mode is the free translation, not a vibration. Real molecules also bend. For \(N\) atoms, the number of vibrational modes is \(3N-6\) (non-linear) or \(3N-5\) (linear molecule): a linear triatomic has 4 (two stretches and a doubly degenerate bend).

One degree of freedom has \(\omega^2=V''(q_0)/T_{11}\). For example, radial oscillation about a circular orbit of a central force: \(\omega_r^2=V_{\rm eff}''(r_0)/m\). For \(V=-k/r\), \(\omega_r=\omega_\theta\) (closed orbits); for \(V=\tfrac12kr^2\), \(\omega_r=2\omega_\theta\).

Physical meaning of the special cases

  • \(\omega^2>0\): oscillation, restoring force.
  • \(\omega^2=0\): free motion (zero restoring force). It signals a symmetry (translation, rotation) of the system, and is not instability, infinite stiffness or absence of motion.
  • \(\omega^2<0\): exponential growth, instability.
  • Degenerate roots: two modes share one frequency, any combination is a mode.

Worked examples

Example 1: equilibrium and stability

\(V=x^2+xy+y^2-3x\). \(V_x=2x+y-3=0\), \(V_y=x+2y=0\) give \(x=2\), \(y=-1\). The Hessian is \(\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}\) with eigenvalues \(1,3\), both positive: stable.

Example 2: normal modes with kinetic coupling

Take \(T=\tfrac12\dot q^T\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}\dot q\) (kinetic coupling) and \(V=q_1^2+q_2^2\), i.e. \(V=2\cdot\mathbf 1\) in the matrix convention \(V=\tfrac12q^TVq\). Then

\(\det\begin{pmatrix}2-2\omega^2&-\omega^2\\-\omega^2&2-2\omega^2\end{pmatrix}=(2-2\omega^2)^2-\omega^4=0\ \Rightarrow\ 2-2\omega^2=\pm\omega^2.\)

So \(\omega^2=\tfrac23\) (mode \(q_1=q_2\)) or \(\omega^2=2\) (mode \(q_1=-q_2\)). Normalised modes: \((1,1)/\sqrt6\) (since \((1,1)T(1,1)^T=6\)) and \((1,-1)/\sqrt2\). The two vectors are orthogonal under \(T\) (\((1,1)T(1,-1)^T=0\)) and also under the dot product here because both are eigenvectors of \(T\). In general they are not orthogonal under the dot product.

Example 3: coupled pendulums

\(g/l=1\) s\(^{-2}\), \(k/m=0.3\) s\(^{-2}\). Frequencies: \(\omega_1^2=1\), \(\omega_2^2=1+0.6=1.6\). Beat period \(=2\pi/(\omega_2-\omega_1)=2\pi/(1.265-1)\approx23.7\) s. Starting with \(\theta_1=\theta_0\), \(\theta_2=0\), at rest: \(\theta_1=\theta_0\cos\!\left(\tfrac{\omega_2-\omega_1}2t\right)\cos\!\left(\tfrac{\omega_1+\omega_2}2t\right)\) and the energy passes entirely to pendulum 2 after half a beat period.

Example 4: linear triatomic \(m,M,m\)

\(m=1\), \(M=3\), \(k=1\): frequencies squared \(0\), \(1\), \(1(1+2/3)=5/3\). Eigenvector check for the third mode: \((1,-2/3,1)\): row 1 of \(T^{-1}K\): \(\dfrac km(x_1-x_2)=1\cdot(1+\tfrac23)=\tfrac53=\omega^2x_1\). ✓. Row 2: \(\dfrac kM(-x_1+2x_2-x_3)=\tfrac13(-1-\tfrac43-1)=-\tfrac{10}9=\tfrac53\cdot(-\tfrac23)\) ✓.

Example 5: instability from a Hessian

An inverted pendulum \(V=mgl\cos\theta\) at \(\theta=0\): \(V''=-mgl<0\), \(\omega^2=V''/ml^2=-g/l<0\), so the deviation grows like \(e^{\sqrt{g/l}\,t}\).

Common traps

  • Orthogonality is with respect to the mass (kinetic) matrix \(T\), not the standard dot product and not the potential matrix alone. (2026-Q46: A, mass matrix.) The identity matrix is the answer only if one already uses mass-weighted coordinates.
  • Zero eigenfrequency is a free (translation or rotation) mode, not instability, infinite stiffness or “no motion” (2026-Q67: B). Instability corresponds to \(\omega^2<0\).
  • Frequency equation is \(\det(V-\omega^2T)=0\), not \(\det(T-\omega^2V)=0\). The second gives \(1/\omega^2\).
  • Kinetic coupling counts. A diagonal \(V\) with a non-diagonal \(T\) still has coupled equations. Solve the full generalised problem, not two separate oscillators.
  • Check the statement on the ratio \(\dot p_1/\dot p_2\): it equals \(q_1/q_2\) for 2025-Q71’s Lagrangian, not \(q_2/q_1\).
  • A stable equilibrium needs a positive definite Hessian, not just \(V'=0\). Positive diagonal entries are not enough (\(V=x^2+4xy+y^2\) is a saddle).
  • Number of modes equals the number of degrees of freedom, including zero modes. The triatomic 1-D model has 3 modes, one of which is a translation.
  • Equilibrium of a rotating-frame problem needs the effective potential, not the bare one.
  • In the double pendulum, the coupling term in \(T\) has \(\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\) which is \(1\) to this order: it still appears as a coupling.

Previous-year questions

2025-Q71: coupled-oscillator Lagrangian

Question. For the system with \(L=\dot q_1^2+\dot q_2^2+\dot q_1\dot q_2-q_1^2-q_2^2\), which statement is not true? (A) \(p_1+p_2=3(\dot q_1+\dot q_2)\) (B) \(\dot p_1+\dot p_2=-2(q_1+q_2)\) (C) \(q_1+q_2\) executes simple harmonic motion given by \(\ddot q_1+\ddot q_2=-\tfrac23(q_1+q_2)\) (D) \(\dot p_1/\dot p_2=q_2/q_1\).

Answer: D. \(p_1=2\dot q_1+\dot q_2\), \(p_2=\dot q_1+2\dot q_2\), so \(p_1+p_2=3(\dot q_1+\dot q_2)\) (A true). \(\dot p_1=\partial L/\partial q_1=-2q_1\), \(\dot p_2=-2q_2\) so (B) is true and \(\dot p_1/\dot p_2=q_1/q_2\) (D false). Adding gives \(3(\ddot q_1+\ddot q_2)=-2(q_1+q_2)\) (C true). The normal frequencies are \(\omega^2=\tfrac23\) (mode \(q_1+q_2\)) and \(\omega^2=2\) (mode \(q_1-q_2\)). (Official key.)

2026-Q46: orthogonality of eigenvectors

Question. In small oscillations, if the eigenvectors are not orthogonal under the standard dot product, what ensures orthogonality? (A) mass matrix (B) potential matrix (C) identity matrix (D) zero matrix.

Answer: A. The eigenvectors of \(V\mathbf a=\omega^2T\mathbf a\) satisfy \(\mathbf a_k^TT\mathbf a_l=0\): they are orthogonal with respect to the kinetic (mass) matrix. (Worked key, unofficial.)

2026-Q67: zero eigenfrequency

Question. A two-degree system has kinetic coupling and potential coupling. After diagonalisation one eigenfrequency becomes zero. Interpret physically: (A) instability (B) free motion mode (C) infinite stiffness (D) no motion.

Answer: B. \(\omega=0\) means zero restoring force for that normal coordinate, so \(\ddot\zeta=0\) and the coordinate moves freely (for example rigid translation or rotation of the whole system). (Worked key, unofficial.)

Quick revision

  • Equilibrium: \(\partial V/\partial q_j=0\); stable if the Hessian \(V_{jk}\) is positive definite.
  • Expand \(T=\tfrac12T_{jk}\dot\eta_j\dot\eta_k\), \(V=\tfrac12V_{jk}\eta_j\eta_k\); equation \(T\ddot\eta+V\eta=0\).
  • Frequencies from \(\det(V-\omega^2T)=0\); eigenvectors from \((V-\omega_k^2T)\mathbf a_k=0\).
  • Eigenvectors are orthogonal with respect to \(T\) (the mass matrix), not the plain dot product.
  • Normal coordinates diagonalise both \(T\) and \(V\): \(T=\tfrac12\sum\dot\zeta^2\), \(V=\tfrac12\sum\omega_k^2\zeta_k^2\).
  • \(\omega^2=0\): free (translation/rotation) mode. \(\omega^2<0\): instability.
  • Two masses and three springs: \(\omega^2=k/m\) and \((k+2\kappa)/m\). Coupled pendulums: \(g/l\) and \(g/l+2k/m\).
  • Equal double pendulum: \(\omega^2=(2\mp\sqrt2)g/l\), mode ratios \(\pm\sqrt2\).
  • Linear \(XYX\): \(\omega^2=0\), \(k/m\), \(k(M+2m)/(mM)\).
  • Vibrational modes: \(3N-6\) (non-linear), \(3N-5\) (linear).
