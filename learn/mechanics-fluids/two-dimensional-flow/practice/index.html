Two-dimensional flow and complex potential

  1. Question 1Easy
    The stream function \(\psi\) of a two-dimensional incompressible flow

    Why

    \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\) is enough for \(\psi\) to exist. It is harmonic only if the flow is also irrotational (\(\nabla^2\psi=\omega_z\)), and steadiness is not needed.
  2. Question 2Easy
    With \(k\) a nonzero real constant, which description is correct for the complex potentials \(w=k\ln z\) and \(w=ik\ln z\) respectively?

    Why

    \(k\ln z\) has \(\phi\) or \(\psi\) proportional to \(\ln r\) and the other to \(\theta\), with radial velocity \(\propto1/r\): a source or sink. \(ik\ln z\) gives velocity \(\propto1/r\) along \(\theta\): a line vortex.
  3. Question 3Easy
    The streamlines of the doublet \(w=\mu/z\) are

    Why

    \(\psi=-\mu\sin\theta/r=-\mu y/(x^2+y^2)\). Setting this constant gives \(x^2+y^2=cy\), circles through the origin with centres on the \(y\)-axis.
  4. Question 4Easy
    For the flow with complex potential \(w=z^3\), the speed at \(z=1+i\) is

    Why

    \(dw/dz=3z^2\), so the speed is \(3\lvert z\rvert^2=3\cdot2=6\). Option B is \(3\lvert z\rvert\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The stagnation points of the flow \(w=U(z+4/z)\) are at

    Why

    \(dw/dz=U(1-4/z^2)=0\) gives \(z^2=4\). (This is flow past a cylinder of radius \(2\).) At \(z=\pm2i\), \(dw/dz=2U\ne0\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    In steady potential flow of speed \(U\) at infinity past a circular cylinder with no circulation, the lowest surface pressure relative to \(p_\infty\) is

    Why

    Surface speed is \(2U\lvert\sin\theta\rvert\), maximum \(2U\). Bernoulli gives \(p-p_\infty=\tfrac12\rho(U^2-4U^2)=-\tfrac32\rho U^2\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    A circular cylinder of radius \(a\) in a stream \(U\) has circulation \(\Gamma\) about it. The two stagnation points on its surface merge when \(\Gamma\) equals

    Why

    Surface speed is \(\left\lvert\dfrac\Gamma{2\pi a}-2U\sin\theta\right\rvert\), zero at \(\sin\theta=\Gamma/(4\pi Ua)\). The two roots merge at \(\sin\theta=1\), i.e. \(\Gamma=4\pi aU\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    Air of density \(1.2\) kg/m\(^3\) flows at \(10\) m/s past a long cylinder with circulation \(5\) m\(^2\)/s. The lift per unit length is

    Why

    Kutta-Joukowski: \(L=\rho U\Gamma=1.2\times10\times5=60\) N/m. The drag is zero.
  9. Question 9Medium
    A line vortex of circulation \(\Gamma=8\pi\) m\(^2\)/s lies \(2\) m from a plane rigid wall. The speed at which it moves parallel to the wall is

    Why

    The image is an opposite vortex at distance \(2h=4\), inducing speed \(\Gamma/(2\pi\cdot4)=\Gamma/(4\pi h)=1\). Option B uses distance \(h\) instead of \(2h\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    Match each singularity with its image in a plane rigid wall. (a) source of strength \(m\); (b) vortex of circulation \(\Gamma\); (c) sink of strength \(m\). Images: (p) source of strength \(m\); (q) vortex of circulation \(-\Gamma\); (r) sink of strength \(m\).

    Why

    In a plane wall a source or sink is imaged by one of the same sign (so no net flow crosses the wall), while a vortex is imaged by one of opposite circulation.
  11. Question 11Hard
    A source of strength \(m\) is at \(z=c\) outside the rigid circular cylinder \(\lvert z\rvert=a\). By the circle theorem the image system inside the cylinder consists of

    Why

    \(w=-m\ln(z-c)-m\ln(a^2/z-\bar c)=-m\ln(z-c)-m\ln(z-a^2/\bar c)+m\ln z+\text{const}\): source at the inverse point, sink at \(z=0\). The net flux through the cylinder is zero.
  12. Question 12Hard
    For the complex potential \(w=-Uz+i\kappa\ln z-\dfrac{Ua^2}z\), Blasius’s theorem \(X-iY=\dfrac{i\rho}2\oint(dw/dz)^2dz\) (contour around the cylinder) gives

    Why

    \(dw/dz=-U+i\kappa/z+Ua^2/z^2\). The residue of \((dw/dz)^2\) at \(0\) is \(-2iU\kappa\), so \(\oint=2\pi i(-2iU\kappa)=4\pi U\kappa\) and \(X-iY=\dfrac{i\rho}2\cdot4\pi U\kappa=2\pi i\rho U\kappa\). Hence \(X=0\), \(Y=-2\pi\rho U\kappa\).
  13. Question 13Medium
    A uniform stream \(U=1\) along \(+x\) is combined with a source of strength \(m=3\) (flux \(2\pi m\)) at the origin, giving a Rankine half-body. The total asymptotic width of the body downstream is

    Why

    The dividing streamline is \(r\sin\theta=m(\pi-\theta)/U\), tending to \(y=\pm\pi m/U=\pm3\pi\); the total width is \(2\pi m/U=6\pi\). This equals the source outflow \(2\pi m\) divided by \(U\). Option B is the half-width.
  14. Question 14Hard
    For potential flow of speed \(U\) past a circular cylinder of radius \(a\) without circulation: (i) the surface speed is \(2U\lvert\sin\theta\rvert\); (ii) the resultant force on the cylinder is zero; (iii) the surface pressure is \(p_\infty+\tfrac12\rho U^2(1-4\sin^2\theta)\); (iv) the stagnation points are at \(\theta=\pm\pi/2\). Which are correct?

    Why

    Stagnation points are at \(\theta=0,\pi\) (where \(\sin\theta=0\)); \(\theta=\pm\pi/2\) is where the speed is greatest. The other three statements are correct.
