Why it matters for the exam

This is the core of the fluid syllabus: complex potential, source, sink, doublet and vortex, images, and flow past a circular cylinder. The 2025 paper asked the relation between \(\phi\) and \(\psi\) (Q72); questions on the standard complex potentials and on the cylinder (stagnation points, surface speed, pressure, lift) are the natural next forms. Learn the table of basic flows and the circle theorem; most numerical items reduce to differentiating a given \(w(z)\).

Core ideas

Stream function

For 2-D incompressible flow, \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\) guarantees a function \(\psi(x,y)\) with \(u=-\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y},\qquad v=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}\qquad\left(\text{polar: }q_r=-\frac1r\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\theta},\ q_\theta=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial r}\right).\)

  • \(\psi=\text{const}\) are the streamlines (since \(\mathbf q\cdot\nabla\psi=0\)).
  • The volume flux per unit depth between two streamlines equals the difference of \(\psi\) values, and does not depend on the path between them.
  • \(\psi\) exists for every 2-D incompressible flow, rotational or not. Its Laplacian is \(\nabla^2\psi=\omega_z\), so \(\psi\) is harmonic exactly when the flow is irrotational.
  • Streamlines \(\psi=\text{const}\) and equipotentials \(\phi=\text{const}\) are orthogonal families.
  • A rigid fixed boundary is a streamline: \(\psi\) constant on it.

(Sign convention here: \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\), \(u=-\psi_y\), \(v=\psi_x\). Modern texts use \(\mathbf q=\nabla\phi\), \(u=\psi_y\), \(v=-\psi_x\). Speeds do not depend on it.)

Complex potential and complex velocity

For irrotational incompressible flow \(\phi\) and \(\psi\) obey the Cauchy-Riemann equations \(\phi_x=\psi_y\), \(\phi_y=-\psi_x\), so \(w(z)=\phi+i\psi,\qquad z=x+iy,\) is an analytic function. Conversely every analytic \(w(z)\) is the complex potential of some irrotational incompressible flow (a possible flow); \(\phi=\operatorname{Re}w\) and \(\psi=\operatorname{Im}w\) are harmonic.

The complex velocity is \(\frac{dw}{dz}=\phi_x+i\psi_x=-u+iv=-q\,e^{-i\alpha},\qquad q=\left|\frac{dw}{dz}\right|,\) where \(\alpha\) is the direction of the velocity. In polar form \(q_r-iq_\theta=-e^{i\theta}\dfrac{dw}{dz}\). Stagnation points are the zeros of \(dw/dz\). With the modern convention \(dw/dz=u-iv\), and \(\lvert dw/dz\rvert=q\) is the same.

Source. \(w=-m\ln z=-m\ln r-im\theta\), so \(\phi=-m\ln r\), \(\psi=-m\theta\); \(q_r=-\phi_r=m/r\). Streamlines are rays \(\theta=\text{const}\), equipotentials are circles. Flux through any circle is \(2\pi r\cdot m/r=2\pi m\). \(\mathbf q\) blows up at the origin (a singularity).

Vortex. \(w=i\kappa\ln z=-\kappa\theta+i\kappa\ln r\), so \(\phi=-\kappa\theta\), \(q_\theta=-\dfrac1r\phi_\theta=\kappa/r\). Streamlines are circles about the origin. Circulation about any curve enclosing the origin is \(2\pi\kappa\); the flow is irrotational elsewhere. The velocity potential is many-valued.

Doublet. A source \(m\) at \((\varepsilon,0)\) and a sink \(m\) at \((-\varepsilon,0)\) give \(w=-m\ln(z-\varepsilon)+m\ln(z+\varepsilon)\to\dfrac{2m\varepsilon}z\) as \(\varepsilon\to0\) with \(\mu=2m\varepsilon\) fixed. Then \(\phi=\mu\cos\theta/r\), \(\psi=-\mu\sin\theta/r\). Streamlines \(\sin\theta/r=\text{const}\) are circles through the origin with centres on the \(y\)-axis (touching the \(x\)-axis at the origin); equipotentials are circles through the origin with centres on the \(x\)-axis. \(q_r=\mu\cos\theta/r^2\), \(q_\theta=\mu\sin\theta/r^2\). Texts differ on whether the axis points from sink to source or the reverse; the sign of \(\mu\) follows.

Combinations. Superposition is allowed because Laplace’s equation is linear.

  • Source \(+\) vortex: \(w=(-m+i\kappa)\ln z\); streamlines are logarithmic spirals.
  • Rankine half-body: uniform stream \(+\) source at origin, \(w=-Uz-m\ln z\). Stagnation point where \(-U-m/z=0\), at \(z=-m/U\) (upstream). The dividing streamline \(\psi=-\pi m\) is \(r\sin\theta=m(\pi-\theta)/U\), which tends to \(y=\pm\pi m/U\) downstream: total width \(2\pi m/U\).
  • Rankine oval: stream \(+\) source \(+\) sink of equal strength (closed body).
  • Equal and opposite vortices a distance \(d\) apart (“vortex pair”) move together, perpendicular to the line joining them, with speed \(\kappa/d=\Gamma/(2\pi d)\).

Method of images

The idea: replace a rigid boundary by fictitious singularities placed so that the boundary is a streamline (\(\psi=\text{const}\), i.e. no normal flow).

Plane wall. For a wall along \(y=0\) and flow in \(y>0\) with singularities at \(z_k\): the image of each singularity is its mirror image in the wall. Result: \(w(z)=f(z)+\overline{f(\bar z)}\) where \(f\) is the potential without the wall.

Real singularity Image in a plane wall
Source \(m\) at \(z_0\) source \(m\) at \(\bar z_0\)
Sink \(m\) sink \(m\)
Vortex \(\kappa\) vortex \(-\kappa\) (opposite sense) at \(\bar z_0\)
Doublet doublet, mirror image of its axis

For a source \(m\) at \((0,a)\) above the wall \(y=0\): \(w=-m\ln(z-ia)-m\ln(z+ia)=-m\ln(z^2+a^2)\). On the wall \(\psi=0\). The velocity on the wall is \(2m\lvert x\rvert/(x^2+a^2)\), which has maximum \(m/a\) at \(x=\pm a\).

A vortex \(\kappa\) at height \(a\) above a wall is carried parallel to the wall by the velocity its image induces, of magnitude \(\kappa/(2a)=\Gamma/(4\pi a)\) (the image is at distance \(2a\)).

Two intersecting walls at \(90^\circ\) need three images; a wedge of angle \(\pi/n\) needs \(2n-1\).

Circle theorem (Milne-Thomson). Let \(f(z)\) be the complex potential of a 2-D flow with all singularities at \(\lvert z\rvert>a\). If a rigid circular cylinder \(\lvert z\rvert=a\) is introduced, the new potential is \(\boxed{\ w(z)=f(z)+\overline{f}\!\left(\frac{a^2}{z}\right)\ },\qquad\overline f(\zeta)=\overline{f(\bar\zeta)} .\) Proof: on \(\lvert z\rvert=a\) we have \(a^2/z=\bar z\), so \(\overline f(a^2/z)=\overline{f(z)}\) and \(w=f+\bar f\) is real: \(\psi=0\) on the circle. The added terms have singularities only inside the circle, so the flow outside has the same singularities as before and nothing new.

Consequences:

  • Uniform stream \(f=-Uz\): \(w=-U(z+a^2/z)\), flow past a cylinder.
  • Source \(m\) at \(c\) (with \(\lvert c\rvert>a\)): \(f=-m\ln(z-c)\) gives \(w=-m\ln(z-c)-m\ln(z-a^2/\bar c)+m\ln z+\text{const}\). The image system is a source \(m\) at the inverse point \(a^2/\bar c\) plus a sink \(m\) at the centre; the net flux through the cylinder is \(0\).
  • Vortex \(\kappa\) at \(c\): image is a vortex \(-\kappa\) at the inverse point and a vortex \(+\kappa\) at the centre.
  • Doublet outside a circle has a doublet image at the inverse point.

Flow past a circular cylinder without circulation

Uniform stream \(U\) in the \(+x\) direction past the cylinder \(r=a\): \(w=-U\left(z+\frac{a^2}z\right),\qquad\phi=-U\left(r+\frac{a^2}r\right)\cos\theta,\qquad\psi=-U\left(r-\frac{a^2}r\right)\sin\theta .\)

  • \(\psi=0\) on \(r=a\) and on \(\theta=0,\pi\): the cylinder surface and the axis form the dividing streamline.
  • Velocity components: \(q_r=U\cos\theta\,(1-a^2/r^2)\), \(q_\theta=-U\sin\theta\,(1+a^2/r^2)\). At \(r=a\): \(q_r=0\), \(q_\theta=-2U\sin\theta\).
  • Stagnation points: \(dw/dz=-U(1-a^2/z^2)=0\), so \(z=\pm a\) (\(\theta=0,\pi\)).
  • Surface speed \(2U\lvert\sin\theta\rvert\), maximum \(2U\) at \(\theta=\pm\pi/2\). At infinity \(q\to U\).
  • Bernoulli: \(p=p_\infty+\tfrac12\rho(U^2-q^2)\). On the surface \(p=p_\infty+\tfrac12\rho U^2(1-4\sin^2\theta)\), pressure coefficient \(C_p=1-4\sin^2\theta\). Minimum \(p_\infty-\tfrac32\rho U^2\) at \(\theta=\pm\pi/2\). \(p=p_\infty\) where \(\sin\theta=\pm\tfrac12\).
  • Pressure is symmetric about both axes, so drag and lift are both zero (d’Alembert’s paradox). Without circulation the flow is acyclic.

Flow past a cylinder with circulation

Add a vortex at the centre (circulation \(\Gamma=2\pi\kappa\) counter-clockwise): \(w=-U\left(z+\frac{a^2}z\right)+i\kappa\ln z .\) The cylinder is still a streamline (the vortex part of \(\psi\) is \(\kappa\ln r\), constant at \(r=a\)) and the flow still tends to \(U\).

  • Surface speed: \(q_\theta=\dfrac\kappa a-2U\sin\theta\), \(q_r=0\).
  • Stagnation points on the surface: \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{\kappa}{2Ua}=\dfrac{\Gamma}{4\pi Ua}\). For \(\Gamma<4\pi Ua\) there are two, symmetric about the \(y\)-axis in the upper half; they approach the top as \(\Gamma\) increases and coincide at \(\theta=\pi/2\) when \(\Gamma=4\pi Ua\). For \(\Gamma>4\pi Ua\) the stagnation point leaves the surface along the \(y\)-axis at \(z=i\left(\dfrac{\kappa}{2U}+\sqrt{\dfrac{\kappa^2}{4U^2}-a^2}\right)\) and the flow is a closed circulating region around the cylinder.
  • Pressure on the surface: \(p=p_\infty+\tfrac12\rho\left[U^2-\left(\tfrac\kappa a-2U\sin\theta\right)^2\right]\).
  • Force: \(X=0\) (no drag) and the lift has magnitude \(\rho U\Gamma\) per unit length (Kutta-Joukowski), perpendicular to the stream and directed toward the side where the circulation adds to the stream speed (the lower-pressure side). For a counter-clockwise \(\Gamma\) in a stream along \(+x\) the force points in \(-y\). This is the Magnus effect.

Blasius theorem (statement)

For steady irrotational 2-D flow past a fixed cylinder whose cross-section is the closed contour \(C\), with complex potential \(w(z)\), the force components \(X,Y\) per unit depth and the moment \(M\) about the origin (counter-clockwise) are \(X-iY=\frac{i\rho}2\oint_C\left(\frac{dw}{dz}\right)^2dz,\qquad M=-\frac\rho2\operatorname{Re}\oint_C z\left(\frac{dw}{dz}\right)^2dz .\) The integral may be evaluated on any contour that encloses the body and no other singularity (use residues). If \(dw/dz=A+B/z+C/z^2+\cdots\) at large \(\lvert z\rvert\), then \(\oint(dw/dz)^2dz=2\pi i(2AB)\) and \(X-iY=-2\pi\rho AB .\) For \(w=-Uz+i\kappa\ln z-Ua^2/z\): \(A=-U\), \(B=i\kappa\), so \(X-iY=2\pi i\rho U\kappa=i\rho U\Gamma\). Hence \(X=0\), \(Y=-\rho U\Gamma\). The formula is unchanged under the sign convention, since \((dw/dz)^2\) does not see the sign of \(dw/dz\).

Formulas and results

  • \(u=-\psi_y,\ v=\psi_x\); \(\phi_x=\psi_y,\ \phi_y=-\psi_x\); \(\nabla^2\psi=\omega_z\).
  • \(w=\phi+i\psi\); \(dw/dz=-u+iv=-qe^{-i\alpha}\); \(q=\lvert dw/dz\rvert\); stagnation: \(dw/dz=0\).
  • Uniform stream: \(-Uz\). Source: \(-m\ln z\) (flux \(2\pi m\)). Sink: \(+m\ln z\). Vortex: \(i\kappa\ln z\) (\(\Gamma=2\pi\kappa\)). Doublet: \(\mu/z\).
  • Half-body: \(-Uz-m\ln z\), stagnation at \(z=-m/U\), asymptotic width \(2\pi m/U\).
  • Wall images: source \(\to\) source, vortex \(\to\) opposite vortex; vortex near wall moves at \(\Gamma/(4\pi a)\).
  • Circle theorem: \(w=f(z)+\overline f(a^2/z)\).
  • Cylinder: \(w=-U(z+a^2/z)\); surface speed \(2U\lvert\sin\theta\rvert\); \(C_p=1-4\sin^2\theta\).
  • With circulation: \(w=-U(z+a^2/z)+i\kappa\ln z\); stagnation \(\sin\theta=\Gamma/(4\pi Ua)\); lift \(\rho U\Gamma\).
  • Blasius: \(X-iY=\tfrac{i\rho}2\oint(dw/dz)^2dz\); \(M=-\tfrac\rho2\operatorname{Re}\oint z(dw/dz)^2dz\).

Worked examples

Example 1: from stream function to complex potential

\(\psi=2xy\). Check that it is a possible flow, find \(\phi\), \(w\), the velocity and the stagnation point.

\(\nabla^2\psi=0\), so a possible irrotational flow. \(\psi=\operatorname{Im}(z^2)\), so \(w=z^2\) (up to a constant), \(\phi=x^2-y^2\). Velocity: \(u=-\psi_y=-2x\), \(v=\psi_x=2y\). Then \(dw/dz=2z\), speed \(=2r\), and the only stagnation point is \(z=0\). Streamlines \(xy=\text{const}\) are hyperbolas; this is the flow in a right-angle corner.

Example 2: Rankine half-body

Uniform stream \(U=1\) along \(+x\), source with \(m=3\) at the origin. Find the stagnation point and the shape of the body.

\(w=-z-3\ln z\), \(dw/dz=-1-3/z=0\) at \(z=-3\). The stagnation point is \((-3,0)\), upstream of the source. \(\psi=-y-3\theta\) takes the value \(-3\pi\) at \(\theta=\pi\), so the body boundary is \(r\sin\theta=3(\pi-\theta)\). Far downstream (\(\theta\to0\)) \(y\to\pm3\pi\): total width \(6\pi\) (equal to \(2\pi m/U\)). At \(\theta=\pi/2\), \(y=3\pi/2\approx4.71\).

Example 3: cylinder with circulation

\(U=10\) m/s, \(a=0.2\) m, air density \(\rho=1.2\) kg/m\(^3\), \(\kappa=1\) m\(^2\)/s (so \(\Gamma=2\pi\) m\(^2\)/s). Find the stagnation points and the lift.

\(\sin\theta=\kappa/(2Ua)=1/(2\cdot10\cdot0.2)=0.25\), so \(\theta=14.48^\circ\) and \(165.52^\circ\). Merging would require \(\kappa=2Ua=4\). Lift per unit length \(=\rho U\Gamma=1.2\times10\times2\pi=75.4\) N/m, directed toward \(-y\) for counter-clockwise circulation with the stream along \(+x\).

Example 4: vortex near a wall

A vortex of strength \(\kappa=\pi\) lies at height \(2\) above a plane wall. Its image is a vortex \(-\pi\) at distance \(2\) below the wall, \(4\) from the real vortex. The vortex moves parallel to the wall with speed \(\kappa/4=\pi/4\approx0.785\). In terms of circulation \(\Gamma=2\pi\kappa\): \(\Gamma/(4\pi a)=2\pi^2/(8\pi)=\pi/4\), the same.

Example 5: source outside a circle

A source \(m\) at \(z=c=3\) outside the cylinder \(\lvert z\rvert=a=1\). Circle theorem: \(w=-m\ln(z-3)-m\ln\!\left(\dfrac1z-3\right)=-m\ln(z-3)-m\ln\!\left(z-\tfrac13\right)+m\ln z+\text{const}\). So the image system is a source \(m\) at \(z=\tfrac13\) and a sink \(m\) at the origin. On \(\lvert z\rvert=1\) the radial velocity \(q_r=-\operatorname{Re}\left(e^{i\theta}\,dw/dz\right)\) vanishes, so the circle is a streamline.

Example 6: force by Blasius

For \(w=-Uz-Ua^2/z+i\kappa\ln z\) use the large-\(z\) expansion \(dw/dz=-U+i\kappa/z+Ua^2/z^2\). Then \(A=-U\), \(B=i\kappa\), \(X-iY=-2\pi\rho(-U)(i\kappa)=2\pi i\rho U\kappa\). Hence \(X=0\) and \(\lvert Y\rvert=2\pi\rho U\kappa=\rho U\Gamma\).

Common traps

  • Convention signs. A source is \(-m\ln z\) in Chorlton and Raisinghania (\(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\)) and \(+m\ln z\) in modern texts. Read what the question defines; speeds and shapes agree.
  • Vortex needs \(i\). \(k\ln z\) with real \(k\) is a source or sink; \(ik\ln z\) is a vortex. \(k/z\) is a doublet.
  • Doublet streamlines are circles through the origin, not concentric circles and not straight lines.
  • Image of a vortex has opposite sign; image of a source has the same sign (in a plane wall). In a circle the images also include a singularity at the centre.
  • Circle theorem needs all singularities of \(f\) outside the circle.
  • Surface speed on a cylinder is \(2U\lvert\sin\theta\rvert\), not \(U\sin\theta\), and the maximum suction is \(C_p=-3\), not \(-1\).
  • Stagnation points on the circulating cylinder are at \(\sin\theta=\Gamma/(4\pi Ua)\), not \(\Gamma/(2\pi Ua)\). Check by the surface speed \(\kappa/a-2U\sin\theta\).
  • Drag is zero even with circulation; only lift \(\rho U\Gamma\) appears.
  • Blasius uses \((dw/dz)^2\) and a contour around the body; the factor is \(i\rho/2\).
  • \(\psi\) exists for rotational 2-D flow; \(w\) only for irrotational flow.

Previous-year questions

2025 Q72 (official key D)

Question: The relations between velocity potential \(\phi\) and stream function \(\psi\) are (a) \(\phi_x=\psi_y\), (b) \(\phi_y=\psi_x\), (c) \(\phi_x=-\psi_y\), (d) \(\phi_y=-\psi_x\). Answer: a, d only. Solution: the Cauchy-Riemann equations for the analytic function \(w=\phi+i\psi\) are \(\phi_x=\psi_y\) and \(\phi_y=-\psi_x\), which are (a) and (d). The sign-flipped pairs (b) and (c) fail.

  • Stream function: \(u=-\psi_y\), \(v=\psi_x\); exists for any 2-D incompressible flow; \(\nabla^2\psi=\omega_z\).
  • \(w=\phi+i\psi\) analytic; \(\lvert dw/dz\rvert=q\); stagnation where \(dw/dz=0\).
  • Source \(-m\ln z\) (flux \(2\pi m\)); vortex \(i\kappa\ln z\); doublet \(\mu/z\).
  • Source/sink speed \(m/r\); vortex speed \(\kappa/r\); doublet speed \(\mu/r^2\).
  • Doublet streamlines: circles through the origin touching the axis there.
  • Wall images: source same sign, vortex opposite sign.
  • Circle theorem: \(w=f(z)+\overline f(a^2/z)\).
  • Cylinder: \(w=-U(z+a^2/z)\), surface speed \(2U\lvert\sin\theta\rvert\), \(C_p=1-4\sin^2\theta\), zero force.
  • With circulation: stagnation \(\sin\theta=\Gamma/(4\pi Ua)\), lift \(\rho U\Gamma\), no drag.
  • Blasius: \(X-iY=\tfrac{i\rho}2\oint(dw/dz)^2dz\).
  • Half-body: stagnation \(z=-m/U\), asymptotic width \(2\pi m/U\).
