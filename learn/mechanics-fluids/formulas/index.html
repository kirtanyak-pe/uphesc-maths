Unit 8 · Mechanics and Fluid Dynamics

Formula sheet

Every formula and result of the unit. Each topic page explains where they come from.

1. Lagrangian mechanics

  • Degrees of freedom: \(3N-k\) for \(N\) particles with \(k\) independent holonomic constraints. Rigid body free in space: 6. One point fixed: 3. Fixed axis: 1.
  • Holonomic: constraint of the form \(f(q_1,\dots,q_n,t)=0\). Scleronomic: no explicit time. Rheonomic: explicit time. Inequalities and non-integrable velocity relations (rolling without slipping on a surface, in general) are non-holonomic.
  • D’Alembert’s principle: \(\sum_i(\mathbf F_i-m_i\ddot{\mathbf r}_i)\cdot\delta\mathbf r_i=0\).
  • Lagrange’s equations: \(\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_j}-\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial q_j}=Q_j\), with \(L=T-V\) and \(Q_j=0\) for conservative forces.
  • Generalised momentum \(p_j=\partial L/\partial\dot q_j\). If \(q_j\) is cyclic (\(\partial L/\partial q_j=0\)) then \(p_j\) is conserved.
  • Energy function \(h=\sum_j\dot q_j\,\partial L/\partial\dot q_j-L\). \(dh/dt=-\partial L/\partial t\). It equals \(T+V\) when \(T\) is quadratic in the velocities and \(V\) depends only on coordinates (constraints scleronomic).
  • Constraints with multipliers: \(\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_j}-\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial q_j}=\sum_s\lambda_s\,\partial f_s/\partial q_j\).
  • Standard Lagrangians: simple pendulum \(L=\tfrac12ml^2\dot\theta^2+mgl\cos\theta\). Central force \(L=\tfrac12m(\dot r^2+r^2\dot\theta^2)-V(r)\), with \(p_\theta=mr^2\dot\theta\) conserved. Atwood machine with massless pulley: \(a=\dfrac{(m_1-m_2)g}{m_1+m_2}\).

2. Hamiltonian mechanics and Poisson brackets

  • \(H(q,p,t)=\sum_jp_j\dot q_j-L\). Canonical equations \(\dot q_j=\partial H/\partial p_j\), \(\dot p_j=-\partial H/\partial q_j\), \(dH/dt=\partial H/\partial t=-\partial L/\partial t\).
  • \(H=T+V\) when \(T\) is a homogeneous quadratic in \(\dot q\) and \(V\) is independent of \(\dot q\) (and the transformation to \(q\) has no explicit time). \(H\) is conserved if \(L\) has no explicit time dependence.
  • Hamilton’s principle: \(\delta\int_{t_1}^{t_2}L\,dt=0\) with fixed end points. It gives Lagrange’s equations (Euler-Lagrange equation for the action).
  • Poisson bracket: \([u,v]=\sum_j\left(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial q_j}\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial p_j}-\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial p_j}\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial q_j}\right)\). Fundamental brackets \([q_j,q_k]=0=[p_j,p_k]\), \([q_j,p_k]=\delta_{jk}\).
  • Properties: \([u,v]=-[v,u]\); \([u,u]=0\); \([u,v+w]=[u,v]+[u,w]\); \([u,vw]=[u,v]w+v[u,w]\); Jacobi \([u,[v,w]]+[v,[w,u]]+[w,[u,v]]=0\).
  • Time evolution \(\dfrac{du}{dt}=[u,H]+\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}\). If \(\partial u/\partial t=0\) and \([u,H]=0\), then \(u\) is a constant of motion. Poisson’s theorem: the bracket of two constants of motion is a constant of motion.
  • Angular momentum: \([L_i,L_j]=\varepsilon_{ijk}L_k\) and \([L^2,L_i]=0\).
  • Canonical transformation \((q,p)\to(Q,P)\): \([Q,P]_{q,p}=1\), \([Q,Q]=[P,P]=0\). Equivalent: the Jacobian matrix \(M\) satisfies \(MJM^T=J\) (symplectic). It preserves Poisson brackets and phase-space volume.
  • Generating functions (with \(K=H+\partial F/\partial t\)):
Type Depends on Relations
\(F_1(q,Q,t)\) \(q,Q\) \(p=\partial F_1/\partial q\), \(P=-\partial F_1/\partial Q\)
\(F_2(q,P,t)\) \(q,P\) \(p=\partial F_2/\partial q\), \(Q=\partial F_2/\partial P\)
\(F_3(p,Q,t)\) \(p,Q\) \(q=-\partial F_3/\partial p\), \(P=-\partial F_3/\partial Q\)
\(F_4(p,P,t)\) \(p,P\) \(q=-\partial F_4/\partial p\), \(Q=\partial F_4/\partial P\)
  • Standard ones: \(F_2=\sum q_jP_j\) is the identity. \(F_1=qQ\) swaps coordinates and momenta (\(p=Q\), \(P=-q\)).
  • Action-angle variables (periodic motion, \(H\) time independent): \(J=\oint p\,dq\) (some texts divide by \(2\pi\)), \(\omega=\partial H/\partial J\), angle \(w=\omega t+\beta\). \(H\) depends only on \(J\) and \(J\) is constant. Harmonic oscillator: \(J=2\pi E/\omega\) with the \(\oint\) convention.

3. Rigid body

  • Euler angles \((\varphi,\theta,\psi)\) and the body-frame components: \(\omega_1=\dot\varphi\sin\theta\sin\psi+\dot\theta\cos\psi\), \(\omega_2=\dot\varphi\sin\theta\cos\psi-\dot\theta\sin\psi\), \(\omega_3=\dot\varphi\cos\theta+\dot\psi\).
  • \(\mathbf L=I\boldsymbol\omega\) (inertia tensor \(I\)), \(T=\tfrac12\boldsymbol\omega^TI\boldsymbol\omega\). Diagonal entries \(I_{xx}=\sum m(y^2+z^2)\); off-diagonal entries \(I_{xy}=-\sum mxy\) (this sign is the usual convention).
  • Parallel axis: \(I=I_{\rm cm}+Md^2\). Perpendicular axis (planar lamina): \(I_z=I_x+I_y\). \(I\) is symmetric and has real non-negative eigenvalues (principal moments); triangle inequality \(I_1\le I_2+I_3\).
  • Standard moments: solid sphere \(\tfrac25MR^2\); thin spherical shell \(\tfrac23MR^2\); solid cylinder about axis \(\tfrac12MR^2\); thin rod about centre perpendicular \(\tfrac1{12}ML^2\); thin ring about axis \(MR^2\); rectangular plate about perpendicular axis through centre \(\tfrac1{12}M(a^2+b^2)\).
  • Euler’s equations (body frame, principal axes, torque \(\mathbf N\)):

\(I_1\dot\omega_1-(I_2-I_3)\omega_2\omega_3=N_1,\) \(I_2\dot\omega_2-(I_3-I_1)\omega_3\omega_1=N_2,\) \(I_3\dot\omega_3-(I_1-I_2)\omega_1\omega_2=N_3.\)

  • Torque-free: \(T\) and \(L^2\) are conserved. Symmetric top (\(I_1=I_2\)): \(\omega_3\) is constant and \(\boldsymbol\omega\) precesses about the body axis with angular frequency \(\Omega=\dfrac{(I_3-I_1)\omega_3}{I_1}\).
  • Stability: rotation about the axis of largest or smallest moment is stable; about the intermediate axis it is unstable (the body tumbles).

4. Small oscillations

  • Near a stable equilibrium: \(T=\tfrac12\dot{\boldsymbol\eta}^TT\dot{\boldsymbol\eta}\), \(V=\tfrac12\boldsymbol\eta^TV\boldsymbol\eta\), equation of motion \(T\ddot{\boldsymbol\eta}+V\boldsymbol\eta=0\).
  • Equilibrium: \(\partial V/\partial q_j=0\). Stable if the Hessian \(V\) is positive definite.
  • Frequencies: \(\det(V-\omega^2T)=0\). Modes \(\mathbf a_k\) solve \((V-\omega_k^2T)\mathbf a_k=0\) and satisfy \(\mathbf a_k^TT\mathbf a_l=0\) for \(k\ne l\) (orthogonal with respect to \(T\), not the dot product).
  • Normal coordinates \(\zeta_k\): \(T=\tfrac12\sum\dot\zeta_k^2\), \(V=\tfrac12\sum\omega_k^2\zeta_k^2\), \(\ddot\zeta_k+\omega_k^2\zeta_k=0\).
  • Two equal pendulums (length \(l\)) coupled by a spring \(k\) (masses \(m\)): \(\omega_1^2=g/l\) (in phase), \(\omega_2^2=g/l+2k/m\) (out of phase). Weak coupling gives beats; complete transfer in time \(\pi/(\omega_2-\omega_1)\).
  • Double pendulum, equal masses and lengths: \(\omega^2=(2\mp\sqrt2)\,g/l\).
  • Linear XYX molecule (masses \(m,M,m\), springs \(k\)): \(\omega^2=0\) (translation), \(k/m\) (symmetric stretch), \(\dfrac km\left(1+\dfrac{2m}M\right)\) (antisymmetric stretch).
  • Vibrational modes: \(3N-6\) (non-linear), \(3N-5\) (linear), for \(N\) atoms.

5. Fluid kinematics, continuity and vorticity

  • Velocity \(\mathbf q=(u,v,w)\). Material derivative \(\dfrac D{Dt}=\dfrac\partial{\partial t}+\mathbf q\cdot\nabla\). Streamlines: \(\dfrac{dx}u=\dfrac{dy}v=\dfrac{dz}w\) (at fixed \(t\)). Steady flow: streamlines, path lines and streak lines coincide.
  • Continuity (conservation of mass): \(\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf q)=0\), equivalently \(\dfrac{D\rho}{Dt}+\rho\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\).
  • Incompressible: \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\). Spherically symmetric flow: \(\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\dfrac1{r^2}\dfrac\partial{\partial r}(r^2\rho q_r)=0\), and for incompressible flow \(r^2q_r\) is independent of \(r\).
  • Vorticity \(\boldsymbol\zeta=\nabla\times\mathbf q\) (equal to twice the local angular velocity). Irrotational: \(\boldsymbol\zeta=0\). Circulation \(\Gamma=\oint_C\mathbf q\cdot d\mathbf r=\iint\boldsymbol\zeta\cdot\mathbf n\,dS\) (Stokes’ theorem).
  • Convention used in these notes (Chorlton, Raisinghania): \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\). Modern texts use \(\mathbf q=+\nabla\phi\). Speeds and pressures do not depend on the choice.
  • Irrotational and incompressible: \(\nabla^2\phi=0\).
  • Two dimensions, incompressible, stream function \(\psi\): \(u=-\psi_y\), \(v=\psi_x\) (modern: \(u=\psi_y\), \(v=-\psi_x\)). Cauchy-Riemann: \(\phi_x=\psi_y\), \(\phi_y=-\psi_x\). \(\psi=\text{const}\) gives streamlines; \(\phi=\text{const}\) gives equipotentials, and the two families are orthogonal.

6. Equations of motion

  • Euler’s equation (perfect fluid): \(\dfrac{\partial\mathbf q}{\partial t}+(\mathbf q\cdot\nabla)\mathbf q=\mathbf F-\dfrac1\rho\nabla p\), with \(\mathbf F=-\nabla\Omega\) for a conservative body force.
  • Lamb’s form: \(\dfrac{\partial\mathbf q}{\partial t}-\mathbf q\times\boldsymbol\zeta+\nabla\left(\tfrac12q^2\right)=-\nabla\Omega-\dfrac1\rho\nabla p\).
  • Bernoulli, steady flow along a streamline: \(\dfrac p\rho+\tfrac12q^2+\Omega=\text{const}\). With constant \(\rho\) and gravity, \(\dfrac p\rho+\tfrac12q^2+gz=\text{const}\). For irrotational steady flow the constant is the same everywhere.
  • Unsteady irrotational (with \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\)): \(-\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\dfrac p\rho+\tfrac12q^2+\Omega=F(t)\). With \(\mathbf q=+\nabla\phi\) the sign of the \(\partial\phi/\partial t\) term is plus.
  • Kelvin’s circulation theorem: for an inviscid fluid with conservative body force and \(p=p(\rho)\), \(D\Gamma/Dt=0\) around a material circuit. Vorticity cannot be created or destroyed in such a flow.
  • Impulsive motion from rest: \(\varpi=\rho\phi\) (with \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\)).

7. Two-dimensional flow and the complex potential

  • \(w=\phi+i\psi\), analytic in \(z=x+iy\). \(\dfrac{dw}{dz}=-u+iv\) (\(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\)); modern convention \(\dfrac{dw}{dz}=u-iv\). Speed \(q=\lvert dw/dz\rvert\). Stagnation points: \(dw/dz=0\).
  • Uniform stream of speed \(U\) in \(+x\) (with \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\)): \(w=-Uz\).
  • Source of strength \(m\) (outflow \(2\pi m\)): \(w=-m\ln z\), \(q_r=m/r\). A sink has \(m<0\). At \(z_0\): \(w=-m\ln(z-z_0)\).
  • Doublet of strength \(\mu\) at the origin: \(w=\mu/z\), with \(\phi=\mu\cos\theta/r\), \(\psi=-\mu\sin\theta/r\). Streamlines are circles touching the \(x\)-axis at the origin.
  • Vortex of circulation \(\Gamma=2\pi\kappa\): \(w=i\kappa\ln z\), \(q_\theta=\kappa/r\) (sign set by the convention).
  • Flow past a circular cylinder of radius \(a\): \(w=-U\left(z+\dfrac{a^2}z\right)\). Surface speed \(2U\lvert\sin\theta\rvert\), \(C_p=1-4\sin^2\theta\), zero drag (d’Alembert’s paradox). Add a vortex: lift per unit length \(\rho U\Gamma\) (Kutta-Joukowski).
  • Milne-Thomson circle theorem: for a flow \(w=f(z)\) with all singularities outside \(\lvert z\rvert=a\), inserting a cylinder gives \(w=f(z)+\overline{f}(a^2/z)\).
  • Rankine half-body: uniform stream plus a source. Image of a source in a plane wall: an equal source on the other side.
  • Volume flow per unit depth across a curve joining \(A\) to \(B\) is \(\lvert\psi_B-\psi_A\rvert\).

8. Sphere in a perfect fluid, viscous flow

  • Sphere of radius \(a\) at rest in a uniform stream \(U\) in \(+x\) (with \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\)): \(\phi=-U\left(r+\dfrac{a^3}{2r^2}\right)\cos\theta\). Surface speed \(\tfrac32U\sin\theta\) (maximum \(\tfrac32U\) at the equator). Zero net force (d’Alembert’s paradox).
  • Sphere moving with velocity \(U\) through fluid at rest: \(\phi=\dfrac{Ua^3\cos\theta}{2r^2}\) (\(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\)). Kinetic energy of the fluid \(\tfrac12\left(\tfrac12M'\right)U^2=\tfrac13\pi\rho a^3U^2\), where \(M'=\tfrac43\pi\rho a^3\) is the mass of fluid displaced. The added mass is half the displaced fluid mass.
  • Navier-Stokes (incompressible, constant viscosity \(\mu\), \(\nu=\mu/\rho\)): \(\rho\left[\dfrac{\partial\mathbf q}{\partial t}+(\mathbf q\cdot\nabla)\mathbf q\right]=\rho\mathbf F-\nabla p+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf q\), together with \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\). No-slip: fluid velocity equals the wall velocity at a solid surface.
  • Vorticity equation (incompressible): \(\dfrac{D\boldsymbol\zeta}{Dt}=(\boldsymbol\zeta\cdot\nabla)\mathbf q+\nu\nabla^2\boldsymbol\zeta\). In 2-D the stretching term vanishes.
  • Reynolds number \(Re=\rho UL/\mu=UL/\nu\): ratio of inertial to viscous forces.
  • Stokes flow past a sphere (\(Re\ll1\), creeping flow): \(\psi=\tfrac12Ur^2\sin^2\theta\left(1-\dfrac{3a}{2r}+\dfrac{a^3}{2r^3}\right)\). Drag \(F=6\pi\mu aU\). Terminal velocity of a falling sphere \(v_t=\dfrac{2a^2(\rho_s-\rho)g}{9\mu}\).
  • With even slight viscosity the drag on the sphere is no longer zero (the inviscid zero-drag result of d’Alembert does not survive), because of the no-slip layer at the surface.
