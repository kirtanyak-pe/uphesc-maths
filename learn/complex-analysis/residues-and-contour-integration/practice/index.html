Residues and contour integration

  1. Question 1Medium
    The residue of \(f(z)=z\cos\dfrac1z\) at \(z=0\) is

    Why

    \(z\cos\frac1z=z\left(1-\frac1{2z^2}+\frac1{24z^4}-\cdots\right)=z-\frac1{2z}+\cdots\). The coefficient of \(z^{-1}\) is \(-\frac12\) (essential singularity: use the Laurent series).
  2. Question 2Easy
    The residue of \(\dfrac1{z^2+1}\) at \(z=i\) is

    Why

    Simple pole: \(\operatorname{Res}=\frac{1}{q'(i)}=\frac1{2i}=-\frac i2\).
  3. Question 3Medium
    The residue of \(\dfrac1{z^2(z-1)}\) at \(z=0\) is

    Why

    Double pole: \(\frac{d}{dz}\left[\frac1{z-1}\right]_{z=0}=-\frac1{(z-1)^2}\Big|_0=-1\). Check: \(\frac1{z^2}\cdot(-1-z-\cdots)\) has \(z^{-1}\) coefficient \(-1\).
  4. Question 4Hard
    The residue of \(\dfrac{1}{z-\sin z}\) at \(z=0\) is

    Why

    \(z-\sin z=\frac{z^3}6\left(1-\frac{z^2}{20}+\cdots\right)\), so \(\frac1{z-\sin z}=\frac6{z^3}\left(1+\frac{z^2}{20}+\cdots\right)=\frac6{z^3}+\frac{3}{10z}+\cdots\). The residue is \(\frac3{10}\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{5+4\sin\theta}=\)

    Why

    For \(\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+b\sin\theta}\) with \(a>|b|\) the value is \(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{25-16}}=\frac{2\pi}3\). Via \(z=e^{i\theta}\) the only pole inside \(|z|=1\) is \(z=-\frac i2\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\cos\theta}{2+\sin\theta}\,d\theta=\)

    Why

    The integrand is \(\frac{d}{d\theta}\ln(2+\sin\theta)\) and \(\ln(2+\sin\theta)\) is \(2\pi\)-periodic, so the integral is \(0\). No residue computation is needed (the residue at the pole inside is \(0\)).
  7. Question 7Medium
    \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(x^2+1)^2}=\)

    Why

    Upper half-plane pole \(z=i\) of order \(2\): \(\operatorname{Res}=\frac{d}{dz}(z+i)^{-2}\big|_{z=i}=-2(2i)^{-3}=\frac{1}{4i}\). Integral \(=2\pi i\cdot\frac1{4i}=\frac\pi2\).
  8. Question 8Hard
    \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^4+1}=\)

    Why

    Poles in the upper half-plane: \(e^{i\pi/4},e^{3i\pi/4}\), with residues \(\frac1{4z^3}\). Sum \(=\frac14(e^{-3i\pi/4}+e^{-9i\pi/4})=\frac14(-i\sqrt2)\). Integral \(=2\pi i\cdot\frac{-i\sqrt2}{4}=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}2=\frac\pi{\sqrt2}\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x^2}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)}\,dx=\)

    Why

    Partial fractions: \(\frac{x^2}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)}=\frac{4}{3(x^2+4)}-\frac1{3(x^2+1)}\). Integrals: \(\frac43\cdot\frac\pi2-\frac13\cdot\pi=\frac\pi3\). Residues at \(i\) and \(2i\) give the same.
  10. Question 10Medium
    \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{x^2+1}\,dx=\)

    Why

    Integrate \(\frac{e^{iz}}{z^2+1}\) over a large semicircle (Jordan’s lemma): \(2\pi i\cdot\frac{e^{-1}}{2i}=\frac\pi e\); the real part is the cosine integral.
  11. Question 11Medium
    The number of zeros of \(z^4+6z+3\) in the annulus \(1<|z|<2\) is

    Why

    On \(|z|=1\): \(|6z|=6>|z^4+3|\le4\), so one zero in \(|z|<1\). On \(|z|=2\): \(|z^4|=16>|6z+3|\le15\), so four zeros in \(|z|<2\). The annulus contains \(4-1=3\).
  12. Question 12Hard
    The number of zeros of \(z^7-5z^3+12\) in the annulus \(1<|z|<2\) is

    Why

    On \(|z|=2\): \(|z^7|=128>|{-5z^3}+12|\le52\), so all \(7\) zeros lie in \(|z|<2\). On \(|z|=1\): \(|12|>|z^7-5z^3|\le6\), so there are no zeros in \(|z|\le1\). Hence all \(7\) lie in \(1<|z|<2\).
  13. Question 13Medium
    Let \(f(z)=\dfrac{z^3(z-2)}{(z+1)^2}\). Then \(\dfrac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=3}\dfrac{f'(z)}{f(z)}dz=\)

    Why

    Argument principle: \(N-P\) inside the circle. Zeros: \(0\) (order \(3\)) and \(2\) (order \(1\)), so \(N=4\). Pole: \(-1\) (order \(2\)), so \(P=2\). The value is \(4-2=2\).
  14. Question 14Hard
    For \(f(z)=\dfrac{z}{z^2+1}\) the residue at infinity is

    Why

    The finite residues are \(\frac12\) (at \(i\)) and \(\frac12\) (at \(-i\)), summing to \(1\). All residues including \(\infty\) add to \(0\), so \(\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)=-1\). (Equivalently \(f=\frac1z-\cdots\) near \(\infty\) and \(\operatorname{Res}=-a_{-1}\).)
