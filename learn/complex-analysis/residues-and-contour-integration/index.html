Why it matters for the exam

This is the most computational part of the unit and the most likely to appear: a residue (2025 Q61), a contour integral by residues (2025 Q63), a trigonometric integral \(\int_0^{2\pi}\) (2025 Q65, 2026 Q92), and a Rouché zero count (2019, 2026 Q77). Every question reduces to a short formula, so speed and the exact formulas matter more than theory.

Core ideas

Residues

If \(f\) has an isolated singularity at \(a\) with Laurent series \(f=\sum a_n(z-a)^n\) on \(0<|z-a|<\rho\), the residue is \(\operatorname{Res}(f,a)=a_{-1}=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|z-a|=r}f(z)\,dz\quad(0<r<\rho).\) At a removable singularity the residue is \(0\) (but a zero residue does not mean removable: \(1/z^2\)).

Formulas for poles.

Situation Residue at \(a\)
simple pole \(\displaystyle\lim_{z\to a}(z-a)f(z)\)
\(f=p/q\), \(p(a)\neq0\), \(q(a)=0\), \(q'(a)\neq0\) \(\dfrac{p(a)}{q'(a)}\)
pole of order \(m\) \(\displaystyle\frac1{(m-1)!}\lim_{z\to a}\frac{d^{m-1}}{dz^{m-1}}\Big[(z-a)^mf(z)\Big]\)
\(f=\dfrac{g(z)}{(z-a)^m}\), \(g\) analytic at \(a\) \(\dfrac{g^{(m-1)}(a)}{(m-1)!}\)
double pole \(f=\dfrac{p}{q}\), \(q\) with double zero \(\dfrac{6p'q''-2pq'''}{3(q'')^2}\) at \(a\)
essential singularity read \(a_{-1}\) from the Laurent series

In the order-\(m\) formula, \(m\) may be taken larger than the true order (it still works, with more differentiation) but never smaller.

Standard residues.

  • \(\dfrac1{z^2+1}\) at \(i\): \(\dfrac1{2i}\); at \(-i\): \(-\dfrac1{2i}\).
  • \(\dfrac{e^z}{z^n}\) at \(0\): \(\dfrac1{(n-1)!}\). \(\dfrac{\cos z}{z^3}\) at \(0\): \(-\dfrac12\). \(\dfrac{\sin z}{z^2}\): \(1\).
  • \(\cot z\) at \(n\pi\): \(1\). \(\dfrac1{\sin z}\) at \(n\pi\): \((-1)^n\). \(\tan z\) at \(\frac\pi2+n\pi\): \(-1\).
  • \(\dfrac{e^z}{e^z-1}\) at \(2\pi ik\): \(1\).
  • \(\dfrac{1}{(z-1)^2(z+2)}\) at \(1\): \(\frac{d}{dz}\frac1{z+2}\Big|_1=-\frac19\); at \(-2\): \(\frac19\).
  • \(e^{1/z}\) at \(0\): \(1\). \(z\cos\frac1z\) at \(0\): \(-\frac12\). \(\sin\frac1z\) at \(0\): \(1\).
  • \(\dfrac1{1-\cos z}=\dfrac2{z^2}+\dfrac16+\cdots\) at \(0\): residue \(0\) (a double pole with zero residue).
  • \(f'/f\) at a zero of order \(m\): \(m\); at a pole of order \(m\): \(-m\).

Residue at infinity

\(\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)=-\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=R}f(z)\,dz\ (R\text{ large, anticlockwise})=-\operatorname{Res}\!\Big(\frac1{w^2}f\!\Big(\frac1w\Big),0\Big).\) In terms of the Laurent series valid for \(|z|>R\): \(\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)=-a_{-1}\) (minus the coefficient of \(1/z\)).

  • If \(f(z)\sim\dfrac cz\) as \(z\to\infty\) then \(\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)=-c\).
  • For a rational function (or any function with finitely many finite singularities) the sum of all residues, including the one at \(\infty\), is \(0\). Hence \(\oint_{|z|=R}f=2\pi i\sum_{\text{finite}}\operatorname{Res}=-2\pi i\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)\). This is a shortcut when the finite poles are many or of high order.
  • Example: \(\oint_{|z|=2}\dfrac{dz}{z^{10}(z-1)}\). Both poles (\(0\) of order \(10\), \(1\) simple) are inside, but \(f=O(z^{-11})\) at \(\infty\), so \(\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)=0\) and the integral is \(0\) with no differentiation needed.
  • A nonzero residue at \(\infty\) can occur at a point where \(f\) is analytic: \(f=1/z\) has residue \(-1\) at \(\infty\).

Residue theorem

Let \(C\) be a positively oriented simple closed contour, \(f\) analytic on and inside \(C\) except for finitely many isolated singularities \(a_1,\dots,a_n\) inside \(C\) (none on \(C\)). Then \(\oint_Cf(z)\,dz=2\pi i\sum_{k=1}^n\operatorname{Res}(f,a_k).\) Cauchy’s integral formulas are the special case where the singularity is a pole created by dividing by \((z-a)^{n+1}\).

Practical steps: (1) locate all singularities; (2) keep only those inside \(C\); (3) classify each and apply the matching formula; (4) multiply the sum by \(2\pi i\); (5) check the orientation.

Type 1: \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}R(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\,d\theta\)

Put \(z=e^{i\theta}\), so \(\theta:0\to2\pi\) traces \(|z|=1\) once: \(\cos\theta=\frac{z+z^{-1}}2,\quad\sin\theta=\frac{z-z^{-1}}{2i},\quad d\theta=\frac{dz}{iz},\quad\cos n\theta=\frac{z^n+z^{-n}}2.\) The integral becomes \(\oint_{|z|=1}F(z)\,dz=2\pi i\sum\operatorname{Res}\) over the poles with \(|z|<1\). Do not forget the pole at \(z=0\) that \(dz/(iz)\) may create.

Results to remember (\(a>|b|\) so the denominator does not vanish): \(\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+b\cos\theta}=\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+b\sin\theta}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}.\)

  • \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{5+4\sin\theta}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{25-16}}=\frac{2\pi}3\) (2025 Q65).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2+\cos\theta}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt3}\), \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2+\cos\theta}=\frac\pi{\sqrt3}\) (half of the full range, because the integrand is even about \(\pi\)).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{1-2p\cos\theta+p^2}=\frac{2\pi}{1-p^2}\) for \(|p|<1\), and \(\frac{2\pi}{p^2-1}\) for \(|p|>1\).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\cos2\theta}{5-4\cos\theta}d\theta=\frac\pi6\); in general \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\cos n\theta}{1-2p\cos\theta+p^2}d\theta=\frac{2\pi p^n}{1-p^2}\) (\(0<p<1\)).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\sin^2\theta}{a+b\cos\theta}d\theta=\frac{2\pi}{b^2}\left(a-\sqrt{a^2-b^2}\right)\).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}e^{\cos\theta}\cos(\sin\theta)\,d\theta=2\pi\) (integrate \(e^z/z\) over \(|z|=1\)).

Don’t over-apply residues. \(\int_0^{2\pi}\dfrac{\cos\theta}{2+\sin\theta}d\theta\) is the integral of \(\frac{d}{d\theta}\ln(2+\sin\theta)\) over a full period, so it is \(0\) (2026 Q92).

Type 2: \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\,dx\)

Conditions: \(P,Q\) polynomials, \(\deg Q\ge\deg P+2\), \(Q\) has no real zeros. Close with the semicircle \(C_R\) in the upper half plane. On \(C_R\), \(|P/Q|\le M/R^2\) for large \(R\), so by the ML inequality \(\left|\int_{C_R}\right|\le\frac{M}{R^2}\pi R\to0\). Hence \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{P}{Q}\,dx=2\pi i\sum_{\operatorname{Im}a_k>0}\operatorname{Res}\!\left(\frac PQ,a_k\right).\)

Standard values:

  • \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{dx}{x^2+a^2}=\frac\pi a\) (\(a>0\)). \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{dx}{(x^2+1)^2}=\frac\pi2\).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{dx}{x^4+1}=\frac\pi{\sqrt2}\); \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{x^4+1}=\frac\pi{2\sqrt2}\) (even integrand).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{x^2\,dx}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)}=\frac\pi3\); \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{dx}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)}=\frac\pi6\).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{dx}{x^2+2x+2}=\pi\).

If \(\deg Q=\deg P+1\) the integral converges only as a principal value (e.g. \(\int\frac{x}{x^2+1}\) is \(0\) as PV).

Type 3: Jordan’s lemma and Fourier-type integrals

Jordan’s lemma. Let \(C_R:z=Re^{i\theta}\), \(0\le\theta\le\pi\) (upper semicircle), \(a>0\). If \(f\) is continuous on \(C_R\) and \(\max_{C_R}|f|\to0\) as \(R\to\infty\), then \(\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{C_R}f(z)e^{iaz}\,dz=0.\) Proof idea: \(|e^{iaz}|=e^{-aR\sin\theta}\) and \(\sin\theta\ge2\theta/\pi\) on \([0,\pi/2]\), so \(\int_0^\pi e^{-aR\sin\theta}d\theta\le\frac{\pi}{aR}\). Therefore it needs only \(f\to0\), not \(f=O(R^{-2})\). For \(a<0\) close in the lower half plane.

Fourier integrals. For \(F(x)=P/Q\) with \(\deg Q\ge\deg P+1\) and no real poles, \(a>0\): \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty F(x)e^{iax}dx=2\pi i\sum_{\operatorname{Im}a_k>0}\operatorname{Res}\big(F(z)e^{iaz},a_k\big).\) Take real and imaginary parts to get \(\int F\cos ax\) and \(\int F\sin ax\).

  • \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{\cos ax}{x^2+b^2}dx=\frac{\pi e^{-ab}}b\) (\(a,b>0\)).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{x\sin ax}{x^2+b^2}dx=\pi e^{-ab}\); for \(a=b=1\) the value is \(\pi/e\).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{\cos2x}{x^2+4}dx=\frac{\pi e^{-4}}{2}\).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{x\cos ax}{x^2+b^2}dx=0\) (odd integrand).

Real poles and indentation (principal value). If \(F\) has a simple pole on the real axis, indent with a small upper semicircle of radius \(\varepsilon\); it contributes \(-\pi i\operatorname{Res}\) (when traversed clockwise), so \(\mathrm{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^\infty F(x)e^{iax}dx=2\pi i\sum_{\text{UHP}}\operatorname{Res}+\pi i\sum_{\text{real}}\operatorname{Res}.\) Application: \(\mathrm{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{e^{ix}}x\,dx=\pi i\), so \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{\sin x}xdx=\pi\) and \(\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}xdx=\frac\pi2\) (the Dirichlet integral; it also appeared in 2026 Q68 under Fourier transforms).

Keyhole contour (for \(\int_0^\infty x^{a-1}R(x)\,dx\)): \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{x^{a-1}}{1+x}dx=\frac\pi{\sin\pi a}\), \(0<a<1\).

Argument principle

Let \(f\) be meromorphic inside and on a simple closed positively oriented contour \(C\), with no zeros or poles on \(C\). Then \(\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz=N-P,\) where \(N\) is the number of zeros and \(P\) the number of poles of \(f\) inside \(C\), each counted with multiplicity.

  • Interpretation: \(N-P\) is the winding number of the image curve \(f(C)\) about \(0\). Equivalently, the change in \(\arg f\) along \(C\) is \(2\pi(N-P)\).
  • If \(f\) is analytic inside \(C\) then \(P=0\) and the integral counts zeros.
  • More general: for \(g\) analytic, \(\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_Cg\frac{f'}f=\sum g(\text{zeros})-\sum g(\text{poles})\).
  • Why: \(f'/f\) has a simple pole with residue \(m\) at a zero of order \(m\) and residue \(-m\) at a pole of order \(m\).

Rouché’s theorem

Statement. Let \(f,g\) be analytic on and inside a simple closed contour \(C\) and suppose \(|g(z)|<|f(z)|\) for all \(z\) on \(C\). Then \(f\) and \(f+g\) have the same number of zeros inside \(C\) (with multiplicity).

(Strict inequality on all of \(C\) is required; neither function can vanish on \(C\).)

Method for a polynomial/equation. To count zeros of \(h\) inside \(|z|=r\): split \(h=f+g\) with \(f\) the dominant term on \(|z|=r\); check \(|g|<|f|\) on the circle using \(|z|=r\) and the triangle inequality; the number of zeros of \(h\) equals the number of zeros of \(f\) in \(|z|<r\) (which is easy, e.g. \(z^n\) has \(n\)).

Zero-location examples (all checked numerically):

Polynomial Result
\(z^4+6z+3\) on \(\lvert z\rvert=1\): \(\lvert6z\rvert=6>\lvert z^4+3\rvert\le4\), so \(1\) zero in \(\lvert z\rvert<1\). On \(\lvert z\rvert=2\): \(\lvert z^4\rvert=16>\lvert6z+3\rvert\le15\), so all \(4\) zeros in \(\lvert z\rvert<2\). Hence \(1\) zero in \(\lvert z\rvert<1\) and \(3\) in \(1<\lvert z\rvert<2\)
\(z^7-5z^3+12\) \(\lvert12\rvert>\lvert z^7-5z^3\rvert\) on \(\lvert z\rvert=1\) (at most \(6\)): no zeros in \(\lvert z\rvert<1\). On \(\lvert z\rvert=2\): \(128>52\), so \(7\) zeros in \(\lvert z\rvert<2\). All \(7\) lie in \(1<\lvert z\rvert<2\)
\(z^4-5z+1\) \(1\) zero in \(\lvert z\rvert<1\), \(3\) in \(1<\lvert z\rvert<2\)
\(e^z-3z\) \(\lvert3z\rvert=3>e\ge\lvert e^z\rvert\) on \(\lvert z\rvert=1\), so exactly \(1\) zero in the unit disc
\(z^5-2z+1\) \(1\) zero in \(\lvert z\rvert<1\) (see Example 3)

Other uses: proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra (compare \(p\) with its leading term on a large circle); Hurwitz’s theorem (a locally uniform limit of zero-free analytic functions is zero-free or \(\equiv0\)).

Formulas and results

  • \(\operatorname{Res}=\lim(z-a)f\) (simple); \(\dfrac{p(a)}{q'(a)}\); \(\dfrac1{(m-1)!}\dfrac{d^{m-1}}{dz^{m-1}}\big[(z-a)^mf\big]_a\) (order \(m\)).
  • \(\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)=-a_{-1}\) of the expansion at \(\infty\); sum of all residues (with \(\infty\)) is \(0\).
  • Residue theorem: \(\oint_Cf=2\pi i\sum\operatorname{Res}\) (inside, positive orientation).
  • Trig integrals: \(z=e^{i\theta}\), \(d\theta=dz/(iz)\), \(\cos\theta=(z+1/z)/2\), \(\sin\theta=(z-1/z)/2i\).
  • \(\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+b\cos\theta}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}\), \(a>|b|\).
  • \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac PQ=2\pi i\sum_{\text{UHP}}\operatorname{Res}\) when \(\deg Q\ge\deg P+2\), no real poles.
  • Jordan: \(\int_{C_R}f e^{iaz}\to0\) (\(a>0\)) if \(f\to0\) uniformly. \(\int\frac{\cos ax}{x^2+b^2}=\frac{\pi e^{-ab}}b\).
  • PV with real simple poles: \(2\pi i\sum_{\text{UHP}}+\pi i\sum_{\text{real}}\).
  • Argument principle: \(\frac1{2\pi i}\oint\frac{f'}f=N-P\).
  • Rouché: \(|g|<|f|\) on \(C\) implies \(f\) and \(f+g\) have equal zero counts inside.

Worked examples

Example 1: residue theorem with an order-\(3\) pole

\(\displaystyle\oint_{|z|=2}\frac{e^{2z}}{(z+1)^4}dz\) (2025 Q63). The pole \(z=-1\) of order \(4\) is inside. Residue \(=\frac1{3!}\frac{d^3}{dz^3}e^{2z}\Big|_{-1}=\frac{8e^{-2}}6=\frac4{3e^2}\). Integral \(=2\pi i\cdot\frac4{3e^2}=\dfrac{8\pi i}{3e^2}\).

Example 2: trigonometric integral (2025 Q65)

\(I=\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{5+4\sin\theta}\). With \(z=e^{i\theta}\): \(5+4\sin\theta=5+\frac{2}{i}(z-z^{-1})=\frac{5iz+2z^2-2}{iz}\). So \(I=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{iz}\cdot\frac{iz}{2z^2+5iz-2}=\oint\frac{dz}{2z^2+5iz-2}.\) Roots of \(2z^2+5iz-2=0\): \(z=\frac{-5i\pm\sqrt{-25+16}}4=\frac{-5i\pm3i}4\), i.e. \(z=-\frac i2\) (inside) and \(z=-2i\) (outside). Residue at \(-\frac i2\): \(\frac1{4z+5i}=\frac1{-2i+5i}=\frac1{3i}\). So \(I=2\pi i\cdot\frac1{3i}=\dfrac{2\pi}3\), matching \(2\pi/\sqrt{25-16}\).

Example 3: Rouché with a boundary root (2026 Q77)

Number of zeros of \(z^5-2z+1\) in \(|z|<1\). Note \(z=1\) is a zero on the circle, so Rouché cannot be applied directly on \(|z|=1\) with \(f=-2z\) (equality \(|z^5+1|=|2z|=2\) holds at \(z=1\)). Use \(|z|=r<1\) with \(r\) close to \(1\): \(|z^5+1|\le1+r^5<2r\) for \(0.52<r<1\) (e.g. \(r=0.9\): \(1.59<1.8\)), so exactly one zero in \(|z|<r\) for every such \(r\), hence exactly one in \(|z|<1\). Factorisation: \(z^5-2z+1=(z-1)(z^4+z^3+z^2+z-1)\); the roots are \(1\), \(0.5188\), \(-1.2906\), \(-0.114\pm1.217i\), and only \(0.5188\) is inside.

Example 4: real integral with higher-order pole

\(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{dx}{(x^2+1)^2}\). UHP pole \(z=i\) of order \(2\). \(\frac{1}{(z+i)^2}\) differentiated: residue \(=\frac d{dz}(z+i)^{-2}\big|_{i}=-2(2i)^{-3}=-\frac2{-8i}=\frac1{4i}\). Value \(=2\pi i\cdot\frac1{4i}=\dfrac\pi2\).

Example 5: Fourier integral

\(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{\cos x}{x^2+1}dx=\operatorname{Re}\int\frac{e^{iz}}{z^2+1}\). Residue at \(z=i\): \(\frac{e^{i\cdot i}}{2i}=\frac{e^{-1}}{2i}\). So \(\int\frac{e^{ix}}{x^2+1}=2\pi i\cdot\frac{e^{-1}}{2i}=\dfrac\pi e\), real, so the cosine integral is \(\pi/e\) and the sine integral is \(0\).

Example 6: argument principle

\(f(z)=\dfrac{z^2-1}{z^3}\), \(C:|z|=2\). Zeros \(\pm1\) (both inside, \(N=2\)); pole at \(0\) of order \(3\) (\(P=3\)). So \(\frac1{2\pi i}\oint\frac{f'}f=2-3=-1\). Check by residues: \(\frac{f'}f=\frac{2z}{z^2-1}-\frac3z\) has residues \(1\), \(1\) at \(\pm1\) and \(-3\) at \(0\), total \(-1\).

Example 7: using the sum of residues

\(\displaystyle\oint_{|z|=3}\frac{dz}{(z-1)(z-2)}\). Residues: at \(1\): \(\frac1{1-2}=-1\); at \(2\): \(\frac1{2-1}=1\). Sum \(0\), so the integral is \(0\). Quicker: \(f=O(z^{-2})\) so \(\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)=0\) and the finite residues must sum to \(0\).

Common traps

  • Include only poles inside the contour; poles on the contour need a principal value, not the plain theorem.
  • \(\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+b\cos\theta}\) requires \(a>|b|\). Check the roots: exactly one lies inside \(|z|=1\) (their product is \(1\)). If \(a<|b|\) the integral does not exist.
  • After substituting \(z=e^{i\theta}\) do not drop the factor \(\frac1{iz}\); that factor may add a pole at \(0\) for integrands with \(\cos n\theta\).
  • For type 2 you need \(\deg Q\ge\deg P+2\); with \(+1\) the semicircle does not vanish. Poles in the upper half plane only; closing below changes the sign.
  • Fourier integrals: use \(e^{iaz}\) (\(a>0\)) and take real/imaginary parts. Taking \(\cos az\) directly in \(z\) makes the integrand blow up on the arc.
  • Jordan’s lemma requires \(a>0\) (upper arc). For \(e^{-iaz}\) close below.
  • Order-\(m\) residue formula: differentiate \(m-1\) times and divide by \((m-1)!\); the power of \((z-a)\) multiplied is the order.
  • Residue at a removable singularity is \(0\); at \(\infty\) it is \(-a_{-1}\), not \(+a_{-1}\).
  • Rouché needs strict inequality \(|g|<|f|\) on all of \(C\), no zeros on \(C\). A root on the circle (as for \(z^5-2z+1\) at \(z=1\)) breaks the argument; use a slightly smaller circle.
  • The dominant term must be the right one: on \(|z|=1\) for \(z^4+6z+3\) the dominant term is \(6z\), on \(|z|=2\) it is \(z^4\).
  • A count in \(|z|<2\) minus a count in \(|z|<1\) gives the zeros in the annulus.
  • \(\oint\bar z\,dz\) is not computed by residues (\(\bar z\) is not analytic); substitute \(\bar z=r^2/z\) on \(|z|=r\) first.
  • Integrals of derivatives of periodic functions over a full period vanish (2026 Q92); no residue calculation needed.

2019 (unnumbered, no. 3): where do the zeros of \(z^4+6z+3\) lie? (options: \(|z|<2\); \(|z|<1\); \(1<|z|<2\); \(|z|>2\))

Answer: all zeros lie in \(|z|<2\). Rouché on \(|z|=2\): \(|z^4|=16>|6z+3|\le15\), so all four zeros are in \(|z|<2\). On \(|z|=1\): \(6>4\), so exactly one zero has \(|z|<1\) (it is \(\approx-0.511\)); the other three satisfy \(1<|z|<2\). So “all zeros in \(1<|z|<2\)” is false (one zero is inside the unit disc) even though the recovered source’s presenter appeared to mark the annulus option. Treat the 2019 mark as unreliable for this item; the mathematically correct statement is “\(|z|<2\)”.

Quick revision

  • Simple pole: \(\operatorname{Res}=\lim(z-a)f=p(a)/q'(a)\). Order \(m\): \(\frac1{(m-1)!}\big[(z-a)^mf\big]^{(m-1)}\).
  • \(\operatorname{Res}\) of \(e^z/z^n\) at \(0\) is \(1/(n-1)!\); of \(\cot z\) at \(n\pi\) is \(1\); of \(z\cos(1/z)\) at \(0\) is \(-\frac12\).
  • Residue at \(\infty\) is minus the \(1/z\) coefficient; all residues including \(\infty\) sum to \(0\).
  • \(\oint_Cf=2\pi i\sum_{\text{inside}}\operatorname{Res}\).
  • Trig: \(z=e^{i\theta}\), \(d\theta=\frac{dz}{iz}\); \(\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+b\cos\theta}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}\).
  • Real line: \(\deg Q\ge\deg P+2\), upper half plane, \(2\pi i\sum\operatorname{Res}\).
  • Jordan (\(a>0\)): \(\int_{C_R}fe^{iaz}\to0\) if \(f\to0\). \(\int\frac{\cos ax}{x^2+b^2}=\frac{\pi e^{-ab}}b\).
  • Real simple poles in PV: add \(\pi i\operatorname{Res}\); \(\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}xdx=\frac\pi2\).
  • Argument principle: \(\frac1{2\pi i}\oint\frac{f'}f=N-P\).
  • Rouché: \(|g|<|f|\) on \(C\) gives the same zero count for \(f\) and \(f+g\).
  • \(z^4+6z+3\): one zero in \(|z|<1\), three in \(1<|z|<2\).
