Why it matters for the exam

Almost every paper has a Möbius or conformal question: the image of a circle or line (2025 Q62, 2026 Q81), the condition for conformality (2026 Q70), or when a cross-ratio is real (2019). These are fast, formula-driven marks. The standard images of lines and circles under \(1/z\) and under \(\frac{z-i}{z+i}\) must be known cold.

Core ideas

Conformal maps

A map \(w=f(z)\) is conformal at \(z_0\) if it preserves angles between curves through \(z_0\), both in magnitude and in orientation (sense).

Key theorem. If \(f\) is analytic at \(z_0\) and \(f'(z_0)\neq0\), then \(f\) is conformal at \(z_0\). Conversely, if \(f\) is conformal at \(z_0\) (and real differentiable there), then \(f\) is analytic with \(f'(z_0)\neq0\). So

\[f\ \text{analytic and conformal at } z_0\iff f'(z_0)\neq0.\]

(This is the answer to 2026 Q70.)

Geometry of \(f'(z_0)\ne0\): a tangent direction at \(z_0\) is rotated by \(\arg f'(z_0)\) and lengths are scaled by \(|f'(z_0)|\) (the local magnification), the same in all directions. Small shapes keep their form: circles go to near-circles.

  • Critical points are the zeros of \(f'\). If \(f'(z_0)=\dots=f^{(m-1)}(z_0)=0\) and \(f^{(m)}(z_0)\neq0\) (so \(f-f(z_0)\) has a zero of order \(m\)), angles at \(z_0\) are multiplied by \(m\). Example: \(z^2\) at \(0\) doubles angles; \(z^3-3z\) has critical points \(\pm1\), doubling angles there.
  • \(f(z)=\bar z\) preserves the size of angles but reverses orientation: it is not conformal in this sense (it is an isogonal / anti-conformal map).
  • An analytic function that is one-one on a domain has \(f'\neq0\) there, so it is conformal everywhere on the domain, and the inverse is analytic. The converse fails: \(e^z\) has \(f'\ne0\) everywhere but is not one-one on \(\mathbb C\) (period \(2\pi i\)).
  • Compositions of conformal maps are conformal; so are inverses.
  • The Riemann mapping theorem: any simply connected domain \(D\ne\mathbb C\) can be mapped conformally and one-one onto the open unit disc. \(\mathbb C\) cannot (Liouville). The map is unique once \(f(a)=0\), \(f'(a)>0\) is prescribed.

Möbius (bilinear, linear fractional) transformations

\[T(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\qquad ad-bc\neq0 .\]

Defined on the extended plane \(\widehat{\mathbb C}=\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}\) with \(T(-d/c)=\infty\), \(T(\infty)=a/c\) (and \(T(\infty)=\infty\) if \(c=0\)).

  • \(T'(z)=\dfrac{ad-bc}{(cz+d)^2}\neq0\): conformal everywhere, one-one and onto \(\widehat{\mathbb C}\).
  • If \(ad-bc=0\) the map is constant: not allowed.
  • Group structure. Compositions and inverses are Möbius. Corresponding matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\): composition corresponds to matrix multiplication; scalar multiples of the matrix give the same map. Inverse: \(T^{-1}(w)=\dfrac{dw-b}{-cw+a}\).
  • Every Möbius map is a composition of: translation \(z+b\), rotation and dilation \(az\) (\(a\ne0\)), inversion \(1/z\). For \(c\neq0\): \(T(z)=\dfrac ac-\dfrac{ad-bc}{c}\cdot\dfrac1{cz+d}\).
  • Three-point rule. Given three distinct points \(z_1,z_2,z_3\) and three distinct image points \(w_1,w_2,w_3\) there is exactly one Möbius map with \(T(z_i)=w_i\). A Möbius map fixing three points is the identity.
  • Circle-preserving. A Möbius map sends circles and lines (together “generalised circles”) to generalised circles. A circle goes to a line iff the circle passes through the pole \(z=-d/c\).
  • Symmetry. It also preserves symmetry: if \(z,z^*\) are symmetric (inverse points) in a circle, \(T(z),T(z^*)\) are symmetric in the image circle. Inverse points of \(|z|=R\): \(z^*=R^2/\bar z\).

Example: \(T(z)=\frac{2z+1}{z+2}\), matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}\), determinant \(3\ne0\); \(T(0)=\frac12\), \(T(\infty)=2\), \(T(-2)=\infty\).

Fixed points

\(T(z)=z\) gives \(cz^2+(d-a)z-b=0\).

  • At most \(2\) fixed points in \(\widehat{\mathbb C}\) unless \(T\) is the identity.
  • \(c=0\) (\(T=az+b\)): \(\infty\) is fixed; the other fixed point is \(z=\frac b{1-a}\) if \(a\ne1\). For \(a=1\) (pure translation) the only fixed point is \(\infty\).
  • \(c\ne0\): \(\infty\) is not a fixed point. Discriminant \((d-a)^2+4bc=(a+d)^2-4(ad-bc)\); one fixed point if it is \(0\) (parabolic).
  • Examples: \(\dfrac{2z+1}{z+2}\): \(z^2=1\), fixed points \(\pm1\). \(\dfrac{z-1}{z+1}\): \(z^2=-1\), fixed points \(\pm i\). \(z+1\): only \(\infty\). \(\dfrac1z\): \(\pm1\).
  • Classification by trace (normalised by \(ad-bc=1\)): parabolic if \((a+d)^2=4\), elliptic if \((a+d)^2\in[0,4)\), hyperbolic if \((a+d)^2>4\) real, loxodromic otherwise.

Cross-ratio

For distinct \(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4\):

\[(z_1,z_2;z_3,z_4)=\frac{(z_1-z_3)(z_2-z_4)}{(z_1-z_4)(z_2-z_3)}.\]

(Texts permute the points; the facts below hold for every convention. If one point is \(\infty\), delete the factors containing it.)

  • Invariance: a Möbius map preserves cross-ratios: \((Tz_1,Tz_2;Tz_3,Tz_4)=(z_1,z_2;z_3,z_4)\).
  • Reality criterion: the cross-ratio of four distinct points is real if and only if the four points lie on one circle or one straight line (concyclic or collinear in \(\widehat{\mathbb C}\)). So if the four points are on a circle, it is real (2019).
  • The Möbius map taking \(z_1,z_2,z_3\) to \(w_1,w_2,w_3\) is given implicitly by \((w,w_1;w_2,w_3)=(z,z_1;z_2,z_3)\). Explicit form for \(z_1,z_2,z_3\to0,1,\infty\): \(T(z)=\dfrac{(z-z_1)(z_2-z_3)}{(z-z_3)(z_2-z_1)}\).
  • Example: \((1,i;-1,-i)=\dfrac{(1+1)(i+i)}{(1+i)(i+1)}=\dfrac{4i}{2i}=2\), real, and the four points \(1,i,-1,-i\) lie on \(|z|=1\).

Images of lines and circles under \(w=1/z\)

Curve in \(z\)-plane Image in \(w\)-plane
circle \(\lvert z\rvert=r\) circle \(\lvert w\rvert=1/r\)
line through \(0\) (e.g. \(y=x\)) line through \(0\) (\(y=-x\) for \(y=x\))
line not through \(0\) (e.g. \(x=1\)) circle through \(0\) (\(\lvert w-\tfrac12\rvert=\tfrac12\) for \(x=1\))
circle through \(0\) (e.g. \(\lvert z-1\rvert=1\)) line not through \(0\) (\(\operatorname{Re}w=\tfrac12\))
circle not through \(0\) circle not through \(0\)
\(\operatorname{Re}z>0\) \(\operatorname{Re}w>0\)
\(\operatorname{Im}z>0\) \(\operatorname{Im}w<0\)

Inversion \(1/z\) also exchanges \(0\) and \(\infty\). \(x=c\) (\(c\neq0\)) goes to the circle with diameter from \(0\) to \(1/c\) on the real axis; \(y=c\) goes to the circle with diameter from \(0\) to \(-i/c\).

Maps between a half-plane and the disc

  • Upper half-plane to unit disc: \(w=e^{i\theta}\dfrac{z-a}{z-\bar a}\) with \(\operatorname{Im}a>0\) (\(a\mapsto0\)). With \(a=i\), \(\theta=0\): \(w=\dfrac{z-i}{z+i}\).
    • If \(z\) is real, \(|z-i|=|z+i|\), so \(|w|=1\): the real axis maps onto the unit circle (2026 Q81).
    • \(z=i\mapsto0\), \(z=0\mapsto-1\), \(z=1\mapsto-i\), \(z=\infty\mapsto1\).
    • \(\operatorname{Im}z>0\): \(z\) is closer to \(i\) than to \(-i\), so \(|w|<1\).
  • Unit disc to upper half-plane (inverse): \(z=i\dfrac{1+w}{1-w}\).
  • Right half-plane to the disc: \(w=\dfrac{z-1}{z+1}\) (\(\operatorname{Re}z>0\Rightarrow|w|<1\)).
  • Disc to disc (automorphisms): the only conformal bijections \(\mathbb D\to\mathbb D\) are \(w=e^{i\theta}\dfrac{z-a}{1-\bar az}\), \(|a|<1\) (\(a\mapsto0\)). For \(|z|=1\), \(|1-\bar az|=|z-a|\), so \(|w|=1\).
  • Upper half-plane to itself: \(w=\dfrac{az+b}{cz+d}\) with real \(a,b,c,d\) and \(ad-bc>0\). A Möbius map sending the real axis to the real axis has (after scaling) real coefficients. If \(ad-bc<0\) it swaps upper and lower half-planes.

Standard maps

\(w=z^2\). Conformal except at \(0\). \(|w|=|z|^2\), \(\arg w=2\arg z\).

  • Sector \(0<\arg z<\alpha\) goes to \(0<\arg w<2\alpha\) (for \(\alpha\le\pi\), one-one).
  • Upper half-plane goes to \(\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\); first quadrant goes onto the upper half-plane.
  • Circle \(|z|=r\) goes to \(|w|=r^2\), traversed twice.
  • Lines \(x=c\) go to parabolas \(u=c^2-\frac{v^2}{4c^2}\); hyperbolas \(xy=k\) go to the lines \(v=2k\).

\(w=e^z\). Conformal everywhere, period \(2\pi i\).

  • Horizontal line \(y=c\) goes to the ray \(\arg w=c\); vertical line \(x=c\) goes to the circle \(|w|=e^c\) (traversed infinitely often).
  • Strip \(0<y<\pi\) goes onto the upper half-plane; strip \(-\frac\pi2<y<\frac\pi2\) onto the right half-plane; strip \(0<y<2\pi\) onto \(\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\) (one-one).
  • Inverse: \(\operatorname{Log}\).

\(w=1/z\). See the table above; it is the Möbius map \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\).

\(w=z+\frac1z\) (Joukowski). Conformal except at \(\pm1\); maps \(|z|=1\) onto the segment \([-2,2]\) and \(|z|>1\) one-one onto \(\mathbb C\setminus[-2,2]\).

\(w=z^{1/2}\), \(\sin z\). \(\sin z\) maps the strip \(|x|<\frac\pi2\) one-one onto \(\mathbb C\setminus\big((-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty)\big)\); \(z^{1/2}\) (principal branch) maps \(\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]\) onto the right half-plane.

Formulas and results

  • \(f\) analytic is conformal at \(z_0\) iff \(f'(z_0)\neq0\). Angle factor at a zero of \(f'\) of order \(m-1\) is \(m\).
  • Möbius: \(T=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\), \(ad-bc\ne0\); inverse \(\frac{dw-b}{-cw+a}\); composition is matrix product.
  • Three points determine a Möbius map; \((w,w_1;w_2,w_3)=(z,z_1;z_2,z_3)\).
  • Fixed points: \(cz^2+(d-a)z-b=0\); at most \(2\) unless identity.
  • Cross-ratio is Möbius-invariant; real iff the four points are concyclic or collinear.
  • Möbius maps send generalised circles to generalised circles; circle through the pole becomes a line.
  • \(\dfrac{z-i}{z+i}\): upper half-plane to unit disc, real axis to unit circle, \(i\mapsto0\).
  • \(e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\bar az}\): disc automorphism. \(i\frac{1+w}{1-w}\): disc to upper half-plane.
  • \(z^2\): angle doubling at \(0\). \(e^z\): strip of height \(\pi\) to a half-plane. \(1/z\): \(\lvert z\rvert=r\mapsto\lvert w\rvert=1/r\).
  • Map \(z_1,z_2,z_3\to0,1,\infty\): \(\dfrac{(z-z_1)(z_2-z_3)}{(z-z_3)(z_2-z_1)}\).

Worked examples

Example 1: image of a circle under a Möbius map (2025 Q62)

\(w=\dfrac{5-4z}{4z-2}\) maps \(|z|=1\) to a circle. Solve for \(z\): \(w(4z-2)=5-4z\Rightarrow z=\dfrac{2w+5}{4(w+1)}\). Condition \(|z|=1\): \(|2w+5|^2=16|w+1|^2\). With \(w=u+iv\):

\[4(u^2+v^2)+20u+25=16(u^2+v^2)+32u+16\ \Longrightarrow\ u^2+v^2+u-\tfrac34=0 .\]

So \((u+\frac12)^2+v^2=1\): centre \(\left(-\tfrac12,0\right)\), radius \(1\). (The pole is \(z=\frac12\), inside the circle, so the image is a circle, not a line.)

Example 2: Möbius map from three points

Find the Möbius map with \(0\mapsto-1\), \(1\mapsto-i\), \(\infty\mapsto1\). Use \((w,-i;-1,1)=(z,1;0,\infty)\). Both sides are the cross-ratio that sends the three given points \(-1,-i,1\) (resp. \(0,1,\infty\)) to \(0,1,\infty\):

\[\frac{(w+1)(-i-1)}{(w-1)(-i+1)}=z .\]

The constant is \(\frac{-i-1}{-i+1}=-\frac{1+i}{1-i}=-\frac{(1+i)^2}{2}=-i\), so \(-i\,\frac{w+1}{w-1}=z\), i.e. \(\frac{w+1}{w-1}=iz\). Solving: \(w=\dfrac{1+iz}{iz-1}\). Check: \(z=0\) gives \(-1\); \(z=1\) gives \(\frac{1+i}{i-1}=-i\); \(z\to\infty\) gives \(1\).

Example 3: image of the real axis and the half-plane

\(w=\dfrac{z-i}{z+i}\) (2026 Q81). For real \(z=x\): \(|x-i|=|x+i|=\sqrt{x^2+1}\), so \(|w|=1\). The real axis together with \(\infty\) maps onto the whole unit circle (\(z=\infty\) goes to \(w=1\)). The upper half-plane goes to \(|w|<1\).

Example 4: fixed points and composition

\(T_1(z)=\dfrac{z+2}{3z+5}\) and \(T_2(z)=\dfrac{2z-1}{z+1}\). Matrices \(\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&5\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}4&1\\11&2\end{pmatrix}\), so \((T_1\circ T_2)(z)=\dfrac{4z+1}{11z+2}\). Fixed points: \(11z^2+2z=4z+1\Rightarrow11z^2-2z-1=0\), \(z=\frac{1\pm2\sqrt3}{11}\) (two fixed points).

Example 5: conformality and critical points

\(f(z)=z^3-3z\): \(f'=3z^2-3=0\) at \(z=\pm1\); \(f\) is conformal everywhere else. At \(\pm1\), \(f''=6z\neq0\), so angles are doubled. \(e^{z}\) and \(\sin z\) are conformal at all points except zeros of the derivative (\(e^z\) never, \(\sin z\) at \(\frac\pi2+n\pi\)).

Example 6: lines to circles under \(1/z\)

Image of \(x=1\): \(w=\frac1{1+iy}=\frac{1-iy}{1+y^2}\), \(u=\frac1{1+y^2}\), \(v=\frac{-y}{1+y^2}\), so \(u^2+v^2=\frac1{1+y^2}=u\): circle \((u-\tfrac12)^2+v^2=\tfrac14\). The line \(y=x\) goes to \(y=-x\).

Example 7: cross-ratio test

Are \(1,2,3,i\) concyclic? \((1,2;3,i)=\frac{(1-3)(2-i)}{(1-i)(2-3)}=\frac{-2(2-i)}{-(1-i)}=\frac{2(2-i)}{1-i}=\frac{2(2-i)(1+i)}2=3+i\), not real, so no (the first three are collinear, the fourth is off that line).

Common traps

  • Conformal needs \(f'(z_0)\ne0\) and analyticity. \(f'(z_0)=0\) (critical point) destroys conformality. \(\bar z\) preserves angle size only.
  • \(e^z\) is conformal everywhere yet not one-one on \(\mathbb C\); “conformal” and “one-one” are separate properties.
  • A Möbius map needs \(ad-bc\ne0\). Check this before using a formula; \(\frac{2z+4}{z+2}\) is the constant \(2\).
  • Image of a circle under Möbius is a line exactly when the circle passes through the pole \(-d/c\).
  • \(1/z\) takes a line not through \(0\) to a circle through \(0\), and the reverse.
  • Cross-ratio is real iff the four points are on a circle or a line; if you recall only “circle”, remember lines count as circles through \(\infty\).
  • A Möbius map that takes the real axis to itself has real coefficients only after scaling; the half-plane is preserved only if the determinant is positive.
  • \(\dfrac{z-i}{z+i}\) sends the upper half-plane inside the disc; with \(\dfrac{z+i}{z-i}\) it goes outside. Check by testing \(z=i\).
  • Three points fix a Möbius map; four do not (their cross-ratio must match).
  • A Möbius map has at most two fixed points unless it is the identity; \(z+1\) has the single fixed point \(\infty\).
  • \(z^2\) is conformal only on \(\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\) and is not one-one on \(\mathbb C\); on \(\operatorname{Re}z>0\) or the upper half-plane it is one-one.
  • For \(w=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\) the inverse is \(\frac{dw-b}{-cw+a}\): swap \(a,d\), negate \(b,c\).

Previous-year questions

2019 (unnumbered, no. 2): the cross-ratio of four points of \(\mathbb C\) is real when

Answer: the four points lie on a circle (the option “all four lie on a circle”). A cross-ratio is real iff the points are concyclic or collinear.

2025 Q62: the transformation \(w=\dfrac{5-4z}{4z-2}\) maps \(|z|=1\) to a circle in the \(w\)-plane whose centre is

Answer: (A) \(\left(-\tfrac12,0\right)\). Solved in Example 1: \(u^2+v^2+u-\frac34=0\), centre \((-\frac12,0)\), radius \(1\).

2026 Q70: \(f\) analytic and conformal at \(z_0\); then

Answer: (A) \(f'(z_0)\neq0\). Analytic and conformal at \(z_0\) is equivalent to \(f'(z_0)\neq0\); conditions such as \(f\) constant or \(f\) harmonic do not give conformality.

2026 Q81: under \(w=\dfrac{z-i}{z+i}\) the image of the real axis is

Answer: (A) the unit circle (options: unit circle, imaginary axis, real axis, circle of radius \(2\)). For real \(z\), \(|z-i|=|z+i|\), so \(|w|=1\).

Quick revision

  • Analytic and conformal at \(z_0\) iff \(f'(z_0)\ne0\); at a critical point of order \(m\) angles are multiplied by \(m\).
  • Möbius: \(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\), \(ad-bc\ne0\); group, matrix product, inverse \(\frac{dw-b}{-cw+a}\).
  • Three points determine a Möbius map; \((w,w_1;w_2,w_3)=(z,z_1;z_2,z_3)\).
  • Möbius maps send circles and lines to circles and lines; circle through the pole becomes a line.
  • Fixed points from \(cz^2+(d-a)z-b=0\): at most two (or identity).
  • Cross-ratio: invariant; real iff the four points are concyclic or collinear.
  • \(\frac{z-i}{z+i}\): upper half-plane to the unit disc, real axis to the unit circle.
  • \(e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\bar az}\): automorphisms of the disc; real \(ad-bc>0\) for the upper half-plane.
  • \(1/z\): \(\lvert z\rvert=r\mapsto1/r\); line not through \(0\leftrightarrow\) circle through \(0\).
  • \(z^2\) doubles angles at \(0\); \(e^z\) maps the strip \(0<y<\pi\) onto the upper half-plane.
  • 2025 Q62: image centre \((-\frac12,0)\), radius \(1\).
