Why it matters for the exam

Cauchy’s theorem and integral formula are the engine of the whole unit. Expect one direct question per paper: evaluate \(\oint\frac{e^{2z}}{(z+1)^4}dz\) (2025 Q63), name Liouville’s theorem (2019 Q33), a Schwarz-lemma style conclusion (2026 Q86), or a Green’s-theorem argument for \(\oint\bar f\,dz\) (2026 Q95). The statements must be known with exact hypotheses: examiners change “simply connected” or “closed curve” to trap you.

Core ideas

Contour integrals

A smooth curve is \(\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb C\) with \(\gamma'\) continuous and non-zero. A contour is a finite chain of smooth curves; it is closed if \(\gamma(a)=\gamma(b)\). For continuous \(f\) on \(\gamma\):

\[\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz=\int_a^bf(\gamma(t))\gamma'(t)\,dt .\]

Properties: linear in \(f\); reversing direction changes sign; additive over pieces; independent of parametrisation (orientation fixed).

  • If \(f=F'\) with \(F\) analytic on a region containing \(\gamma\), then \(\int_\gamma f\,dz=F(\gamma(b))-F(\gamma(a))\). For a closed curve the integral is \(0\).
  • Standard integral: for the circle \(C\): \(|z-a|=r\) traversed once anticlockwise,
\[\oint_C(z-a)^n\,dz=\begin{cases}2\pi i&n=-1\\0&n\neq-1\ (n\in\mathbb Z).\end{cases}\]
  • Length of \(\gamma\): \(L=\int_a^b|\gamma'(t)|\,dt\).

ML inequality. If \(|f(z)|\le M\) on \(\gamma\) and \(L\) is the length of \(\gamma\), then

\[\left|\int_\gamma f\,dz\right|\le ML.\]

Used to show the integral over a large arc tends to \(0\) (Jordan lemma, Fourier integrals) and to prove Liouville.

Non-analytic integrands. \(\oint_{|z|=1}\bar z\,dz=\int_0^{2\pi}e^{-it}\,ie^{it}\,dt=2\pi i\ne0\). In general, for a positively oriented simple closed curve enclosing area \(A\),

\[\oint\bar z\,dz=2iA,\qquad \oint z\,dz=0 .\]

Example (2019 Q32): \(\oint x\,dz\) over the ellipse \(\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1\) (anticlockwise). Write \(x=\frac{z+\bar z}2\): \(\oint x\,dz=\frac12\oint z\,dz+\frac12\oint\bar z\,dz=0+\frac12(2i\pi ab)=i\pi ab\).

Cauchy-Goursat theorem

Statement. If \(f\) is analytic on a simply connected domain \(D\) and \(\gamma\) is any closed contour in \(D\), then \(\oint_\gamma f\,dz=0\).

Goursat’s version needs only that \(f\) is complex differentiable (no continuity of \(f'\) assumed), first for triangles, then for polygons and general curves.

Equivalent forms:

  • Independence of path: in a simply connected domain, \(\int_\gamma f\) depends only on the endpoints, and \(F(z)=\int_{z_0}^zf\) is an analytic primitive.
  • Deformation: if \(f\) is analytic in an annular region between simple closed contours \(C_1\) (outer) and \(C_2\) (inner), both anticlockwise, then \(\oint_{C_1}f=\oint_{C_2}f\).
  • Multiply connected form: for a region bounded by an outer contour \(C\) and inner contours \(C_1,\dots,C_n\), \(\oint_Cf=\sum\oint_{C_k}f\).

The theorem fails if \(f\) has a singularity inside: \(\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}z=2\pi i\). Simply connected is needed: \(1/z\) is analytic on \(\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\) yet integrates to \(2\pi i\).

Cauchy’s integral formula

Statement. Let \(f\) be analytic on and inside a simple closed positively oriented contour \(C\) (analytic on an open set containing \(C\) and its interior). For \(a\) inside \(C\):

\[f(a)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{f(z)}{z-a}\,dz.\]

Derivatives. \(f\) has derivatives of all orders at \(a\), and

\[f^{(n)}(a)=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\,dz,\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad\oint_C\frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\,dz=\frac{2\pi i}{n!}f^{(n)}(a).\]

Consequences:

  • An analytic function is infinitely differentiable; \(f',f'',\dots\) are analytic.
  • If \(a\) is outside \(C\) the integrand is analytic inside, so the integral is \(0\).
  • If several singular points are inside \(C\), split with partial fractions or use small circles around each (deformation), or use the residue theorem (topic 4).

Cauchy’s inequality (estimates). If \(f\) is analytic on \(|z-a|\le R\) and \(|f|\le M\) on \(|z-a|=R\), then

\[|f^{(n)}(a)|\le\frac{n!\,M}{R^n}.\]

Proof: apply the ML inequality to the derivative formula (\(L=2\pi R\)).

Mean value property. If \(f\) is analytic on and inside \(|z-a|=r\):

\[f(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(a+re^{i\theta})\,d\theta .\]

(Take \(z=a+re^{i\theta}\) in the integral formula.) Harmonic functions satisfy the same property.

Morera’s theorem (converse of Cauchy)

Statement. If \(f\) is continuous on a domain \(D\) and \(\oint_Tf\,dz=0\) for every triangle \(T\) in \(D\) (equivalently, every closed contour in \(D\)), then \(f\) is analytic in \(D\).

Proof idea: \(F(z)=\int_{z_0}^zf\) is well defined and \(F'=f\), so \(F\) is analytic, hence so is \(F'=f\) (derivatives of analytic functions are analytic).

  • The hypotheses are continuity plus vanishing integrals over all triangles (or all closed curves) in \(D\).
  • Use: uniform limits of analytic functions are analytic (Weierstrass), proofs that integrals \(\int_0^1f(z,t)dt\) of analytic functions are analytic, removable-singularity arguments.
  • The triangle must lie in \(D\) together with its interior. In \(D=\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\) the function \(1/z\) is analytic although \(\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}z=2\pi i\): no triangle surrounding \(0\) lies in \(D\), so Morera is not contradicted. Vanishing over triangles is a local property; it gives vanishing over every closed curve only when \(D\) is simply connected.

Why continuity and “all triangles” are both needed. \(f(z)=\bar z\) is continuous but \(\oint_T\bar z\,dz=2i\cdot\text{Area}(T)\ne0\), so it does not satisfy the hypothesis (and is not analytic). Integrals over circles only, or over a few triangles, say nothing: the hypothesis must hold for every triangle in \(D\).

Weierstrass’s theorem (the standard use). If \(f_n\) are analytic in \(D\) and \(f_n\to f\) uniformly on compact subsets of \(D\), then \(f\) is analytic and \(f_n'\to f'\). Proof: \(f\) is continuous, and for every triangle \(T\subset D\), \(\oint_Tf\,dz=\lim\oint_Tf_n\,dz=0\) (uniform convergence on the compact set \(T\)), so Morera applies. Example: partial sums of \(\sum z^n/n!\) give \(e^z\) entire. Uniform convergence is essential: \(f_n(z)=z^n\) on \(|z|\le1\) converges pointwise to a function that is \(0\) for \(|z|<1\) and \(1\) at \(z=1\), which is not even continuous.

Parameter integrals. \(F(z)=\int_0^1e^{zt}\,dt\) is entire: it is continuous, and by Fubini \(\oint_TF\,dz=\int_0^1\oint_Te^{zt}\,dz\,dt=0\).

Liouville’s theorem

Statement. A bounded entire function is constant.

Proof: \(|f|\le M\) everywhere. Cauchy’s inequality at \(a=0\): \(|f'(0)|\le M/R\) for every \(R\). Let \(R\to\infty\): \(f'(0)=0\). The same at every point gives \(f'\equiv0\).

Extensions:

  • If \(f\) entire and \(|f(z)|\le A+B|z|^k\) for all \(z\) then \(f\) is a polynomial of degree \(\le k\).
  • If \(f\) is entire and \(f(\mathbb C)\) misses a disc (is not dense), \(f\) is constant. Picard’s little theorem (stronger): a non-constant entire function takes every complex value with at most one exception. \(e^z\) omits \(0\).
  • Entire and \(\operatorname{Re}f\ge0\) (or \(\operatorname{Re}f\) bounded one side) implies constant: \(e^{-f}\) is entire and bounded.
  • If \(f\) is entire and \(|f(z)|\le|z|^k\) for all \(z\) (\(k\ge1\) an integer), then \(f(z)=az^k\) with \(|a|\le1\): the inequality forces a zero of order \(\ge k\) at \(0\), so \(f/z^k\) is entire and bounded by \(1\).

Fundamental theorem of algebra

Every non-constant polynomial \(p\) over \(\mathbb C\) has a root. Proof: if \(p\) has no zeros, \(1/p\) is entire. Since \(|p(z)|\to\infty\) as \(|z|\to\infty\), \(1/p\) is bounded (it is bounded on a large disc by compactness and small outside). Liouville makes \(1/p\), hence \(p\), constant. So a polynomial of degree \(n\) has exactly \(n\) roots counted with multiplicity.

Maximum modulus principle

Statement. If \(f\) is analytic and non-constant on a domain \(D\), then \(|f|\) has no local maximum in \(D\).

  • If \(D\) is bounded and \(f\) is non-constant, analytic on \(D\) and continuous on \(\overline D\), then \(\max_{\overline D}|f|\) is attained on the boundary \(\partial D\) only: \(|f(z)|\le\max_{\partial D}|f|\).
  • Minimum modulus: if \(f\) is non-constant analytic and zero-free on \(D\) then \(|f|\) has no local minimum in \(D\) (apply to \(1/f\)). If \(f\) has a zero, the minimum \(0\) is attained inside.
  • The principle needs \(D\) bounded for the boundary form. \(f(z)=\exp(e^z)\) on the strip \(\lvert\operatorname{Im}z\rvert<\pi/2\) shows what can go wrong on unbounded domains: on the boundary lines \(e^z\) is purely imaginary, so \(\lvert f\rvert=1\) there, yet inside, on the real axis, \(f(x)=\exp(e^x)\to\infty\). So \(\lvert f\rvert\le\max_{\partial D}\lvert f\rvert\) fails.
  • \(\operatorname{Re}f\), \(\operatorname{Im}f\) (harmonic) obey the maximum principle too.
  • Example: on \(|z|\le1\), \(|e^z|=e^x\) has maximum \(e\), attained at the boundary point \(z=1\) only.

Schwarz lemma

Statement. Let \(f\) be analytic on the open unit disc \(\mathbb D\) with \(|f(z)|\le1\) there and \(f(0)=0\). Then

\[|f(z)|\le|z|\ (z\in\mathbb D),\qquad|f'(0)|\le1 .\]

If \(|f(z_0)|=|z_0|\) for some \(z_0\ne0\), or \(|f'(0)|=1\), then \(f(z)=\lambda z\) with \(|\lambda|=1\) (a rotation).

Proof: \(g(z)=f(z)/z\) (with \(g(0)=f'(0)\)) is analytic; on \(|z|=r<1\), \(|g|\le1/r\). By maximum modulus \(|g|\le1/r\) in \(|z|\le r\). Let \(r\to1\).

  • General versions: for \(f\) with \(|f|\le M\) on \(|z|<R\) and \(f(0)=0\), \(|f(z)|\le M|z|/R\).
  • If \(f\) has a zero of order \(\ge k\) at \(0\) and \(|f|\le1\) on \(\mathbb D\), then \(|f(z)|\le|z|^k\).
  • Variant (2026 Q86): \(f\) entire, \(|f(z)|\le|z|^3\) on \(\mathbb C\) and \(f(0)=f'(0)=0\). Then \(g=f/z^3\) is entire and \(|g|\le1\), so \(g\) is constant by Liouville, \(f(z)=az^3\) with \(|a|\le1\).
  • Schwarz-Pick: for \(f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb D\) analytic, \(|f'(z)|\le\dfrac{1-|f(z)|^2}{1-|z|^2}\). Analytic bijections of \(\mathbb D\) onto itself are \(e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\bar az}\).

Other consequences to know

  • An analytic function on \(\mathbb D\), continuous on \(\overline{\mathbb D}\), is determined by its boundary values on \(|z|=1\) (Cauchy formula).
  • Open mapping theorem: a non-constant analytic function on a domain maps open sets to open sets.
  • Analytic function with constant modulus is constant (also from maximum modulus or open mapping).
  • Cauchy integral formula for exterior: for \(a\) outside \(C\), \(\oint_C\frac{f(z)}{z-a}dz=0\).
  • Winding number \(n(\gamma,a)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma\frac{dz}{z-a}\) counts how many times \(\gamma\) goes around \(a\). General Cauchy formula: \(n(\gamma,a)f(a)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma\frac{f(z)}{z-a}dz\).

Formulas and results

  • \(\int_\gamma f\,dz=\int_a^bf(\gamma(t))\gamma'(t)dt\). ML bound: \(|\int_\gamma f|\le ML\).
  • \(\oint_{|z-a|=r}(z-a)^ndz=2\pi i\) if \(n=-1\), else \(0\).
  • \(\oint\bar z\,dz=2iA\) (area \(A\), anticlockwise simple curve).
  • Cauchy-Goursat: analytic on simply connected \(D\) implies \(\oint_\gamma f=0\).
  • Cauchy integral: \(f(a)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint\frac{f(z)}{z-a}dz\); \(f^{(n)}(a)=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}dz\).
  • Cauchy estimate: \(|f^{(n)}(a)|\le n!M/R^n\).
  • Mean value: \(f(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(a+re^{i\theta})d\theta\).
  • Morera: continuous with zero integrals around every triangle implies analytic.
  • Liouville: bounded entire implies constant. FTA via Liouville.
  • Maximum modulus: no interior maximum for non-constant analytic; boundary maximum on bounded domains.
  • Schwarz: \(f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb D\), \(f(0)=0\) gives \(|f(z)|\le|z|\), \(|f'(0)|\le1\), equality only for rotations.

Worked examples

Example 1: Cauchy formula with a higher derivative (2025 Q63)

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\oint_{|z|=2}\frac{e^{2z}}{(z+1)^4}\,dz\).

The point \(z=-1\) is inside \(|z|=2\) and \(e^{2z}\) is entire. With \(n=3\): \(\oint\frac{f(z)}{(z+1)^4}dz=\frac{2\pi i}{3!}f'''(-1)\), \(f=e^{2z}\), \(f'''=8e^{2z}\). So the integral is \(\frac{2\pi i}{6}\cdot8e^{-2}=\dfrac{8\pi i}{3e^2}\).

Example 2: two singular points

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\oint_{|z|=3}\frac{dz}{z^2+1}\).

Partial fractions: \(\frac1{z^2+1}=\frac1{2i}\left(\frac1{z-i}-\frac1{z+i}\right)\). Both \(\pm i\) lie inside, each integral gives \(2\pi i\), so the total is \(\frac1{2i}(2\pi i-2\pi i)=0\). (Alternative: \(f(z)=1/(z^2+1)=O(1/z^2)\) so \(|\oint|\le\frac{1}{R^2-1}2\pi R\to0\) as \(R\) grows, and the integral is independent of \(R>1\), hence zero.)

Example 3: ML bound

Bound \(\left|\displaystyle\int_\gamma\frac{dz}{z^2+1}\right|\) where \(\gamma\) is the upper half of \(|z|=R\), \(R>1\). On \(|z|=R\), \(|z^2+1|\ge R^2-1\), so \(|f|\le\frac1{R^2-1}\), \(L=\pi R\), and the integral is at most \(\dfrac{\pi R}{R^2-1}\to0\).

Example 4: Liouville application

An entire \(f\) satisfies \(|f(z)|\le|z|^{3/2}\) for all \(z\). Show \(f\equiv0\). From \(|f|\le A+B|z|^{3/2}\), Cauchy’s estimate gives \(f^{(n)}(0)=0\) for \(n\ge2\) (\(|f^{(n)}(0)|\le n!BR^{3/2}/R^n\to0\)). So \(f=az+b\). \(f(0)=0\) gives \(b=0\). Then \(|az|\le|z|^{3/2}\) for \(|z|<1\) gives \(|a|\le|z|^{1/2}\to0\), so \(a=0\).

Example 5: integrals with \(\bar z\) and \(|z|\)

On \(|z|=2\): \(|z|=2\) is constant, so \(\oint|z|\,dz=2\oint dz=0\). And \(\bar z=4/z\) on the circle, so \(\oint_{|z|=2}\bar z\,dz=4\oint\frac{dz}z=8\pi i\). This agrees with \(2i\times\text{area}=2i\cdot4\pi\). The function \(\bar z\) is not analytic, so Cauchy’s theorem does not apply.

Example 6: Schwarz lemma

If \(f\) is analytic on \(\mathbb D\), \(|f|<1\), \(f(0)=0\), find the best bound on \(|f(1/2)|\) and \(|f'(0)|\). \(|f(1/2)|\le1/2\), \(|f'(0)|\le1\). If moreover \(f(1/2)=1/2\) then \(f(z)=z\).

Common traps

  • Cauchy’s theorem needs analytic on all of the interior. A single singular point inside ruins it: \(\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}z=2\pi i\).
  • Liouville needs entire and bounded. \(\sin z\) is entire but unbounded; \(1/(1+z^2)\) is bounded on \(|z|>2\) but not entire; \(e^{-|z|^2}\) is bounded but not analytic.
  • A non-constant bounded analytic function on the disc exists (\(z\) on \(\mathbb D\)): Liouville is only for \(\mathbb C\).
  • Maximum modulus: \(|f|\) can have an interior minimum only at a zero. \(\operatorname{Re}f\) obeys max and min principles, \(|f|\) obeys only the max.
  • Morera needs continuity and all triangles; and the conclusion is analyticity, not constancy. Do not confuse it with the Green’s-theorem argument for \(\oint\bar f\,dz=0\) (2026 Q95), which forces \(f\) constant.
  • \(\oint_{|z|=r}\frac{dz}{z-a}=2\pi i\) only if \(|a|<r\); it is \(0\) if \(|a|>r\).
  • The Cauchy formula uses \(\frac1{2\pi i}\) and \(\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\); mixing in \((n-1)!\) or forgetting \(n!\) is the commonest slip. For \(\oint\frac{f}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\) the power \(n+1\) corresponds to the \(n\)-th derivative.
  • The Schwarz lemma needs \(f(0)=0\) and \(|f|\le1\) on the unit disc. Without \(f(0)=0\) use Schwarz-Pick.
  • \(\oint\bar z\,dz\neq0\) (equals \(2i\times\) area).
  • ML inequality: the bound is \(M\cdot L\) where \(L\) is the length of the path, not the area, and \(M\) is a maximum of \(|f|\) on the path.

Previous-year questions

2019 Q32: \(\oint x\,dz\) over the ellipse \(\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1\)

Answer: \(i\pi ab\). \(x=(z+\bar z)/2\); \(\oint z\,dz=0\) and \(\oint\bar z\,dz=2i\cdot\text{Area}=2i\pi ab\). So \(\oint x\,dz=i\pi ab\). The orientation is anticlockwise; reversing it changes the sign. (A mark of \(\pi ab\), without the factor \(i\), would be a slip: the integral is purely imaginary.)

2019 Q33: name of the result “a bounded entire function is constant”

Answer: Liouville’s theorem. This is the statement of that theorem.

2025 Q63: \(\oint_{|z|=2}\frac{e^{2z}}{(z+1)^4}dz\)

Answer: (B) \(\dfrac{8\pi i}{3e^2}\). Cauchy formula for the third derivative: \(\frac{2\pi i}{3!}\cdot8e^{-2}\).

2026 Q86: \(f\) analytic with \(|f(z)|\le|z|^3\) and \(f(0)=f'(0)=0\); then

Answer: (A) \(f(z)=az^3\) (with \(|a|\le1\)). Options were \(az^3\), \(0\), \(az^2\), \(az\). \(f/z^3\) is entire and bounded by \(1\), so constant by Liouville. (The condition \(|f|\le|z|^3\) is assumed on all of \(\mathbb C\); \(f=0\) is a special case, but the form \(az^3\) is the general conclusion.)

2026 Q95: \(f\) analytic in a simply connected domain and \(\oint\overline{f(z)}\,dz=0\) for all closed \(C\); \(f\) must be

Answer: (D) constant (options: harmonic, analytic, zero, constant). The integrand is \(\overline{f(z)}\), with an overbar. Without the bar every analytic \(f\) would work (Cauchy-Goursat). With the bar: Green’s theorem gives \(\oint\bar f\,dz=2i\iint\overline{f'}\,dA\), which vanishes for every region only if \(f'\equiv0\).

Quick revision

  • \(\oint_{|z-a|=r}(z-a)^ndz=2\pi i\,\delta_{n,-1}\).
  • ML: \(|\int_\gamma f|\le ML\), \(L=\) length.
  • Cauchy-Goursat: analytic on simply connected domain gives closed integrals zero.
  • Cauchy formula: \(f(a)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint\frac{f}{z-a}\); \(f^{(n)}(a)=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint\frac f{(z-a)^{n+1}}\).
  • Cauchy estimate: \(|f^{(n)}(a)|\le n!M/R^n\).
  • Mean value: \(f(a)\) equals the average of \(f\) on any circle about \(a\).
  • Morera: continuous plus zero triangle integrals implies analytic.
  • Liouville: bounded entire implies constant; FTA follows from it.
  • Maximum modulus: non-constant analytic \(f\) has no interior maximum of \(|f|\).
  • Schwarz: \(f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb D\), \(f(0)=0\) gives \(|f(z)|\le|z|\), \(|f'(0)|\le1\).
