Why it matters for the exam

Every paper has one or two direct questions here: find \(f\) from its real part (2025 Q64), principal value of a logarithm (2019 Q31), which of a list of functions is analytic on all of \(\mathbb C\) (2019 Q100), or a Cauchy-Riemann condition that no analytic function can satisfy (2026 Q100). The ideas (Cauchy-Riemann, harmonic conjugates, Milne-Thomson, the branches of \(\log\)) are also the tools used in every later topic. Learn them completely.

Core ideas

Limits and continuity

\(\lim_{z\to z_0} f(z)=L\) means: for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there is \(\delta>0\) with \(|f(z)-L|<\varepsilon\) whenever \(0<|z-z_0|<\delta\). The point \(z\) may approach \(z_0\) from any direction in the plane, so the limit must be the same along every path.

  • \(f=u+iv\) has limit \(L=a+ib\) at \(z_0=x_0+iy_0\) if and only if \(u\to a\) and \(v\to b\) as \((x,y)\to(x_0,y_0)\).
  • To show a limit does not exist, find two paths with different limits. Example: \(\dfrac{z}{\bar z}\) at \(0\) tends to \(1\) along the real axis and to \(-1\) along the imaginary axis.
  • \(f\) is continuous at \(z_0\) if \(\lim_{z\to z_0}f(z)=f(z_0)\). Sums, products, quotients (denominator \(\neq 0\)) and compositions of continuous functions are continuous. \(f\) is continuous iff \(u\) and \(v\) are.
  • \(\lim_{z\to\infty}f(z)=L\) means \(\lim_{w\to0}f(1/w)=L\).

Complex differentiability

\(f'(z_0)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(z_0+h)-f(z_0)}{h}\)

where \(h\to 0\) in any manner in \(\mathbb C\). This is much stronger than real differentiability of \((u,v)\).

  • Differentiable at \(z_0\) implies continuous at \(z_0\). The converse is false (\(\bar z\), \(|z|\)).
  • The usual rules (sum, product, quotient, chain rule, power rule \((z^n)'=nz^{n-1}\)) hold word for word.
  • \(f(z)=\bar z\) is nowhere differentiable: the quotient is \(\bar h/h\), which is \(1\) along the real axis and \(-1\) along the imaginary axis.
  • \(f(z)=|z|^2=z\bar z\) is differentiable only at \(z=0\) (where \(f'(0)=0\)), and nowhere analytic.

Analytic (holomorphic) and entire functions

\(f\) is analytic at \(z_0\) if it is differentiable at every point of some open disc around \(z_0\). \(f\) is analytic on an open set \(D\) if it is analytic at each point of \(D\). An entire function is analytic on all of \(\mathbb C\).

“Differentiable at a point” and “analytic at a point” are different: \(|z|^2\) is differentiable at \(0\) but analytic nowhere. On an open set the two words mean the same.

A function analytic on an open set is automatically infinitely differentiable, and all its derivatives are analytic (this is proved from Cauchy’s integral formula in the next topic). So \(u,v\) are \(C^\infty\).

Cauchy-Riemann equations

Necessary condition. If \(f=u+iv\) is differentiable at \(z_0=x_0+iy_0\), then the first partials of \(u,v\) exist at \((x_0,y_0)\) and

\(u_x=v_y,\qquad u_y=-v_x .\)

Proof idea: let \(h\to0\) along the real axis, then along the imaginary axis. The two limits are \(u_x+iv_x\) and \(v_y-iu_y\); they must be equal.

Then \(f'(z_0)=u_x+iv_x=v_y-iu_y\).

Sufficient condition. If \(u,v\) have continuous first-order partial derivatives in a neighbourhood of \((x_0,y_0)\) and satisfy the CR equations at \((x_0,y_0)\), then \(f\) is differentiable at \(z_0\). (Weaker: \(u,v\) real-differentiable at the point and CR holds there.)

On an open set: \(f\) is analytic iff \(u,v\) are \(C^1\) and satisfy CR everywhere.

CR equations alone, without continuity of the partials, are not sufficient. Standard counterexample: \(f(z)=z^5/|z|^4\) for \(z\ne0\), \(f(0)=0\). At \(0\) it satisfies CR (on the axes \(f(x)=x\), \(f(iy)=iy\)), yet \(f(z)/z=e^{4i\theta}\) has no limit.

Polar form (\(z=re^{i\theta}\), \(r>0\)):

\(u_r=\frac{1}{r}v_\theta,\qquad v_r=-\frac1r u_\theta,\qquad f'(z)=e^{-i\theta}(u_r+iv_r).\)

Complex form: CR is the single equation \(\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial\bar z}=0\). A function whose expression involves \(\bar z\), \(|z|\), \(\operatorname{Re}z\), \(\operatorname{Im}z\) in an essential way is not analytic.

Consequences of Cauchy-Riemann

If \(f\) is analytic on a domain (open connected set) \(D\) and any one of the following holds, then \(f\) is constant:

  • \(f'\equiv0\);
  • \(\operatorname{Re}f\) is constant, or \(\operatorname{Im}f\) is constant;
  • \(|f|\) is constant;
  • \(\bar f\) is also analytic;
  • \(f\) is real-valued.

Proof for constant \(|f|=c\): \(u^2+v^2=c^2\); differentiate in \(x\) and \(y\) and use CR to get \(u_x=v_x=0\). For \(c=0\) trivial.

Harmonic functions and harmonic conjugates

A real function \(u(x,y)\) of class \(C^2\) is harmonic if \(u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\).

  • If \(f=u+iv\) is analytic, then \(u\) and \(v\) are both harmonic (from CR: \(u_{xx}=v_{yx}=v_{xy}=-u_{yy}\)).
  • \(v\) is a harmonic conjugate of \(u\) if \(u+iv\) is analytic. Then \(-u\) is a conjugate of \(v\).
  • On a simply connected domain every harmonic \(u\) has a harmonic conjugate, unique up to an additive real constant.
  • Not on every domain: \(u=\ln|z|=\ln r\) is harmonic on \(\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\) but has no conjugate there (the candidate \(\theta=\arg z\) is not single valued). The domain \(\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\) is not simply connected.
  • The CR condition is on the pair: two harmonic functions \(u,v\) are not automatically conjugates. \(u=x\), \(v=x\) are both harmonic but \(x+ix\) is not analytic.
  • If \(f=u+iv\) is analytic then \(u^2-v^2=\operatorname{Re}f^2\) and \(uv=\tfrac12\operatorname{Im}f^2\) are harmonic. But \(u^2+v^2=|f|^2\) is not harmonic unless \(f\) is constant, and \(|f|^2\) is subharmonic.
  • If \(f\) is analytic and zero-free then \(\ln|f|=\operatorname{Re}\log f\) is harmonic locally.
  • Laplacian of \(|f|^2\): \(\Delta|f|^2=4|f'|^2\).

Constructing \(f\) from \(u\) or \(v\)

Method 1 (integrate CR): from \(u\) compute \(v_y=u_x\) and \(v_x=-u_y\), integrate, fix the function of the other variable.

Method 2 (Milne-Thomson): since \(f'(z)=u_x-iu_y\) and both sides are analytic functions of \(z\), put \(x=z\), \(y=0\):

\(f'(z)=u_x(z,0)-i\,u_y(z,0),\qquad f(z)=\int f'(z)\,dz + C.\)

If \(v\) is given: \(f'(z)=v_y(z,0)+i\,v_x(z,0)\).

If \(u\) is given the constant \(C\) is purely imaginary (\(C=ic\), \(c\) real) when \(u\) is the real part; if \(v\) is given the constant is real. If \(f(0)\) or \(f(z_0)\) is specified, use it to fix \(C\).

Elementary functions

Exponential. \(e^z=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)\).

  • Entire, \((e^z)'=e^z\), never zero, \(|e^z|=e^x\), \(\arg e^z=y\).
  • Periodic with period \(2\pi i\): \(e^{z+2\pi i}=e^z\). Not one-one on \(\mathbb C\).
  • \(e^z=w\) (\(w\neq0\)) has infinitely many solutions \(z=\ln|w|+i(\arg w+2\pi k)\).
  • Unbounded on \(\mathbb C\) (but \(|e^z|\le1\) on the left half plane).

Trigonometric. \(\sin z=\dfrac{e^{iz}-e^{-iz}}{2i}\), \(\cos z=\dfrac{e^{iz}+e^{-iz}}{2}\). Entire, period \(2\pi\), all real identities persist (\(\sin^2z+\cos^2z=1\), addition formulas).

  • \(\sin(x+iy)=\sin x\cosh y+i\cos x\sinh y\), \(\cos(x+iy)=\cos x\cosh y-i\sin x\sinh y\).
  • \(|\sin z|^2=\sin^2x+\sinh^2y\), \(|\cos z|^2=\cos^2x+\sinh^2y\).
  • Unbounded on \(\mathbb C\) (as \(y\to\infty\), \(|\sin z|\to\infty\)). So \(|\sin z|\le1\) is false in general.
  • Zeros: \(\sin z=0\) only at \(z=n\pi\); \(\cos z=0\) only at \(z=\frac\pi2+n\pi\). All zeros are real and simple.
  • \(\tan z=\sin z/\cos z\) has simple poles at \(\frac\pi2+n\pi\); not entire.

Hyperbolic. \(\sinh z=\dfrac{e^z-e^{-z}}2\), \(\cosh z=\dfrac{e^z+e^{-z}}2\). Entire, period \(2\pi i\). \(\cosh z=\cos(iz)\), \(\sinh z=-i\sin(iz)\). Zeros: \(\sinh z=0\) at \(z=in\pi\); \(\cosh z=0\) at \(z=i(\frac\pi2+n\pi)\). \(\cosh^2z-\sinh^2z=1\).

Logarithm

For \(z\neq0\): \(\log z=\ln|z|+i\arg z\), where \(\arg z=\theta_0+2\pi k\), \(k\in\mathbb Z\). So \(\log z\) is multi-valued with infinitely many values differing by \(2\pi i\).

  • Principal value \(\operatorname{Log}z=\ln|z|+i\operatorname{Arg}z\), with \(-\pi<\operatorname{Arg}z\le\pi\).
  • \(\operatorname{Log}z\) is analytic on \(\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]\) (the plane cut along the non-positive real axis) with derivative \(1/z\). It is discontinuous across the negative real axis.
  • Branch point: \(0\) (and \(\infty\)). Other branches: choose \(\arg z\in(\alpha,\alpha+2\pi)\); the cut is a ray from \(0\).
  • \(e^{\log z}=z\) always. \(\log e^z=z+2\pi ik\); \(\operatorname{Log}e^z=z\) only if \(-\pi<\operatorname{Im}z\le\pi\).
  • \(\log(z_1z_2)=\log z_1+\log z_2\) holds as sets of values, but \(\operatorname{Log}(z_1z_2)\neq\operatorname{Log}z_1+\operatorname{Log}z_2\) in general. Example: \(z_1=z_2=-1\): \(\operatorname{Log}(1)=0\) but \(\operatorname{Log}(-1)+\operatorname{Log}(-1)=2\pi i\).
  • Values to know: \(\operatorname{Log}(-1)=i\pi\), \(\operatorname{Log}i=i\pi/2\), \(\operatorname{Log}(-i)=-i\pi/2\), \(\operatorname{Log}(1+i)=\frac12\ln2+i\frac\pi4\), \(\operatorname{Log}(-1+i\sqrt3)=\ln2+\frac{2\pi i}{3}\).

Complex powers

\(z^a=e^{a\log z}\) (\(z\ne0\)). Multi-valued unless \(a\) is an integer.

  • \(a=p/q\) in lowest terms: exactly \(q\) values. \(z^{1/n}\) has \(n\) values \(|z|^{1/n}e^{i(\theta+2\pi k)/n}\).
  • \(a\) irrational real or non-real: infinitely many values.
  • Principal value: use \(\operatorname{Log}\). \(i^i=e^{i\log i}=e^{-\pi/2-2\pi k}\), all real; principal value \(e^{-\pi/2}\approx0.2079\).
  • \((1+i)^i\) principal value \(=e^{i(\frac12\ln2+i\frac\pi4)}=e^{-\pi/4}\left(\cos\frac{\ln2}{2}+i\sin\frac{\ln2}{2}\right)\).
  • \(z^{1/2}\) with a branch cut is analytic off the cut; \(\sqrt z\) is not entire.

Inverse trigonometric functions

\(\sin^{-1}z=-i\log\left(iz+\sqrt{1-z^2}\right)\), \(\cos^{-1}z=-i\log\left(z+i\sqrt{1-z^2}\right)\), \(\tan^{-1}z=\frac{1}{2i}\log\frac{1+iz}{1-iz}\). All multi-valued. \(\sin z=2\) has solutions \(z=\frac\pi2\pm i\ln(2+\sqrt3)+2\pi n\), so \(\sin z\) takes the value \(2\) (real trig is bounded, complex is not).

Which standard functions are analytic on all of \(\mathbb C\)?

Function Entire? Reason
polynomial, \(e^z\), \(\sin z\), \(\cos z\), \(\sinh z\), \(\cosh z\) yes power series with infinite radius
sums, products, compositions of entire functions yes rules of differentiation
\(\bar z\), \(\operatorname{Re}z\), \(\operatorname{Im}z\), \(\lvert z\rvert\), \(\lvert z\rvert^2\) no CR fails (or holds only at \(0\))
\(1/z\), rational function with a pole no poles
\(\tan z\), \(\cot z\), \(\sec z\), \(\csc z\), \(\tanh z\) no poles at zeros of denominator

Formulas and results

  • CR (Cartesian): \(u_x=v_y\), \(u_y=-v_x\). CR (polar): \(u_r=v_\theta/r\), \(v_r=-u_\theta/r\).
  • \(f'=u_x+iv_x=v_y-iu_y=e^{-i\theta}(u_r+iv_r)\).
  • Sufficient: continuous partials in a neighbourhood plus CR at the point.
  • Harmonic: \(\Delta u=0\). Analytic implies \(u,v\) harmonic. Conjugate exists on simply connected domains.
  • Milne-Thomson: \(f'(z)=u_x(z,0)-iu_y(z,0)=v_y(z,0)+iv_x(z,0)\).
  • \(\Delta|f|^2=4|f'|^2\).
  • \(e^z=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)\), period \(2\pi i\), never \(0\).
  • \(|\sin z|^2=\sin^2x+\sinh^2y\); \(|\cos z|^2=\cos^2x+\sinh^2y\).
  • \(\log z=\ln|z|+i(\operatorname{Arg}z+2\pi k)\); \(\operatorname{Log}\) analytic off \((-\infty,0]\).
  • \(z^a=e^{a\log z}\); \(i^i=e^{-\pi/2}\) (principal).
  • \(\sinh z=-i\sin iz\), \(\cosh z=\cos iz\).
  • Constant \(\operatorname{Re}f\), \(\operatorname{Im}f\), \(|f|\) or \(\arg f\) on a domain forces \(f\) constant.

Worked examples

Example 1: where is \(f(z)=|z|^2\) differentiable?

\(u=x^2+y^2\), \(v=0\). CR: \(u_x=2x=v_y=0\) and \(u_y=2y=-v_x=0\). Both hold only at \((0,0)\). The partials are continuous, so \(f\) is differentiable at \(0\) and nowhere else. It is analytic nowhere because no open disc lies in \(\{0\}\).

Example 2: find \(f\) with \(u=e^x(x\cos y-y\sin y)\) (2025 Q64)

Compute \(u_x=e^x(x\cos y-y\sin y+\cos y)\) and \(u_y=e^x(-x\sin y-\sin y-y\cos y)\). Put \(x=z\), \(y=0\): \(u_x=e^z(z+1)\), \(u_y=0\). Hence \(f'(z)=(z+1)e^z\). Integrate: \(f(z)=ze^z+C\) (since \(\int(z+1)e^zdz=ze^z\)). The real part of \(ze^z\) is exactly \(u\), so \(C\) is purely imaginary. Without further data the answer is \(ze^z+C\).

Example 3: find \(f\) from \(v=3x^2y-y^3\)

\(v_y=3x^2-3y^2\), \(v_x=6xy\). Put \(y=0\), \(x=z\): \(f'(z)=v_y+iv_x=3z^2\). So \(f(z)=z^3+C\) with \(C\) real. Check: \(\operatorname{Im}z^3=3x^2y-y^3\).

Example 4: choose constants so that \(f\) is analytic

\(f=(x^2+2axy+by^2)+i(cx^2+2dxy+y^2)\). CR: \(u_x=2x+2ay=v_y=2dx+2y\) gives \(d=1\), \(a=1\). \(u_y=2ax+2by=-v_x=-2cx-2dy\) gives \(c=-a=-1\), \(b=-d=-1\). So \(a=1,b=-1,c=-1,d=1\), and \(f=(1-i)z^2\).

Example 5: polar CR

Given \(u=r^2\cos2\theta-r\cos\theta\). Polar CR: \(v_\theta=ru_r=2r^2\cos2\theta-r\cos\theta\), so \(v=r^2\sin2\theta-r\sin\theta+g(r)\). Then \(v_r=2r\sin2\theta-\sin\theta+g'(r)\) must equal \(-u_\theta/r=2r\sin2\theta-\sin\theta\), so \(g'=0\). Therefore \(f(z)=z^2-z+ic\).

Example 6: values of logarithms and powers

  • \(\operatorname{Log}(-1+i\sqrt3)\): modulus \(2\), \(\operatorname{Arg}=\frac{2\pi}3\) (second quadrant, \(\tan^{-1}(\sqrt3/(-1))\) adjusted). Answer \(\ln2+i\frac{2\pi}{3}\) (2019 Q31).
  • \(\log(-1)=i(2k+1)\pi\).
  • \(1^{\sqrt2}=e^{\sqrt2\cdot2\pi ik}\): infinitely many values, only \(k=0\) gives \(1\).
  • \(i^{i}=e^{i\cdot i(\pi/2+2\pi k)}=e^{-\pi/2-2\pi k}\).

Common traps

  • CR at a point is not enough for differentiability; you also need continuity of the partials (or real differentiability). Examiners use \(z^5/|z|^4\)-type or \(\sqrt{|xy|}\) examples.
  • Differentiable at a point is not analytic at the point. \(|z|^2\) satisfies CR only at \(0\), which is a set with empty interior.
  • \(\operatorname{Log}\) is not entire; \(\operatorname{Log}(z_1z_2)\neq\operatorname{Log}z_1+\operatorname{Log}z_2\).
  • \(\operatorname{Arg}z\in(-\pi,\pi]\). Arguments in the second and third quadrants need the correct \(\pm\pi\) adjustment: \(\operatorname{Arg}(-1+i\sqrt3)=2\pi/3\), not \(-\pi/3\).
  • \(\sin z\) and \(\cos z\) are unbounded; \(\sin z=2\) has solutions; zeros of \(\sin,\cos\) are only the real ones.
  • \(\tan z\), \(\cot z\), \(\bar z\), \(1/z\) are not entire. Polynomial and \(\cosh z\) are.
  • Harmonic conjugate need not exist on non-simply-connected domains (\(\ln r\) on \(\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\)).
  • \(u\) and \(v\) both harmonic does not make \(u+iv\) analytic; CR must hold.
  • If \(u\) is given the arbitrary constant is imaginary; if \(v\) is given it is real. Do not put a real constant in \(f=ze^z+C\) when only \(\operatorname{Re}f\) is specified.
  • \(e^z\) is not one-one and has period \(2\pi i\); \(i^i\) is real.
  • An analytic function with constant modulus, or constant real part, is constant. \(|f|^2\) harmonic forces \(f\) constant.

Previous-year questions

2019 Q31: principal value of \(\log(-1+i\sqrt3)\)

Answer: \(\log2+i\frac{2\pi}{3}\). Modulus \(=\sqrt{1+3}=2\). The point is in the second quadrant with reference angle \(\pi/3\), so \(\operatorname{Arg}=\pi-\pi/3=2\pi/3\). Hence \(\operatorname{Log}=\ln2+\frac{2\pi i}3\). (Source: a re-typed copy of the paper in a solution video; presenter’s tick is on this option.)

2019 Q100: which of \(\bar z\), a polynomial, \(\tan z\), \(\cosh z\) are analytic on all of \(\mathbb C\)?

Answer: the polynomial and \(\cosh z\) only. \(\bar z\) fails CR (\(u_x=1\), \(v_y=-1\)). \(\tan z\) has poles at \(\frac\pi2+n\pi\). A polynomial and \(\cosh z\) are entire. (Option read from the presenter’s tick in the recovered source: “(ii) and (iv)”.)

2025 Q64: analytic \(f=u+iv\) with \(u=e^x(x\cos y-y\sin y)\)

Answer: (A) \(ze^z+C\). Milne-Thomson: \(f'(z)=u_x(z,0)-iu_y(z,0)=(z+1)e^z\), so \(f=ze^z+C\). The other options (\(e^z\), \(ze^{-z}\), \(e^{-z}\)) have different real parts (e.g. \(\operatorname{Re}e^z=e^x\cos y\)).

2026 Q100: entire \(f=u+iv\) with \(u^2+v^2=e^{2x}+y^2\) and \(f(0)=1\); determine \(f\)

Answer: (D), taken to read “no such function”. The worked key says option D was cut off in the photograph, so this is the key’s reading and is not fully certain. Reason: \(|f|^2=e^{2x}+y^2>0\) everywhere, so \(f\) has no zeros. Then \(f=e^g\) with \(g\) entire (a zero-free entire function has an entire logarithm), so \(\ln|f|^2=2\operatorname{Re}g\) must be harmonic. But with \(h=e^{2x}+y^2\) one gets \(h\,\Delta h-|\nabla h|^2=2e^{2x}+4y^2e^{2x}-2y^2\), which equals \(2\neq0\) at the origin, so \(\ln h\) is not harmonic. Options \(e^z\), \(e^z+iz\), \(e^z+z\) give moduli that do not match (e.g. \(|e^z|^2=e^{2x}\)).

Quick revision

  • CR: \(u_x=v_y\), \(u_y=-v_x\); polar \(u_r=v_\theta/r\), \(v_r=-u_\theta/r\); \(f'=u_x+iv_x\).
  • Analytic needs differentiability on an open set; CR plus continuous partials gives differentiable.
  • \(|z|^2\) is differentiable only at \(0\); \(\bar z\) nowhere.
  • Harmonic: \(\Delta u=0\); conjugate exists on simply connected domains, unique up to a constant.
  • Milne-Thomson: \(f'(z)=u_x(z,0)-iu_y(z,0)\), or \(v_y(z,0)+iv_x(z,0)\).
  • Constant \(|f|\), \(\operatorname{Re}f\) or \(\operatorname{Im}f\) on a domain implies \(f\) constant.
  • \(e^z\): period \(2\pi i\), never \(0\), \(|e^z|=e^x\).
  • \(\sin z\), \(\cos z\) unbounded; zeros only real.
  • \(\operatorname{Log}z\) analytic on \(\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]\); \(\operatorname{Log}(-1+i\sqrt3)=\ln2+2\pi i/3\).
  • \(i^i=e^{-\pi/2}\) (principal); \(z^a=e^{a\log z}\) multi-valued.
  • Entire: polynomials, \(e^z\), \(\sin\), \(\cos\), \(\sinh\), \(\cosh\). Not entire: \(\tan z\), \(1/z\), \(\operatorname{Log}z\), \(\bar z\).
