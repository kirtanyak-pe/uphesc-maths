Series and singularities

  1. Question 1Easy
    The radius of convergence of \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty(3+4i)^nz^n\) is

    Why

    It is a geometric series in \((3+4i)z\), convergent iff \(|3+4i||z|<1\). Since \(|3+4i|=5\), \(R=\frac15\).
  2. Question 2Medium
    The radius of convergence of \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{z^{2n}}{2^n}\) is

    Why

    Substitute \(w=z^2\): \(\sum(w/2)^n\) converges iff \(|w|<2\), i.e. \(|z|^2<2\), so \(R=\sqrt2\).
  3. Question 3Medium
    In the annulus \(1<|z|<2\), the Laurent series of \(\dfrac{1}{(z-1)(z-2)}\) has coefficient of \(z^{-1}\) equal to

    Why

    Partial fractions: \(\frac1{(z-1)(z-2)}=\frac1{z-2}-\frac1{z-1}\). For \(|z|>1\): \(-\frac1{z-1}=-\sum_{n\ge1}z^{-n}\); for \(|z|<2\): \(\frac1{z-2}=-\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^n}{2^{n+1}}\). The coefficient of \(z^{-1}\) is \(-1\).
  4. Question 4Easy
    The order of the zero of \(\sin z-z\) at \(z=0\) is

    Why

    \(\sin z-z=-\frac{z^3}{6}+\frac{z^5}{120}-\cdots=z^3\big(-\frac16+\cdots\big)\), so the order is \(3\).
  5. Question 5Hard
    The order of the zero of \(f(z)=2\cos z-2+z^2\) at \(z=0\) is

    Why

    \(2\cos z=2-z^2+\frac{z^4}{12}-\frac{z^6}{360}+\cdots\), so \(f(z)=\frac{z^4}{12}-\cdots\). The \(z^2\) terms cancel and \(f\) is even, so there is no \(z^3\) term: order \(4\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    The point \(z=0\) is, for \(f(z)=\dfrac{1-\cos z}{z^3}\),

    Why

    \(1-\cos z=\frac{z^2}{2}-\frac{z^4}{24}+\cdots\), so \(f=\frac1{2z}-\frac z{24}+\cdots\): a simple pole, \(a_{-1}=\frac12\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    Which function has an essential singularity at \(z=0\)?

    Why

    \(\sin\frac1z=\frac1z-\frac1{6z^3}+\cdots\) has infinitely many negative powers. \(\frac{\sin z}z\) is removable, \(\frac1{e^z-1}\) has a simple pole, and \(\frac{\sin z}{z^3}=\frac1{z^2}-\frac16+\cdots\) has a double pole.
  8. Question 8Hard
    Is there a function \(f\) analytic on \(|z|<1\) with \(f(1/n)=\dfrac{(-1)^n}{n}\) for all integers \(n\ge2\)?

    Why

    On \(z_n=\frac1{2n}\), \(f(z_n)=z_n\), and these points accumulate at \(0\) inside the disc, so \(f(z)\equiv z\) by the identity theorem. But then \(f(\frac13)=\frac13\ne-\frac13\), a contradiction.
  9. Question 9Medium
    If \(f\) is analytic on \(|z|<1\) and \(f(1/n)=\dfrac{1}{n+1}\) for all \(n\ge2\), then \(f(z)=\)

    Why

    Let \(g(z)=\frac z{1+z}\), analytic on the disc, with \(g(\frac1n)=\frac{1/n}{1+1/n}=\frac1{n+1}\). The zeros of \(f-g\) accumulate at \(0\) in the disc, so \(f=g\).
  10. Question 10Easy
    At \(z=\infty\) the function \(f(z)=z^3+z\) has

    Why

    With \(w=1/z\): \(f=w^{-3}+w^{-1}\), a pole of order \(3\) at \(w=0\). In general a polynomial of degree \(n\) has a pole of order \(n\) at \(\infty\); \(e^z\) and \(\sin z\) have essential singularities there.
  11. Question 11Medium
    The radius of convergence of the Taylor series of \(\dfrac{1}{z^2+4}\) about \(z=1\) is

    Why

    The singularities are \(\pm2i\). The distance from \(1\) to \(2i\) is \(\sqrt{1+4}=\sqrt5\); the series converges in the largest disc free of singularities.
  12. Question 12Medium
    Assertion (A): \(f(z)=\sin(1/z)\) has zeros at \(z=\frac1{n\pi}\), which accumulate at \(0\), yet \(f\not\equiv0\). Reason (R): The identity theorem needs the accumulation point to lie in the domain of analyticity, and \(0\) is not in the domain \(\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\).

    Why

    \(\sin\frac1{(1/n\pi)}=\sin n\pi=0\). The accumulation point \(0\) is an essential singularity, outside the domain, so no contradiction with the identity theorem. R explains A.
  13. Question 13Hard
    In the Taylor series \(\dfrac{z}{e^z-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}\dfrac{B_n}{n!}z^n\), the coefficient of \(z^4\) is

    Why

    \(\frac z{e^z-1}=1-\frac z2+\frac{z^2}{12}-\frac{z^4}{720}+\cdots\); here \(B_4=-\frac1{30}\) and \(\frac{B_4}{4!}=-\frac1{720}\). The radius of convergence is \(2\pi\) (nearest poles \(\pm2\pi i\)).
