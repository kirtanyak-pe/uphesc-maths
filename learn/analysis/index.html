What this unit covers

Unit 3 runs from classical real analysis to the basics of functional analysis, in eight topics:

  1. Sequences and series of functions, with uniform convergence and the interchange theorems.
  2. The Riemann integral, improper integrals, Beta and Gamma functions, and convergence tests for series.
  3. Functions of bounded variation and Fourier series.
  4. Multivariable calculus: limits, partial and total derivatives, extrema.
  5. Lebesgue measure, measurable functions and the Lebesgue integral.
  6. Metric spaces: convergence, continuity, uniform continuity, compactness, connectedness, completeness.
  7. Normed and Banach spaces, and the four big theorems (Hahn-Banach, open mapping, closed graph, uniform boundedness).
  8. Hilbert spaces: orthogonal complement, orthonormal bases, Gram-Schmidt.

The official sheet asks for at least six questions per unit; in practice the Unit 3 count is lower (see below), but the same ideas also feed Unit 5 (metric-space compactness and completeness) and Unit 7.

Patterns:

  • Uniform convergence is the single most frequent topic, nearly always as “on which interval” or “does the interchange hold”.
  • Functional analysis questions are statement-checking: which space is Banach or Hilbert, what a bounded operator’s kernel looks like, the hierarchy of spaces. Know the standard counterexamples.
  • Measure questions are about null sets (\(\mathbb{Q}\), Cantor set) and the Riemann versus Lebesgue contrast.
  • The 2026 paper tends to chain two ideas in one question; check each link.
  • Metric-space compactness, total boundedness and separability also appear under Unit 5 (2025 Q94-96, 2026 Q54), so read topic 6 with that in mind.

High-yield list

  • Uniform convergence by the sup test; the table of standard examples (\(x^n\), \(\frac{nx}{1+n^2x^2}\), \(\frac{x}{1+nx^2}\), \(nxe^{-nx^2}\)); uniform limit of uniformly continuous functions.
  • Lebesgue’s criterion for Riemann integrability; Dirichlet function (Riemann no, Lebesgue yes).
  • \(\int_0^\infty x^{-p}\) never converges; Beta and Gamma evaluations; Raabe’s test.
  • BV: examples separating continuity, BV and differentiability; total variation of simple functions.
  • Fourier series value at a jump; Parseval sums \(\frac{\pi^2}6\), \(\frac{\pi^4}{90}\); coefficient decay for BV and \(L^2\) functions.
  • Path test for limits in \(\mathbb{R}^2\); continuous plus partials but not differentiable; Hessian classification; Lagrange multipliers.
  • \(m(\mathbb{Q})=0\); Cantor set; MCT, Fatou, DCT with their hypotheses.
  • Compact \(\iff\) complete and totally bounded; closed and bounded is enough only in \(\mathbb{R}^n\); Banach contraction; Baire.
  • Which spaces are Banach or Hilbert; norm of the identity is \(1\); kernel of a bounded operator is closed; equivalence of norms in finite dimension.
  • Hahn-Banach, open mapping and closed graph theorems: hypotheses (Banach, bounded, surjective, closed graph) and conclusions.
  • Hilbert: self-adjoint iff \(\langle Tx,x\rangle\) real; \(M\oplus M^\perp\) needs \(M\) closed; Parseval for an orthonormal basis.

Official syllabus

Sequence and series of functions, uniform convergence, Fourier series, Riemann integral, improper integrals, functions of bounded variations, Lebesgue measure, measurable functions, Lebesgue integral, Multivariable calculus- functions of several variables, directional derivative, partial derivative and total derivative, maxima and minima, Elements of metric spaces- convergence, continuity, uniform continuity, compactness, connectedness, completeness. Normed linear spaces, Banach spaces, open mapping theorem, closed graph theorem, Hahn- Banach theorem, Hilbert spaces, Orthogonal complement of a subspace in a Hilbert space, Orthogonal basis, Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization process.
