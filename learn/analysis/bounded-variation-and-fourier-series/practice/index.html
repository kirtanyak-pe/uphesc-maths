  1. Question 1Easy
    Which function (value \(0\) at \(x=0\)) is of bounded variation on \([0,1]\)?

    Why

    \(x^2\sin(1/x)\) has bounded derivative \(2x\sin(1/x)-\cos(1/x)\), so it is Lipschitz and BV. The other three oscillate with total variation \(\sim\sum\frac1k=\infty\) (the second even though it is differentiable everywhere).
  2. Question 2Medium
    The total variation of \(f(x)=x^2-4x\) on \([0,5]\) is

    Why

    \(f\) decreases on \([0,2]\) from \(0\) to \(-4\) (change \(4\)) then increases on \([2,5]\) from \(-4\) to \(5\) (change \(9\)). \(V=4+9=13\). Option A is \(\lvert f(5)-f(0)\rvert\), the variation of a monotone function.
  3. Question 3Easy
    Consider: (i) every continuous function on \([a,b]\) is of bounded variation; (ii) every BV function is bounded; (iii) every BV function is continuous; (iv) every monotone function is BV. Which are true?

    Why

    \(x\sin(1/x)\) is continuous but not BV, so (i) fails; step functions are BV but not continuous, so (iii) fails. (ii) and (iv) are standard.
  4. Question 4Easy
    The total variation of \(f(x)=\lfloor x\rfloor\) on \([0,2.5]\) is

    Why

    The function jumps by \(1\) at \(x=1\) and at \(x=2\), and is constant otherwise. \(V=1+1=2\) (the point \(x=3\) is outside the interval).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The Fourier sine coefficient \(b_n\) of \(f(x)=x\) on \((-\pi,\pi)\) is

    Why

    \(b_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^\pi x\sin nx\,dx=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi x\sin nx\,dx=\frac2\pi\cdot\frac{\pi(-1)^{n+1}}{n}=\frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}n\). Option B has a sign error; C misses the factor \(2\) (it is the coefficient for \(f=\frac x2\)); D would hold for even functions.
  6. Question 6Easy
    Let \(f(x)=1\) for \(0<x<\pi\) and \(f(x)=0\) for \(-\pi<x<0\), extended \(2\pi\)-periodically. At \(x=0\) the Fourier series of \(f\) converges to

    Why

    \(f\) satisfies Dirichlet’s conditions, so at the jump the series converges to \(\frac{f(0^+)+f(0^-)}2=\frac{1+0}2=\frac12\), whatever the value of \(f(0)\) is.
  7. Question 7Hard
    Using Parseval’s identity for \(f(x)=x^2\) on \((-\pi,\pi)\), \(\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^4}\) equals

    Why

    \(a_0=\frac{2\pi^2}3\), \(a_n=\frac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}\). Parseval: \(\frac1\pi\int x^4=\frac{2\pi^4}5=\frac{a_0^2}2+16\sum\frac1{n^4}=\frac{2\pi^4}9+16\sum\frac1{n^4}\), so \(\sum\frac1{n^4}=\frac{\pi^4}{90}\). \(\frac{\pi^4}{96}\) is the sum over odd \(n\) only.
  8. Question 8Medium
    Putting \(x=0\) in \(x^2=\dfrac{\pi^2}3+4\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}\cos nx\) gives \(\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^2}=\)

    Why

    \(0=\frac{\pi^2}3+4\sum\frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}\), so \(\sum\frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}=-\frac{\pi^2}{12}\) and the alternating sum starting with \(+1\) is \(+\frac{\pi^2}{12}\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    In the half-range cosine series of \(f(x)=x\) on \((0,\pi)\), written as \(\frac{a_0}2+\sum a_n\cos nx\), the coefficient \(a_0\) is

    Why

    \(a_0=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi x\,dx=\frac2\pi\cdot\frac{\pi^2}2=\pi\), so the constant term \(\frac{a_0}2=\frac\pi2\) is the mean value of \(x\) on \((0,\pi)\). Option A is the constant term, not \(a_0\).

    Why

  12. Question 12Hard
    Near a jump discontinuity, the partial sums of a Fourier series overshoot the larger one-sided limit, and as the number of terms grows the overshoot tends to about

    Why

    This is the Gibbs phenomenon. For the square wave \(\pm1\) the maxima tend to \(\frac2\pi\mathrm{Si}(\pi)\approx1.179\), an overshoot of \(0.179\) above \(1\), which is \(\approx8.95\%\) of the jump \(2\). (The \(18\%\) figure is the overshoot relative to half of the jump.)
