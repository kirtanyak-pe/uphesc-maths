  1. Question 1Easy
    In a metric space \(X\) with the discrete metric (\(d(x,y)=1\) for \(x\ne y\)), which statement is true?

    Why

    \(B(x,\tfrac12)=\{x\}\), so every singleton is open and every set is a union of singletons, hence open. Complements are open too, so every set is also closed. Convergent sequences are eventually constant.
  2. Question 2Easy
    Which of the following metric spaces is complete?

    Why

    A uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, so \(C[0,1]\) is complete in the sup metric. \(\mathbb{Q}\) and \((0,1)\) are missing limits, and \(C[0,1]\) with the \(L^1\) metric has Cauchy sequences converging to discontinuous step functions.
  3. Question 3Easy
    Which function is uniformly continuous on its stated domain?

    Why

    \(\lvert\sin x-\sin y\rvert\le\lvert x-y\rvert\) (Lipschitz). The other three fail: take \(x_n=n+\frac1n,y_n=n\) for \(x^2\); \(\frac1n,\frac1{n+1}\) for \(1/x\); and points where \(\sin(1/x)\) equals \(1\) and \(-1\) for the third.
  4. Question 4Easy
    Which subset is compact (in the stated space with the usual metric)?

    Why

    In \(\mathbb{R}^n\) compact means closed and bounded (Heine-Borel). The unit ball of \(\ell^2\) is closed and bounded but not compact (\(e_n\) has no convergent subsequence); \(\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]\) is not complete; \(\mathbb{Z}\) is unbounded.
  5. Question 5Medium
    The best Lipschitz constant of \(T(x)=1+\dfrac1x\) on \([\tfrac32,2]\), which is a contraction of this interval into itself, is

    Why

    \(T'(x)=-1/x^2\) and \(\lvert T'\rvert\) is largest at \(x=\frac32\), where it equals \(\frac49\). \(T\) maps \([\frac32,2]\) onto \([\frac32,\frac53]\subseteq[\frac32,2]\). The value \(\frac14\) is \(\lvert T'(2)\rvert\), \(\frac23\) is \(1/x\) at \(x=\frac32\) (the square is forgotten).
  6. Question 6Medium
    \(T(x)=\frac x2+1\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) is a contraction with \(\alpha=\frac12\). Starting at \(x_0=0\), the smallest \(n\) for which the a priori bound \(\dfrac{\alpha^n}{1-\alpha}d(x_1,x_0)\) is below \(10^{-3}\) is

    Why

    \(x_1=1\) so \(d(x_1,x_0)=1\), and the bound is \(2\cdot2^{-n}=2^{1-n}\). Need \(2^{1-n}<10^{-3}\), i.e. \(2^{n-1}>1000\), so \(n-1\ge10\) and \(n=11\) (\(2^{-10}\approx0.00098\) is the bound at \(n=11\); at \(n=10\) it is \(0.00195\)).
  7. Question 7Medium
    Consider: (I) a totally bounded metric space is bounded; (II) a bounded metric space is totally bounded; (III) a totally bounded metric space is separable. Which are true?

    Why

    (I) is true: finitely many \(\varepsilon\)-balls. (III) is true: the union of finite \(\frac1k\)-nets is a countable dense set. (II) is false: an infinite set with the discrete metric is bounded but needs infinitely many balls of radius \(\frac12\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    Which property is NOT preserved by a homeomorphism between metric spaces?

    Why

    \(\tan:(-\frac\pi2,\frac\pi2)\to\mathbb{R}\) is a homeomorphism from an incomplete space onto a complete one. Compactness, connectedness and separability are topological properties.
  9. Question 9Medium
    Assertion A: \((0,1)\) with the usual metric is totally bounded but not compact. Reason R: \((0,1)\) is not complete.

    Why

    A metric space is compact iff it is complete and totally bounded. \((0,1)\) is totally bounded, so it fails to be compact exactly because it is not complete (the Cauchy sequence \(1/n\) has no limit in \((0,1)\)). R explains A.
  10. Question 10Medium
    Which of the following metric spaces can be written as a countable union of nowhere dense subsets?

    Why

    \(\mathbb{Q}=\bigcup_n\{q_n\}\) and each singleton is nowhere dense in \(\mathbb{Q}\). The other three are complete, so by the Baire category theorem they are not countable unions of nowhere dense sets.
  11. Question 11Hard
    The number of connected components of \(\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2: x^2-y^2\ne0\}\) is

    Why

    Removing the lines \(y=x\) and \(y=-x\) leaves four open sectors, determined by the signs of \(x-y\) and \(x+y\). Each sector is convex, hence connected, and distinct sectors are separated by the removed lines.
  12. Question 12Hard
    Which map has a fixed point guaranteed by the Banach contraction principle (a contraction of a complete metric space into itself)?

    Why

    On \([1,2]\): \(T\) maps into \([\sqrt2,\frac32]\subseteq[1,2]\) and \(\lvert T'\rvert=\frac12\lvert1-\frac2{x^2}\rvert\le\frac12\), so it is a contraction of a complete space (fixed point \(\sqrt2\)). \(x+1/x\) is a strict non-uniform contraction with no fixed point, \(x/2\) lives on a non-complete space, and \(x+1\) is an isometry with no fixed point.
  13. Question 13Hard
    Which subset of \((C[0,1],d_\infty)\) is totally bounded?

    Why

    By Arzelà-Ascoli a bounded equicontinuous set is relatively compact, hence totally bounded; the third set qualifies. The unit ball contains \(t^n\), which has no Cauchy subsequence (for \(m\ge2n\), \(\lVert t^n-t^m\rVert_\infty\ge\frac14\)); \(\{nt\}\) is unbounded.
