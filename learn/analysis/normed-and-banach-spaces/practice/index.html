Normed and Banach spaces

13 questions. Pick an answer to see if it is right and why.

  1. Question 1Easy
    The right shift \(S(x_1,x_2,\dots)=(0,x_1,x_2,\dots)\) on \(\ell^2\) has operator norm

    Why

    \(\lVert Sx\rVert_2=\lVert x\rVert_2\) for every \(x\), so \(S\) is an isometry and \(\lVert S\rVert=1\). It is bounded, so the norm is finite.
  2. Question 2Easy
    Which of the following normed spaces is a Banach space?

    Why

    \(\ell^p\) is complete for \(1\le p\le\infty\). The other three are dense proper subspaces of Banach spaces (or have Cauchy sequences leaving the space), so they are incomplete.
  3. Question 3Easy
    A non-zero linear functional \(f\) on a normed space \(X\) is bounded if and only if its kernel is

    Why

    If \(f\) is bounded, \(\ker f=f^{-1}(\{0\})\) is closed. If \(f\) is unbounded, its kernel is dense (and not \(X\)). Neither \(\{0\}\) nor finite dimension is required (the kernel has codimension \(1\)).
  4. Question 4Easy
    For \(A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-3&5\end{pmatrix}\) acting on \((\mathbb{R}^2,\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)\), the operator norm \(\lVert A\rVert\) is

    Why

    \(\lVert A\rVert_\infty\) is the maximum absolute row sum: \(\max(2+1,\ 3+5)=8\). The maximum column sum \(\max(5,6)=6\) is the norm on \((\mathbb{R}^2,\lVert\cdot\rVert_1)\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    The dual space of \(\ell^{4/3}\) is isometrically isomorphic to

    Why

    \((\ell^p)^*=\ell^q\) with \(\frac1p+\frac1q=1\). For \(p=\frac43\): \(\frac1q=1-\frac34=\frac14\), so \(q=4\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    The functional \(\varphi(x)=\sum_{i\ge1}3^{-(i-1)}x_i\) on \(\ell^2\) has norm

    Why

    The norm is \(\lVert a\rVert_2\) with \(a_i=3^{-(i-1)}\): \(\sum9^{-(i-1)}=\frac1{1-1/9}=\frac98\), so \(\lVert\varphi\rVert=\sqrt{9/8}=\frac{3}{2\sqrt2}\). \(\frac32=\sum a_i\) is the norm on \(\ell^\infty\), \(1=\sup a_i\) is the norm on \(\ell^1\), and \(\frac98\) forgets the square root.
  7. Question 7Medium
    Which pair of norms is NOT equivalent?

    Why

    All norms on a finite-dimensional space are equivalent. On \(C[0,1]\), \(\lVert f\rVert_1\le\lVert f\rVert_\infty\) but \(f_n=t^n\) has \(\lVert f_n\rVert_1/\lVert f_n\rVert_\infty=\frac1{n+1}\to0\), so there is no reverse inequality.
  8. Question 8Medium
    Let \(T:X\to Y\) be a bounded linear operator that is onto. The open mapping theorem concludes that \(T\) is open provided

    Why

    The theorem requires both spaces to be complete (and \(T\) bounded, linear, surjective). Counterexample if the target is not complete: the identity \((C[0,1],\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)\to(C[0,1],\lVert\cdot\rVert_1)\) is bounded and bijective but not open.
  9. Question 9Medium
    Let \(D:C^1[0,1]\to C[0,1]\), \(Df=f'\), with the sup norm on both spaces. Its graph is closed and \(D\) is linear on its whole domain, yet \(D\) is unbounded. Why does this not contradict the closed graph theorem?

    Why

    The closed graph theorem needs a Banach domain and codomain. \(C[0,1]\) is Banach, but \((C^1[0,1],\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)\) is not (uniform limits of \(C^1\) functions need not be \(C^1\)). With the norm \(\lVert f\rVert_\infty+\lVert f'\rVert_\infty\) the domain is complete and \(D\) is bounded.
  10. Question 10Medium
    Assertion A: The null space of a bounded linear operator on a normed space is closed. Reason R: Every linear operator on a finite-dimensional normed space is bounded.

    Why

    A is true because \(N(T)=T^{-1}(\{0\})\) is the preimage of a closed set under a continuous map. R is also true but it is not the reason: A holds in every normed space for every bounded \(T\).
  11. Question 11Hard
    Which statement is FALSE?

    Why

    \((\ell^1)^*=\ell^\infty\) but \((\ell^\infty)^*\) is strictly larger than \(\ell^1\) (it contains functionals such as a Banach limit that vanish on \(c_0\)). The other three statements are true: Riesz’s lemma, finite-dimensional subspaces are complete hence closed, and \(B(X,\mathbb{F})\) is always Banach.
  12. Question 12Hard
    Let \(T=\operatorname{diag}(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\dots)\) on \(\ell^2\). Which statement about its range \(R(T)\) is true?

    Why

    \(R(T)\supseteq c_{00}\), which is dense, so \(R(T)\) is dense. It is not all of \(\ell^2\): \(y=(1/n)\in\ell^2\) but \(T^{-1}y=(1,1,1,\dots)\notin\ell^2\). A dense proper subspace is not closed. \(T\) is bounded and injective but \(T^{-1}\) is unbounded.
  13. Question 13Hard
    The operator norm of \(A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\) on \((\mathbb{R}^2,\lVert\cdot\rVert_2)\) is

    Why

    \(A^{\mathsf T}A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}\) has eigenvalues \(\frac{3\pm\sqrt5}{2}\), so \(\lVert A\rVert_2=\sqrt{\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}}=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\) (since \(\big(\frac{1+\sqrt5}2\big)^2=\frac{3+\sqrt5}2\)).
