Normed and Banach spaces

15 questions. Pick an answer to see if it is right and why.

  1. Question 1Easy
    The right shift \(S(x_1,x_2,\dots)=(0,x_1,x_2,\dots)\) on \(\ell^2\) has operator norm

    Why

    \(\lVert Sx\rVert_2=\lVert x\rVert_2\) for every \(x\), so \(S\) is an isometry and \(\lVert S\rVert=1\). It is bounded, so the norm is finite.
  2. Question 2Easy
    Which of the following normed spaces is a Banach space?

    Why

    \(\ell^p\) is complete for \(1\le p\le\infty\). The other three are dense proper subspaces of Banach spaces (or have Cauchy sequences leaving the space), so they are incomplete.
  3. Question 3Medium
    The dual space of \(\ell^{4/3}\) is isometrically isomorphic to

    Why

    \((\ell^p)^*=\ell^q\) with \(\frac1p+\frac1q=1\). For \(p=\frac43\): \(\frac1q=1-\frac34=\frac14\), so \(q=4\).
  4. Question 4Easy
    Which of the following is a seminorm but not a norm on \(C^1[0,1]\)?

    Why

    \(\sup\lvert f'\rvert\) is non-negative, homogeneous and satisfies the triangle inequality, but it vanishes on every constant function, so definiteness fails: a seminorm that is not a norm. The first and second wrong options are norms. The square of the sup norm is not homogeneous (\(p(2f)=4p(f)\)), so it is not even a seminorm.
  5. Question 5Easy
    Which of the following spaces is reflexive?

    Why

    For \(1<p<\infty\), \((\ell^p)^*=\ell^q\) and \((\ell^q)^*=\ell^p\), so the canonical map into the second dual is onto. The spaces \(\ell^1\) and \(c_0\) have \(\ell^\infty\) and \(\ell^1\) as duals but their second duals are bigger than themselves. \(\ell^\infty\) is not reflexive (and not even separable, while a reflexive space with a separable dual is separable).
  6. Question 6Medium
    Which of the following statements is true?

    Why

    If \(X^*\) is separable then so is \(X\) (a standard consequence of Hahn-Banach). The converse fails: \(\ell^1\) is separable but \((\ell^1)^*=\ell^\infty\) is not. A subspace of a separable metric space is separable, but a space containing a separable subspace need not be: the indicator sequences \(\chi_A\) (\(A\subseteq\mathbb{N}\)) are \(2^{\aleph_0}\) points pairwise at distance \(1\) in \(\ell^\infty\). In \(L^\infty[0,1]\) the functions \(\chi_{[0,t]}\) are pairwise at distance \(1\), and \(C[0,1]\) is closed, not dense, in \(L^\infty\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    On \((C[0,1],\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)\) let \((Tf)(x)=\displaystyle\int_0^1(x+t)f(t)\,dt\). Then \(\lVert T\rVert\) equals

    Why

    For a kernel operator on \(C[0,1]\) with the sup norm, \(\lVert T\rVert=\sup_x\int_0^1\lvert k(x,t)\rvert\,dt\). Here \(\int_0^1(x+t)\,dt=x+\frac12\), whose maximum on \([0,1]\) is \(\frac32\) at \(x=1\); the bound is attained for \(f\equiv1\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    A subspace \(M\) of a normed space \(X\) is dense in \(X\) if and only if

    Why

    If \(M\) is not dense, pick \(x_0\notin\overline M\). By Hahn-Banach there is \(f\in X^*\) with \(f\vert_{\overline M}=0\) and \(f(x_0)\ne0\). Conversely if \(M\) is dense and \(f\) vanishes on \(M\), continuity gives \(f=0\). Completeness of \(M\) would make it closed, so it is the opposite of density unless \(M=X\), and a finite-dimensional subspace is closed, hence dense only if equal to \(X\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    Let \(f(t,t)=t\) on \(M=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1)\}\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^2,\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)\). The number of norm-preserving linear extensions of \(f\) to \(\mathbb{R}^2\) is

    Why

    \(\lVert f\rVert=\sup\frac{\lvert t\rvert}{\lVert(t,t)\rVert_\infty}=1\). An extension is \(F(x,y)=ax+by\) with \(a+b=1\) and norm \(\lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert\) (the dual norm of \(\ell^\infty\) is \(\ell^1\)). The norm equals \(1\) exactly when \(a,b\ge0\), so every \(a\in[0,1]\), \(b=1-a\) works. Hahn-Banach guarantees existence but not uniqueness.
  10. Question 10Medium
    Let \(X\) be a Banach space, \(Y\) a normed space, \(T_n\in B(X,Y)\), and suppose \((T_nx)\) converges for every \(x\in X\). Then

    Why

    A convergent sequence is bounded, so \(\sup_n\lVert T_nx\rVert<\infty\) for each \(x\); the uniform boundedness principle gives \(\sup\lVert T_n\rVert<\infty\) and then \(\lVert Tx\rVert\le\liminf\lVert T_n\rVert\lVert x\rVert\). Pointwise convergence does not give norm convergence (for example \(T_nx=x_n\) on \(\ell^2\) tends to \(0\) pointwise but \(\lVert T_n\rVert=1\)).
  11. Question 11Hard
    On \(c_{00}\) with the sup norm let \(T_n(x)=nx_n\). Which statement is correct?

    Why

    Each \(x\in c_{00}\) has \(x_n=0\) for large \(n\), so \(T_nx=0\) eventually and \(\sup_n\lvert T_nx\rvert<\infty\). But \(T_n(e_n)=n\) with \(\lVert e_n\rVert=1\), so \(\lVert T_n\rVert=n\to\infty\). The principle needs a Banach domain, and \((c_{00},\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)\) is dense in \(c_0\) but not complete.
  12. Question 12Medium
    Let \(T:X\to Y\) be a bounded linear operator that is onto. The open mapping theorem concludes that \(T\) is open provided

    Why

    The theorem requires both spaces to be complete (and \(T\) bounded, linear, surjective). Counterexample if the target is not complete: the identity \((C[0,1],\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)\to(C[0,1],\lVert\cdot\rVert_1)\) is bounded and bijective but not open.
  13. Question 13Medium
    Assertion A: The null space of a bounded linear operator on a normed space is closed. Reason R: Every linear operator on a finite-dimensional normed space is bounded.

    Why

    A is true because \(N(T)=T^{-1}(\{0\})\) is the preimage of a closed set under a continuous map. R is also true but it is not the reason: A holds in every normed space for every bounded \(T\).
  14. Question 14Hard
    Which statement is FALSE?

    Why

    \((\ell^1)^*=\ell^\infty\) but \((\ell^\infty)^*\) is strictly larger than \(\ell^1\) (it contains functionals such as a Banach limit that vanish on \(c_0\)). The other three statements are true: Riesz’s lemma, finite-dimensional subspaces are complete hence closed, and \(B(X,\mathbb{F})\) is always Banach.
  15. Question 15Hard
    Let \(T=\operatorname{diag}(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\dots)\) on \(\ell^2\). Which statement about its range \(R(T)\) is true?

    Why

    \(R(T)\supseteq c_{00}\), which is dense, so \(R(T)\) is dense. It is not all of \(\ell^2\): \(y=(1/n)\in\ell^2\) but \(T^{-1}y=(1,1,1,\dots)\notin\ell^2\). A dense proper subspace is not closed. \(T\) is bounded and injective but \(T^{-1}\) is unbounded.
