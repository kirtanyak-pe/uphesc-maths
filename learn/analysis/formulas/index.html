Unit 3 · Real and Functional Analysis

Formula sheet

Every formula and result of the unit. Each topic page explains where they come from.

1. Sequences and series of functions

  • \(f_n\to f\) uniformly on \(E\) iff \(M_n=\sup_E\lvert f_n-f\rvert\to0\). Uniform Cauchy: \(\sup_E\lvert f_n-f_m\rvert\to0\).
  • Weierstrass M-test: \(\lvert f_n\rvert\le M_n\), \(\sum M_n<\infty\Rightarrow\) uniform and absolute convergence. Dirichlet and Abel tests for \(\sum f_ng_n\).
  • Uniform limit of continuous (uniformly continuous, bounded) functions is continuous (uniformly continuous, bounded).
  • Integration: \(f_n\to f\) uniformly on \([a,b]\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(\int f_n\to\int f\). Differentiation: needs \(f_n'\to g\) uniformly and \(f_n(x_0)\) convergent; then \(f'=g\).
  • Dini: compact set, continuous \(f_n\) monotone in \(n\), continuous limit \(\Rightarrow\) uniform convergence.
  • Power series: \(R=1/\limsup\lvert a_n\rvert^{1/n}\); uniform on \([c-r,c+r]\) for \(r<R\); same \(R\) after termwise differentiation or integration; Abel’s theorem at an end point.
  • Weierstrass approximation: polynomials are dense in \(C[a,b]\) (sup norm). Arzela-Ascoli: bounded equicontinuous sequences in \(C[a,b]\) have uniformly convergent subsequences.
  • Sups to remember: \(\frac{nx}{1+n^2x^2}\) has max \(\frac12\); \(\frac{x}{1+nx^2}\) has max \(\frac1{2\sqrt n}\); \(nxe^{-nx^2}\) has max \(\sqrt{n/2}\,e^{-1/2}\).

2. Riemann and improper integrals

  • Integrable iff \(\forall\varepsilon\ \exists P\): \(U(P,f)-L(P,f)<\varepsilon\). Bounded \(f\) is Riemann integrable iff its discontinuity set has measure zero (Lebesgue’s criterion).
  • Continuous, monotone, BV, bounded with countably many discontinuities: integrable. Dirichlet function: not Riemann integrable (but Lebesgue integrable, integral \(0\)).
  • FTC I: \(F'(x_0)=f(x_0)\) where \(f\) is continuous. FTC II: \(\int_a^bf=G(b)-G(a)\) if \(G'=f\) and \(f\) is integrable.
  • \(\int_1^\infty x^{-p}\) converges iff \(p>1\); \(\int_0^1x^{-p}\) iff \(p<1\); \(\int_0^\infty x^{-p}\) never. \(\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}x=\frac\pi2\) (conditional).
  • \(\Gamma(n)=\int_0^\infty x^{n-1}e^{-x}dx\), \(\Gamma(n+1)=n\Gamma(n)\), \(\Gamma(\frac12)=\sqrt\pi\), \(\Gamma(n)\Gamma(1-n)=\frac\pi{\sin n\pi}\).
  • \(B(m,n)=\frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(m+n)}\); \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^p\cos^q=\frac12B(\frac{p+1}2,\frac{q+1}2)\).
  • Series: nth-term, comparison, ratio, root, Raabe, integral, Leibniz. \(\sum\frac1{n(\ln n)^p}\) converges iff \(p>1\).

3. Bounded variation and Fourier series

  • \(V_a^b(f)=\sup_P\sum\lvert f(x_i)-f(x_{i-1})\rvert\); monotone: \(\lvert f(b)-f(a)\rvert\); \(C^1\): \(\int\lvert f'\rvert\).
  • BV \(\Rightarrow\) bounded, countably many discontinuities, a.e. differentiable. \(f\) BV iff \(f\) is a difference of increasing functions (Jordan).
  • \(x\sin\frac1x\): continuous, not BV. \(x^2\sin\frac1x\): BV. \(x^2\sin\frac1{x^2}\): differentiable, not BV.
  • \(a_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^\pi f\cos nx\), \(b_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^\pi f\sin nx\); series \(\frac{a_0}2+\sum(a_n\cos nx+b_n\sin nx)\). Even: \(b_n=0\); odd: \(a_n=0\). Half-range: \(\frac2L\int_0^L\).
  • Dirichlet/Jordan: series converges to \(\frac{f(x^+)+f(x^-)}2\). Continuous piecewise-\(C^1\): uniform convergence.
  • Bessel: \(\frac{a_0^2}2+\sum(a_n^2+b_n^2)\le\frac1\pi\int f^2\); Parseval: equality.
  • \(\sum\frac1{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}6\); \(\sum\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{12}\); \(\sum_{\text{odd}}\frac1{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}8\); \(\sum\frac1{n^4}=\frac{\pi^4}{90}\); \(\sum_{\text{odd}}\frac1{n^4}=\frac{\pi^4}{96}\). BV gives \(a_n=O(\frac1n)\); \(L^2\) gives \(\sum a_n^2<\infty\).

4. Multivariable calculus

  • Limit must agree along all paths. Different paths give different values means no limit. Polar squeeze proves a limit.
  • \(C^1\Rightarrow\) differentiable \(\Rightarrow\) continuous and all directional derivatives exist. Partials existing gives nothing more.
  • \(D_uf=\nabla f\cdot u\) when \(f\) is differentiable. Schwarz: \(f_{xy}=f_{yx}\) if a mixed partial is continuous.
  • Jacobians: polar \(r\); spherical \(\rho^2\sin\varphi\); \(\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)}\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}=1\). Euler: \(xf_x+yf_y=nf\).
  • Implicit function theorem: \(y'=-F_x/F_y\) if \(F_y\ne0\).
  • Hessian test: \(D=rt-s^2\); \(D>0,r>0\) min; \(D>0,r<0\) max; \(D<0\) saddle; \(D=0\) no information. Lagrange: \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\), \(g=0\).

5. Lebesgue measure and integral

  • Outer measure \(m^*(A)=\inf\{\sum\ell(I_k):A\subset\bigcup I_k\}\). \(E\) is measurable (Caratheodory) if \(m^*(A)=m^*(A\cap E)+m^*(A\setminus E)\) for every \(A\). Measurable sets form a \(\sigma\)-algebra containing all Borel sets and all null sets.
  • Countable additivity; continuity from below always, and from above if some set has finite measure. Translation invariant; \(m(cE)=\lvert c\rvert m(E)\).
  • Countable sets have measure \(0\) (\(m(\mathbb{Q})=0\), so the irrationals in \([0,1]\) have measure \(1\)). The Cantor set is uncountable, closed, of measure \(0\). A Vitali set is not measurable (needs the axiom of choice).
  • \(f\) measurable iff \(\{f>a\}\) is measurable for all \(a\). Sums, products, \(\sup\), \(\limsup\) and a.e.-limits of measurable functions are measurable.
  • \(\int f=\int f^+-\int f^-\); \(f\) is integrable iff \(\int\lvert f\rvert<\infty\). \(f\ge0\) and \(\int f=0\iff f=0\) a.e.
  • Monotone convergence: \(0\le f_n\uparrow f\Rightarrow\int f_n\uparrow\int f\). Fatou: \(\int\liminf f_n\le\liminf\int f_n\) (\(f_n\ge0\)). Dominated convergence: \(f_n\to f\) a.e., \(\lvert f_n\rvert\le g\in L^1\Rightarrow\int f_n\to\int f\).
  • Riemann integrable (bounded, \([a,b]\)) implies Lebesgue integrable with the same value; the converse fails (Dirichlet function).
  • Egorov (finite measure): a.e. convergence is uniform off a set of small measure. Lusin: a measurable function is continuous on a closed set whose complement has small measure.
  • \(L^p\): \(\lVert f\rVert_p=(\int\lvert f\rvert^p)^{1/p}\); Holder \(\int\lvert fg\rvert\le\lVert f\rVert_p\lVert g\rVert_q\) (\(\frac1p+\frac1q=1\)), Minkowski; \(L^p\) is complete (Riesz-Fischer). On a finite measure space \(L^q\subset L^p\) for \(p<q\); for sequences \(\ell^p\subset\ell^q\) for \(p<q\).

6. Metric spaces

  • Metric: \(d\ge0\), \(d(x,y)=0\iff x=y\), symmetry, triangle inequality. Continuity: preimage of open is open \(\iff\) sequentially continuous.
  • Compact \(\iff\) sequentially compact \(\iff\) complete and totally bounded. Compact \(\Rightarrow\) closed and bounded (converse holds in \(\mathbb{R}^n\), Heine-Borel). Continuous image of compact is compact; continuous function on a compact space is uniformly continuous and attains its bounds.
  • Connected: no separation into two disjoint non-empty open sets. Continuous image of connected is connected. Connected subsets of \(\mathbb{R}\) are exactly the intervals. Path-connected \(\Rightarrow\) connected; converse fails (topologist’s sine curve).
  • Complete: every Cauchy sequence converges. Closed subset of a complete space is complete; a complete subspace is closed. \(\mathbb{R}^n\), \(C[a,b]\) (sup), \(\ell^p\) are complete; \(\mathbb{Q}\) is not.
  • Uniformly continuous maps send Cauchy sequences to Cauchy sequences. Totally bounded \(\Rightarrow\) bounded and separable.
  • Cantor intersection: nested non-empty closed sets with diameter \(\to0\) in a complete space meet in one point. Baire: a complete metric space is not a countable union of nowhere dense sets. Banach contraction principle: a contraction on a complete space has a unique fixed point.

7. Normed and Banach spaces

  • Norm: \(\lVert x\rVert\ge0\), \(=0\iff x=0\), \(\lVert\alpha x\rVert=\lvert\alpha\rvert\lVert x\rVert\), triangle inequality. Banach = complete normed.
  • \(\ell^p\) (\(1\le p\le\infty\)), \(C[a,b]\) with sup norm, \(L^p\) (\(1\le p\le\infty\)) are Banach. \(C[a,b]\) with the \(L^1\) or \(L^2\) norm is not complete. \(\ell^p\) for \(0<p<1\) is not normed.
  • Finite dimension: all norms are equivalent; every finite-dimensional normed space is complete; the closed unit ball is compact. Riesz lemma: the closed unit ball is compact iff the space is finite dimensional.
  • Linear \(T\) is continuous iff bounded; \(\lVert T\rVert=\sup_{\lVert x\rVert\le1}\lVert Tx\rVert\). \(B(X,Y)\) is Banach if \(Y\) is Banach. \(\ker T\) is closed for bounded \(T\). \(\lVert I\rVert=1\).
  • Dual \(X^*=B(X,\mathbb{K})\) is always Banach. \((\ell^p)^*=\ell^q\) for \(1\le p<\infty\) (\(\frac1p+\frac1q=1\)), \(c_0^*=\ell^1\), \((\ell^1)^*=\ell^\infty\).
  • Hahn-Banach: a bounded functional on a subspace extends to \(X\) with the same norm; hence for each \(x\ne0\) there is \(f\) with \(\lVert f\rVert=1\), \(f(x)=\lVert x\rVert\), and \(X^*\) separates points.
  • Open mapping: a bounded linear surjection between Banach spaces is open. Bounded inverse theorem: a bounded linear bijection between Banach spaces has a bounded inverse. Closed graph: a linear map between Banach spaces with closed graph is bounded. Uniform boundedness: a pointwise-bounded family of operators from a Banach space is uniformly bounded.

8. Hilbert spaces

  • Inner product: linear in the first slot, conjugate symmetric, \(\langle x,x\rangle>0\) for \(x\ne0\). Cauchy-Schwarz: \(\lvert\langle x,y\rangle\rvert\le\lVert x\rVert\lVert y\rVert\). Parallelogram law: \(\lVert x+y\rVert^2+\lVert x-y\rVert^2=2\lVert x\rVert^2+2\lVert y\rVert^2\), which holds in a normed space iff the norm comes from an inner product.
  • Hilbert = complete inner product space. \(\ell^2\) and \(L^2\) are Hilbert; \(\ell^p\), \(L^p\) for \(p\ne2\) and \(C[a,b]\) (sup norm) are not.
  • Hierarchy: Hilbert \(\Rightarrow\) Banach \(\Rightarrow\) normed \(\Rightarrow\) metric \(\Rightarrow\) topological space.
  • Projection theorem: for a closed subspace \(M\), \(H=M\oplus M^\perp\). \(M^\perp\) is always closed; \(M^{\perp\perp}=\overline M\).
  • Orthonormal set: Bessel \(\sum\lvert\langle x,e_n\rangle\rvert^2\le\lVert x\rVert^2\). Orthonormal basis (complete): \(x=\sum\langle x,e_n\rangle e_n\) and Parseval \(\lVert x\rVert^2=\sum\lvert\langle x,e_n\rangle\rvert^2\); equivalent to \(\{e_n\}^\perp=\{0\}\).
  • Separable Hilbert spaces have countable orthonormal bases, and all infinite-dimensional ones are isometric to \(\ell^2\).
  • Gram-Schmidt: \(u_k=v_k-\sum_{j<k}\frac{\langle v_k,u_j\rangle}{\langle u_j,u_j\rangle}u_j\), \(e_k=\frac{u_k}{\lVert u_k\rVert}\).
  • Riesz representation: every bounded linear functional is \(f(x)=\langle x,y\rangle\) for a unique \(y\), and \(\lVert f\rVert=\lVert y\rVert\). Adjoint: \(\langle Tx,y\rangle=\langle x,T^*y\rangle\); \(\lVert T^*T\rVert=\lVert T\rVert^2\).
  • On a complex Hilbert space, \(T\) is self-adjoint iff \(\langle Tx,x\rangle\) is real for all \(x\). Eigenvalues of a self-adjoint operator are real, and eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal.
