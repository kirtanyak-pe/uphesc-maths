  1. Question 1Easy
    Which of the following norms on \(\mathbb{R}^2\) is induced by an inner product?

    Why

    \(\sqrt{x^2+4y^2}\) comes from \(\langle(x_1,y_1),(x_2,y_2)\rangle=x_1x_2+4y_1y_2\). The other three fail the parallelogram law for \(u=(1,0)\), \(v=(0,1)\): the left side is \(2\) times the squared norm of \(u\pm v\), namely \(8\), \(2\) and \(2\cdot2^{2/3}\) respectively, while the right side is \(4\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    Which of the following is a Hilbert space?

    Why

    \(\ell^2\) is a complete inner product space. \(\ell^1\) and the sup norm violate the parallelogram law. \(C[0,1]\) with the \(L^2\) inner product is an inner product space but is not complete.
  3. Question 3Easy
    In an inner product space, \(\lVert x+y\rVert=7\), \(\lVert x-y\rVert=5\) and \(\lVert x\rVert=4\). Then \(\lVert y\rVert\) equals

    Why

    Parallelogram law: \(49+25=2\cdot16+2\lVert y\rVert^2\), so \(\lVert y\rVert^2=21\). Dropping the factor \(2\) gives \(\sqrt{58}\); using \(74-2\cdot16\) without halving gives \(\sqrt{42}\).
  4. Question 4Easy
    On a complex Hilbert space, a bounded operator \(T\) has \(\langle Tx,x\rangle\) purely imaginary for every \(x\). Then \(T\) is

    Why

    \(\langle(T+T^*)x,x\rangle=2\operatorname{Re}\langle Tx,x\rangle=0\) for all \(x\), and on a complex space this forces \(T+T^*=0\). Real values of \(\langle Tx,x\rangle\) would instead give self-adjoint.
  5. Question 5Medium
    In \(\mathbb{R}^3\), let \(M=\{x:x_1+x_2+x_3=0\}\). The distance from \((1,2,3)\) to \(M\) is

    Why

    \(M^\perp=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1,1)\}\) and the distance is the length of the projection onto it: \(\frac{\lvert1+2+3\rvert}{\sqrt3}=2\sqrt3\). \(2\) forgets the square root, \(6\) forgets to divide by \(\lVert n\rVert\), \(\sqrt{14}\) is \(\lVert x\rVert\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    Apply Gram-Schmidt to \(v_1=(1,1,0)\), \(v_2=(1,0,1)\) in \(\mathbb{R}^3\). The second (unnormalised) orthogonal vector is

    Why

    \(u_2=v_2-\frac{\langle v_2,u_1\rangle}{\langle u_1,u_1\rangle}u_1=(1,0,1)-\frac12(1,1,0)=(\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,1)\). It is orthogonal to \(u_1\): \(\frac12-\frac12+0=0\). Subtracting the whole of \(u_1\) gives the wrong vector \((0,-1,1)\).
  7. Question 7Medium
    The function \(f(x)=x\) on \((-\pi,\pi)\) has Fourier series \(f=\sum_{n\ge1}b_n\sin nx\) with \(b_n=\frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}n\). By Parseval (using the orthonormal basis \(\sin nx/\sqrt\pi\)), \(\sum_{n\ge1}b_n^2\) equals

    Why

    In this scaling \(\lVert f\rVert^2=\pi\sum b_n^2\), so \(\sum b_n^2=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}x^2dx=\frac{2\pi^2}{3}\). Check: \(\sum\frac4{n^2}=4\cdot\frac{\pi^2}6\). \(\frac{2\pi^3}{3}\) is \(\lVert f\rVert^2\) itself, without dividing by \(\pi\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    Let \(M\) be a linear subspace of a Hilbert space \(H\). Which statement is true?

    Why

    \(M^\perp=\bigcap_{m\in M}\{x:\langle x,m\rangle=0\}\) is an intersection of closed sets. The others need \(M\) closed: in general \(M^{\perp\perp}=\overline M\), and a dense proper subspace such as \(c_{00}\subset\ell^2\) has \(M^\perp=\{0\}\) but \(M\ne H\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    The functional \(f(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}2^{-n}x_n\) on \(\ell^2\) has norm

    Why

    By Riesz, \(f=\langle\cdot,y\rangle\) with \(y=(2^{-n})\) and \(\lVert f\rVert=\lVert y\rVert_2=\big(\sum4^{-n}\big)^{1/2}=\sqrt{1/3}\). \(1=\sum2^{-n}\) is the norm on \(\ell^\infty\); \(\frac13\) forgets the square root; \(\frac12\) is \(\sup y_n\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    Let \(S\) be the right shift \(S(x_1,x_2,\dots)=(0,x_1,x_2,\dots)\) on \(\ell^2\). Which statement is true?

    Why

    \(S^*\) is the left shift, so \(S^*Sx=x\) but \(SS^*x=(0,x_2,x_3,\dots)\ne x\). \(S\) is an isometry that is not onto, hence not unitary (and not normal).
  11. Question 11Hard
    Assertion A: On a real Hilbert space, \(\langle Tx,x\rangle=0\) for all \(x\) implies \(T=0\). Reason R: The rotation of \(\mathbb{R}^2\) by \(90^\circ\) satisfies \(\langle Tx,x\rangle=0\) for all \(x\).

    Why

    R is true: \(T(x,y)=(-y,x)\) gives \(\langle Tx,x\rangle=-xy+xy=0\). Since \(T\ne0\), A is false. The implication holds on complex spaces only.
  12. Question 12Hard
    Let \(M=c_{00}\subset\ell^2\) (finitely supported sequences). Which statement is true?

    Why

    A vector orthogonal to every \(e_n\) has all coordinates \(0\), so \(M^\perp=\{0\}\) and \(M^{\perp\perp}=\ell^2=\overline M\). \(M\) is not closed, so \(M\oplus M^\perp\ne\ell^2\): the projection theorem needs a closed subspace.
  13. Question 13Hard
    In \(\ell^2\) let \(x=(3,4,5,0,0,\dots)\) and consider the orthonormal set \(\{e_2,e_3,e_4,\dots\}\) (without \(e_1\)). Then \(\sum_n\lvert\langle x,e_n\rangle\rvert^2\) over this set equals

    Why

    The sum is \(4^2+5^2=41\), strictly below \(\lVert x\rVert^2=9+16+25=50\) because the set is not complete (Bessel is strict). \(50\) would be Parseval, and \(9\) is the missing term.
  14. Question 14Hard
    Which matrix on \(\mathbb{C}^2\) is normal but neither self-adjoint nor unitary?

    Why

    For \(A=\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\i&1\end{pmatrix}\), \(AA^*=A^*A=2I\), so \(A\) is normal; \(A^*\ne A\); and \(AA^*\ne I\). The first two matrices are not normal (\(AA^*\ne A^*A\)), and the diagonal real matrix is self-adjoint.
