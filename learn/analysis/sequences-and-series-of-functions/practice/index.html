Sequences and series of functions

15 questions. Pick an answer to see if it is right and why.

0 of 15 answered · 0 correct

  1. Question 1Easy
    The sequence \(f_n(x)=x^n\) on \([0,1]\) satisfies

    Why

    The pointwise limit is \(0\) for \(x<1\) and \(1\) at \(x=1\). It is discontinuous while every \(x^n\) is continuous, so the convergence cannot be uniform. The statement that gives the right limit but claims uniform convergence fails because a uniform limit of continuous functions must be continuous.
  2. Question 2Easy
    For which of the following sets does \(f_n(x)=e^{-nx}\) converge uniformly to its pointwise limit?

    Why

    The pointwise limit is \(0\) for \(x>0\) and \(1\) at \(x=0\). On \([\frac12,\infty)\) the sup of \(e^{-nx}\) is \(e^{-n/2}\to0\). On \((0,1]\) and on \((0,\infty)\) the sup is \(1\) (approached as \(x\to0^+\)), and on \([0,1]\) the limit function is discontinuous while each \(f_n\) is continuous.
  3. Question 3Medium
    For \(f_n(x)=\dfrac{x}{1+nx^2}\) on \(\mathbb{R}\), \(\sup_x\lvert f_n(x)\rvert\) equals

    Why

    \(f_n'(x)=\frac{1-nx^2}{(1+nx^2)^2}\) vanishes at \(x=1/\sqrt n\), giving \(\frac{1/\sqrt n}{2}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt n}\to0\). So \(f_n\to0\) uniformly on \(\mathbb{R}\).
  4. Question 4Easy
    Consider: (i) a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous; (ii) a pointwise limit of continuous functions is continuous; (iii) if \(f_n\to f\) uniformly and each \(f_n\) is differentiable, then \(f\) is differentiable. Which are true?

    Why

    (i) is the standard theorem. (ii) fails for \(x^n\) on \([0,1]\). (iii) fails: \(\sqrt{x^2+1/n}\to\lvert x\rvert\) uniformly but \(\lvert x\rvert\) is not differentiable at \(0\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    Which of the following series converges uniformly on the stated domain?

    Why

    \(\lvert\cos nx/n^2\rvert\le 1/n^2\), so the M-test gives uniform convergence on \(\mathbb{R}\). \(\sum x^n\) and \(\sum x^n/n\) are not uniform on \((-1,1)\) (the sums blow up as \(x\to1^-\)). \(\sum\sin nx/n\) has a jump at \(0\) in its sum, so it is not uniform on \([0,2\pi]\).
  6. Question 6Easy
    The radius of convergence of \(\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^2}\,x^n\) is

    Why

    \(\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}=\frac{(n+1)^2}{(2n+2)(2n+1)}=\frac{n+1}{2(2n+1)}\to\frac14\). The number \(4\) is the growth rate of the coefficients \(\binom{2n}{n}\), which is the reciprocal of the radius.
  7. Question 7Medium
    The radius of convergence of \(\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\Bigl(\frac n{n+1}\Bigr)^{n^2}x^n\) is

    Why

    Root test: \(\lvert a_n\rvert^{1/n}=\left(\frac n{n+1}\right)^n=\left(1+\frac1n\right)^{-n}\to\frac1e\), so \(R=e\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    The power series \(\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac{x^n}{n}\) has radius \(1\). Which statement is correct?

    Why

    At \(x=1\) it is the harmonic series (diverges); at \(x=-1\) it is the alternating harmonic series (converges). Uniform convergence holds on compact subsets of the open interval but not on all of \((-1,1)\) (the sum \(-\ln(1-x)\to\infty\) as \(x\to1^-\)).
  9. Question 9Medium
    Assertion (A): \(f_n(x)=\dfrac{\sin nx}{\sqrt n}\) converges uniformly to \(0\) on \(\mathbb{R}\). Reason (R): hence \(f_n'\) converges uniformly to \(0\) on \(\mathbb{R}\).

    Why

    \(\sup\lvert f_n\rvert=1/\sqrt n\to0\), so A is true. But \(f_n'(x)=\sqrt n\cos nx\) and \(f_n'(0)=\sqrt n\to\infty\), so R is false: uniform convergence of \(f_n\) says nothing about \(f_n'\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 n^2x\,e^{-nx}\,dx\) equals

    Why

    With \(u=nx\), \(\int_0^1n^2xe^{-nx}dx=\int_0^nue^{-u}du=1-(n+1)e^{-n}\to1\). The integrands tend to \(0\) at every \(x\), so the limit of the integrals is not the integral of the limit; the convergence is not uniform and no integrable dominating function exists.
  11. Question 11Medium
    Which statement about the Maclaurin series of \(\arctan x\) and \(\ln(1+x)\) is correct?

    Why

    Both have radius \(1\). At \(x=1\) both reduce to alternating series that converge (Leibniz), giving \(\frac\pi4\) and \(\ln2\). At \(x=-1\) the \(\arctan\) series is again an alternating series with decreasing terms and converges, while the \(\ln(1+x)\) series becomes \(-\sum\frac1n\), which diverges.
  12. Question 12Medium
    In Dini’s theorem the hypotheses are: compact domain, continuous \(f_n\), monotone in \(n\), continuous limit. For \(f_n(x)=x^n\) on \([0,1]\) the hypothesis that fails is

    Why

    \(x^n\) decreases in \(n\), is continuous, and \([0,1]\) is compact. The pointwise limit (\(0\) on \([0,1)\), \(1\) at \(1\)) is discontinuous, and the convergence is indeed not uniform.
  13. Question 13Hard
    For \(\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{x^2}{(1+x^2)^n}\) on \([0,1]\), which statement is true?

    Why

    For \(x\ne0\) this is a geometric series with ratio \(\frac1{1+x^2}<1\) and sum \(\frac{x^2/(1+x^2)}{1-1/(1+x^2)}=1\). At \(x=0\) every term is \(0\), so the sum is \(0\). A uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, but this sum jumps at \(0\), so the convergence cannot be uniform.
  14. Question 14Hard
    Let \(f\) be continuous on \([0,1]\) with \(\int_0^1f(x)x^n\,dx=0\) for \(n=0,1,2,\dots\). Then

    Why

    By the Weierstrass approximation theorem choose polynomials \(p_k\to f\) uniformly. Then \(\int f\,p_k=0\) for every \(k\), and \(\int f^2=\lim\int f\,p_k=0\). A continuous \(f\) with \(\int f^2=0\) is identically \(0\).
  15. Question 15Hard
    Let \(x_n=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{n+k}\). Then

    Why

    \(x_{n+1}-x_n=\frac1{2n+1}-\frac1{2n+2}>0\), so \((x_n)\) is strictly increasing and its smallest term is \(x_1=\frac12\). Also \(x_n=\frac1n\sum\frac1{1+k/n}\to\int_0^1\frac{dx}{1+x}=\ln2\), so the supremum is \(\ln2\), and since the sequence is strictly increasing it is never attained.
