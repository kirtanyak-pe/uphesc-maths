Sequences and series of functions

14 questions. Pick an answer to see if it is right and why.

  1. Question 1Easy
    The sequence \(f_n(x)=x^n\) on \([0,1]\) satisfies

    Why

    The pointwise limit is \(0\) for \(x<1\) and \(1\) at \(x=1\). It is discontinuous while every \(x^n\) is continuous, so the convergence cannot be uniform. Option B names the right limit but a uniform limit of continuous functions must be continuous.
  2. Question 2Easy
    For \(f_n(x)=\dfrac{nx}{1+n^2x^2}\) on \([0,\infty)\), \(\sup_{x\ge0}\lvert f_n(x)-0\rvert\) equals

    Why

    \(f_n\) is maximal at \(x=1/n\) where it equals \(\frac{1}{1+1}=\frac12\) for every \(n\). Since the sup does not tend to \(0\), the convergence to \(0\) is not uniform.
  3. Question 3Medium
    For \(f_n(x)=\dfrac{x}{1+nx^2}\) on \(\mathbb{R}\), \(\sup_x\lvert f_n(x)\rvert\) equals

    Why

    \(f_n'(x)=\frac{1-nx^2}{(1+nx^2)^2}\) vanishes at \(x=1/\sqrt n\), giving \(\frac{1/\sqrt n}{2}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt n}\to0\). So \(f_n\to0\) uniformly on \(\mathbb{R}\).
  4. Question 4Easy
    Consider: (i) a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous; (ii) a pointwise limit of continuous functions is continuous; (iii) if \(f_n\to f\) uniformly and each \(f_n\) is differentiable, then \(f\) is differentiable. Which are true?

    Why

    (i) is the standard theorem. (ii) fails for \(x^n\) on \([0,1]\). (iii) fails: \(\sqrt{x^2+1/n}\to\lvert x\rvert\) uniformly but \(\lvert x\rvert\) is not differentiable at \(0\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    Which of the following series converges uniformly on \(\mathbb{R}\)?

    Why

    \(\lvert\cos nx/n^2\rvert\le 1/n^2\), so the M-test gives uniform convergence on \(\mathbb{R}\). \(\sum x^n\) and \(\sum x^n/n\) are not uniform on \((-1,1)\) (the sums blow up as \(x\to1^-\)). \(\sum\sin nx/n\) has a jump at \(0\) in its sum, so it is not uniform on \([0,2\pi]\).
  6. Question 6Easy
    The radius of convergence of \(\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(n!)^2}{(2n)!}\,x^n\) is

    Why

    \(\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}=\frac{(2n+2)(2n+1)}{(n+1)^2}=\frac{2(2n+1)}{n+1}\to4\). Option D is the limit of the reciprocal ratio, i.e. a common slip.
  7. Question 7Medium
    The radius of convergence of \(\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\Bigl(1+\frac1n\Bigr)^{n^2}x^n\) is

    Why

    Root test: \(\lvert a_n\rvert^{1/n}=(1+\frac1n)^n\to e\), so \(R=1/e\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    The power series \(\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac{x^n}{n}\) has radius \(1\). Which statement is correct?

    Why

    At \(x=1\) it is the harmonic series (diverges); at \(x=-1\) it is the alternating harmonic series (converges). Uniform convergence holds on compact subsets of the open interval but not on all of \((-1,1)\) (the sum \(-\ln(1-x)\to\infty\) as \(x\to1^-\)).
  9. Question 9Medium
    Assertion (A): \(f_n(x)=\dfrac{\sin nx}{\sqrt n}\) converges uniformly to \(0\) on \(\mathbb{R}\). Reason (R): hence \(f_n'\) converges uniformly to \(0\) on \(\mathbb{R}\).

    Why

    \(\sup\lvert f_n\rvert=1/\sqrt n\to0\), so A is true. But \(f_n'(x)=\sqrt n\cos nx\) and \(f_n'(0)=\sqrt n\to\infty\), so R is false: uniform convergence of \(f_n\) says nothing about \(f_n'\).
  10. Question 10Medium
    \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 nx\,e^{-nx^2}\,dx\) equals

    Why

    \(\int_0^1 nxe^{-nx^2}dx=\frac{1-e^{-n}}{2}\to\frac12\), whereas the pointwise limit function is \(0\) with integral \(0\). The convergence is not uniform, and the integral interchange fails here.
  11. Question 11Hard
    For \(f_n(x)=\dfrac{nx}{1+n^2x^2}\) on \([0,1]\) consider: (i) \(f_n\) does not converge uniformly; (ii) \(\lim\int_0^1 f_n=\int_0^1\lim f_n\); (iii) the pointwise limit is discontinuous. Which are true?

    Why

    The limit is \(0\) (continuous), so (iii) is false. Sup \(=\frac12\), so (i) is true. \(\int_0^1f_n=\frac{\ln(1+n^2)}{2n}\to0=\int_0^1 0\), so (ii) is true even though convergence is not uniform.
  12. Question 12Medium
    In Dini’s theorem the hypotheses are: compact domain, continuous \(f_n\), monotone in \(n\), continuous limit. For \(f_n(x)=x^n\) on \([0,1]\) the hypothesis that fails is

    Why

    \(x^n\) decreases in \(n\), is continuous, and \([0,1]\) is compact. The pointwise limit (\(0\) on \([0,1)\), \(1\) at \(1\)) is discontinuous, and the convergence is indeed not uniform.
  13. Question 13Hard
    The series \(\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-1)^n}{n+x^2}\) converges uniformly on \(\mathbb{R}\). The correct justification is

    Why

    \(\sup_x\frac{1}{n+x^2}=\frac1n\) and \(\sum\frac1n\) diverges, so the M-test cannot work (option A). The terms \(\frac1{n+x^2}\) decrease in \(n\) and tend to \(0\) uniformly, so the Leibniz remainder bound is uniform in \(x\). \(\sum(-1)^n\) does not converge (option C), and Dini needs a compact set.
  14. Question 14Hard
    Let \(f_n(x)=\dfrac{x}{n}\). Which statement is correct?

    Why

    On \([-a,a]\), \(\sup\lvert f_n\rvert=a/n\to0\). On \(\mathbb{R}\) the sup is infinite for each \(n\) (take \(x_n=n\) to get \(\lvert f_n(x_n)\rvert=1\)), so the convergence is not uniform there. It is therefore uniform on compact sets only.
