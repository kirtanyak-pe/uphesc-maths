Riemann and improper integrals

15 questions. Pick an answer to see if it is right and why.

  1. Question 1Easy
    Which of the following functions on \([0,1]\) is NOT Riemann integrable?

    Why

    The Dirichlet function has every lower sum \(0\) and every upper sum \(1\). It is discontinuous at every point, a set of positive measure, so Lebesgue’s criterion fails. A is continuous, B monotone, D is discontinuous only on the countable set \(\mathbb{Q}\).
  2. Question 2Medium
    \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{(2n)!}{n!\,n^n}\right)^{1/n}\) equals

    Why

    \(\frac{(2n)!}{n!\,n^n}=\prod_{k=1}^n\frac{n+k}{n}\), so the logarithm of the \(n\)th root is \(\frac1n\sum\ln\left(1+\frac kn\right)\to\int_0^1\ln(1+x)\,dx=2\ln2-1\). The limit is \(e^{2\ln2-1}=\frac4e\).
  3. Question 3Easy
    \(B(5,4)=\displaystyle\int_0^1x^4(1-x)^3\,dx\) equals

    Why

    \(B(5,4)=\frac{\Gamma(5)\Gamma(4)}{\Gamma(9)}=\frac{4!\,3!}{8!}=\frac{144}{40320}=\frac1{280}\).
  4. Question 4Hard
    \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin x\cos x)\,dx\) equals

    Why

    Use \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\sin x\,dx=\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\cos x\,dx=-\frac\pi2\ln2\) (the substitution \(x\to\frac\pi2-x\) swaps them, and adding the two gives \(\int\ln\frac{\sin2x}{2}\)). The sum of the two integrals is \(-\pi\ln2\).
  5. Question 5Medium
    \(\displaystyle\int_0^1x^2\Bigl(\ln\frac1x\Bigr)^3dx\) equals

    Why

    Put \(x=e^{-t}\): the integral becomes \(\int_0^\infty t^3e^{-3t}dt=\frac{\Gamma(4)}{3^4}=\frac{6}{81}=\frac2{27}\).
  6. Question 6Medium
    The improper integral \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{x^p}\) converges for

    Why

    Split at \(1\): \(\int_0^1x^{-p}\) needs \(p<1\) and \(\int_1^\infty x^{-p}\) needs \(p>1\). Both cannot hold, so the integral never converges.
  7. Question 7Hard
    Which of the following improper integrals diverges?

    Why

    With \(u=\ln(1/x)\) the divergent integral becomes \(\int_{\ln2}^\infty\frac{du}{u}=\infty\). The others converge: \(\int_0^1x^{-1/2}dx=2\), \(\int_1^\infty\frac{\lvert\sin x\rvert}{x^2}\le\int_1^\infty x^{-2}\), and the Gaussian integral equals \(\frac{\sqrt\pi}2\).
  8. Question 8Medium
    For a bounded function \(f\) on \([a,b]\) consider: (i) \(\lvert f\rvert\) Riemann integrable implies \(f\) Riemann integrable; (ii) \(f\) integrable implies \(\lvert f\rvert\) integrable; (iii) \(f^2\) integrable implies \(f\) integrable; (iv) \(f\) integrable implies \(f^2\) integrable. Which are true?

    Why

    The functions \(\lvert f\rvert\) and \(f^2\) are continuous functions of \(f\) so they are integrable when \(f\) is. The converses fail for \(f=1\) on \(\mathbb{Q}\cap[a,b]\) and \(-1\) elsewhere: \(\lvert f\rvert=f^2=1\) is integrable but \(f\) is not.
  9. Question 9Easy
    For real sequences \((a_n)\), which implication is always valid?

    Why

    If \(\sum\lvert a_n\rvert<\infty\) then \(\lvert a_n\rvert\le1\) eventually, so \(a_n^2\le\lvert a_n\rvert\) and comparison applies. The other implications fail: \(a_n=\frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt n}\) has \(\sum a_n\) convergent but \(\sum a_n^2=\sum\frac1n\) divergent, \(a_n=\frac1n\) has \(\sum a_n^2<\infty\) but \(\sum a_n=\infty\), and the same alternating series is not absolutely convergent.
  10. Question 10Hard
    The series \(\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}a_n\) with \(a_n=\frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt n}+\frac1n\) is

    Why

    \(\sum\frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt n}\) converges (Leibniz) and \(\sum\frac1n\) diverges, so the sum of the two series diverges. The Leibniz test cannot be applied to \(a_n\) itself, because \(\lvert a_n\rvert\) equals \(\frac1{\sqrt n}\pm\frac1n\) and is not monotone decreasing along the alternating pattern.
  11. Question 11Medium
    Which of the following series converges?

    Why

    By the integral test, \(\int_2^\infty\frac{dx}{x(\ln x)^p}=\int\frac{du}{u^p}\) converges iff \(p>1\). So only \(p=2\) converges. The others diverge (\(\frac1{\ln n}>\frac1n\), \(p=1\), and \(p=\frac12\) series).
  12. Question 12Medium
    For \(f(x)=x^2\) and \(g(x)=x^3\) on \([1,2]\), the point \(c\) given by Cauchy’s mean value theorem is

    Why

    We need \(\frac{f'(c)}{g'(c)}=\frac{f(2)-f(1)}{g(2)-g(1)}\), that is \(\frac{2c}{3c^2}=\frac{3}{7}\). Hence \(\frac{2}{3c}=\frac37\) and \(c=\frac{14}9\approx1.556\), which lies in \((1,2)\).
  13. Question 13Easy
    Let \(f\) be differentiable on \(\mathbb{R}\) with \(f(0)=0\) and \(f'(x)\le2\) for all \(x\). Which value of \(f(3)\) is impossible?

    Why

    By the mean value theorem \(f(3)-f(0)=3f'(c)\le6\), so \(f(3)\le6\) and \(7\) is impossible. The value \(6\) occurs for \(f(x)=2x\); the lower values are possible because there is no lower bound on \(f'\).
  14. Question 14Medium
    Let \(f=1\) on \([\tfrac12,1]\) and \(0\) on \([0,\tfrac12)\), and \(F(x)=\int_0^xf(t)\,dt\) on \([0,1]\). Then

    Why

    \(F(x)=\max(0,x-\frac12)\): continuous, with a corner at \(\frac12\). FTC I gives \(F'=f\) only at points where \(f\) is continuous.
  15. Question 15Medium
    Which statement about the Gamma and Beta functions is correct?

    Why

    The correct statement is the reflection formula together with the standard value \(\Gamma(\frac12)=\sqrt\pi\). The usual errors are inverting the quotient in \(B(m,n)=\frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(m+n)}\), treating \(\Gamma(0)\) as a number (it is a pole), and dropping the factor \(\frac12\) in \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^p\theta\cos^q\theta\,d\theta=\frac12B\left(\frac{p+1}2,\frac{q+1}2\right)\).
