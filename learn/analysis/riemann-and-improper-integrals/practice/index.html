  1. Question 1Easy
    Which of the following functions on \([0,1]\) is NOT Riemann integrable?

    Why

    The Dirichlet function has every lower sum \(0\) and every upper sum \(1\). It is discontinuous at every point, a set of positive measure, so Lebesgue’s criterion fails. A is continuous, B monotone, D is discontinuous only on the countable set \(\mathbb{Q}\).
  2. Question 2Easy
    \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{n}{n^2+k^2}\) equals

    Why

    The sum is \(\frac1n\sum\frac{1}{1+(k/n)^2}\to\int_0^1\frac{dx}{1+x^2}=\frac\pi4\).
  3. Question 3Medium
    \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty x^4e^{-x^2}\,dx\) equals

    Why

    \(\int_0^\infty x^ae^{-x^2}dx=\frac12\Gamma\left(\frac{a+1}2\right)\). With \(a=4\): \(\frac12\Gamma(\frac52)=\frac12\cdot\frac{3\sqrt\pi}4=\frac{3\sqrt\pi}8\). Option B forgets the factor \(\frac12\).

  6. Question 6Medium
    The improper integral \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{x^p}\) converges for

    Why

    Split at \(1\): \(\int_0^1x^{-p}\) needs \(p<1\) and \(\int_1^\infty x^{-p}\) needs \(p>1\). Both cannot hold, so the integral never converges.
  7. Question 7Medium
    The improper integral \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx\)

    Why

    It equals \(\frac\pi2\) (Dirichlet test: \(\int_0^x\sin\) is bounded, \(\frac1x\downarrow0\)). But \(\int^\infty\frac{\lvert\sin x\rvert}{x}dx\) diverges like \(\frac2\pi\ln R\).
  8. Question 8Easy
    \(\displaystyle\int_0^1\ln x\,dx\) equals

    Why

    \(\int_\varepsilon^1\ln x\,dx=[x\ln x-x]_\varepsilon^1=-1-\varepsilon\ln\varepsilon+\varepsilon\to-1\).
  9. Question 9Medium
    For a bounded function \(f\) on \([a,b]\) consider: (i) \(\lvert f\rvert\) Riemann integrable implies \(f\) Riemann integrable; (ii) \(f\) integrable implies \(\lvert f\rvert\) integrable; (iii) \(f^2\) integrable implies \(f\) integrable; (iv) \(f\) integrable implies \(f^2\) integrable. Which are true?

    Why

    The functions \(\lvert f\rvert\) and \(f^2\) are continuous functions of \(f\) so they are integrable when \(f\) is. The converses fail for \(f=1\) on \(\mathbb{Q}\cap[a,b]\) and \(-1\) elsewhere: \(\lvert f\rvert=f^2=1\) is integrable but \(f\) is not.
  10. Question 10Hard
    Assertion (A): \(\sum_{n\ge1}\binom{2n}{n}4^{-n}\) diverges. Reason (R): with \(c_n=\binom{2n}{n}4^{-n}\), \(\lim n\left(\frac{c_n}{c_{n+1}}-1\right)=\frac12<1\) (Raabe’s test).

    Why

    \(\frac{c_n}{c_{n+1}}=\frac{2n+2}{2n+1}\), so \(n\left(\frac{c_n}{c_{n+1}}-1\right)=\frac{n}{2n+1}\to\frac12<1\). Raabe’s test gives divergence (the ratio test alone gives limit \(1\) and is silent).
  11. Question 11Medium
    Which of the following series converges?

    Why

    By the integral test, \(\int_2^\infty\frac{dx}{x(\ln x)^p}=\int\frac{du}{u^p}\) converges iff \(p>1\). So only \(p=2\) converges. The others diverge (\(\frac1{\ln n}>\frac1n\), \(p=1\), and \(p=\frac12\) series).
  12. Question 12Medium
    For \(\sum_{n\ge1}\dfrac{n!}{n^n}\) the ratio test gives \(\lim\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\), and the series

    Why

    \(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\frac{(n+1)!\,n^n}{(n+1)^{n+1}n!}=\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^n\to\frac1e<1\), so it converges. For \(\sum\frac{n^n}{n!}\) the ratio would be \(e>1\) (divergence).
  13. Question 13Medium
    Let \(f=1\) on \([\tfrac12,1]\) and \(0\) on \([0,\tfrac12)\), and \(F(x)=\int_0^xf(t)\,dt\) on \([0,1]\). Then

    \(F(x)=\max(0,x-\frac12)\): continuous, with a corner at \(\frac12\). FTC I gives \(F'=f\) only at points where \(f\) is continuous.
