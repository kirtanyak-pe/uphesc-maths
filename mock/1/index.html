Question 34 of 120Abstract Algebra

Assertion (A): \(2\) is a prime element of \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\). Reason (R): \(2\) is irreducible in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\).

Question 35 of 120Abstract Algebra

Question 36 of 120Abstract Algebra

The smallest \(n\) such that the quaternion group \(Q_8\) is isomorphic to a subgroup of \(S_n\) is

\(Q_8\) has a unique involution \(-1\), and every nontrivial subgroup of \(Q_8\) contains it. In a faithful action on \(\{1,\dots,n\}\), some orbit is moved nontrivially by \(-1\); the stabiliser of a point in that orbit cannot contain \(-1\), hence is trivial, so that orbit has \(8\) points and \(n\ge8\). Cayley gives \(n=8\). The tempting answer \(4\) belongs to \(D_4\), not \(Q_8\).

Question 37 of 120Abstract Algebra

Question 49 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

On \([0,\infty)\), for which sequence does the Monotone Convergence Theorem give \(\lim\int f_n=\int\lim f_n\)?

Question 52 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

\(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1\frac{1+nx^2}{(1+x^2)^n}\,dx\) equals

Bernoulli’s inequality gives \((1+x^2)^n\ge1+nx^2\), so the integrands lie in \([0,1]\) and are dominated by the integrable function \(1\). For each \(x>0\) the integrand tends to \(0\) (exponential beats linear), and the single point \(x=0\) is null. By dominated convergence the limit is \(0\).

Question 53 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

The series \(\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}a_n\) with \(a_n=\frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt n}+\frac1n\) is

\(\sum\frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt n}\) converges (Leibniz) and \(\sum\frac1n\) diverges, so the sum of the two series diverges. The Leibniz test cannot be applied to \(a_n\) itself, because \(\lvert a_n\rvert\) equals \(\frac1{\sqrt n}\pm\frac1n\) and is not monotone decreasing along the alternating pattern.

Question 54 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Question 55 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Which statement about \(T=\operatorname{diag}(1,\frac12,\frac13,\dots)\) on \(\ell^2\) is true?

The eigenvalues are \(\frac1n\) with eigenvectors \(e_n\), and \(Tx=0\) forces \(x=0\), so \(0\) is not an eigenvalue. But \(T\) is not invertible with bounded inverse (\(T^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(n)\) is unbounded and not defined on all of \(\ell^2\)), so \(0\in\sigma(T)\); the spectrum is closed and equals \(\{1/n\}\cup\{0\}\). \(T\) is a norm limit of finite-rank operators, hence compact, and self-adjoint since the entries are real.

Question 67 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Question 68 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Which statement describes all continuous functions \(f:(\mathbb R,\text{cofinite})\to(\mathbb R,\text{usual})\)?

If \(f(x)\ne f(y)\), disjoint open intervals around the two values pull back to disjoint nonempty open sets of the cofinite topology, which is impossible. So \(f\) is constant.

Question 71 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Using the standard definitions (\(T_3\) means regular and \(T_1\)), which implication holds for every topological space?

In a \(T_3\) space points are closed, so a point \(x\ne y\) can be separated from the closed set \(\{y\}\) by disjoint open sets, which gives \(T_2\). The cofinite topology on an infinite set shows \(T_1\not\Rightarrow T_2\), the Sierpinski space shows \(T_0\not\Rightarrow T_1\), and the \(K\)-topology on \(\mathbb R\) shows \(T_2\not\Rightarrow T_3\).

Question 76 of 120Differential Equations

Which of these first-order equations is quasilinear (of Lagrange type) but not linear?

In \(zp+yq=x\) the coefficient of \(p\) contains \(z\), so it is quasilinear and not linear. \(xp+yq=z\) is linear. \(p^2+q=z\) and \(pq=x\) contain \(p^2\) and \(pq\), so they are nonlinear in the derivatives.

Question 94 of 120Mechanics and Fluid Dynamics

Which velocity field can represent an incompressible fluid?

Need \(u_x+v_y=0\). For \(u=x^2y,\ v=-xy^2\) the divergence is \(2xy-2xy=0\). The field \(u=x,\ v=y\) has divergence \(2\) (irrotational but not incompressible), \(u=xy,\ v=xy\) gives \(y+x\), and \(u=x^2,\ v=y^2\) gives \(2x+2y\). Only the field with \(u=x^2y\) has zero divergence.
