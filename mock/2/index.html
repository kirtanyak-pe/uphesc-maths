Question 35 of 120Abstract Algebra

The quotient group \((\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z)/\langle(1,2)\rangle\) is isomorphic to

The map \((a,b)\mapsto b-2a\) is a surjective homomorphism \(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z\) with kernel \(\{b=2a\}=\langle(1,2)\rangle\). By the first isomorphism theorem the quotient is \(\mathbb Z\). Quotienting by one generator of a rank-\(2\) free group leaves rank \(1\), and no torsion appears because \((1,2)\) is not a multiple of any other lattice vector.

Question 39 of 120Abstract Algebra

Let \(G\) be a nonabelian simple group having a subgroup of index \(6\). Then \(\lvert G\rvert\) necessarily divides

\(G\) acts on the \(6\) cosets of the subgroup, giving a homomorphism \(G\to S_6\). Its kernel is a proper normal subgroup (the action is transitive), hence trivial, so \(G\hookrightarrow S_6\). The image lies in \(A_6\), since otherwise \(G\cap A_6\) would be a normal subgroup of index \(2\). So \(\lvert G\rvert\) divides \(\lvert A_6\rvert=360\). No smaller bound holds: \(A_6\) itself has an index-\(6\) subgroup (\(A_5\)), and \(360\) divides neither \(36\), \(120\) nor \(1260\).

The general integral of \(xp+yq=z\) is

\(\dfrac{dx}{x}=\dfrac{dy}{y}=\dfrac{dz}{z}\) gives \(y/x=c_1,\ z/x=c_2\). Both are constant along \((x,y,z)\)-rays; none of the other pairs is.

Question 81 of 120Differential Equations

The characteristic curves of \(u_{xx}-2u_{xy}-3u_{yy}=0\) are the two families

\(A=1,\ B=-1,\ C=-3\) and \(B^2-AC=4\). \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{B\pm\sqrt{B^2-AC}}{A}=-1\pm2=1\) or \(-3\), so \(y-x=c_1\) and \(y+3x=c_2\). Equivalently \(dy^2+2\,dx\,dy-3\,dx^2=(dy-dx)(dy+3\,dx)=0\).

The centre value is the mean of the boundary values (the constant term \(\dfrac{a_0}2\)). \(\cos^2\theta=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\cos2\theta\), so the mean is \(\tfrac12\). The value \(1\) would be \(a_0\) itself, not \(\dfrac{a_0}2\).

Question 83 of 120Differential Equations

\(y_1=x\) solves \((1-x^2)y''-2xy'+2y=0\) on \((-1,1)\). A second independent solution (by reduction of order) is

Question 85 of 120Calculus of Variations, Integral Equations and Transforms

For \(J[y]=\displaystyle\int_0^b\big(y'^2-y^2\big)\,dx\) with \(y(0)=y(b)=0\), the extremal \(y\equiv0\) gives a minimum

Legendre’s condition \(F_{y'y'}=2>0\) holds for every \(b\). The Jacobi equation is \(\eta''+\eta=0\), \(\eta(0)=0\), so \(\eta=\sin x\); its first zero after \(0\) is the conjugate point \(x=\pi\). Jacobi’s condition (\(b<\pi\)) fails for \(b>\pi\), where \(\eta=\sin(\pi x/b)\) gives \(J=\frac{\pi^2-b^2}{2b}<0\) while \(J[0]=0\). For \(b<\pi\) the second variation is positive definite.

Question 86 of 120Calculus of Variations, Integral Equations and Transforms

Let \(K\) be a real symmetric kernel of rank \(2\) on \([a,b]\). (i) and (ii) hold for any symmetric kernel. (iii) is false: the determinant \(\det(I-\lambda A)\) has degree at most \(2\), so there are at most two eigenvalues. (iv) is true because \(\varphi=\lambda\sum c_ia_i(x)\). So (i), (ii), (iv).

Question 88 of 120Calculus of Variations, Integral Equations and Transforms

Question 92 of 120Calculus of Variations, Integral Equations and Transforms

Which of the following kernels on \([0,1]\) is symmetric but not separable?

\(xt\), \(x+t=x\cdot1+1\cdot t\) and \(\sin(x+t)=\sin x\cos t+\cos x\sin t\) are all finite sums \(\sum a_i(x)b_i(t)\), hence separable (and symmetric). \(\min(x,t)\) is symmetric but needs infinitely many terms.

Question 101 of 120Mechanics and Fluid Dynamics

A system with one degree of freedom has bounded periodic motion. In action-angle variables \((J,w)\), with \(J=\dfrac1{2\pi}\oint p\,dq\) and \(H=H(J)\), which statement is correct?
