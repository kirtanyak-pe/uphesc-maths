Question 32 of 120Abstract Algebra

Question 33 of 120Abstract Algebra

The polynomial \(x^4+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\). Which statement is true?

\(x^4+1=\Phi_8\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) (\(f(x+1)\) is Eisenstein at \(2\)). Mod an odd \(p\) it factors into factors of degree \(\operatorname{ord}_8(p)\le2\) (since \(p^2\equiv1\pmod8\)), and \(x^4+1=(x+1)^4\) mod \(2\). So a mod-\(p\) irreducibility test can fail for every \(p\).

Question 34 of 120Abstract Algebra

Question 35 of 120Abstract Algebra

Question 36 of 120Abstract Algebra

Then \(G=\mathbb Z_{15}\rtimes\mathbb Z_2\) with the involution acting on each factor either trivially or by inversion. The four choices give \(\mathbb Z_{30}\), \(D_{15}\), \(\mathbb Z_3\times D_5\) and \(\mathbb Z_5\times S_3\).

The matrix \(M=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&1&0\\0&2&0&1\\0&0&2&0\\0&0&0&2\end{pmatrix}\) has Jordan block sizes

Question 43 of 120Linear Algebra

\(A\) is a real \(3\times3\) matrix with \(A^3=I\) and \(A\neq I\). Then \(\operatorname{tr}A\) equals

Eigenvalues are cube roots of unity. \(A\) is diagonalisable over \(\mathbb{C}\) (distinct roots). Non-real eigenvalues come in conjugate pairs, so the possibilities are \(1,1,1\) (which forces \(A=I\), excluded) or \(1,\omega,\bar\omega\). Then \(\operatorname{tr}A=1+\omega+\bar\omega=1+2\cos\tfrac{2\pi}{3}=0\). Example: the cyclic permutation matrix.

Question 44 of 120Linear Algebra

Question 45 of 120Linear Algebra

Question 56 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Which of the following defines a metric on \(\mathbb{R}\)?

Question 75 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Question 76 of 120Differential Equations

Question 77 of 120Differential Equations

Question 82 of 120Differential Equations

Which of the following is a solution of \(x\,y''-(2x+1)\,y'+(x+1)\,y=0\) (\(x>0\))?

Write \(P=-\frac{2x+1}{x}\) and \(Q=\frac{x+1}{x}\). Then \(1+P+Q=\frac{x-2x-1+x+1}{x}=0\), so \(y=e^x\) is a solution. Direct substitution shows the other three fail: for \(y=x\) the left side is \(x^2-x-1\), for \(e^{-x}\) it is \((4x+2)e^{-x}\), for \(x^2\) it is \(x^3-3x^2\).

Question 84 of 120Differential Equations

The Neumann problem \(u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\) in the disc \(r<1\) with \(u_r=g(\theta)\) on \(r=1\) has a solution only if

Question 103 of 120Operations Research and Numerical Analysis

In a balanced transportation problem every supply \(a_i\) and demand \(b_j\) is a whole number. Which statement about its basic feasible solutions is correct?

The \(m+n-1\) basic cells form a spanning tree of the source-destination graph. This holds for every BFS, degenerate or not, and does not involve the costs.

Question 106 of 120Operations Research and Numerical Analysis

A maximisation LPP has alternative optimal solutions. Which statement about its dual must be true?

Strong duality gives the dual a finite optimum, which rules out infeasible and unbounded. A non-degenerate dual optimal BFS would force a unique primal optimum (complementary slackness gives a square nonsingular system for the primal variables), so alternative primal optima make every dual optimal BFS degenerate. Which of the following integers can be written as a sum of two squares of integers?

By the two-squares theorem, \(n\) is a sum of two squares iff every prime \(\equiv3\pmod4\) divides \(n\) to an even power. \(45=3^2\cdot5\) qualifies: \(45=36+9\). But \(21=3\cdot7\), \(33=3\cdot11\) and \(77=7\cdot11\) each contain a prime \(\equiv3\pmod4\) to an odd power.
