Question 33 of 120Abstract Algebra

The number of non-isomorphic groups of order \(39\) is

\(n_{13}\) divides \(3\) and is \(\equiv1\pmod{13}\), so \(n_{13}=1\) and \(G=\mathbb Z_{13}\rtimes\mathbb Z_3\). The action is a homomorphism \(\mathbb Z_3\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z_{13})\cong\mathbb Z_{12}\); since \(3\mid12\) a nontrivial one exists and all nontrivial choices give isomorphic groups. So there are two groups: \(\mathbb Z_{39}\) and the nonabelian one.

Question 34 of 120Abstract Algebra

The number of monic irreducible polynomials of degree \(3\) over \(\mathbb Z_3\) is

A monic cubic is reducible iff it has a root. Each monic irreducible cubic has \(3\) roots in \(\mathbb F_{27}\setminus\mathbb F_3\), and there are \(27-3=24\) such elements, so there are \(24/3=8\) (formula \(\frac13(3^3-3)\)). Equivalently, of the \(27\) monic cubics exactly \(8\) have no root in \(\mathbb Z_3\).

Question 35 of 120Abstract Algebra

The number of subfields of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_9)\), including \(\mathbb Q\) and \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_9)\) themselves, is

\(\mathbb Q(\zeta_9)/\mathbb Q\) is Galois with group \(U(9)\cong\mathbb Z_6\) (cyclic, generated by \(2\)). A cyclic group of order \(6\) has one subgroup per divisor of \(6\), so \(4\) subgroups, hence \(4\) subfields: \(\mathbb Q\), the quadratic field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\), the real cubic field \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_9+\zeta_9^{-1})\), and \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_9)\).

Question 40 of 120Linear Algebra

Consider the following statements for real \(n\times n\) matrices. I. If \(A\) has \(n\) distinct real eigenvalues, then \(A\) is diagonalisable over \(\mathbb{R}\). II. If \(A\) is invertible and diagonalisable, then its eigenvalues are distinct. III. If \(\chi_A\) has a repeated root, then \(A\) is not diagonalisable. IV. If \(A^2=A\), then \(A\) is diagonalisable. Which statements are true?

I is true (eigenvectors of distinct eigenvalues are independent). II is false: \(I_n\) is invertible and diagonalisable with a single eigenvalue. III is false: \(I_n\) has \(\chi=(x-1)^n\) yet is diagonalisable; a repeated root only allows non-diagonalisability. IV is true: \(x^2-x\) has distinct roots.

Question 41 of 120Linear Algebra

Which of the following can be the rank of a non-zero real \(5\times5\) skew-symmetric matrix?

A real skew-symmetric matrix is normal, and its non-zero eigenvalues come in pairs \(\pm ib\), so its rank (the number of non-zero eigenvalues) is even. Odd rank is impossible, and rank \(5\) would make a skew-symmetric matrix of odd order invertible. Only \(4\) is possible among the options (the rank may be \(2\) or \(4\)).

Question 42 of 120Linear Algebra

Question 43 of 120Linear Algebra

Which of the following linear maps exists?

An onto map needs \(\dim\) domain \(\ge\dim\) codomain, a one-one map needs \(\dim\) domain \(\le\dim\) codomain; the inclusion \((x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y,z,0,0)\) is one-one. On equal dimensions one-one forces onto.

Question 53 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Which of the following series converges?

By the integral test, \(\int_2^\infty\frac{dx}{x(\ln x)^p}=\int\frac{du}{u^p}\) converges iff \(p>1\). So only \(p=2\) converges. The others diverge (\(\frac1{\ln n}>\frac1n\), \(p=1\), and \(p=\frac12\) series).

Question 54 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Consider: (i) a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous; (ii) a pointwise limit of continuous functions is continuous; (iii) if \(f_n\to f\) uniformly and each \(f_n\) is differentiable, then \(f\) is differentiable. Which are true?

(i) is the standard theorem. (ii) fails for \(x^n\) on \([0,1]\). (iii) fails: \(\sqrt{x^2+1/n}\to\lvert x\rvert\) uniformly but \(\lvert x\rvert\) is not differentiable at \(0\).

Question 55 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Which of the following statements is true?

If \(X^*\) is separable then so is \(X\) (a standard consequence of Hahn-Banach). The converse fails: \(\ell^1\) is separable but \((\ell^1)^*=\ell^\infty\) is not. A subspace of a separable metric space is separable, but a space containing a separable subspace need not be: the indicator sequences \(\chi_A\) (\(A\subseteq\mathbb{N}\)) are \(2^{\aleph_0}\) points pairwise at distance \(1\) in \(\ell^\infty\). In \(L^\infty[0,1]\) the functions \(\chi_{[0,t]}\) are pairwise at distance \(1\), and \(C[0,1]\) is closed, not dense, in \(L^\infty\).

Question 56 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

For \(u=x^2-y^2\) and \(v=2xy\), the Jacobian \(\dfrac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)}\) equals

\(\det\begin{pmatrix}2x&-2y\\2y&2x\end{pmatrix}=4x^2+4y^2\). This is the Jacobian of \(z\mapsto z^2\) (here \(u+iv=(x+iy)^2\)), which equals \(\lvert2z\rvert^2\) and vanishes only at the origin.

Question 57 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Question 64 of 120Complex Analysis

Let \(f\) be entire with \(|f(z)|\le 3+|z|^{3/2}\) for all \(z\in\mathbb C\). Then \(f\) is

Cauchy’s estimate on \(|z|=R\) gives \(|f''(0)|\le \dfrac{2\,M(R)}{R^2}\le\dfrac{2(3+R^{3/2})}{R^2}\to0\) as \(R\to\infty\), and the same bound holds at every centre \(a\), so \(f''\equiv0\) and \(f(z)=az+b\). Question 65 of 120Complex Analysis

Let \(f_n\) be entire functions converging uniformly on every compact subset of \(\mathbb C\) to a function \(f\). Then

Each \(f_n\) is continuous with \(\oint_Tf_n\,dz=0\) for every triangle \(T\). Uniform convergence on the compact set \(T\) passes to the limit: \(f\) is continuous and \(\oint_Tf\,dz=\lim\oint_Tf_n\,dz=0\). By Morera’s theorem \(f\) is entire (Weierstrass’s theorem). Uniform convergence on compact sets is enough; it need not be uniform on \(\mathbb C\) (for example \(\sum z^n/n!\)).

Question 67 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Arrange these topologies on \(\mathbb R\) from coarsest to finest (each is contained in the next): cofinite, lower limit, usual, discrete.

A cofinite set has finite complement, hence is open in the usual topology. Usual-open sets are \(\mathbb R_\ell\)-open, and everything is discrete-open. The lower limit topology is strictly finer than the usual one.

Question 69 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Question 70 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Question 75 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Which of the following is NOT part of the definition of a topological manifold?

A topological manifold is Hausdorff, second countable and locally Euclidean. Compactness is not required: \(\mathbb R^n\) is a manifold and is not compact.

Question 76 of 120Differential Equations

Using \(u=x+ay\) for the equation \(pq=z\), a complete integral is

Question 77 of 120Differential Equations

Which pair is linearly independent on \(\mathbb R\)?

Question 78 of 120Differential Equations

Charpit’s method applied to \(px+qy=p^2+q^2\) gives the complete integral
