Question 53 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Question 55 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

For which of the following \(F\) does \(\displaystyle\lim_{s\to0}s\,f(s)\) exist while \(\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}F(t)\) does not?

For \(F=\sin t\), \(sf=\frac s{s^2+1}\to0\) as \(s\to0\), yet \(\sin t\) oscillates: the poles of \(sf\) at \(\pm i\) are on the imaginary axis, so the final value theorem does not apply. The other three functions tend to \(0\), \(1\) and \(0\), matching \(\lim_{s\to0}sf=0,1,0\).

Question 107 of 120Operations Research and Numerical Analysis

A maximisation LPP has alternative optimal solutions. Which statement about its dual must be true?

Strong duality gives the dual a finite optimum, which rules out infeasible and unbounded. A non-degenerate dual optimal BFS would force a unique primal optimum (complementary slackness gives a square nonsingular system for the primal variables), so alternative primal optima make every dual optimal BFS degenerate. Example: \(\max2x_1+4x_2\) s.t. \(x_1+2x_2\le5\), \(x_1+x_2\le4\) has dual optimum \((2,0)\), with three constraints active in two dimensions.

Which statement about Gauss elimination with partial pivoting is correct?
