Question 38 of 120Abstract Algebra

Question 40 of 120Linear Algebra

Assertion (A): every nilpotent matrix has trace \(0\). Reason (R): every eigenvalue of a nilpotent matrix is \(0\).

If \(N^k=0\) and \(Nv=\lambda v\) with \(v\neq0\), then \(0=N^kv=\lambda^kv\), so \(\lambda=0\). The trace is the sum of the eigenvalues (over \(\mathbb{C}\), with multiplicity), hence \(0\). So R is true and explains A.

Question 44 of 120Linear Algebra

Question 45 of 120Linear Algebra

\(S\) is any subset of a finite-dimensional inner product space \(V\) and \(W=\operatorname{span}S\). Which statement is NOT always true?

A vector is orthogonal to \(S\) iff it is orthogonal to \(\operatorname{span}S\), so \(S^\perp=W^\perp\) and \(\dim S^\perp=\dim V-\dim W\). If \(x\in S\cap S^\perp\) then \(\langle x,x\rangle=0\), so \(x=0\). But \(S\cup S^\perp\) need not be closed under addition: for \(S=\{(1,0)\}\) in \(\mathbb R^2\) the union is \(\{(1,0)\}\cup\{(0,t)\}\), which contains \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) but not their sum.

Question 49 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Question 50 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

For which of the following sets does \(f_n(x)=e^{-nx}\) converge uniformly to its pointwise limit?

The pointwise limit is \(0\) for \(x>0\) and \(1\) at \(x=0\). On \([\frac12,\infty)\) the sup of \(e^{-nx}\) is \(e^{-n/2}\to0\). On \((0,1]\) and on \((0,\infty)\) the sup is \(1\) (approached as \(x\to0^+\)), and on \([0,1]\) the limit function is discontinuous while each \(f_n\) is continuous.

Question 51 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Let \(M\) be a linear subspace of a Hilbert space \(H\). Which statement is true?

\(M^\perp=\bigcap_{m\in M}\{x:\langle x,m\rangle=0\}\) is an intersection of closed sets. The others need \(M\) closed: in general \(M^{\perp\perp}=\overline M\), and a dense proper subspace such as \(c_{00}\subset\ell^2\) has \(M^\perp=\{0\}\) but \(M\ne H\).

Question 52 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Which statement about measurable functions on \(\mathbb{R}\) is true?

\((f\circ g)^{-1}(U)=g^{-1}(f^{-1}(U))\) with \(f^{-1}(U)\) open, so it is measurable whenever \(f\) is continuous and \(g\) is measurable. For \(g\circ f\) the preimage under a continuous \(f\) of a measurable set can be non-measurable. Products of measurable functions are always measurable, so boundedness is not needed. A supremum over an uncountable family can fail to be measurable: the supremum of \(\chi_{\{x\}}\) over \(x\in V\) is \(\chi_V\) with \(V\) a Vitali set.

Question 53 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Which of the following functions on \([0,1]\) is NOT Riemann integrable?

The Dirichlet function has every lower sum \(0\) and every upper sum \(1\). It is discontinuous at every point, a set of positive measure, so Lebesgue’s criterion fails. A is continuous, B monotone, D is discontinuous only on the countable set \(\mathbb{Q}\).

Question 54 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Near a jump discontinuity, the partial sums of a Fourier series overshoot the larger one-sided limit, and as the number of terms grows the overshoot tends to about

Question 55 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Let \(f(x,y)=\dfrac{xy(x^2-y^2)}{x^2+y^2}\) for \((x,y)\ne(0,0)\), \(f(0,0)=0\). With \(f_{xy}=\partial_y(\partial_xf)\) and \(f_{yx}=\partial_x(\partial_yf)\), the value of \(f_{xy}(0,0)-f_{yx}(0,0)\) is

Question 57 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

In a metric space \(X\) with the discrete metric (\(d(x,y)=1\) for \(x\ne y\)), which statement is true?

Question 64 of 120Complex Analysis

Question 67 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Assertion (A): A curve with constant curvature \(\kappa=2\) and constant torsion \(\tau=3\) is a plane curve. Reason (R): A curve with \(\kappa\ne0\) is a plane curve if and only if \(\tau\equiv0\). Choose the correct option.

Question 68 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Let \(X\) be an infinite set with the cofinite topology. Which statement is true?

Any open cover has a member that misses only finitely many points; finitely many more members cover the rest, so \(X\) is compact. Two nonempty open sets always meet, so \(X\) is not Hausdorff, hence not metrizable. Singletons are not open, so it is not discrete.

Question 69 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Which of the following is NOT necessarily preserved when a continuous map \(f:X\to Y\) is applied?

Continuous images of compact, connected and path-connected sets are compact, connected and path-connected. The image of a closed set need not be closed: \(x\mapsto\tfrac1{1+x^2}\) maps the closed set \(\mathbb R\) onto \((0,1]\).

Question 70 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Assertion (A): Urysohn’s metrization theorem shows that \(\mathbb R_\ell\) is metrizable. Reason (R): \(\mathbb R_\ell\) is regular, \(T_1\) and separable.

\(\mathbb R_\ell\) is regular, \(T_1\) and separable, so R is true. But the theorem needs second countability, which \(\mathbb R_\ell\) lacks, and \(\mathbb R_\ell\) is not metrizable. So A is false.

Question 71 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Assertion (A): The interval \([0,1]\) is not a topological manifold without boundary. Reason (R): The point \(0\) has no neighbourhood in \([0,1]\) homeomorphic to an open subset of \(\mathbb R\).

A small connected neighbourhood \([0,\varepsilon)\) of \(0\) stays connected when \(0\) is removed, whereas removing a point from an interval in \(\mathbb R\) disconnects it; a homeomorphism would preserve this. So R is true and is exactly why A holds (it is a manifold with boundary).

Question 73 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Question 74 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Complete integral \(z=ax+by+2s\) with \(s=\sqrt{1+a^2+b^2}\). Then \(x=-\dfrac{2a}{s},\ y=-\dfrac{2b}{s}\) and \(z=ax+by+2s=\dfrac2s\). So \(x^2+y^2+z^2=\dfrac{4(a^2+b^2+1)}{s^2}=4\), a sphere of radius \(2\) (the upper half for the plus sign).

Question 88 of 120Calculus of Variations, Integral Equations and Transforms

Question 89 of 120Calculus of Variations, Integral Equations and Transforms

The smallest number of terms \(n\) in a representation \(K(x,t)=\sum_{i=1}^na_i(x)b_i(t)\) of \(K(x,t)=(x+t)^2\) is

\((x+t)^2=x^2\cdot1+x\cdot2t+1\cdot t^2\). The functions \(x^2,x,1\) are linearly independent and so are \(1,2t,t^2\), so no representation with fewer terms exists: \(n=3\) (the rank of the kernel). Expanding into four terms \(x^2\cdot1+x\cdot t+x\cdot t+1\cdot t^2\) is not minimal.
