Question 31 of 120Abstract Algebra

The number of Sylow \(3\)-subgroups of \(S_5\) is

Question 32 of 120Abstract Algebra

Assertion (A): the ideal \((x^2+1)\) is a maximal ideal of \(\mathbb Z[x]\). Reason (R): \(\mathbb Z[x]/(x^2+1)\cong\mathbb Z[i]\) is an integral domain.

R is true. But an ideal is maximal iff the quotient is a field, and \(\mathbb Z[i]\) is not a field (\(2\) has no inverse). So \((x^2+1)\) is prime but not maximal: A is false.

Question 38 of 120Abstract Algebra

The polynomial \(x^4+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\). Which statement is true?

\(x^4+1=\Phi_8\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) (\(f(x+1)\) is Eisenstein at \(2\)). Mod \(p\) it factors into factors of degree \(\operatorname{ord}_8(p)\le2\) for every odd \(p\) (since \(p^2\equiv1\pmod8\)), and \((x+1)^4\) mod \(2\). A search over all primes up to \(37\) confirms. So a mod-\(p\) test can fail for every \(p\).

Question 39 of 120Abstract Algebra

Which of the following rings is an integral domain?

Question 41 of 120Linear Algebra

Question 43 of 120Linear Algebra

The row rank of \(A\) is the column rank of \(A^T\), which is \(\operatorname{rank}T^t=\operatorname{rank}T=\) column rank of \(A\). R is the standard proof of A.

Question 44 of 120Linear Algebra

Question 45 of 120Linear Algebra

Question 46 of 120Linear Algebra

Let \(N\) be a nilpotent \(n\times n\) matrix over \(\mathbb{R}\). Consider: I. \(N^n=0\). II. \(I+N\) is invertible. III. If \(N'\) is another nilpotent \(n\times n\) matrix, then \(N+N'\) is nilpotent. IV. \(\operatorname{tr}N=0\). Which are always true?

I: the minimal polynomial is \(x^k\) with \(k\le n\). II: \((I+N)^{-1}=I-N+N^2-\cdots\). IV: all eigenvalues are \(0\). III fails: \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\) has square \(I\).

Question 47 of 120Linear Algebra

If \(A\) is a \(4\times4\) real matrix of rank \(3\), the rank of \(\operatorname{adj}A\) is

\(A\operatorname{adj}A=\det A\cdot I=0\), so \(\operatorname{range}(\operatorname{adj}A)\subseteq\ker A\), which has dimension \(1\). Some \(3\times3\) minor of \(A\) is non-zero, so \(\operatorname{adj}A\neq0\). Rank \(1\) (confirmed on examples).

Question 49 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

\(T(x)=\frac x2+1\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) is a contraction with \(\alpha=\frac12\). Starting at \(x_0=0\), the smallest \(n\) for which the a priori bound \(\dfrac{\alpha^n}{1-\alpha}d(x_1,x_0)\) is below \(10^{-3}\) is

Question 50 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Consider: (i) a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous; (ii) a pointwise limit of continuous functions is continuous; (iii) if \(f_n\to f\) uniformly and each \(f_n\) is differentiable, then \(f\) is differentiable. Which are true?

(i) is the standard theorem. (ii) fails for \(x^n\) on \([0,1]\). (iii) fails: \(\sqrt{x^2+1/n}\to\lvert x\rvert\) uniformly but \(\lvert x\rvert\) is not differentiable at \(0\).

Question 51 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Let \(M\) be a linear subspace of a Hilbert space \(H\). Which statement is true?

\(M^\perp=\bigcap_{m\in M}\{x:\langle x,m\rangle=0\}\) is an intersection of closed sets. The others need \(M\) closed: in general \(M^{\perp\perp}=\overline M\), and a dense proper subspace such as \(c_{00}\subset\ell^2\) has \(M^\perp=\{0\}\) but \(M\ne H\).

Question 52 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Which statement is true?

\((f\circ g)^{-1}(U)=g^{-1}(f^{-1}(U))\) with \(f^{-1}(U)\) open, so it is measurable. For \(g\circ f\) the preimage of a measurable set under a continuous map can be non-measurable. Products of measurable functions are always measurable, so the third statement is false. The supremum statement fails: the supremum of \(\chi_{\{x\}}\) over \(x\in V\) is \(\chi_V\) with \(V\) a Vitali set.

Question 53 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Question 54 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

The total variation of \(f(x)=x^2-4x\) on \([0,5]\) is

Question 55 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Question 56 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

The right shift \(S(x_1,x_2,\dots)=(0,x_1,x_2,\dots)\) on \(\ell^2\) has operator norm

\(\lVert Sx\rVert_2=\lVert x\rVert_2\) for every \(x\), so \(S\) is an isometry and \(\lVert S\rVert=1\). It is bounded, so the norm is finite.

Question 57 of 120Real and Functional Analysis

Which function is uniformly continuous on its stated domain?

\(\lvert\sin x-\sin y\rvert\le\lvert x-y\rvert\) (Lipschitz). The other three fail: take \(x_n=n+\frac1n,y_n=n\) for \(x^2\); \(\frac1n,\frac1{n+1}\) for \(1/x\); and points where \(\sin(1/x)\) equals \(1\) and \(-1\) for the third.

Question 58 of 120Complex Analysis

Which of the following functions is analytic on the whole of \(\mathbb C\)?

\(\cosh z=\frac{e^z+e^{-z}}2\) is entire. \(\tan z\) has poles at \(\frac\pi2+n\pi\), \(\operatorname{Log}z\) is not defined (or analytic) on \((-\infty,0]\), and \(\frac1{z^2+1}\) has poles at \(\pm i\).

Question 59 of 120Complex Analysis

The number of distinct values of \(1^{\sqrt2}\) is

\(1^{\sqrt2}=e^{\sqrt2\log1}=e^{2\pi ik\sqrt2}\), \(k\in\mathbb Z\). Since \(\sqrt2\) is irrational no two values coincide, so there are infinitely many (only \(k=0\) gives \(1\)).

Question 60 of 120Complex Analysis

Is there a function \(f\) analytic on \(|z|<1\) with \(f(1/n)=\dfrac{(-1)^n}{n}\) for all integers \(n\ge2\)?

On \(z_n=\frac1{2n}\), \(f(z_n)=z_n\), and these points accumulate at \(0\) inside the disc, so \(f(z)\equiv z\) by the identity theorem. But then \(f(\frac13)=\frac13\ne-\frac13\), a contradiction.

Question 61 of 120Complex Analysis

\(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{5+4\sin\theta}=\)

For \(\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+b\sin\theta}\) with \(a>|b|\) the value is \(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{25-16}}=\frac{2\pi}3\). Via \(z=e^{i\theta}\) the only pole inside \(|z|=1\) is \(z=-\frac i2\).

Question 62 of 120Complex Analysis

The function \(f(z)=z^3-3z\) is analytic everywhere but fails to be conformal exactly at

A map analytic at \(z_0\) is conformal there iff \(f'(z_0)\ne0\). \(f'(z)=3z^2-3=0\) at \(z=\pm1\).

Question 63 of 120Complex Analysis

Assertion (A): \(f(z)=\sin(1/z)\) has zeros at \(z=\frac1{n\pi}\), which accumulate at \(0\), yet \(f\not\equiv0\). Reason (R): The identity theorem needs the accumulation point to lie in the domain of analyticity, and \(0\) is not in the domain \(\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\).

Question 65 of 120Complex Analysis

The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra (every non-constant polynomial has a root in \(\mathbb C\)) is most directly proved by applying Liouville’s theorem to

Question 67 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

\(\bar\gamma'=3t(\cos t,\sin t)\), speed \(3t\); the involute of a plane curve has curvature \(1/\lvert c-s\rvert\) and here \(\kappa=\dfrac1{at}=\dfrac1{3t}\). At \(t=2\): \(\dfrac16\). (The circle itself has curvature \(\tfrac13\).)

Question 68 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

In the indiscrete topology on a set \(X\) with at least two points, the constant sequence \((a,a,a,\dots)\) converges to

The only neighbourhood of any point is \(X\) itself, which contains every term of the sequence. So the sequence converges to every point.

Question 69 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Which of the following is NOT necessarily preserved when a continuous map \(f:X\to Y\) is applied?

Continuous images of compact, connected and path-connected sets are compact, connected and path-connected. The image of a closed set need not be closed: \(x\mapsto\tfrac1{1+x^2}\) maps the closed set \(\mathbb R\) onto \((0,1]\).

Question 70 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

The Sorgenfrey plane \(\mathbb R_\ell\times\mathbb R_\ell\) is

\(\mathbb R_\ell\) is regular and a product of regular spaces is regular. The antidiagonal \(\{(x,-x)\}\) is an uncountable closed discrete set, and the plane is separable; the standard argument shows it is not normal. So normality is not productive.

Question 71 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

The dimension of the Grassmannian \(G(2,5)\) of \(2\)-planes in \(\mathbb R^5\) is

\(\dim G(k,n)=k(n-k)\); a chart near a plane is given by the \(2\times3\) matrices expressing it as a graph over a coordinate \(2\)-plane. So \(2\cdot3=6\).

Question 72 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

In which topology on \(\prod_\alpha X_\alpha\) can the product fail to be compact even though every \(X_\alpha\) is compact?

Tychonoff’s theorem is for the product topology (which is the coarsest topology making the projections continuous, i.e. pointwise convergence). In the box topology \(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\) is an uncountable discrete space, so it is not compact.

Question 73 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

For the helicoid \(\mathbf r=(u\cos v,\,u\sin v,\,2v)\), the Gaussian curvature at \(u=2\) is

For \(\mathbf r=(u\cos v,u\sin v,cv)\), \(K=-\dfrac{c^2}{(c^2+u^2)^2}\). With \(c=2\), \(u=2\): \(K=-\dfrac{4}{64}=-\dfrac1{16}\). A helicoid is a ruled minimal surface, so \(K\le0\) everywhere.

Question 74 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Consider: (i) a shortest path between two points on a surface is a geodesic; (ii) every geodesic is a shortest path between its endpoints; (iii) a geodesic has constant speed; (iv) every circle of latitude on a sphere is a geodesic. Which statements are correct?

(i) True. (ii) False: the long arc of a great circle is a geodesic but not the shortest path. (iii) True: \(\lVert\gamma'\rVert\) is constant for geodesics. (iv) False: only the equator is a geodesic.

Question 75 of 120Topology and Differential Geometry

Question 76 of 120Differential Equations

\(\phi_5=\sin5x\) has zeros at \(\frac{k\pi}{5}\), \(k=1,\dots,4\) in \((0,\pi)\). The \(n\)-th eigenfunction has \(n-1\) interior zeros.

Question 80 of 120Differential Equations

The singular integral of \(z=px+qy+pq\) is

Complete integral \(z=ax+by+ab\). \(\partial_a\): \(x+b=0\); \(\partial_b\): \(y+a=0\). So \(a=-y,\ b=-x\) and \(z=-xy-xy+xy=-xy\). Option C is the singular integral of \(z=px+qy+p^2+q^2\).

Question 81 of 120Differential Equations

Clairaut with \(f(p)=1/p\). \(x=-f'(p)=1/p^2\) and \(y=px+1/p=2/p\), so \(y^2=4/p^2=4x\).

Question 82 of 120Differential Equations
